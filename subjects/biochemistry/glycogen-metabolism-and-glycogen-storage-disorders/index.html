Sample MCQ #1

A 5-year-old boy presents with hepatomegaly, hypoglycaemia, and ketosis. What is the diagnosis?

AMucopolysaccharidosis
BGlycogen storage disorder
CLipopolysaccharidosis
DDiabetes mellitus
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Glycogen storage disorder

Patients with type I GSD may present in the neonatal period with hypoglycemia and lactic acidosis These children often have doll-like faces with fat cheeks, relatively thin extremities, short stature, and a protuberant abdomen that is due to massive hepatomegaly; the kidneys are also enlarged, whereas the spleen and heart are normal The biochemical hallmarks of type 1 GSD (von Gierke's disease) are hypoglycemia, lactic acidosis, hyperuricemia, and hyperlipidemia. Reference: Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry, 32 nd Edition, Page Number-538.

Sample MCQ #2

During starvation, which of the following hormones is primarily produced to maintain blood glucose levels?

AGlucagon
BInsulin
CEpinephrine
DCortisol
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Glucagon

Explanation: Glucagon helps maintain blood glucose levels during starvation by stimulating gluconeogenesis and glycogenolysis. During starvation, insulin levels decrease and counterregulatory hormones increase. (Option B ruled out) Hormones are released based on their glycemic threshold in the order: Glucagon&rarr;Epinephrine&rarr;Cortisol & GH. (Option C & D ruled out) Glucagon acts on liver, stimulating gluconeogenesis and glycogenolysis. Thus, maintaining blood glucose levels during fasting and starvation. Key Takeaway: During starvation, glucagon is the primary hormone released in response to hypoglycemia. It maintains blood glucose levels by stimulating heapatic gluconeogenesis and glycogenolysis.

Sample MCQ #5

In the normal resting state of humans, most of the blood glucose brunt as 'fuel' is consumed by:

ALiver
BBrain
CAdipose tissue
DMuscles
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Brain

Between meals, the liver acts to maintain the blood glucose concentration by breaking down glycogen (glycogenolysis) and, together with the kidney, by converting noncarbohydrate metabolites such as lactate, glycerol, and amino acids to glucose (gluconeogenesis). The maintenance of an adequate blood concentration of glucose is essential for those tissues for which it is either the major fuel (the brain) or the only fuel (erythrocytes). Reference: Harper's 32 nd edition, page no. 170

Sample MCQ #6

Acetone is increase in serum of a patient of von gierke's disease, so what is the reasons for ketosis in this disease are all except:

AHypoglycemia
BOxaloacetate is necessary for gluconeogenesis
CLow blood glucose less than 40 mg%
DFatty acid mobilization is low
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Fatty acid mobilization is low

Glucose-6 phosphatase deficiency leads to hypoglycaemia. This enzyme is required for both glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis, the two pathways by which blood glucose levels are maintained. Pyruvate and lactate are converted to oxaloacetate for gluconeogenesis. Due to hypoglycaemia, there is high mobilisation of fatty acid from adipose tissue. Hypoglycaemia lowers blood insulin levels, which is an inhibitor of hormone sensitive lipase (HSL); low insulin levels result in loss of this inhibition, and TG stores in adipocytes are broken down by the action of HSL. Reference : Harper's 32 nd edition, Page no.179

Sample MCQ #8

Where can glucose 6 phosphate not be converted to glucose ?

AMuscle
BLiver
CAdipose tissue
DKidney
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Muscle

Ans. is 'a' i.e., Muscle [Ref : Harper 250 le p. 178-179] Glucose-6-phosphatase is absent in muscles therefore, glucose-6- phosphate cannot be degraded to free glucose in muscles. Moreover, glucose-6-phosphate cannot diffuse out of the muscles. Therefore, muscle cannot provide glucose to maintain blood glucose level. Rather, muscle glycogen acts as a source of energy; the glucose-6-phosphate enters the glycolysis to produce energy.
