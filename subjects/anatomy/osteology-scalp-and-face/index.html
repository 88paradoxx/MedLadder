Sample MCQ #1

Which of the following foramina pierce the sphenoid bone?

AThe foramen ovale
BThe foramen ovale and rotundum
CThe foramen ovale, rotundum and spinosum
DThe foramen ovale, rotundum and spinosum and foramen lacerum
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. The foramen ovale, rotundum and spinosum

Explanation: The greater wing of the sphenoid bone extends from the sphenoid body in a lateral, superior, and posterior direction. It contributes to the floor of the middle cranial fossa , lateral wall of the skull , and posterolateral wall of the orbit . Three key foramina pierce the greater wing of the sphenoid: Foramen rotundum &rarr; transmits the maxillary nerve Foramen ovale &rarr; transmits the mandibular nerve Foramen spinosum &rarr; transmits the middle meningeal vessels These foramina are critical anatomical landmarks and are involved in neurovascular transmission. Explanation of Other Options Option A & B: Incomplete - omit one or more foramina that pierce the sphenoid. Option D - Includes foramen lacerum: Incorrect - the foramen lacerum is located between the sphenoid, temporal, and occipital bones and is not a true opening through the sphenoid bone.

Sample MCQ #2

Which of the following structure is situated internally on the left of Pterion ?

AOptic nerve
BBroca's area
CCircle of Willis
DSylvian fissure
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Broca's area

Internally, the pterion is related to various anatomical structures. Namely, the anterior division of the middle meningeal vessels, middle cerebral vessels, Sylvain fissure, circle of Willis, insula and Broca's motor speech area (on the left) and optic nerve. Reference: Textbook of Gray's Anatomy, Volume 2, 3rd Edition, Page number - 858

Sample MCQ #3

All of the following muscles help in elevation of the temporo-mandibular joint, except:

AMedial pterygoid
BLateral pterygoid
CMasseter
DTemporalis
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Lateral pterygoid

Explanation: The lateral pterygoid does not elevate the mandible. Its primary function is depression (opening the mouth) and protrusion of the mandible. It actively pulls the condyle and articular disc forward during mouth opening. Movements of the TM Joint and Muscle Actions: Elevation (closing the mouth): Masseter (Option C) Temporalis (especially anterior and middle fibers) (Option D) Medial pterygoid (Option A) Depression (opening the mouth): Lateral pterygoid Assisted by digastric, mylohyoid, and geniohyoid Protrusion: Lateral pterygoid (primary) Medial pterygoid (assists) Retraction: Posterior fibers of temporalis Deep fibers of masseter Side-to-side movements: Alternating contraction of medial and lateral pterygoids
