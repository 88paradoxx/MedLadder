Sample GI Tract MCQs with answers

Sample MCQ #1

Which of the following statements about the developing duodenum is NOT true?

AIt is a derivative of the foregut and the midgut
BThe yolk stalk is attached to the apex of the duodenal loop
CIt is supplied by branches of the foregut and midgut arteries
DIt becomes C-shaped as it develops and the stomach rotates
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. The yolk stalk is attached to the apex of the duodenal loop

During development, it is the MIDGUT to which the yolk stalk or vitelline duct is attached. Duodenum is both foregut & midgut in origin, so both celiac & superior mesenteric arteries supply it. During duodenal development, epithelial growth temporarily closes its lumen. Reference - Textbook of Langman's, 12th Edition, Digestive System Embryology Page number - 211

Sample MCQ #2

How much does the midgut rotate around the superior mesenteric artery?

A270&deg; counterclockwise
B180&deg; counterclockwise
C270&deg; clockwise
D180&deg; clockwise
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. 270&deg; counterclockwise

The midgut normally undergoes a 270&deg; counterclockwise rotation around the axis formed by the superior mesenteric artery. The midgut forms a U-shaped loop (midgut loop) that herniates through the primitive umbilical ring into the extra-embryonic celom in the umbilicus. During the development of the intestines, the midgut loop rotates a total of 270&deg; counter-clockwise around the superior mesenteric artery. Reference - Textbook of Langman's, 12th Edition, Digestive System Embryology Page number - 208

Sample MCQ #3

Which of the following arteries supply derivatives of the caudal portion of the foregut?

ACeliac trunk
BInferior mesenteric artery
CPulmonary artery
DUmbilical artery
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Celiac trunk

Celiac trunk is the artery of the caudal portion of the foregut, supplying the stomach, liver, gall bladder, pancreas and upper portion of the duodenum. The superior mesenteric is considered the artery of the midgut, and inferior mesenteric is considered the artery of the hindgut. Reference - Textbook of Langman's, 12th Edition, Digestive System Embryology Page number - 211

Sample MCQ #4

Which of the following is the artery of the midgut?

ACeliac trunk
BInferior mesenteric
CProper hepatic
DSuperior mesenteric
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Superior mesenteric

The superior mesenteric artery is known as the artery of the midgut because it supplies the midgut from the ampullary region of the second part of the duodenum to the splenic flexure of the large intestine. Incorrect options- Option A- The celiac trunk is the artery of the foregut. Option B- The inferior mesenteric is the artery of the hindgut. Option C- The splenic artery is a branch of the celiac artery, and the proper hepatic artery is a branch of the common hepatic artery, which is a branch of the celiac artery. Reference: Gray's Anatomy for Students: Volume 1, 3rd Edition, Page Number-170.

Sample MCQ #5

As the stomach acquires its adult shape, it rotates around its longitudinal axis. Which of the following events does not result from this rotation?

AThe ventral border of the stomach moves to the right
BThe dorsal border moves to the left
CThe dorsal mesogastrium is carried to the left
DThe dorsal part of the stomach grows more rapidly
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. The dorsal part of the stomach grows more rapidly

During stomach rotation, the original ventral border comes to face to the right & superiorly while the original dorsal border comes to face left & inferiorly. Duodenum rotates to the right, and dorsal mesogastrium is carried to the left. It is true that the dorsal border of stomach grows more rapidly, but this is not related. Reference: Langman's Medi'cal Embryology, 12th Edition, Page Number-211.

Sample MCQ #6

Among the following, which is the final structure to drain lymphatics from the stomach?

ACoeliac nodes
BIntestinal lymph trunk
CPara-aortic nodes
DLumbar lymph trunk
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Intestinal lymph trunk

Among the given options, the intestinal lymph trunk is the last structure to drain lymphatics from the stomach. The intestinal lymph trunk is a major lymphatic channel in the abdomen. It receives lymphatics from the pre-aortic group of lymph nodes such as coeliac nodes, superior mesenteric nodes, and inferior mesenteric nodes. The intestinal lymph trunk finally drains into the cisterna chyli. Reference: Textbook of Gray's Anatomy for students, 3rd Edition, Abdomen, Page number - 164

Sample MCQ #7

During the surgical repair of a hiatal hernia, the celiac branch of the posterior vagal trunk was severed accidentally. The damage to this nerve would affect the muscular movements, as well as some secretory activities, of the gastrointestinal tract (GI). Which segment is least likely to be affected by the nerve damage?

AAscending colon
BCecum
CJejunum
DSigmoid colon
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Sigmoid colon

The vagus nerve supplies parasympathetic fibers to all of the abdominal organs which receive blood from the celiac trunk or superior mesenteric artery. This means that the vagus supplies parasympathetics to the entire GI tract, up to the last part of the transverse colon. The end of transverse colon and all GI structures distal to that point receive parasympathetic innervation from the pelvic splanchnic nerves and blood from the inferior mesenteric artery. So, the ascending colon, cecum, jejunum, and ileum would all be affected by damage to the vagus nerve. The sigmoid colon, which receives parasympathetic innervation from the pelvic splanchnics, would not be affected. Reference: Textbook of Gray's Anatomy for students, 3rd Edition, Abdomen, Page number - 164

Sample MCQ #8

A patient was diagnosed with bleeding ulcer of the lesser curvature of the stomach. Which artery is most likely involved?

AGastroduodenal
BLeft gastric
CLeft gastro-omental (epiploic)
DRight gastro-omental (epiploic)
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Left gastric

The left gastric artery is the artery that supplies the lesser curvature of the stomach (along with the right gastric artery.) These two arteries would be most likely to cause bleeding at the lesser curvature of the stomach. The left gastric is one of the three arteries that comes off of the celiac trunk. The left and right gastro-omental arteries are the two arteries that supply the greater curvature of the stomach. The gastroduodenal artery is a branch off the common hepatic artery that supplies the duodenum, head of the pancreas, and the greater curvature of the stomach. The short gastric arteries are 4 or 5 small arteries from the splenic artery that supply the fundus of the stomach. Reference : Textbook of Gray's Anatomy for students, 3rd Edition, Abdomen, Page number - 158
