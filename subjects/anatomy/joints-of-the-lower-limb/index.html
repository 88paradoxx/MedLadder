Sample MCQ #1

What is not true about the Chopart ligament?

AIt is also known as bifurcate ligament
BIt is a strong Y shaped structure
CIt stabilizes the calcaneocuboid joint
DIt is part of calcaneonavicular ligament
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. It is part of calcaneonavicular ligament

The bifurcate ligament (BL) of the foot, also called Chopart ligament, is a strong 1-shaped structure that stabilizes the calcaneocuboid joint. The BL also stabilizes the talocalcaneonavicular and midtarsal joints, and therefore, has been described as the keystone of the transverse tarsal joint. The ligament is anatomically found on the dorsal surface of the calcaneocuboid joint and has a main proximal stem that attaches to the calcaneal sulcus, which then divides distally to attach to the navicular and dorsomedial aspect of the cuboid. Consequently, the BL has two constituent bands: the calcaneonavicular ligament (CNL) and calcaneocuboid ligament (CCL). Reference: Gray's Anatomy for Students, 3rd Edition, Page Number-668.

During eversion of the foot, which of the following ligaments is most likely to be injured?

AAnterior talofibular
BDeltoid ligament
CPosterior talofibular
DCalcaneofibular
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Deltoid ligament

Explanation: Among the given options, the deltoid ligament, also known as the medial collateral ligament of the ankle, is most likely to be injured during eversion of the foot Inversion injury: An inversion force with the foot in plantar flexion results in the sprain of the anterior talofibular ligament of the ankle. It is the most common ligament injury occurring in the body. Eversion injury: Eversion forces on the foot result in the deltoid ligament injuries. On examination, there is tenderness over the medial malleolus. Explanation of other options: Anterior talofibular (Option A): The most common Injury occurs during Inversion & plantarflexion Posterior talofibular (Option C): Injury happens during inversion Calcaneofibular (Option D): Injury happens during inversion
