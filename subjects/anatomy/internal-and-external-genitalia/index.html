Sample MCQ #2

If the venous drainage of the anal canal above the pectinate line is impaired in a patient with portal hypertension, there may be an increase in blood flow downward to the systemic venous system via anastomoses with the inferior rectal vein, which is a tributary of the:

AExternal iliac
BInferior gluteal
CInferior mesenteric
DInternal pudendal
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Internal pudendal

The rectal venous plexus is one of the four portal/systemic anastomoses. Blood from the portal system can flow into the venous system at this junction. This means that portal blood, from the superior rectal vein, could flow through the rectal venous plexus, into the inferior rectal vein and into the systemic venous drainage. Now, you just need to figure out what the inferior rectal vein drains into. And it drains into the internal pudendal vein. Reference - Textbook of Gray's Anatomy for students, 3rd Edition, Pelvis and Perineum, Page number - 226

Sample MCQ #3

A 20-year-old male came to the emergency department with a complaint of sudden onset of severe pain in his left testicle for 2 hours. He had no history of trauma or fever. On examination, the left testicle is tender on palpation, and the cremasteric reflex is absent on the left side. What is the diagnosis in the above case?

ATesticular torsion
BVaricocele
CHydrocele
DEpididymitis
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Testicular torsion

Explanation: Sudden onset unilateral testicular pain along with an absent cremasteric reflex is the typical presentation of testicular torsion . It mostly occurs without any preceding trauma or fever and is a surgical emergency . The affected testicle appears elevated (Termed as a high-riding testicle ) Varicocele usually presents as a painless , "bag of worms" feeling in the scrotum and does not cause acute severe pain or an absent cremasteric reflex (Option B) . Hydrocele presents as painless swelling of the testis (Option C) . Epididymitis presents with gradual-onset pain, often associated with fever and dysuria, and the cremasteric reflex is usually intact. There is also relief from pain with the elevation of the affected testicle (Option D) .
