Sample MCQ #1

Which is the first site of scar deposition in liver biopsy under the microscope?

APortal triad
BBetween bile canaliculi
CAround central vein
DIn space of Disse
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. In space of Disse

Scar formation may follow very severe acute injury, but occurs more often as a reaction to chronic injury. The principal cell type involved in scar deposition is the peri-sinusoidal hepatic stellate cell. Following liver injury, stellate cells may become activated and convert into highly fibro genic my fibroblasts, which produce the fibrous scar. Stellate cell activation involves complex interactions between Kupffer cells, hepatocytes, and inflammatory cells. Reference: Robbins Basic Pathology, 10 th ed, Pg-639

Sample MCQ #3

Lewis triple response is caused by:

AHistamine
BAxon reflex
CInjury to endothelium
DIncrease vascular permeability
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Histamine

Histamine is vasoactive amine that is located in most body tissues but is highly concentrated in the lungs, skin and gastrointestinal tract. It is stored in mast cell and basophils. When it is injected intradermally, it causes the triple response: Red spot: due to dilatation Wheal: due to exudation of fluid from capillaries Flare: Redness in surrounding area due to arterial dilatation Reference: Goodman and Gilman, 10 th ed, Pg: 650.

Sample MCQ #4

All of the followings are the promoter of inflammation EXCEPT:

ALipoxins
BLeukotrienes
CProstaglandins
DPlatelet activating factor
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Lipoxins

Arachidonic acid metabolites(prostaglandins and leukotrienes), platelet activating factors are involved in vascular reactions, leukocyte chemotaxis and other reactions of inflammation; antagonized by lipoxins. Lipoxins also are generated from arachidonic acid by the lipoxygenase pathway, but unlike prostaglandins and leukotrienes, the lipoxins suppress inflammation by inhibiting the recruitment of leukocytes. Reference: Robbins and Cotran Pathologic Basis of Diseases, 10 th ed, Pg: 78.

Sample MCQ #6

In hepatitis B 'Ground glass' hepatocytes are seen. BECAUSE In hepatitis B endoplasmic reticulum is swollen by HBsAg.

AIf both statements are true and causally related
BIf both statements are true but not causally related
CIf the first statement is true and second is false
DIf the first statement is false and second is true
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. If both statements are true and causally related

In chronic hepatitis B, 'ground-glass' hepatocytes (cells with endoplasmic reticulum swollen by HBsAg) are a diagnostic hallmark, and the presence of viral antigen in these cells can be confirmed by immunostaining. 'ground-glass' hepatocytes are liver cells which have eosinophilic granular, glassy cytoplasm on light microscopy. This appearance corresponds to a proliferated smooth endoplasmic reticulum ultrastructure. These changes may be drug-induced or associated with hepatitis b antigenaemia, particularly in carriers. Reference: Robbins Basic Pathology, 10 th ed, Pg-649

Sample MCQ #8

A 58-year-old male patient presented with weakness, hematuria and other vague symptoms. Proper clinical examination shows presence of palpable purpura. Laboratory investigations performed show presence of proteinuria (2+) and MPO-ANCA positivity. Histopathological examination of the vessel shows fibrinoid necrosis without granuloma as shown below. Based on the above histopathological findings, what is your probable diagnosis?

AGiant cell arteritis
BKawasaki disease
CPolyarteritis nodosa
DMicroscopic polyangiitis
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Microscopic polyangiitis

Microscopic polyangiitis is a necrotizing vasculitis that generally affects capillaries as well as small arterioles and venules. Unlike PAN, all lesions of microscopic polyangiitis tend to be of the same age in any given patient, suggesting a single episode of antibody or immune complex deposition. It is also called hypersensitivity vasculitis or leukocytoclastic vasculitis. The skin, mucous membranes, lungs, brain, heart, gastrointestinal tract, kidneys, and muscle all can be involved. Most cases are associated with MPO-ANCA positivity. Microscopic polyangiitis is characterized by segmental fibrinoid necrosis of the media with focal transmural necrotizing lesions; granulomatous inflammation is absent. Reference : Robbins 10th Ed pg 514
