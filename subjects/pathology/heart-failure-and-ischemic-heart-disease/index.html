Sample MCQ #1

A 65-year-old man dies due to a myocardial infarction. Which stains can be used to see the infarct in the heart while conducting an autopsy?

AOil Red O
BTriphenyl tetrazolium chloride
CSudan black B
DMasson's trichrome
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Triphenyl tetrazolium chloride

Explanation: Triphenyl tetrazolium chloride (TTC) is a histochemical stain frequently used in autopsies to detect myocardial infarction (MI) . Within the first 4 hours of MI, gross examination may not reveal any visible infarct. However, when tissue slices are immersed in TTC solution, the stain differentiates viable from necrotic myocardium: viable myocardial tissue with intact lactate dehydrogenase activity stains brick-red , while infarcted areas remain pale due to leakage of dehydrogenases from damaged cell membranes.

Sample MCQ #2

A patient is suspected of having a myocardial infarction. A triphenyl tetrazolium chloride (TTC) stain is used to identify myocardial ischemic damage. When is the best time to use TTC staining for optimal visualisation of myocardial necrosis?

A<4 hours
B2-3 hours
C10-24 hours
D36 hours
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. 2-3 hours

Explanation: Myocardial infarcts that are less than 12 hours old are typically not grossly visible. However, those older than 3 hours (Option A ruled out) can be identified using vital stains like triphenyl tetrazolium chloride (TTC), which is a substrate for lactate dehydrogenase. Infarcts can be grossly visualised as red-blue areas due to trapped blood by 12 to 24 hours (Option C and D ruled out) .

Sample MCQ #3

A 46-year-old male presented in the emergency room with sudden onset of chest pain. The ECG done shows normal wave pattern. Analysis of which pair of serum markers given below would be most helpful in excluding a diagnosis of acute myocardial infarction in this patient?

ACardiac troponin-I and myoglobin
BCK-BB and myoglobin
CCK-MB and cardiac troponin-I
DCK-MM and lactate dehydrogenase (LDH)-1
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. CK-MB and cardiac troponin-I

The laboratory evaluation of MI is based on measuring the blood levels of proteins that leak out of irreversibly damaged myocytes; previously, this involved measurement of the MB fraction of creatine kinase (CK- MB). Currently, the most clinically useful biomarkers of myocardial damage are cardiac-specific troponins T and I (cTnT and cTnI), proteins that normally regulate calcium- mediated contraction of cardiac muscle. Reference : Robbins 10th Ed pg 550

Sample MCQ #4

All the following are microscopic features of acute pulmonary congestion EXCEPT:

ABlood-engorged alveolar capillaries
BAlveolar septal oedema.
CIntra alveolar haemorrhage
DAlveolar septal fibrosis.
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Alveolar septal fibrosis.

Cut surfaces of hyperemic or congested tissues feel wet and typically ooze blood. On microscopic examination, acute pulmonary congestion is marked by blood-engorged alveolar capillaries and variable degrees of alveolar septal edema and intraalveolar hemorrhage. In chronic pulmonary congestion, the septa become thickened and fibrotic, and the alveolar spaces contain numerous macrophages laden with hemosiderin (&ldquo;heart failure cells&rdquo;) derived from phagocytosed red cells. Reference: Robbins and Cotran Pathologic Basis of Diseases, 10&#7511;&#688; ed Pg:97

Sample MCQ #6

Which of the following is not true regarding Ranolazine?

AUseful in patients with refractory chronic angina
BInhibitor of If current in the SA node cells
CAdministered at a dose of 500-1000 mg/day
DContraindicated in patients with QTc prolongation
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Inhibitor of If current in the SA node cells

Ranolazine inhibits the late sodium current in myocardial cells. It is contraindicated in patients with liver dysfunction and QTc prolongation. Ivabradine helps control the heart rate by inhibiting the funny current (If) in the SA node cells. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition

Sample MCQ #7

A man who is on antihypertensives has several symptoms and his medications require reviewing. Which one of the following is a complication of thiazide diuretic therapy?

ANeutrophilia
BHyperkalaemia
CGynaecomastia
DHyperuricemia
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Hyperuricemia

Side effects of thiazide diuretics: Hypokalaemia(Blocking NaCl channels) Hyponatremia Hypercalcaemia Hyperglycemia hyperuricemia Hyperkalemia is caused by ACE inhibitors and potassium sparing diuretics Loop diuretics can also cause hyperuricemia and gout. Gynaecomastia is caused by spironolactone. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st ed, Ch: 277, Pg:20.
