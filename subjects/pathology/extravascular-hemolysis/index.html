Which is/are the factors affect rate and degree of sickling in sickle cell disease? i Mean corpuscular hemoglobin concentration ii Haemoglobin S level iii Haemoglobin F level iv Mean corpuscular hemoglobin

AOnly i, ii, and iii are correct
BOnly i and iii are correct
COnly ii and iv are correct
DOnly iv is correct

✓ Correct Answer: A. Only i, ii, and iii are correct

Following factors determine the rate at which the polymerization of HbS and consequent sickling take place: Presence of non HbS hemoglobin Intracellular concentration of HbS Total hemoglobin concentration Extent of deoxygenation Acidosis and dehydration Increased concentration of 2, 3 BPG in the red cells Reference: Harsh mohan, 6 th edition, page number-319

Sample MCQ #2

In a patient suffering with sickle cell anemia, eletrophoretic mobility of HbS in relation to HbA will be?

ARetarded
BAccelerated
CSame
DWill depend upon concentration of HbS
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Retarded

Pathogenesis of Sickle cell anemia: Sequence of codon 6 of the Beta-chain changed from GAG in the normal gene to GTG in the sickle cell gene, resulting in substitution of valine for glutamic acid Concept: So if you see, the charge of amino acid is changing from charged (glutamic acid, more negative) to neutral amino acid (valine), so when we do an electrophoresis, the mobility of HbS will be lesser than HbA towards Anode (i.e retarded) (which is positively charged). Reference: Complete review of Pathology 1st/343

Sample MCQ #3

Which of the following test guide us to the hereditary spherocytosis?

AMeasurement of folate and B12 concentration
BHemoglobin electrophoresis
CDirect Coombs test
DOsmotic fragility testing
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Osmotic fragility testing

This disorder stems from inherited (intrinsic) defects in the red cell membrane that lead to the formation of spherocytes, nondeformable cells that are highly vulnerable to sequestration and destruction in the spleen. Hereditary spherocytosis is usually transmitted as an autosomal dominant trait; a more severe, autosomal recessive form of the disease affects a small minority of patients. The characteristic features are anemia, splenomegaly, and jaundice. The anemia is variable in severity, ranging from subclinical to profound; most commonly it is moderate in degree. Because of their spherical shape, red cells in hereditary spherocytosis show increased osmotic fragility when placed in hypotonic salt solutions, a characteristic that can help establish the diagnosis. Reference: Robbins, 10 th edition, page number445
