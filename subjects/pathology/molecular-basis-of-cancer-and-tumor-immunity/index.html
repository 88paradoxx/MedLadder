Sample MCQ #1

Cancer cell survival is enhanced by which of the following events? i. Overexpression of p53 gene ii. Suppression of p53 protein iii. BAX iv. BCL-2

AOnly i, ii and iii are correct
BOnly i and iii are correct
COnly ii and iv are correct
DOnly iv is correct
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Only ii and iv are correct

Activation of p53 leads to cell cycle arrest and it also directs the transcription of several pro-apoptotic genes such as BAX and PUMA which are believed to tip the balance in favour of cell death via intrinsic pathway. Reference: Robbins and Cotran Pathologic Basis of Diseases, 10 th ed, Pg: 295.

Sample MCQ #2

Which of the following gene is the Governor of proliferation of the cells in neoplasia?

AP53
BRAS
CRB
DNF-1
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. RB

RB , a key negative regulator of the cell cycle, is directly or indirectly inactivated in most human cancers. The function of the RB protein is to regulate the G1/S checkpoint , the portal through which cells must pass before DNA replication commences. RB exerts anti-proliferative effects by controlling the G1-to-S transition of the cell cycle. Reference: Robbins and Cotran Pathologic Basis of Diseases, 10 th ed, Pg: 208-211.

BOnly i and iii are correct

✓ Correct Answer: A. Only i, ii and iii are correct

UV radiation decreases its activity and causes cell cycle arrest. p53, also known as Tumor protein P53, cellular tumor antigen p53 or transformation-related protein 53 is a regulatory protein that is often mutated in human cancers. The p53 proteins are crucial in vertebrates, where they prevent cancer formation. Activated p53 promotes cell cycle arrest to allow DNA repair and/or apoptosis to prevent the propagation of cells with serious DNA damage through the transactivation of its target genes implicated in the induction of cell cycle arrest and/or apoptosis. Reference: Robbins and Cotran Pathologic Basis of Diseases, 10 th ed, Pg: 211.

Sample MCQ #5

The tumour suppressor gene p53 induces to inhibit cell cycle at which phase?

AG2M phase
BG0-G1 phase
CG1-S phase
DG0 phase
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. G1-S phase

The tumour suppressor gene that is most commonly affected by point mutations in cancer is TP53, a prototypical "guardian" type tumour suppressor gene. DNA damage leads to activation of p53 by phosphorylation. Activated p53 drives transcription of CDKN1A(p21), which prevents RB phosphorylation, thereby causing a G1-S block in the cell cycle. This pause allows the cells to repair the DNA damage. If the DNA can be repaired, p53 activates other genes to fix the damage. If the DNA cannot be repaired, this protein prevents the cell from dividing and signals it to undergo apoptosis. By stopping cells with mutated or damaged DNA from dividing, p53 helps prevent the development of tumors. Reference: Robbins and Cotran Pathologic Basis of Diseases, 10 th Edition, Page Number 212 & 213.

Sample MCQ #7

Which of the following statement is NOT TRUE for Telomerase enzyme?

AIs present in germ cells
BIs responsible for telomere shortening
CPresent in less amounts in stem cells
DImmortalizes cancer cells by maintaining the telomere length
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Is responsible for telomere shortening

Unregulated proliferation in incipient tumours leads to telomere shortening, followed by chromosomal instability and the accumulation of mutations. If telomerase is then reactivated in these cells, telomeres are extended and these mutations become fixed, contributing to tumour growth. The telomere acts as a buffer to ensure the important genetic information coded for on the chromosome is completely copied for each new cell and protected from damage. Over time, this results in fewer and fewer DNA repeats making up the telomeres after each cell division i.e., gradually the telomeres shorten. Reference: Robbins and Cotran Pathologic Basis of Diseases, 10 th ed, Pg: 219.
