Sample Gall Bladder and Pancreas MCQs with answers

Sample MCQ #1

A 45-year-old male patient who is chronic alcoholic drinker and also having uncontrolled diabetes since 5 years presented with sudden onset on pain abdomen and vomiting. Pain is intense and referred to back. Patient was sent for ultrasound, which revealed diagnosis of acute pancreatitis and conservative management was done. If biopsy was taken at this stage, what will be the histopathological findings you observe?

AParenchymal fibrosis
BAcinar atrophy
CVariable sized duct dilation
DNecrotizing pancreatitis
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Necrotizing pancreatitis

(a, b, c) Chronic pancreatitis is characterized by parenchymal fibrosis, acinar atrophy and dropout, and variable ductal dilation. (d) In acute necrotizing pancreatitis, acini, ducts, and even islets undergo necrosis. In the most severe form, hemorrhagic pancreatitis, extensive parenchymal necrosis is accompanied by intraparenchymal hemorrhage due to vascular injury. Reference: Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Diseases 10 th edition Chapter 18, pg 882

Sample MCQ #2

A 40-year-old female have gall stones since 5 years. Now since 2 weeks she had intermittent pain abdomen. Now she presented in emergency with acute severe pain in the right upper quadrant associated with mild fever, nausea, vomiting and tachycardia. He underwent laparoscopic cholecystectomy and specimen sent for histopathological examination. What are the histological findings to be seen in chronic cholecystitis?

AMural hypertrophy
BMild to moderate lymphocytic infiltration
CSubserosal fibrosis
DAll of the above
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. All of the above

All of these are features of chronic cholecystitis.

Sample MCQ #3

Empyema of the gallbladder and Gangrenous cholecystitis are seen in-

AAcute Calculous Cholecystitis
BAcute Acalculous Cholecystitis
CChronic Cholecystitis
DNone of the above
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Acute Calculous Cholecystitis

In acute cholecystitis, the gallbladder usually is enlarged and tense, and has a bright red or blotchy, violaceous colour, the latter imparted by subserosal hemorrhages. The serosa frequently is covered by a fibrinous or, in severe cases, a fibrinopurulent exudate. In 90% of cases, stones are present, often obstructing the neck of the gallbladder or the cystic duct. The gallbladder lumen is filled with cloudy or turbid bile that may contain fibrin, blood, and pus. When the contained exudate is mostly pus, the condition is referred to as empyema of the gallbladder. In mild cases, the gallbladder wall is thickened, edematous, and hyperemic. In more severe cases, the gallbladder wall is green-black and necrotic-a condition termed gangrenous cholecystitis. Reference: Robbins Basic Pathology, 10 th ed, Pg-674
