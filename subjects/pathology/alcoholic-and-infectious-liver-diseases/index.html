Sample MCQ #1

A patient presented with cirrhosis. On liver biopsy showed eosinophilic material cytokeratin positive. This is most likely composed of:

AActin
BMicrotubule
CIntermediate filaments
DFibronectin
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Intermediate filaments

Mallory hyaline are tangled skeins of intermediate filaments such as keratin 8 and keratin 18 that are partially degraded and ubiquitinated Reference: Robin's basic pathology 10 th edition, 842

Sample MCQ #2

Phenomenon of Quasi-species is seen in which viral hepatitis?

AHepatitis A
BHepatitis B
CHepatitis C
DHepatitis D
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Hepatitis C

Because of the low fidelity of the HCV RNA polymerase, the viral genome is inherently unstable, giving rise to new genetic variants at a high pace. This has led to the appearance of six major HCV genotypes worldwide, each with one or more "subspecies." Infections in most individuals are due to a virus of a single genotype, but new genetic variants are generated in the host as long as viral replication persists. As a result, each patient usually comes to be infected with a population of divergent but closely related HCV variants known as quasi species. Reference: Robbins Basic Pathology, 10 th ed, Pg-646

Sample MCQ #3

A 37-year-old male presents to an acute care clinic with complaints of abdominal pain, which began a few days ago. His vital signs are a temperature of 98.7&deg;F, pulse of 101 bpm, respiratory rate of 19 breaths per minute, and blood pressure of 129/82 mm Hg. A physical examination reveals an enlarged and tender liver. Laboratory testing reveals an AST of 271 U/L, ALT of 46 U/L, GGT of 190 U/L, and alkaline phosphatase of 210 U/L. Of the following, what is the most likely diagnosis?

AViral hepatitis
BAlcoholic hepatitis
CAcetaminophen toxicity
DAcute Cholecystitis
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Alcoholic hepatitis

The individual has laboratory evidence of liver damage, with elevated AST, ALT, GGT, and alkaline phosphatase. The AST: ALT ratio is around 6:1, which is most characteristic of an alcoholic hepatitis. Reference: Robbins Basic Pathology, 10 th ed, Pg-652

Sample MCQ #4

A 42-year-old male labourer consulted a doctor because he noticed yellow discoloration of sclera and also gives history of passing yellow urine since few days. He was advised complete blood counts, liver function test and a liver biopsy was undertaken. The liver biopsy shows yellowish brown pigment on H & E stain. Which of the following is the most likely pigment?

AMelanin pigment
BBile pigment
CHomogentisic acid pigments
DPorphyrin Pigments
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Bile pigment

The condition reflects Jaundice with Hyperbilirubinemia. Bilirubin is the normal non-iron containing pigment present in the bile. Reference: Robbins and Cotran Pathologic Basis of Diseases, 10 th ed Pg: 52-53

Sample MCQ #5

A healthcare worker is seropositive for HBsAg, HBeAg, and anti-HBc IgM, indicating acute HBV infection with high infectivity. Which liver biopsy feature is least likely to be seen?

ABallooning change of hepatocytes
BGround glass hepatocytes
CFocal or spotty necrosis
DConfluent necrosis of hepatocytes
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Ground glass hepatocytes

Explanation: In acute hepatitis B, biopsy findings typically include ballooning degeneration, focal necrosis, and confluent necrosis in severe cases. Ground-glass hepatocytes, however, are absent in acute infection and instead signify chronic HBV infection. Explanation of other options: Ballooning change (Option A): Seen in acute hepatitis as hepatocyte swelling and degeneration. Focal or spotty necrosis (Option C): Common in acute HBV, involving scattered apoptotic hepatocytes. Confluent necrosis (Option D): Seen in severe acute hepatitis with widespread cell death. Key Takeaways: Acute HBV is marked by hepatocyte ballooning and necrosis, without ground-glass cells. Ground-glass hepatocytes are pathognomonic for chronic HBV infection. Serology differentiates acute from chronic HBV.

Sample MCQ #6

A 23-year-old pregnant female presents to the emergency room complaining of pain in the upper portion of the right side of her abdomen. Physical examination reveals a fluid wave and hepatomegaly, with her liver palpable 6 cm below the costal margin. Laboratory testing reveals an AST of 213 U/L, ALT of 178 U/L, and alkaline phosphatase of 52 mg/dL. A complete blood count reveals a hemoglobin of 13.4 mg/dL and a platelet count of 224 * 103 cells/&mu;L. Of the following, what is the most likely diagnosis?

APlacenta accreta
BHELLP syndrome
CBudd-Chiari syndrome
DAcetaminophen toxicity
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Budd-Chiari syndrome

Budd-Chiari syndrome is due to thrombosis of the hepatic veins or inferior vena cava. Risk factors include pregnancy, use of oral contraceptives, Factor V Leiden, and other coagulation disorders. Patients can develop ascites due to the acute increased pressure in the portal system. Reference: Robbins Basic Pathology, 10 th ed, Pg-667

Sample MCQ #7

A 44-year-old female patient presented to the emergency department with history of abdominal pain which is associated frequently on and off vomiting. Ultrasound performed shows presence of gallstones and patient was planned for cholecystectomy. PAC examination shows HBsAg positivity with value of 4.42 IU by ELISA method. Further investigations revealed presence of HBeAg. If Liver biopsy performed at this time, it will show all the following features; except:

APortal inflammation
BLobular inflammation
CGround glass appearance
DHepatocyte necrosis
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Ground glass appearance

HBeAg, HBV DNA, and HBV DNA polymerase are detectable in the serum soon after HBsAg and signify active viral replication. (a & b) Microscopically, there is a portal and lobular inflammatory infiltrate comprised predominantly of lymphocytes and variably admixed with plasma cells and eosinophils. (c) In chronic hepatitis B, the swollen endoplasmic reticulum of hepatocytes are swollen and filled with HBsAg, leading to a "ground-glass" appearance. (d) The hepatocyte injury may result in necrosis or apoptosis. Necrosis of groups of hepatocytes (i.e., confluent necrosis) may be seen in severe cases and can progress to necrosis of the entire lobule (i.e., panlobular or panacinar necrosis) or connect vascular structures (i.e., bridging necrosis). Reference: Robbins 10th Ed, pg 837

Sample MCQ #8

A 42-year-old male patient presented with complaints of nausea fatigability and yellowish discoloration sclera. He gave history that he is taking alcohol in large quantities since long time. On proper clinical evaluation, serum bilirubin level is raised. Liver biopsy was performed and diagnosis of alcoholic hepatitis was made. Which of the following histological features can be seen in this diagnosis?

AMallory hyaline body
BLobular lymphocytic infiltrate
CPericellular/pericellular fibrosis
DAll of the above
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. All of the above

The morphologic findings in alcoholic hepatitis include the following: (a) Ballooned hepatocytes. These are injured, swollen hepatocytes with cleared-out cytoplasm and cytoskeletal damage, which when extensive, results in formation of Mallory hyaline. (b) Lobular lymphocytic infiltrates are common, and portal lymphocytic infiltrates also may occur. (c) Perivenular/pericellular fibrosis. Steatohepatitis is often accompanied by fibrosis. Reference: Robbins 10th Ed, pg 844
