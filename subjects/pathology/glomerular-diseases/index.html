Sample Glomerular Diseases MCQs with answers

Sample MCQ #1

A 35-year-old man complains of recurrent hematuria since his youth. The hematuria typically occurs following upper respiratory tract infections. Vital signs are normal. Urinalysis shows proteinuria, hematuria, and a few red blood cell casts. Laboratory studies disclose normal levels of BUN and creatinine. The ANA and ANCA tests are negative. Which of the characteristic findings that leads to diagnosis of disease is:

APredominantly deposition on IgG in mesangium
BPredominantly deposition on IgG in Subendothelial
CPredominantly deposition on IgA in Subendothelium
DPredominantly deposition on IgA in mesangium
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Predominantly deposition on IgA in mesangium

IgA nephropathy (Berger Disease)- IgA nephropathy, characterized by the presence of prominent IgA deposits in the mesangial regions and recurrent hematuria, is the most common type of glomerulonephritis worldwide. Reference: Robbins 10th Ed pg 922

Sample MCQ #2

Which is the most common mechanism leads to edema in nephrotic syndrome?

ANa+ and water restriction
BIncreased venous pressure
CDecreased serum albumin
DDecreased fibrinogen
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Decreased serum albumin

Nephrotic syndrome is the most important cause of albumin loss from the blood. In diseases that are characterized by nephrotic syndrome, the glomerular capillaries become leaky, leading to the loss of albumin (and other plasma proteins) in the urine and the development of generalized edema. Reference: Robbins and Cotran Pathologic Basis of Diseases, 10&#7511;&#688; ed Pg:99

Sample MCQ #3

A 17-year-old female patient presented with facial puffiness. Physical examination revealed edema. Laboratory findings revealed creatinine of 3.8 mg/dL along with proteinuria. A differential diagnosis of nephrotic syndrome was made. All of these metabolic findings will be seen in this patient; except:

AHypoalbuminemia
BHyperlipidemia
CLoss of anticoagulant
DLoss of fibrinogen
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Loss of fibrinogen

Nephrotic syndrome is caused by a derangement in glomerular capillary walls resulting in increased permeability to plasma proteins. The manifestations of the syndrome include, Massive proteinuria, Hypoalbuminemia, hyperlipidemia, lipiduria along with loss of anticoagulants. Due to low albumin levels, fibrinogen and vWD levels are raised. Reference: Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Diseases 10 th edition Chapter 20, pg 909

Sample MCQ #4

Which of the following would immunofluorescence staining of the kidney with Focal segmental glomerulosclerosis most likely reveal?

AIgG positivity with a granular pattern
BIgG and IgM positivity with a granular pattern
CIgG positivity with a linear pattern
DEntrapped IgM
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Entrapped IgM

FSGS has entrapped IgM and C3 noted by immunofluorescence staining. IgG positivity with a granular pattern is seen in membranous glomerulonephropathy, IgG and IgM positivity in postinfectious glomerulonephritis, and IgG positivity with a linear pattern in type I rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis. IgM positivity with a linear pattern is not normally described in diseases of the kidney. Reference : Robbins Basic Pathology, 10 th edition, Page- 556

Sample MCQ #5

A patient presenting with haemoptysis and renal failure with anti-basement membrane antibodies positives on renal biopsy. What is your diagnosis?

AGood pasture's syndrome
BWegener's granulomatosis
CChurg Strauss disease
DHenoch-scholein purpura
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Good pasture's syndrome

Anti-GBM antibody-mediated crescentic GN (Goodpasture disease) is characterized by linear deposits of IgG and, in many cases, C3 in the GBM. In some patients, the anti-GBM antibodies also bind to pulmonary alveolar capillary basement membranes to produce the clinical picture of pulmonary hemorrhages associated with renal failure. These patients are said to have Goodpasture syndrome. Anti-GBM antibodies are present in the serum and are helpful in diagnosis. It is important to recognize anti-GBM antibody-mediated crescentic GN, because affected individuals benefit from plasmapheresis, which removes pathogenic antibodies from the circulation. Reference : Robbins Basic Pathology, 10th edition, Page- 562-563

The most common nephropathy in malignancy is membranous nephropathy . Reference : Robbins Basic Pathology, 10 th edition, Page- 558
