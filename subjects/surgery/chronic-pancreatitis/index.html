Sample MCQ #1

A 60 year old man presents with severe pain in his left loin radiating to the left groin. Bedside testing of urine demonstrates haematuria. Abdominal ultrasound does not demonstrate any abnormality and X-ray KUB does not show any visible stones. Which form of calculus might the patient have?

ACalcium oxalate stone
BCystine stone
CUric acid stone
DTriple phosphate stone
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Uric acid stone

Radiopaque stones are: Calcium oxalate, calcium phosphate, triple phosphate, cystine stones. Radiolucent stones are: Uric acid, xanthine stones. Uric acid stones can be visualized by helical CT. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st ed, Ch: 318, Pg: 2370.

Sample MCQ #2

A 60 year old man has presented with chronic projectile vomiting and significant weight loss. Which of the following electrolyte abnormality is seen in this patient?

AHypokalemic hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis with hyponatremia
BHypokalemic hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis with hypernatremia
CHypokalemic hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis with hypercalcemia
DHypokalemic hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis with hypomagnesemia
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Hypokalemic hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis with hyponatremia

Metabolic effects Vomiting hydrochloric acid results in hypochloremic alkalosis . Initially the sodium and potassium may be relatively normal; however, as dehydration progresses, more profound metabolic abnormalities arise, partly related to renal dysfunction. Initially, the urine has a low chloride and high bicarbonate content , reflecting the primary metabolic abnormality. With time the patient becomes progressively hyponatremic and more profoundly dehydrated. Because of the dehydration , sodium is retained and potassium and hydrogen ions are excreted . This results in the urine becoming paradoxically acidic and hypokalaemia ensues . Alkalosis leads to a lowering in the circulating ionized calcium, and tetany can occur.

Which among the following is not an aspect of assessing the patient with suspected chronic liver disease?

ADiagnosing of underlying liver disease
BEstimation of functional hepatic reserve
CHepatic hemodynamic evaluation
DSite of lower gastrointestinal bleed
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Site of lower gastrointestinal bleed

The key aspects of assessing a patient with suspected chronic liver disease or complications of portal hypertension are the following - Diagnosis of the underlying liver disease Estimation of functional hepatic reserve - helps to determine whether definitive (e.g., hepatic transplantation) or palliative (e.g., endoscopic variceal ligation or a shunt procedure) treatment is indicated Definition of portal venous anatomy and hepatic hemodynamic evaluation Identification of the site of upper gastrointestinal hemorrhage , if present. Sample MCQ #5

Which of these is incorrectly paired?

AChronic pancreatitis - Manheim score
BAchalasia - Los Angeles classification
CColonoscopy - Boston score
DParathyroid resection - Miami criteria
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Achalasia - Los Angeles classification

Explanation: The Los Angeles (LA) classification grades reflux esophagitis (endoscopic mucosal breaks). Achalasia is classified by high-resolution manometry using the Chicago classification (types I-III). So LA classification does not apply to achalasia Explanation of other options: Chronic pancreatitis (Option A)- M-ANNHEIM : the M-ANNHEIM classification (pronounced "Mannheim") is an accepted classification and scoring system for chronic pancreatitis (etiology, stage, severity). So this pairing is essentially correct (note the common spelling M-ANNHEIM). Colonoscopy - Boston score (Option C) : the Boston Bowel Preparation Scale is used to grade bowel prep quality for colonoscopy. Parathyroid resection - Miami criteria (Option D) : t he Miami intraoperative PTH criteria (drop of >50% from baseline at 10 min) guides adequacy of parathyroidectomy.

Sample MCQ #6

Which among the following is not a risk factor for chronic rejection of kidney transplant?

APoor HLA match
BRaised blood lipids
CCytomegalovirus infection
DShort warm ischaemic time
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Short warm ischaemic time

Long cold ischemic time is a risk factor. Chronic rejection usually occurs after the first 6 months. All types of transplants are susceptible to chronic rejection, and it is the major cause of allograft failure. Interestingly, the liver is more resistant than other organs to the destructive effects of chronic rejection. The risk factors for chronic rejection of a kidney transplant are: Previous episodes of acute rejection Poor HLA match Long cold ischaemia time Cytomegalovirus (CMV) infection Raised blood lipids Inadequate immunosuppression (including poor compliance) The single most important risk factor for chronic rejection after kidney transplantation is acute rejection (with vascular inflammation) and recurrent episodes of acute rejection . Reference : Bailey & Love's short practice of surgery 27th Edition, Chapter 82, Transplantation, Pg: 1536.

Sample MCQ #8

A chronic smoker starts having pain in the calf on walking distance of 400 meters initially. Gradually he starts developing pain on walking distance of 200 meters only. Gradually he is having pain even during sleeping so decides to sleep in sitting position which makes him feel better. What is the likely dioagnosis?

AVenous claudication
BNeurological claudication
CSciatica
DRest pain
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Sciatica

Rest pain As disease progression occurs the claudication distance decreases and perfusion to the leg may be so severely compromised that anaerobic respiration occurs even at rest , typically affecting the foot and/or calf. The pain is exacerbated by lying down or elevation of the foot because of the loss of gravitational effects on the perfusion pressure in the foot. The patient characteristically describes pain that is worse at night and may be lessened by hanging the foot out of bed or by sleeping in a chair (effects of gravity restored). Even the pressure of bedclothes on the foot may exacerbate the pain.
