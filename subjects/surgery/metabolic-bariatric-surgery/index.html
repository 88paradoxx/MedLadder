Sample MCQ #1

The immediate postoperative period is associated with predictable hormonal and metabolic shifts. Several electrolyte and metabolic abnormalities commonly occur, while some do not. Which one of the following is not typically seen in the immediate postoperative period?

ANegative nitrogen balance
BHypokalemia
CGlucose intolerance
DHyponatremia
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Hypokalemia

Explanation: In the immediate postoperative period, patients often develop hyperkalaemia or normal potassium levels due to tissue breakdown, acidosis and decreased renal excretion. Hypokalaemia is not typically seen . Normal metabolic changes in the immediate postoperative period include: Negative nitrogen balance due to catabolic stress hormones (Option A) Glucose intolerance due to increased cortisol, catecholamines and insulin resistance (Option C) Hyponatremia due to ADH surge and water retention (Option D) Potassium tends to be normal or high , not low, because of increased catabolism, acidemia and reduced renal clearance

Sample MCQ #3

Which among the following statements is false for action of thyroid hormones?

AMetabolic effects are due to bound T3 and T4
BT3 is more important physiological hormone
CT3 acts quickly
DT4 acts slowly
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Metabolic effects are due to bound T3 and T4

The metabolic effects of the thyroid hormones are due to unbound free T4 and T3 (0.03% and 0.3% of the total circulating hormones, respectively). T3 is the more important physiological hormone and is also produced in the periphery by conversion from T4. T3 is quick acting (within a few hours), whereas T4 acts more slowly (4-14 days). Reference : Bailey & Love's short practice of surgery, chapter: 55, Edition: 28 th , Pg: 852

Sample MCQ #4

Which of the following is the most common deficiency observed after gastric bypass/bariatric surgery?

AIron
BCopper
CZinc
DCalcium
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Iron

Explanation: Iron deficiency is the most common nutritional deficiency following Roux-en-Y gastric bypass (RYGB). This occurs due to two main reasons: Bypass of the Duodenum: The primary site of iron absorption is bypassed. Reduced Gastric Acid: The new gastric pouch produces little acid, and iron requires an acidic environment for conversion into its absorbable ferrous (Fe2+) form. This often leads to iron-deficiency anemia, presenting as fatigue and pallor. Copper and zinc deficiencies may occur but are less common than iron deficiency, while calcium deficiency is frequent due to reduced intake and vitamin D malabsorption, yet iron deficiency remains the most common. (Options B,C and D ruled out) Key Takeaways: Iron deficiency is the most prevalent micronutrient deficiency after gastric bypass. Lifelong supplementation and monitoring are required after bariatric surgery.

Sample MCQ #5

All of the following metabolic responses are seen in response to starvation except:

AAfter 12 hours of fast liver glycogen converted into glucose.
BAfter 24 hours of fasting, gluconeogenesis started from non-carbohydrate precursors.
CAfter 1week of fasting, Central nervous system adapts to using ketone bodies as their primary Fuel.
DEven though muscle contain glycogen it cannot be utilized directly.
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. After 1week of fasting, Central nervous system adapts to using ketone bodies as their primary Fuel.

Option A : After 12 hours of fast plasma insulin level falls and glucagon level rise which Facilitates conversion of liver glycogen into glucose. Option B : After more than 24 hours of fasting, Gluconeogenesis started from Amino acids particularly glutamine and alanine. Option C : After 48-72 hours not a week, central nervous system may adapt to using ketone bodies as their primary fuel source. Option D : Glycogen also exist in muscle but cannot be utilized directly. Muscle Glycogen is converted to lactate which is then exported to liver where It is converted to glucose. Reference: Bailey & Love's short practice of surgery, chapter: 25, Edition: 28th, Pg: 329

Sample MCQ #6

All of the following are true regarding intussusception except?

AIn majority, the cause is hyperplasia of Peyer's patches
BIn 10% children, there is pathological lead point, such as a Meckel's diverticulum, enteric duplication of cyst or small bowel lymphoma
CChild may pass 'redcurrant jelly stool'
DSausage shaped mass is palpated in infra umbilical region
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Sausage shaped mass is palpated in infra umbilical region

In the majority, the cause is hyperplasia of Peyer&rsquo;s patches (lymphoid tissue), which may be secondary to a viral infection. In 10% of children, there is a pathological lead point, such as a Meckel&rsquo;s diverticulum, enteric duplication cyst or even a small bowel lymphoma. Such cases are more likely in children over the age of 2 years and in those with recurrent intussusception. Classically, a previously healthy infant presents with colicky pain and vomiting (milk then bile). Between episodes, the child initially appears well. Later, they may pass a &lsquo;redcurrant jelly&rsquo; stool. Clinical signs include dehydration, abdominal distension and a palpable sausage-shaped mass in the right upper quadrant. Rectal examination may reveal blood or rarely the apex of the intussusceptum. Reference: Bailey and love&rsquo;s short practice of surgery, 27 th edition, Page &ndash;129

Sample MCQ #7

The first structure to encounter at renal hilum during laparoscopic nephrectomy is?

ARenal artery
BRenal vein
CLumbar vein
DRenal pelvis
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Renal vein

At the renal hilum, The first structure encountered (i.e. the uppermost) is the renal vein Then the renal artery Lastly the renal pelvis . Reference : Bailey & Love's short practice of surgery 27 th Edition, Chapter 76, The Kidney and Ureter, Pg: 1399.

Sample MCQ #8

Which among the following structures is not removed in the patey's mastectomy?

ABreast tissue
BSkin and nipple areola complex
CPectoralis major
DFascia and lymph nodes
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Pectoralis major

In Patey's mastectomy, the breast and associated structures are dissected en bloc and the excised mass is composed of - Whole breast tissue Skin over the breast tissue Nipple areola complex Fat, fascia and lymph nodes of the axilla. The pectoralis minor muscle is either divided or retracted to gain access to the upper two-thirds of the axilla. The axillary vein and nerves to the serratus anterior and latissimus dorsi should be preserved . Reference : Bailey & Love's short practice of surgery, chapter: 58, Edition: 28 th , Pg: 935
