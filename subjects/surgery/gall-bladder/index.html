Sample MCQ #1

Which among the following is not a component of Reynold's pentad?

AJaundice
BAltered mental status
CHypothermia
DShock
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Hypothermia

Clinical Manifestations Cholangitis may present as anything from a mild, self-limited episode to a fulminant, potentially life-threatening septicaemia. Patients with gallstone-induced cholangitis are most commonly older and female. The most common presentation is fever, epigastric or right upper quadrant pain,and jaundice. These classic symptoms, known as Charcot's triad , are present in about two-thirds of patients. The illness can progress rapidly with the development of shock and altered mental status, known as Reynolds' pentad (e.g., fever, jaundice, right upper quadrantpain, septic shock, and mental status changes ). However, the presentation may be atypical, with little if any fever, jaundice, or pain. Reference : Schwartz's Principles of Surgery 11 th edition, Chapter 32, Gallbladder and the Extrahepatic Biliary System, pg no: 1408

What do you expect in a lady after gallbladder removal most commonly?

AWeight loss
BSteatorrhoea
CHigh cholesterol
DHypoglycaemia
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Steatorrhoea

Complications of cholecystectomy Cholecystectomy affects the storage and excretion of bile acids (BAs), which can alter the metabolism of BAs and the gut microbiota.These changes can lead to metabolic disorders Hyperglycemia :Cholecystectomy can lead to hyperglycemia, or high blood glucose level Dyslipidemia :Cholecystectomy can lead to dyslipidemia, or abnormal levels of lipids and lipoprotein Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) :Cholecystectomy can increase the risk of NAFLD Metabolic syndrome :Cholecystectomy can increase the risk of metabolic syndrome (MetS), even in patients without other risk factors like hypertension, diabetes, or dyslipidemia

Sample MCQ #3

Which is the most common histological subtype of gallbladder carcinoma?

ASquamous cell carcinoma
BAdenocarcinoma
CPapillary carcinoma
DTeratomatous carcinoma
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Adenocarcinoma

Pathology and Staging Gallbladder cancer is generally adenocarcinoma . It is staged by the standard tumour node metastasis (TNM) staging system. Gallbladder cancer spreads via lymphatics, hematogenously, and notoriously into the peritoneal cavity or along biopsy or surgical wound tracts. Reference : Sabiston Textbook of Surgery 21 st Edition, SECTION X ABDOMEN, 55. Biliary System, Pg no: 1516.
