Sample MCQ #1

A 44 year old Asian man presents with epigastric pains of burning nature. The pain was worst at night. He was prescribed combinations of antacids for a year and but this did not relieve his symptoms entirely. He had an endoscopy which did not show any significant abnormalities 3 months ago. Which is the best test for further investigation for this patient?

ARepeat endoscopy
BUrease breath test
CGastric biopsy
D24 hour oesophageal pH study
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. 24 hour oesophageal pH study

This patient presents with features of gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD). In some cases of GERD, endoscopic signs may be absent due to- A partially treated disease In nonerosive reflux disease - most common cause. In such instances, 24 hour oesophageal pH study can demonstrate excessive esophageal exposure to refluxed gastric fluid, the physiologic abnormality of GERD. Urease breath test is done to identify Helicobacter pylori. Gastric biopsy is done in cases of suspected gastric carcinoma. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st Ed, Ch: 323, Pg: 2424

Sample MCQ #3

A 58-year-old male presents with dysphagia, retrosternal discomfort and weight loss. Studies reveal achalasia. Which of the following is most definitive way to provide symptomatic relief?

ABuscopan
BNifedipine
COmeprazole
DSurgical cardiomyotomy
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Surgical cardiomyotomy

The only durable therapies for achalasia are pneumatic dilation and LES myotomy (Heller myotomy). The patient shows excellent symptom improvement. Reflux is the most common side effect and a concomitant anti-relfux procedure (Nissen's fundoplication) may be performed. Both calcium channel blockers (nifedipine) and nitrates relax the lower oesophageal sphincter. However the effect on symptoms is variable, short-lived and usually suboptimal. Use is frequently limited by adverse effects. Consequently medical therapy is typically limited to those patients too frail or unwilling to undergo definitive treatment or other therapies. Latest treatment is POEM. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st ed, Ch: 23,Pg: 2427-2428

Sample MCQ #4

A 42 year old man who usually drinks only 2 units of alcohol a day went on an alcohol binge with his friends. On that day, he vomited 10 times and was brought to hospital feeling very unwell. He has not previously had any symptoms of dyspepsia or abdominal pains. During physical assessment, he vomits a large bowlful of blood. What is the likely cause of his haemetemesis?

AOesophageal varices
BDuodenal ulcer
CMallory Weiss tear
DGastritis
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Mallory Weiss tear

This is a case of Mallory Weiss tear . Longitudinal tear in the mucosa at the gastroesophageal junction . Caused by forceful or prolonged vomiting or coughing. They may also be caused by epileptic convulsions. Usually seen in alcoholics, bulimia nervosa patients. The tear may be followed by vomiting bright red blood or by passing blood in the stool. Active bleed can be treated by endoscopy (band ligation or coaptive coagulation). A transmural rupture at gastroesophageal junction is called Boerhaave's syndrome. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st ed, Ch: 323, Pg: 2433

Sample MCQ #5

A 40-year-old man is referred with gastro-oesophageal reflux disease (GERD).Which of the following concerning GERD is correct?

AAcid suppressant therapy should not be given continuously
BEndoscopy is mandatory
CEsophageal pH monitoring is a good guide to therapy
DSymptoms do not correlate with mucosal status at endoscopy
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Symptoms do not correlate with mucosal status at endoscopy

Symptoms of GERD do not correlate with the mucosal appearances at endoscopy. PPIs can be given continuously where the diagnosis has been satisfactorily proven and relapse of symptoms persist after withdrawal. Although endoscopy can be performed in cases that are not clear cut or do not respond to proton pump inhibitors (PPIs), it is not mandatory. The doubt of Barrett's oesophagus can only be clarified with biopsy. Monitoring of pH is not a good guide to therapy. Symptomatic improvement is a good guide to the efficacy of therapy. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st , Ch: 323, Pg: 2429-2431
