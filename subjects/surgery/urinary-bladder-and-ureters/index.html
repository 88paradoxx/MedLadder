Sample MCQ #1

According to Weigert-Meyer's rule of ureteral duplication, the lower pole ureter inserts into the urinary bladder:

ALateral and cephalad to the upper pole ureter
BLateral and caudal to the upper pole ureter
CMedial and cephalad to the upper pole ureter
DMedial and caudal to the upper pole ureter
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Lateral and cephalad to the upper pole ureter

Explanation: The Weigert-Meyer law describes the anatomical relationship of the upper and lower renal moieties in duplicated collecting systems and their corresponding inferior drainage into the bladder. Weigert Meyer's rule states that The upper and lower renal moieties each have separate ureters, each with its own orifice in the bladder. The upper renal moiety ureter typically inserts ectopically, medially and inferior to the lower renal moiety ureter, often leading to a The lower renal moiety ureter inserts orthotopically , laterally, and superiorly to the upper moiety's ureter, which has a risk of vesicoureteral reflux.

Sample MCQ #2

A patient underwent laparoscopic cholecystectomy and was discharged after drain removal on day 1. On day 2, he returned with abdominal pain, tachycardia, fever, and ultrasound showing a 5 &times; 5 cm subhepatic collection. What is the most likely cause?

AInfection
BSurgical site pain
CBile leak
DRetained gallstones
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Bile leak

Explanation: Early post-cholecystectomy subhepatic collections are most often due to a bile leak, commonly from the cystic duct stump or an accessory bile duct. Explanation of other options: Infection (Option A): Takes 5-7 days to develop; not an early cause of subhepatic collection. Surgical site pain (Option B): Does not explain fever, tachycardia, or localized fluid collection. Retained gallstones (Option D): Can cause colic or cholangitis, but not an early subhepatic collection.

A500 ml/day
B0.5 ml/min
C0.5-1.5 ml/kg/hour
D1 ml/kg/min

✓ Correct Answer: C. 0.5-1.5 ml/kg/hour

Explanation: The normal urine output is around 1500 ml/day for a healthy adult, which roughly translates to 0.5-1.5 ml/kg/hour . (1x70(kg)x24(hours) ~ 1700 ml). Minimum urine output to excrete end products of protein metabolism: 500ml/day Ideal urine output in a normal 70kg adult: 1500 ml
