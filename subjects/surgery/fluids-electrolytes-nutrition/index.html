Sample MCQ #1

All of the following can be seen in refeeding syndrome except:

AHypophosphatemia
BHypomagnesemia
CHypocalcemia
DHypomanganesemia
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Hypomanganesemia

This is characterized by shift in fluid and electrolyte shift in malnourished patients undergoing refeeding. It is more common with parenteral fluids . It results in hypophosphatemia, hypocalcemia and hypomagnesemia. Reference: Bailey & Love's short practice of surgery, chapter: 25, Edition: 28 th , Pg: 338

Sample MCQ #2

Early management of shock focuses on restoring circulating volume, maintaining perfusion and using appropriate fluids. Several principles guide initial resuscitation. One of the following statements is false. Identify the incorrect statement.

AFirst line therapy is putting a short wide bore intravenous cannula and start infusion
BAlbumin should not be the start in immediate period
CRinger lactate is most physiological fluid and ideal crystalloid
DIt is ideal to give inotropic agents regardless of preload to heart
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. It is ideal to give inotropic agents regardless of preload to heart

Explanation: Inotropic agents should never be started without ensuring adequate preload. In shock, the first step is to restore circulating volume with crystalloids. Starting inotropes before correcting hypovolemia can worsen ischemia, increase oxygen demand and cause arrhythmias. Principles of fluid therapy in shock: The first step is to gain rapid venous access with a short, wide-bore cannula and begin crystalloid infusion. (Option A) Ringer lactate is considered the most physiological crystalloid solution due to its electrolyte composition. ( Option C) Colloids (like albumin) are not recommended in the immediate resuscitation period because crystalloids are faster, safer and more accessible. (Option B) Inotropes are only considered after adequate fluid resuscitation if hypotension persists. (Option D)
