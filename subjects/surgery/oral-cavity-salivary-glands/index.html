Sample MCQ #1

Premalignant lesion of tongue includes:

AErythroplakia
BPsoriasis
CAphthous ulcer
DFordyce's spot
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Erythroplakia

The premalignant lesions of oral cavity are leukoplakia, erythroplakia, oral submucous fibrosis, lichen planus, chronic hyperplastic candidiasis. Erythroplakia is a red patch or plaque on the oral mucosal surface. Aphthous ulcers are recurrent and superficial ulcers usually involving movable mucosa of oral cavity. Fordyce Spots - Aberrant sebaceous glands present under the buccal or labial mucosa and shine through it as yellowish or yellow-brown spots. They are considered normal. Reference: Diseases of Ear, Nose and Throat & Head and Neck Surgery by P L Dhingra, 8 th Edition, Pg. no. 265, 257, 255.

A 45-year-old male patient, known case of carcinoma tongue, underwent hemiglossectomy with modified radical neck dissection. His primary tumour and excised lymph nodes were sent for histopathology examination. In which of the following conditions will adjuvant radiotherapy not be required by the patient?

ASingle lymph node involvement
BPositive surgical margins
CExtra capsular nodal extension
DStage T3 or T4

✓ Correct Answer: A. Single lymph node involvement

Explanation: Multiple lymph node involvement is an indication for adjuvant radiotherapy, not single lymph node involvement. Indications of Adjuvant radiotherapy: Advanced stage T3 or T4. Sample MCQ #3

A 55-year-old male smoker presented with a mass on the left side of the neck at the level 2 region. An FNAC was done, which was suggestive of metastatic squamous cell carcinoma. A careful endoscopic and radiological examination was done, which revealed the absence of any primary tumour. Which of the following is not a theory for this condition?

ASquamous element of brachial remnants in lymph nodes turning malignant.
BSubclinical primary with nodal metastasis, either due to faster growth rate of the nodal metastasis or sloughing of necrotic carcinoma.
CPrimary regressing due to immune hyperactivity.
DNone of the above.
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. None of the above.

Explanation: The incidence of a neck node with unknown primary origin in head and neck region is about 3%. Carcinoma of unknown primary is defined as histological confirmation for at least one of the cervical lymph nodes to be metastatic , in the absence of a primary tumour, which is confirmed by thorough physical, endoscopic and radiological examination. Theories of occurrence include the following: The squamous element of brachial remnants in lymph nodes turning malignant. (Option A) The subclinical primary with nodal metastasis, either due to the faster growth rate of the nodal metastasis or the sloughing of necrotic carcinoma. (Option B) The primary regressing due to immune hyperactivity. (Option C) The small hidden primary in tonsil/base of tongue crypts with aggressive nodal metastasis.
