Sample MCQ #1

A 46 year woman is admitted due to a single episode of haematemesis after taking a tablet of ibuprofen 6 hours previously. On examination, she had a blood pressure of 120/75 mmHg (lying) and 95/60 mmHg (standing). Her haemoglobin concentration is 7.5 g/dL. What is the likely cause of the haemetemesis?

ADuodenal ulcer
BOesophagitis
CMallory Weiss Tear
DGastric erosions
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Gastric erosions

The likely answer is gastric erosions because the bleeding occurred after only one dose of Ibuprofen. There is no previous history of dyspepsia or upper GI bleeding. Nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs ( NSAIDs ) such as aspirin, ibuprofen, and naproxen cause gastric erosions via - Can be direct irritants of gastric mucosa Prostaglandins have a protective role in maintaining mucosal integrity. NSAIDs inhibit prostaglandin production . Because of gravity, the irritants lie on the greater curvature of the stomach, and therefore, gastritis and ulcers are seen distally on or near the greater curvature of the stomach . Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st Ed, Ch: 48, Pg: 312

Sample MCQ #2

A 52 year old lady complains of epigastric burning pains due to stress. She finally undergoes an UGI endoscopy which shows gastro-oesophageal reflux. Which medication is the most effective for her condition?

ANizatidine
BBismuth
CMagnesium hydroxide
DPantoprazole
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Pantoprazole

The main pharmacological therapy for GERD is inhibition of gastric acid suppression . Proton pump inhibitors such as omeprazole, lansoprazole and pantoprazole are more effective than H2 receptor blockers such as ranitidine, cimetidine or nizatidine. Healing in oesophagitis is better with a PPI. Bismuth is a mucosal protective agent used in the treatment of peptic ulcer disease. Magnesium hydroxide is an antacid. It is not as effective as PPI for GERD. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st ed, Ch: 323, Pg: 2429-2431

Sample MCQ #3

A 35 years old male with gastric outlet obstruction presented to emergency with multiple episodes of vomiting. Which of the following fluids is preferred for this patient?

ANormal saline
B20% dextrose with water
CRinger lactate
D5% dextrose
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Normal saline

Treatment of gastric outlet obstruction: Treating the patient involves correcting the metabolic abnormalities and dealing with the mechanical obstruction. The patient should be rehydrated with intravenous isotonic saline with potassium supplementation. Replacing the sodium chloride and water allows the kidney to correct the acid-base abnormality. The metabolic abnormalities may be less if the obstruction is due to malignancy, as the acid-base disturbance is less pronounced.
