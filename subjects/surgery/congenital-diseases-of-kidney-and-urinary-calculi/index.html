Sample MCQ #1

A 27-year-old woman presents with recurrent episodes of headaches and sweating. Her mother had renal calculi and died of a tumour in her neck. On examination a nodule is felt in the patient's neck in the region of the thyroid gland. A surgeon advises complete thyroidectomy. What is the most important investigation the surgeon must undertake prior to surgery?

ASerum TSH
BSerum calcitonin
C24-h urine test for 5-hydroxyindoleacetic acid
D24-hour urinary metanephrines
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. 24-hour urinary metanephrines

This patient probably has medullary carcinoma of the thyroid , which is usually associated with phaeochromocytoma and hyperparathyroidism as part of the MEN-2A syndrome. Here, the serum TSH level is unchanged, while a 24-h urine test for 5-hydroxyindoleacetic acid is performed for the diagnosis of carcinoid syndrome. Phaeochromocytoma must be excluded before surgery. If present, the effect of catecholamines must be blocked prior to surgery. MEN-1 is associated with Pituitary adenoma, Parathyroid hyperplasia and Pancreatic tumours. MEN-2A (multiple endocrine neoplasia) is associated with Parathyroid hyperplasia, Medullary carcinoma thyroid and Pheochromocytoma MEN-2B is also associated with a marfanoid appearance, Mucosal neuromas whereas MEN-2a is not. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Page:2953

Sample MCQ #2

A 40-year-old patient with renal calculi has further investigation. High urinary levels of the following substances predispose to urinary tract stone formation, EXCEPT FOR:

ACystine
BCitrate
CCalcium
DUrate
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Citrate

Animal protein, oxalate, cystine and urate are all stone-forming substances, so high urinary levels promote stone formation. Low urinary citrate levels promote stone formation. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal medicine, 21 st ed, Ch: 318, Pg: 2369.

Sample MCQ #3

A 42-year-old man presents to his primary care physician for left flank pain and hematuria. Urinalysis reveals a pH of 6.9 and hematuria only. There is no evidence of UTI. Renal ultrasound shows the presence of kidney stones. He has no deafness. Serum chemistry and ABG show mild hypokalemic hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis. Serum creatinine is normal. He has a family history of kidney stones, and several members have mild hypokalemic hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis. Which one of the following renal tubular acidoses (RTAs) is the MOST likely diagnosis in this patient?

AProximal RTA
BDistal RTA
CType IV RTA with aldosterone deficiency
DIncomplete RTA
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Distal RTA

This patient has hereditary form of distal RTA with autosomal dominant inheritance. It occurs in adults. Autosomal recessive forms are diagnosed in infancy and childhood with or without deafness. Hyperkalemic RTA with a defect in voltage gradient is seen in patients with mild to moderate renal dysfunction. Type 4 RTA patients with aldosterone deficiency have mild renal impairment and can acidify their urine. Patients with incomplete RTA may have a combination of both proximal and distal RTA. They may present with acid urine, but the diagnosis is established only after an acid load. Proximal RTA patients present with severe hypokalemia, but nephrolithiasis is uncommon. Thus, options A, C, and D are incorrect. Reference: Harrisons Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st Ed, Ch:55 Pg: 364

Sample MCQ #4

A young girl presents with continuous urinary dribbling since birth, despite normal voiding. What is the most likely diagnosis?

AEctopic ureter
BUreterocele
CCongenital megaureter
DVesicoureteric reflux
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Ectopic ureter

Explanation: In females, an ectopic ureter that opens below the sphincter (into the urethra or vagina) presents as continuous dribbling of urine despite normal voiding . This history is pathognomonic. Explanation of other options: Ureterocele (Option B): Causes recurrent UTIs and bladder outlet obstruction, not continuous dribbling. Congenital megaureter (Option C): Presents with hydronephrosis and infections, not continuous leakage. Vesicoureteric reflux (Option D): Causes recurrent UTIs and hydronephrosis, not continuous wetting.
