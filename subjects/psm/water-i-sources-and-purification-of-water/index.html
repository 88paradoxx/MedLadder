Sample MCQ #2

According to public health guidelines, what is the recommended minimum concentration of free residual chlorine that must be maintained in well water after chlorination, following a contact period of one hour, to ensure it is effectively disinfected and safe for drinking?

A0.5 ppm after 1 hour
B0.5 ppm after 2 hours
C0.8 ppm after 1 hour
D1.0 ppm after 30 minutes
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. 0.5 ppm after 1 hour

Explanation: The standard public health guideline for effective disinfection of drinking water via chlorination is a free residual chlorine level of 0.5 parts per million (ppm) after a contact time of at least 1 hour . (Option B,C and D ruled out) This combination ensures that a sufficient concentration of the disinfectant has been in contact with the water long enough to inactivate pathogenic microorganisms. Key Takeaways: The benchmark for effective well water chlorination is 5 ppm free residual chlorine after 1 hour of contact.

Sample MCQ #3

In community well water, Horrock's apparatus first shows 3rd cup blue. What will be the requirement of bleaching powder for disinfecting 9100 litre of water

A120 grams
B160 grams
C200 grams
D360 grams
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. 120 grams

The correct amount of beaching powder required to disinfect 455 L of water with a chlorine concentration of 3 mg/L is approximately 6 grams. Horrock's apparatus first shows the 3rd cup blue, indicating a chlorine concentration of 3 mg/L. Volume of water to be treated = 9100 Liters. If it takes 6gm to disinfect 455L Then how much does it take to disinfect 9100L Amount of bleaching powder required = (6*9100)/455 = 54600/455 = 120 grams Therefore, option A (120 grams) is the correct choice. In summary, the correct amount of bleaching powder required to disinfect 9100 Liters of water with a chlorine concentration of 3 mg/L, as indicated by the Horrock's apparatus, is approximately 120 grams. Reference: Park's Preventive and social medicine, 27th EDITION Pg 855
