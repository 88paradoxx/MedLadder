Sample MCQ #1

A test with high specificity has a low

AType I error
BType II error
C1-&beta;
D1-&alpha;
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Type I error

A test with high specificity has a low probability of falsely classifying individuals without the disease as positive (Type I error). Explanation for why other options are wrong: b. Type II error: Type II error refers to the probability of incorrectly failing to reject a false null hypothesis, which is more related to sensitivity than specificity. High specificity reduces the likelihood of Type I errors, not Type II errors. c. 1-&beta; represents the power of a statistical test, which is related to sensitivity rather than specificity. Higher specificity does not directly influence the power of the test. d. 1-&alpha; Represents the significance level of a statistical test, which is determined by the researcher and is not directly influenced by the specificity of the test. Reference: Park's Preventive and social medicine, 27th EDITION Pg 156

Sample MCQ #2

In a 3x4 contingency table, the number of degrees of freedom equals to

A1
B5
C6
D12
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. 6

In a contingency table, the number of degrees of freedom is calculated using the formula: Degrees of freedom = (r - 1) * (c - 1) where r is the number of rows and c is the number of columns in the contingency table. Given that the contingency table in this scenario is 3x4 (3 rows and 4 columns), the calculation for degrees of freedom would be: Degrees of freedom = (3 - 1) * (4 - 1) = 2*3 = 6 Therefore, the correct answer is c. 6 Reference: Park's Preventive and social medicine, 27th EDITION Pg 980

Sample MCQ #3

While applying chi square test to a contingency table of 4 rows and 4 columns, the degrees of freedom would be

A1
B4
C9
D8
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. 9

In a chi-square test for a contingency table, the degrees of freedom (df) are calculated using the formula: df = (number of rows - 1) &times; (number of columns - 1) In this case, the contingency table has 4 rows and 4 columns. So, applying the formula: df = (4 - 1) &times; (4 - 1) = 3 &times; 3 = 9 Therefore, the degrees of freedom for a contingency table of 4 rows and 4 columns would be 9. Reference: Park's Preventive and social medicine, 27th EDITION Pg 980
