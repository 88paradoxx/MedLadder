Sample MCQ #1

Organism included in Category A Bioterrorism is/are a. Vibrio cholerae b. Clostridium botulinum c. Yersinia pestis d. Bacillus anthracis

Aa, b, c, d
Ba, b, c
Ca, c, d
Db, c, d
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. b, c, d

Category A bioterrorism agents are those that pose the highest risk to national security because they can be easily disseminated or transmitted from person to person, result in high mortality rates, and have the potential for major public health impact. Explanation for why not the other options: a. Vibrio cholerae: Vibrio cholerae is the bacterium responsible for causing cholera, a severe diarrheal disease. While cholera can cause significant morbidity and mortality, it is not typically considered a Category A bioterrorism agent due to its lower potential for deliberate dissemination and widespread impact compared to other agents in Category A. Reference: Park's Textbook of Preventive and Social Medicine, 27th Edition, Page no.:982

Sample MCQ #2

The causative agent of endemic typhus is:

ARickettsia prowazekii
BRickettsia typhi
CSalmonella typhi
DOrientia tsutsugamushi
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Rickettsia typhi

Explanation: Endemic typhus (also called murine typhus ) is caused by Rickettsia typhi . Transmission: Flea bite (commonly from rats). Clinical features: Mild febrile illness, headache, myalgia, sometimes rash. Usually self-limiting. Differentiation from epidemic typhus: Epidemic typhus &rarr; caused by Rickettsia prowazekii , transmitted by body lice , more severe. Explanation of Incorrect Options: Rickettsia prowazekii (Option A): Causes epidemic typhus , not endemic typhus. Salmonella typhi (Option C): Causes typhoid fever , a bacterial enteric fever, not a rickettsial infection. Orientia tsutsugamushi (Option D): Causes scrub typhus , transmitted by chiggers , not fleas.

Sample MCQ #3

What is the correct composition and dosing schedule of the Shanchol vaccine?

AMonovalent 2 doses
BMonovalent 1 dose
CBivalent 2 doses
DBivalent 1 dose
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Bivalent 2 doses

Explanation: Shanchol is a bivalent oral cholera vaccine given in 2 doses This refers to a vaccine that targets two different strains of Vibrio cholerae and requires two doses for complete immunisation. The Shanchol vaccine is bivalent, meaning it covers two different strains of Vibrio cholerae (01 and 0139) and typically requires two doses for full protection.
