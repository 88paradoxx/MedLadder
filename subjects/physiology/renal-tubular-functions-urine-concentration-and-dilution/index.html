Sample MCQ #3

The first part of PCT has which of the following transport proteins?

ASodium potassium pump
BNa glucose co transporter
CSodium hydrogen antiport
DAll of the above
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. All of the above

PCT reabsorbs 67% of Nacl, water, K+ & filtered urea. 70% of calcium, 100% of glucose & amino acids, 80% of phosphate & bicarbonate. Sodium reabsorption is mainly due to Na+ - H+ antiport Another major transport protein for sodium is SGLT2. Other important sodium transporters in PCT are Na/Pi cotransporter, Na amino acid cotransporter & Na/lactate CT cotransporter. Na+-H+ antiporter is the main determinant of Na & H20 reabsorption in PCT. Na & H+ exchange is linked directly to reabsorption of HCO3- through Na+ Hco3- cotransporter at basal membrane Key Takeaway: The first part of the PCT contains Na+/K+ ATPase, Na+-glucose co-transporter (SGLT2), and Na+-H+ antiporter (NHE3), all playing crucial roles in sodium and solute reabsorption.

Sample MCQ #4

Impaired function of aquaporin results in:

ANephrogenic DI
BLiddle syndrome
CCystic fibrosis
DBarter syndrome
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Nephrogenic DI

normal or elevated levels of ADH are present but the renal tubular segments cannot respond appropriately. This condition is referred to as nephrogenic diabetes insipidus because the abnormality resides in the kidneys. This abnormality can be due to failure of the countercurrent mechanism to form a hyperosmotic renal medullary interstitium or failure of the distal and collecting tubules and collecting ducts to respond to ADH (due to impaired function/defect in aquaporin-2 receptor or V2 receptor. In either case, large volumes of dilute urine are formed, which causes dehydration unless fluid intake is increased by the same amount as urine volume is increased.

Sample MCQ #6

Which of the following is the diluting segment of kidney?

APCT
BCollecting duct
CAscending thick loop Henle
DDescending loop of Henle
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Ascending thick loop Henle

Thick ascending loop of Henle - "the diluting segment" Thick ascending loop of Henle is permeable to solutes and impermeable to water Sodium, chloride and potassium moves out of the thick ascending limb through Na-K-2Cl cotransporter Because of selective movement of solutes leaving water inside fluid inside thick ascending limb becomes hypotonic to plasma Reference: Ganong,'s Review of Medical Physiology, 25th edition., ch-37, page no 684
