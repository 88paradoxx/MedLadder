Sample MCQ #2

All are effects of sympathetic stimulation except

AIncreased conduction velocity
BIncreased heart rate
CIncreased refractory period
DIncreased contractility of heart
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Increased refractory period

Sympathetic stimulation to the heart results in, Increase in heart rate (positive chronotropy) Increase in myocardial contractility (positive inotropy Increase in cardiac conduction velocity (positive Dromotropy) Increase in cardiac excitability (positive Bathmotropy) Augments cardiac relaxation (positive lusitropy) Decrease in refractory period Reference: Ganong, 25th edition page. 592

Sample MCQ #3

Which of the following JVP wave changes is most likely seen in atrial fibrillation?

AAbsent a wave
BSteep a wave
CAbsent c waves
DAbsent x descent
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Absent a wave

Explanation: In the jugular venous pulse, the "a" wave corresponds to right atrial contraction . In atrial fibrillation , there is no effective atrial contraction because the atria fibrillate instead of contracting in a coordinated manner. As a result, a wave is absent from the JVP tracing. This absence of "a" waves is a classic diagnostic clue in atrial fibrillation. Explanation of other options: Steep a waves (Option B): Seen in tricuspid stenosis or pulmonary hypertension when right atrial contraction occurs against resistance. Absent c waves (Option C): The c wave represents bulging of the tricuspid valve into the right atrium during ventricular systole; it is not typically absent in atrial fibrillation. Absent x descent (Option D): Loss of x descent is seen in conditions like tricuspid regurgitation and constrictive pericarditis , not in atrial fibrillation.
