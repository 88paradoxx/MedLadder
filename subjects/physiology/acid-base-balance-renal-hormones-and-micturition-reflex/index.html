Sample MCQ #1

Erythropoietin is produced by which of the following? 1. Kidney 2. Liver 3. Spleen 4. Lungs

A1 only
B2 only
C1 and 2
D1, 2, 3 and 4
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. 1 and 2

Explanation: Erythropoietin (EPO) is a glycoprotein hormone that regulates red blood cell (RBC) production. Approximately 90% of erythropoietin is synthesized in the kidneys , while the remaining 10% is produced in the liver . In the kidneys, fibroblast-like interstitial cells in the cortex and outer medulla are the main sites of production, responding to tissue hypoxia. The liver contributes mainly during the fetal stage , serving as the predominant site of erythropoietin synthesis before birth. When hypoxia occurs, the hypoxia-inducible factor-1 (HIF-1) activates the erythropoietin gene , increasing hormone synthesis to stimulate erythropoiesis in the bone marrow. Key Takeaways: Kidney &rarr; 90% of erythropoietin production Liver &rarr; 10%, predominant in fetal life Stimulus: Tissue hypoxia via HIF-1 pathway Function: Stimulates proerythroblast formation and maturation in bone marrow

Sample MCQ #6

A 45-year-old chronic alcoholic presents with confusion, irritability, and generalised weakness. Laboratory tests reveal elevated blood lactate levels suggestive of lactic acidosis. Deficiency of which of the following vitamins is the most likely cause?

ANiacin
BRiboflavin
CPantothenic acid
DThiamine
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Thiamine

Explanation: Thiamine (Vitamin B1) is an essential cofactor for several enzymes involved in carbohydrate metabolism, including pyruvate dehydrogenase, &alpha;-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase, and transketolase . Thiamine deficiency leads to impaired conversion of pyruvate to acetyl-CoA. As a result, pyruvate accumulates and is diverted towards lactate production , leading to lactic acidosis . This explains the elevated lactate levels seen in thiamine deficiency, particularly in alcoholics. Explanation of other options: Niacin (Option A): Deficiency causes pellagra (dermatitis, diarrhoea, dementia), but not lactic acidosis. Riboflavin (Option B): Deficiency leads to cheilitis, glossitis, and angular stomatitis , but does not cause lactic acidosis. Pantothenic acid (Option C): Component of Coenzyme A ; its deficiency is rare and does not cause lactic acidosis.
