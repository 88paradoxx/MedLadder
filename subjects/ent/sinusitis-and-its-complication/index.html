Sample MCQ #1

An ENT surgeon is performing diagnostic nasal endoscopy in a patient of suspected sinusitis. He passes the endoscope at 0 degrees in the middle meatus, what is this pass called?

AFirst pass
BSecond pass
CThird pass
DFourth pass
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Third pass

Diagnostic nasal endoscopy is done in three passes. First pass &ndash; Examination of nasopharynx and inferior meatus. Second pass - Examination of the sphenoethmoidal recess, superior meatus and openings of sphenoid sinus and posterior ethmoidal cells. Endoscope is passed medial to middle turbinate. Third pass - Examination of the middle meatus in detail. Endoscope is passed from the front into the middle meatus. Can examine uncinate process, bulla ethmoidalis, hiatus semilunaris, sinus of the turbinate (cavity on lateral side of middle turbinate) and the frontal recess. Reference &ndash; Diseases of Ear, Nose and Throat & Head and Neck Surgery by P L Dhingra ,8 th Edition, pg. no -492- 493

Sample MCQ #2

A 20 year old male patient with a long standing history of unilateral foul smelling ear discharge presents to the OPD with severe headache, spontaneous nystagmus and a staggering gait. What is the probable diagnosis?

ACavernous Sinus Thrombosis
BSigmoid Sinus Thrombosis
COtogenic Temporal Lobe abscess
DOtogenic Cerebellar abscess
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Otogenic Cerebellar abscess

A long standing history of unilateral foul smelling ear discharge is possibly due to atticoantral CSOM. The onset of severe headache, spontaneous nystagmus and a staggering gait is suggestive of cerebellar abscess. Treatment - High dose antibiotics, neurosurgical approach. Reference: Diseases of Ear, Nose and Throat & Head and Neck Surgery by P L Dhingra, 8 th Edition, Page - 99, 100.

Sample MCQ #3

A diabetic patient presented to OPD with blood stained nasal discharge and progressive blackish nasal discoloration. Which of the following is the choice of therapy?

AAmphotericin B
BAzithromycin
CKetoconazole
DStreptomycin
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Amphotericin B

The given clinical scenario is suggestive of Mucormycosis . Mucormycosis is acute invasive fungal infection involving nose and paranasal sinuses, where fungal hyphae invade blood vessels causing ischaemic necrosis. Commonly involves lateral nasal walls and turbinates and quickly spreads to orbit, palate, face and cranium. Treatment is surgical debridement and Amphotericin-B.

Sample MCQ #5

A post covid patient presented with black discharge from nasal cavity. On examination a black nasal mass extending onto orbit. Most likely cause is:

ATumor
BNasal polyp
CAcute bacterial sinusitis
DMucormycosis
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Mucormycosis

The most likely cause of the black discharge and nasal mass extending onto the orbit in a post-COVID patient is mucormycosis . Mucormycosis, also known as black fungus , is a rare but serious fungal infection that can affect individuals with weakened immune systems, such as those who have recovered from COVID-19. It typically invades the sinuses and can spread to nearby structures like the orbit. Prompt diagnosis and treatment, including surgical debridement and antifungal therapy , are crucial in managing mucormycosis.

Sample MCQ #6

A patient presents with a cyst like swelling in the superiomedial side of the eye. There is no pain when it is pressed. This entity affects which paranasal sinus most commonly?

AFrontal
BMaxillary
CEthmoid
DSphenoid
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Frontal

The given description is suggestive of a mucocele . Mucocele of paranasal sinuses - Expansion of bony wall of sinus. Frontal sinus is most commonly affected followed by ethmoidal, maxillary & sphenoidal. Treatment of choice is FESS. Frontal mucocele Usually presents in the superomedial quadrant of orbit - displacing the eyeball forward, downward and laterally. Swelling is cystic, non-tender, egg shell crackling may be present Reference &ndash; Diseases of Ear, Nose and Throat & Head and Neck Surgery by P L Dhingra ,8 th Edition, pg. no &ndash; 231

Sample MCQ #7

Which site is most commonly obstructed by a nasal polyp arising from the maxillary sinus?

AInferior meatus
BSuperior meatus
CMiddle meatus
DSphenoethmoidal recess
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Middle meatus

Explanation: The most common location for an obstruction caused by a nasal polyp originating from the maxillary sinus is the middle meatus . Nasal polyps are benign, non-cancerous growths that develop in the nasal passages or paranasal sinuses due to chronic inflammation , often associated with chronic rhinosinusitis, asthma, or allergies . When arising from the maxillary sinus , these polyps typically expand into the middle meatus , which is the drainage pathway of the maxillary sinus . Obstruction at this site can lead to blocked sinus drainage, recurrent sinus infections, nasal congestion, and difficulty breathing.

Sample MCQ #8

What is the first step in functional endoscopic sinus surgery?

AUncapping bulla ethmoidalis
BMiddle turbinectomy
CUncinectomy
DUncapping Agger Nasi
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Uncinectomy

Uncinectomy where the uncinate process is incised with sickle knife and removed with Blakesley forceps is the first definitive surgical step in FESS. Also known as infundibulotomy.
