Sample Tonsils MCQs with answers

Sample MCQ #1

A 10 year old child of 2 weeks post tonsillectomy ate a packet of chips, 2 days after surgery and came to hospital with an oral bleed. What will be the best plan of management?

AAntibiotic & semisolid food
Burgent surgical exploration and shift to operating room
CIntravenous tranexamic acid and semi solid food for 5 days
Dwait and watch
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Antibiotic & semisolid food

Bleeding after tonsillectomy can be primary (within 24 hours after the operation) or secondary (after 24 hours until 2 weeks). The most likely explanation for secondary bleeding is infection with Streptococcus in the granulating tonsil bed. Treatment depends on the severity and potential cause of the bleed. Antibiotics are advised for secondary bleeding. Severe primary or secondary bleeds need to be controlled in there.

Sample MCQ #2

A 7-year-old post-tonsillectomy child was lying in the ward. He started severely bleeding in the ward and is turning pale and sick. What would be your treatment plan as an Intern in this case?

AStart Intravenous line and Shift to Operation Theatre immediately
BStart Intravenous Antibiotics
CAdrenaline-soaked packing in the Tonsillar fossa and observation in the ward
DCold saline gargles
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Start Intravenous line and Shift to Operation Theatre immediately

If a patient is actively bleeding, the patient should be taken urgently for control of the hemorrhage. Until the patient is transferred to the operating room, if hemorrhaging is significant, direct pressure, either with a throat pack or gauze, should be applied to the tonsillar fossa if the patient is cooperative and an intravenous line can be started before the patient is shifted to the OT.

Sample MCQ #3

All of the following are closely related to the bed of the tonsil except

ASuperior constrictor muscle
BStyloglossus muscle
CGlossopharyngeal nerve
DPalatopharyngeus muscle
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Palatopharyngeus muscle

Palatopharyngeus muscle does not form the bed of the tonsil. Bed of the tonsil- Formed by the superior constrictor and styloglossus muscles . The glossopharyngeal nerve and styloid process , if enlarged, may lie in relation to the lower part of tonsillar fossa. Both these structures can be surgically approached through the tonsil bed after tonsillectomy. Internal carotid artery lies one inch lateral to the tonsillar bed Reference: Diseases of Ear, Nose and Throat & Head and Neck Surgery by P L Dhingra, 8 th Edition, Pg. no. 303
