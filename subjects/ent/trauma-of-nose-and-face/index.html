Sample MCQ #1

A patient with head injury presents with unilateral conductive hearing loss and bleeding in the ear. Tympanic membrane is intact on examination. The battle sign is positive in the patient. The most probable cause for deafness could be:

ACollection of fluid in the middle ear
BCochlear damage
CDislocation of the incudostapedial joint
DStapes dislocation
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Dislocation of the incudostapedial joint

This is a case of longitudinal fracture of temporal bone. The ear bleeding is a feature of longitudinal fracture. This fracture is due to parietal blow. Battle sign is ecchymosis around the mastoid tip area which is seen in temporal bone fractures. This fracture causes ossicular dislocation and hence conductive hearing loss. Reference: Diseases of Ear, Nose and Throat & Head and Neck Surgery by P L Dhingra, 8 th Edition, Page - 116.

Sample MCQ #3

All of the following investigations are helpful in the diagnostic assessment of a patient of CSF Rhinorrhea except

ACT Cisternography
BX Ray of paranasal sinus
CNasal endoscopy
DBeta 2 transferrin
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. X Ray of paranasal sinus

X ray of paranasal sinuses has no role in the diagnosis of CSF rhinorrhea. Beta-2 transferrin is the best confirmatory test. CT cisternogram - requires intrathecal injection of iohexol and a CT scan to localize the site of leak in cases when beta-2 transferrin cannot be done. Nasal endoscopy can be done to identify the site of leak and for its repair. OTHER TESTS FOR LOCALIZATION OF SITE OF CSF LEAK- High-resolution CT scan - important to see the bony defects. MRI - requires that CSF leak is active at the time of scan. Intrathecal fluorescein study - Dye appears bright yellow but when seen with a blue filter it appears fluorescent green. Reference &ndash; Diseases of Ear, Nose and Throat, 8 th edition, page 192

Sample MCQ #4

An young adult presents with nasal deformity following a fall from a ladder. CT scan reveals a displaced nasal bone fracture. What is the optimal timing for fracture reduction in this case?

AWithin 12 hours following the injury
B5-7 days following injury
CAfter 2 weeks of injury
DAfter 4 weeks of injury
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. 5-7 days following injury

Explanation: In nasal fractures, the ideal time for closed reduction is typically within 5-7 days after injury . Timing of Nasal Fracture Reduction: Early reduction (within 12 hours): May be considered only if swelling is minimal and there is marked deformity. However, early reduction is often difficult due to swelling that can hinder the realignment of the nasal bones. (Option A ruled out) Delayed reduction (5-7 days): Preferred in most cases once swelling has reduced, allowing precise correction. This period allows swelling to subside , improving visualization and enabling accurate realignment of the nasal bones. Also ensures better cosmetic and functional outcomes while minimizing the risk of complications. Late reduction (>2 weeks) Not usually recommended, as bone healing begins, making manipulation difficult and resulting in suboptimal. (Option C, D ruled out)

Sample MCQ #5

23 year old male had a history trauma to his nose with nasal bleeding which stopped on it&#39;s own. Following this trauma he developed deformity of his nose with crepitus felt on palpation. X-ray Nasal bone lateral view shows nasal fracture. Ideal time to reduce the nasal bone fracture in this patient is

AWithin 12 hrs following injury
B5 to 7 days following injury
CAfter 2 weeks
DAfter 4 weeks
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Within 12 hrs following injury

The nasal bone fracture should be reduced immediately within 1-2 hours before edema starts. If edema is already there, then wait for 7 days for the edema to subside and then reduce the fracture. It is difficult to reduce a nasal fracture after 2 weeks because the process of callus formation would have started. Reference &ndash; Diseases of Ear, Nose and Throat, 8th edition, page 213

Sample MCQ #6

Specific indicator to identify CSF:

ASugar content
BProtein content
CGlobulins
D&beta;2 transferrin
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. &beta;2 transferrin

&beta;2 transferrin is a protein seen in CSF and not in nasal discharge. Its presence is a specific and sensitive test and requires only a few drops of CSF. Perilymph and aqueous humour are the only other fluids which contain this protein. Another protein called beta trace protein is also specific for CSF and is widely used in Europe. It is secreted by meninges and choroid plexus. Reference &ndash; Diseases of Ear, Nose and Throat, 8 th edition, page 192

Sample MCQ #7

After parotidectomy paralysis of the lower lip occurs due to the injury to which structure

AMain trunk of facial
BCervical branch of facial
CParotid duct
DTemporal branch of facial
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Cervical branch of facial

The cervical branch of the facial nerve is a branch of the facial nerve that supplies the muscles of the lower lip and chin region. During parotidectomy, which involves surgical removal of the parotid gland (a salivary gland located near the ear), the cervical branch of the facial nerve can be at risk of injury, leading to paralysis of the lower lip. Main trunk of facial - This is the main nerve that supplies the muscles of the face. It is not injured during parotidectomy. Parotid duct - This is the duct that carries saliva from the parotid gland to the mouth. It is not injured during parotidectomy. Temporal branch of facial - This is a branch of the facial nerve that supplies the muscles of the forehead and temple. It is not injured during parotidectomy.

Sample MCQ #8

All of the following are true about CSF rhinorrhoea except

AIatrogenic trauma is the most common cause now a days
BImmediate transnasal endoscopic surgical closure of leak site should be done
CHRCT skull base is the radiological investigation of choice to detect site of leak
DCSF is secreted by the choroid plexus in lateral, third and fourth ventricles.
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Immediate transnasal endoscopic surgical closure of leak site should be done

Early cases of CSF rhinorrhoea can be managed by conservative measures such as bed rest, elevating the head of the bed, stool softeners, and avoidance of nose blowing, sneezing and straining. Prophylactic antibiotics can be used to prevent meningitis. Acetazolamide decreases CSF formation. If it does not resolve, surgical repair is planned. Reference &ndash; Diseases of Ear, Nose and Throat, 8 th edition, page 193
