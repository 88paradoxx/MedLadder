Sample MCQ #1

Which statement is true about Ethmoidal polyp?

ABetter seen on posterior rhinoscopy
BEndoscopic removal is the first treatment of choice
CMore common in children
DRecurrences are common after surgery
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Recurrences are common after surgery

Recurrences are more common in Ethmoidal polypi after removal as they are allergic in origin. Antrochoanal (AC) polyp is more common in children and its better seen on posterior rhinoscopy. FESS is the treatment of choice for AC polyp. For recurrent AC polyp (second recurrence), Caldwell Luc&rsquo;s operation is done. Reference &ndash; Diseases of Ear, Nose and Throat & Head and Neck Surgery by P L Dhingra ,8 th Edition, pg. no &ndash; 200, 202

Sample MCQ #2

The medial maxillectomy is the preferred treatment now a days for the management of

AAngiofibroma
BRingertz tumour
CAntrochoanal polyp
DMalignancy of maxillary sinus
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Ringertz tumour

Medial maxillectomy is the treatment of choice for Ringertz tumor (Inverted papilloma of nose). These days endoscopic approach is preferred. Option a &ndash; Surgical approach is the treatment of choice for angiofibroma. The type of approach depends on the stage of the tumor. Radiotherapy and chemotherapy singly or in combination have also been used. Medial maxillectomy alone is not useful. Option c &ndash; Antrochoanal polyps is removed via FESS. Option d &ndash; Treatment of maxillary sinus malignancy depends on the stage of tumor. Treatment is usually surgery combined with radiotherapy and chemotherapy as needed. Reference &ndash; Diseases of Ear, Nose and Throat & Head and Neck Surgery by P L Dhingra ,8 th Edition, pg. no - 236

Sample MCQ #4

The most common organism to cause septal abscess is

AStreptococcus pneumonia
BStaphylococcus aureus
CHaemophilus influenzae
DPseudomonas aueriogenosa
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Staphylococcus aureus

Septal abscess is most commonly caused by Staphylococcus aureus. AETIOLOGY- Secondary infection of septal haematoma &ndash; most common cause . Furuncle of the nose or upper lip Typhoid or measles - rare TREATMENT- Immediate incision and drainage Systemic antibiotics COMPLICATONS- Saddle nose Meningitis or cavernous sinus thrombosis &ndash; rare but serious Reference &ndash; Diseases of Ear, Nose and Throat & Head and Neck Surgery by P L Dhingra ,8 th Edition, pg. no &ndash; 168, 174

Sample MCQ #5

A patient presents with nasal stuffiness and nasal obstruction. On nasal endoscopic examination, he has been found to have bilateral nasal polypi . He also mentions episodes of wheezing and difficulty in breathing which are resolved by the use of an inhaler. Which of the following drugs would you avoid in this patient?

ASteroids
BAspirin
CRanitidine
DAzithromycin
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Aspirin

This patient with a history of bronchial asthma (episodes of wheezing resolved by inhaler usage) has presented with a nasal polyp now. Aspirin should be avoided in this patient as he may be intolerant to it. Samter&rsquo;s triad is classically described as - Nasal polypi ( Ethmoidal) Asthma ( Intrinsic) Aspirin intolerance ( NSAIDs) Reference &ndash; Diseases of Ear, Nose and Throat & Head and Neck Surgery by P L Dhingra ,8th Edition, pg. no &ndash; 201

Sample MCQ #6

The most effective medical treatment of Ethmoidal nasal polypi is?

ATopical decongestant nasal drops
BAntihistaminics
CTopical steroids
DNonsteroid anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs)
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Topical steroids

Nasal polyp means ethmoidal nasal polyp. Allergy is the main aetiology. So the treatment of choice is topical corticosteroid nasal spray (fluticasone, mometasone). They can be commonly associated with bronchial asthma. Early polypoidal changes with oedematous mucosa may revert to normal with antihistaminics. Reference &ndash; Diseases of Ear, Nose and Throat & Head and Neck Surgery by P L Dhingra , 8 th Edition, pg. no &ndash; 203

Sample MCQ #7

A boxer has been hit on face by fellow player. He has developed nasal blockage on both sides and there is swelling around the nose. What is the further treatment?

AAspiration of septal swelling
BAnterior Nasal packing
CStart Intravenous steroids
DStart Topical Corticosteroid nasal spray
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Aspiration of septal swelling

This is a case of septal hematoma. Bilateral nasal obstruction is the most common presenting symptom. Treatment- Immediate aspiration or incision and drainage. Followed by bilateral nasal packing to prevent reaccumulation. Systemic antibiotics &ndash; to prevent formation of septal abscess. Complications - If not drained, may organize into fibrous tissue leading to a permanently thickened septum. Septal abscess Saddle nose deformity Reference: Diseases of Ear, Nose and Throat & Head and Neck Surgery by P L Dhingra, 8 th Edition, pg. no- 173, 174 Scott Brown's Otolaryngology, Head and Neck Surgery, 8 th edition - Volume 1 - Page - 434

Sample MCQ #8

You are an ENT surgeon. A patient with traumatic head injury presents to you after two weeks with a watery nasal discharge that cannot be sniffed back. He has visited a general physician two weeks ago and was put on bed rest and antibiotics. What is your treatment approach now?

AWait and watch for 7-10 days
BRepair of leak site endoscopically
CPack the nasal cavity immediately
DMitomycin - C therapy
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Repair of leak site endoscopically

A watery nasal discharge that cannot be sniffed back following head trauma is CSF rhinorrhea. Initial management is conservative &ndash; bed rest, antibiotics, elevating the head of the bed, stool softeners, and avoidance of nose blowing, sneezing and straining. If it does not resolve in 7- 10 days, surgical repair is planned. Transnasal endoscopic approach is the preferred modality these days. Most of the leaks from the anterior cranial fossa and sphenoid sinus can be managed endoscopically with a success rate of 90%. Cartilage graft - If bony defect is larger than 2 cm, it is repaired with cartilage (from nasal septum or auricular concha) followed by placement of mucosa. Fascial graft - Fascia can be obtained from thigh. Or fat from thigh or abdomen can be used. Reference &ndash; Diseases of Ear, Nose and Throat, 8 th edition, page 193
