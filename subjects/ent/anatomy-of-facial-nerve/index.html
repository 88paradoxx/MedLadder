Sample MCQ #1

Which of the following is the surgical landmark for first genu of facial nerve?

APyramid
BPromontory
CProcessus cochleariformis
DPonticulus
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Processus cochleariformis

Processuscochleariformis Hook like projection on medial wall of middle ear. Surgical landmark for first genu of facial nerve. It demarcates the geniculate ganglion which lies just anterior to it. Tympanic segment of the facial nerve starts at this level. Reference: Diseases of Ear, Nose and Throat & Head and Neck Surgery by P L Dhingra, 8 th Edition, Pg. no. - 7, 108.

Sample MCQ #2

Which of the following is not a boundary of facial recess?

AFossa incudis
BTympanic segment of the facial nerve
CMastoid segment of the facial nerve
DChorda tympani nerve
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Tympanic segment of the facial nerve

Facial recess is the surgical landmark for posterior tympanotomy. Boundaries: Medial boundary - Vertical segment of the facial nerve (mastoid segment) Lateral boundary - Chorda tympani Superior boundary - Fossa incudis (short process of incus) Reference: Diseases of Ear, Nose and Throat & Head and Neck Surgery by P L Dhingra, 8th Edition, Page Number-6.

SURGICAL LANDMARKS OF FACIAL NERVE For middle ear and mastoid surgery Processus It demarcates the geniculate ganglion which lies just anterior to it. Tympanic segment of the nerve starts at this level. Oval window and horizontal The facial nerve runs above the oval window (stapes) and below the horizontal canal. Sample MCQ #4

A 50-year-old female presented with complaints of progressive facial weakness. MRI showed a facial nerve Schwannoma. What is the most common site of the above lesion?

AGeniculate ganglion
BLabyrinthine segment
CTympanic segment
DMastoid segment
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Labyrinthine segment

Explanation: Schwannomas are rare nerve sheath tumours that can occur throughout the body, and are symptomatic based on location, size, and impingement on adjacent structures. These tumours are often benign lesions that occur sporadically or as part of genetic conditions such as neurofibromatosis. Schwannomas may arise from peripheral nerves, gastrointestinal nerves, spinal nerve roots and cranial nerves. Facial nerve Schwannomas arise from cranial nerve VII and commonly involve the geniculate ganglion, labyrinthine segment, and internal auditory canal . While small lesions are asymptomatic, larger lesions can cause facial nerve paralysis and facial spasms. Lesions in the internal auditory canal can cause hearing loss, tinnitus, vertigo, and otalgia.

Sample MCQ #5

The physiological bottleneck of facial nerve is present in which part?

ALabyrinthine segment
BExtracranial segment
CHorizontal segment
DVertical segment
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Labyrinthine segment

Labyrinthine segment is the narrowest part of the facial nerve. Hence it is the physiological bottleneck of facial nerve. Reference: Diseases of Ear, Nose and Throat & Head and Neck Surgery by P L Dhingra, 8 th Edition, Page - 107, 108.

DMasseter

Sample MCQ #8

In a patient of facial nerve injury, Schirmer's test shows decreased lacrimation, taste sensation is impaired and stapedial reflex is absent. Where is the level of injury?

ALabyrinthine segment of facial nerve
BHorizontal segment of facial nerve
CExtratemporal part of facial nerve
DVertical segment of facial nerve
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Labyrinthine segment of facial nerve

Topodiagnostic Tests: Schirmer's Test - Reduced lacrimation - lesion proximal to geniculate ganglion(first genu) Stapedial Reflex - Lost in lesions above Nerve. to Stapedius(second genu) Taste test - Impairment of taste means lesion above chorda tympani origin In this question, Lacrimation - absentl Stapedial reflex - Abscent Taste sensation - Impaired So the level of lesion is above the first genu, in the labyrinthine segment. Reference: Diseases of Ear, Nose and Throat & Head and Neck Surgery by P L Dhingra, 8 th Edition, Page - 108.
