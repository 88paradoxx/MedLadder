Sample MCQ #2

All of the following are true about temporal bone fractures except

ATransverse fractures are more commonly associated with facial palsy
BEar bleeding is suggestive of longitudinal fractures
CCSF otorrhoea is more common in transverse fractures
DLongitudinal fractures more commonly cause conductive deafness
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. CSF otorrhoea is more common in transverse fractures

Temporal bone fracture are two types: Longitudinal fracture This is due to falling on the side. 80% of total fractures. Less dangerous. Cause Ossicular dislocation. Cause CHL Vertigo is uncommon. EAC bleeding and CSF otorrhoea are more Common Facial palsy is less common and delayed, it is due to edema of nerve. So, treatment is or oral steroids. Transverse fracture Due to Fall on front or the back. 20% of total fractures. More dangerous. Damage cochlea. Cause SNHL Vertigo is common. EAC bleeding and CSF otorrhoea less common Facial palsy is more common and immediate it is due to direct injury to nerve by fracture line. So, treatment is immediate Surgery. Reference : Diseases of Ear, Nose and Throat & Head and Neck Surgery by P L Dhingra, 8th Edition, Chapter name: Facial Nerve and its Disorders, Page number: 114

Sample MCQ #3

Which of the following is associated with olfactory nerve dysfunction?

ADown syndrome
BTurner syndrome
CParkinson disease
DMarfan syndrome
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Parkinson disease

Olfactory deficits have been described in a number of neurological disorders. Olfactory dysfunction may be the first clinical sign of Alzheimer&rsquo;s disease (AD) and idiopathic Parkinson&rsquo;s disease (PD). In PD, bilateral olfactory deficits occur before the onset of most of the classical neurological signs. Hence smell testing maybe useful. The remaining syndromes are not associated with olfactory nerve dysfunction. Reference &ndash; Scott Brown&rsquo;s Otolaryngology, Head and Neck Surgery, 8 th edition &ndash; Volume 1 &ndash; Page &ndash; 1237

Sample MCQ #5

A patient of head injury has bluish discoloration over mastoid area, bleeding from external auditory meatus with normal facial nerve function. After three days, patient develops facial paralysis. What is the treatment plan for this patient?

AImmediate facial nerve decompression surgery
BElectrophysiological nerve study
CStart oral steroids for 3 weeks
DPhysiotherapy of facial muscles
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Start oral steroids for 3 weeks

Based on the findings given, this is a case of longitudinal fracture of temporal bone. It presents with delayed onset of facial paralysis. This should be managed by 3 weeks of steroid therapy(as the cause is oedema). Reference: Diseases of Ear, Nose and Throat & Head and Neck Surgery by P L Dhingra, 8 th Edition, Page - 116.

Sample MCQ #6

A patient of bell's palsy shows no improvement with 3 weeks of steroid therapy. His electroneuronography reveals no significant facial nerve degeneration. What should be the next step in the management?

AAcyclovir should be added to treatment
BContinue with steroids and repeat assessment
CFacial nerve decompression
DTranstympanic dexamethasone
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Continue with steroids and repeat assessment

Treatment of Bell's palsy: Oral steroids for 3 weeks. If no improvement ->Electroneuronography is done If electroneuronography shows no significant facial nerve degeneration, steroid therapy is continued with repeat assessment. Facial nerve decompression would have been done if electroneuronography showed significant facial nerve degeneration Reference: Diseases of Ear, Nose and Throat & Head and Neck Surgery by P L Dhingra, 8 th Edition, Page - 113.

Sample MCQ #7

A patient of Bell's palsy shows no improvement with 3 weeks of steroid therapy. His electrophysiological nerve testing reveals degeneration in >90% nerve fibres. What should be the next step in the management?

ASurgical repair of facial nerve with nerve grafting
BIncrease the dose of steroid and repeat assessment after one week
CFacial nerve decompression surgery
DTranstympanic dexamethasone
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Facial nerve decompression surgery

If there is no recovery after 3 weeks of steroid therapy, then next management is to do. Electrophysiological Nerve testing to check for extent for nerve degeneration. If significant degeneration is noted (i.e. 90% fibres show degeneration), Then treatment is switched to surgery called surgical decompression of facial nerve. Sample MCQ #8

What is the intervention for facial nerve injury immediately following head trauma and fracture of temporal bone?

AFacial Nerve Decompression surgery
BElectrophysiological nerve testing
CElectrical stimulation of facial muscles
DOral Steroid for 3 weeks
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Facial Nerve Decompression surgery

Immediate onset facial paralysis following temporal bone fracture - Surgery in the form of nerve decompression, re-anastomosis of cut ends or cable nerve graft. Delayed onset facial paralysis following temporal bone fracture is treated conservatively like Bell's palsy. Reference: Diseases of Ear, Nose and Throat & Head and Neck Surgery by P L Dhingra, 8 th Edition, Page - 115.
