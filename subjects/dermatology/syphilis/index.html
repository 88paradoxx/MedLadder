Sample MCQ #1

All are true regarding secondary syphilis except :

AGeneralised painless lymphadenopathy
BVesicular rash
CMoth eaten alopecia
DSnail track ulcers
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Vesicular rash

Secondary syphilis presents with a variety of symptoms. The cutaneous manifestations are also varied A diffuse mucocutaneous rash (generally coppery red, non-pruritic and bilaterally symmetrical), coppery brown scaly macules on palms and soles are seen. Most often, the lesions are macular, discrete, reddish brown, and 5 mm or smaller in diameter; however, they can be pustular, annular, or scaling. Vesicular rash is typically absent. Generalized non-tender lymphadenopathy is typical, especially epitrochlear LN. Mucosal - condyloma lata, mucous patches, snail track ulcers - they contain a high load of spirochetes, these lesions are highly contagious. Hair - patchy non-scarring alopecia (Moth-eaten alopecia) Reference: Rooks textbook of dermatology, 9 th edition, chapter 29, page 12

Sample MCQ #5

All of the following statements are correct regarding jarisch-Herxheimer reaction except

AIt occurs within 24 hours of penicillin administration
BIt is a type 3 hypersensitivity reaction
CFever and increased prominence of skin lesions seen
DAll patients are given steroids to reduce symptoms
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. All patients are given steroids to reduce symptoms

Jarisch Herxheimer reaction (JHR) is a transient clinical phenomenon that occurs in patients infected by spirochetes who undergo antibiotic treatment. It is a type 3 hypersensitivity reaction to breakdown products of the spirochete after the use of antibiotics The symptoms usually begin 2 hours from the administration of the antibiotic and resolve within 24 hours Along with systemic symptoms - body aches, fevers, nausea and vomiting, and flushing, exacerbation of skin lesions is seen Mild reactions are self-limiting and often resolve spontaneously within 24 hours. The patient should be treated for symptoms with antipyretics and kept under observation. More severe cases may need steroids Reference: Rooks textbook of dermatology, 9 th edition, chapter 29, page 26

Sample MCQ #6

Which of the following statement is incorrect regarding syphilis?

AThe incubation period varies from 9-90 days
BPainless non-indurated chancre is seen
CSerum from the chancre is infectious
DPainless lymphadenopathy
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Painless non-indurated chancre is seen

Syphilis is a bacterial infection caused by the spirochete Treponema pallidum . Due to its many protean clinical manifestations, it has been named the "great imitator and mimicker." T. pallidum is the only treponemal agent that causes venereal disease. Primary syphilis appears 10 to 90 days after exposure to the infection and comprises a painless, indurated ulcer (chancre) at the site of inoculation with the T. pallidum . Regional lymphadenopathy is common and consists of rubbery lymph nodes. The chancre oozes serum, which is teeming with treponema Reference: Rooks textbook of dermatology, 9 th edition, chapter 29, page 8.

Sample MCQ #7

The pathology of positive Buschke-ollendorff sign in secondary syphilis is

ALobular Panniculitis
BObliterative end-arteritis
CInterface dermatitis
DNodular vasculitis
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Obliterative end-arteritis

First described by Abraham Buschke and Helene Ollendorff Curth, this sign was named after their observation of slight tenderness on applying pressure with a dull probe on papular lesions of palms in secondary syphilis and is due to cutaneous vasculitis. Necrotic keratinocytes are one of the characteristics of secondary syphilis along with plasma cell infiltrate and obliterative end-arteritis are seen. Further, treponema are also present in the dermis. Reference: Rooks textbook of dermatology, 9 th edition, chapter 29, page 13

Sample MCQ #8

All of the following are true about latent syphilis except

ASerology for syphilis is positive
BNo clinical symptoms seen
CMajority patients self resolve
DDoes not require treatment
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Does not require treatment

Latent syphilis is a stage at which the features of secondary syphilis have resolved, though patients remain seroreactive They are asymptomatic during the latent phase, and the disease is detected only by serologic tests Latent syphilis is divided into early latent and late latent. The distinction is important because treatment for each is different. The early latent period is the first year after the resolution of primary or secondary syphilis. Late latency syphilis is not infectious About one third of untreated latent syphilis patients go on to develop tertiary syphilis, whereas the rest remain asymptomatic Reference: Rooks textbook of dermatology, 9 th edition, chapter 29, page 13
