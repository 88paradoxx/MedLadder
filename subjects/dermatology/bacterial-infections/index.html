Sample Bacterial Infections MCQs with answers

Sample MCQ #1

Pemphigus neonatorum is related to

ABullous impetigo
BEpidermolysis bullosa
CCongenital syphilis
DPemphigus
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Bullous impetigo

Staphylococcal Scalded Skin Syndrome, also known as Ritter disease is a disease characterized by denudation of the skin caused by exotoxin producing strains of the Staphylococcus species, typically from a distant site. Also called as pemphigus neonatorum. Reference: Rooks textbook of Dermatology, 9 th Edition, Chapter 50, page 27

Sample MCQ #2

Which of the following diseases is not caused by Staphylococcus?

AScalded skin syndrome
BEcthyma
CErysipelas
DImpetigo
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Erysipelas

Characterized by a painful bright red appearance of the involved skin, which forms a sharply demarcated plaque. Involve upper dermal lymphatics. Classic cause: group A streptococci.

Sample MCQ #3

What is the most common cause of follicular pyodermas

AStaphylococcus aureus
BStreptococcus pyogenes
CPseudomonas aeruginosa
DPriopionobacterium acnes
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Staphylococcus aureus

Staphylococcus aureus is the most common pathogen of folliculitis and furuncles. Gram-negative pathogens including Klebsiella , Enterobacter , and Proteus species may replace the gram-positive flora on facial skin, nasal mucous membranes, and neighbouring areas, causing gram-negative folliculitis and boils. 'Hot tub' folliculitis is caused by Pseudomonas aeruginosa contamination of undertreated water in saunas or whirlpools.

Sample MCQ #4

Ozenoxacin belongs to which group?

AQuinolone antibiotic
BNon-steroidal anti-inflammatory
CAzole antifungal
DLincomycin antibiotic
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Quinolone antibiotic

It has a non-fluorinated quinolone, antibiotic, showing potent antimicrobial activities against Gram-negative and Gram-positive bacteria. Ozenoxacin inhibit the enzymes DNA gyrase and topoisomerase IV, involved in the DNA synthesis in bacteria. Approved in to treat non-bullous impetigo in adults and children aged &ge;6 months. Also used in treatment of acne

Sample MCQ #5

All of the following are caused by streptococcus pyogenes except

ABullous impetigo
BEcthyma
CScarlet fever
DCellulitis
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Bullous impetigo

Streptococcus pyogenes (group A Streptococcus ) is one of the most important bacterial causes of skin and soft tissue infections worldwide. Specifically, this organism causes infections in the superficial epidermis (Non-Bullous Impetigo), the dermal lymphatics (erysipelas), the subcutaneous tissue (cellulitis), the fascia (necrotizing fasciitis), or muscle (myositis and myonecrosis). It is also the etiologic agent of scarlet fever and Streptococcal Toxic Shock Syndrome (StrepTSS). Bullous impetigo - caused by staphylococcus aureus

Sample MCQ #6

Most common skin infection in children is:

AScabies
BImpetigo contagiosa
CMolluscum contagiosa
DWarts
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Impetigo contagiosa

Impetigo is the most common bacterial skin infection in children and manifests as thin, superficial vesiculopustular which soon rupture to form a honey colored crusts.

Sample MCQ #7

Mupirocin which mainly acts as?

AAntiviral
BAntifungal
CAnti-inflammatory
DAntibacterial
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Antibacterial

Mupirocin belongs to the topical antibiotic class of medications and is utilized for managing and treating various skin and soft tissue infections Mupirocin is believed to exert its antimicrobial activity by inhibiting isoleucyl-transfer RNA, thereby obstructing bacterial protein and RNA synthesis, ultimately resulting in cell death. Mupirocin is recognized as the most widely used topical antibiotic globally for treating methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA), particularly in cases of impetigo Mupirocin has consistently demonstrated its efficacy in eradicating intranasal MRSA colonization in healthcare settings

Sample MCQ #8

An 18-year-old female presents to the clinic complaining of foul smelling watery vaginal discharge for the past 7 days. A vaginal swab is taken, which shows a pH of 4.9. The discharge gives off a fishy odor when KOH is applied onto it. What is this test called?

AWhiff test
BKOH test
CFish test
DClue test
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Whiff test

This patient is probably suffering from bacterial vaginosis, as she has typical foul smelling watery discharge, pH greater 4.5, and positive whiff test. Wet mount/gram stain of discharge Bacterial vaginosis can be diagnosed in this case as she meets three out of four Amsel criteria. The first-line treatment for bacterial vaginosis is metronidazole. REFERENCE: IADVL textbook of dermatology, 4 th edition chapter 4
