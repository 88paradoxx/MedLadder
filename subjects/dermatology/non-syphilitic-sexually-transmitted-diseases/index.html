Sample MCQ #1

Correct about stages of LGV is :

AStage 1 : painful genital ulcer
BStage 2 : painless genital ulcer
CStage 3 : inguinal bubo
DStage 2: inguinal bubo
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Stage 2: inguinal bubo

Primary Stage: The patient characteristically develops a painless genital ulcer or papules which are about 1 to 6 mm in size. secondary stage presents with the development of unilateral or bilateral tender inguinal and/or femoral lymphadenopathy (also called buboes), which occurs two to six weeks after the primary stage; this is called the inguinal syndrome. Late stage : necrosis of lymph node when left untreated. Reference: Rooks textbook of dermatology, 9 th edition, chapter 30, page 17

Sample MCQ #2

You are posted as MO on a rural PHC. A patient comes with painful genital ulcer. You issue a STI kit to him. Which of the following will be provided?

AKit 1
BKit 2
CKit 5
DKit 6
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Kit 5

As a PHC MO, follow the guidelines of syndromic management NACO guidelines. According to it, a painful genital ulcer patient should be given red kit- kit no. 5 It contains tab acyclovir 400mg. Dose : Three tablets a day for 7 days (21 tablets total) Reference: NACO guidelines for Syndromic Management of STI

Sample MCQ #3

Which of the following statements is false about disseminated gonococcal infection?

AIt occurs as a result of direct spread by ascending infections
BClassic presentations is with a dermatitis - arthritis syndrome
CSkin lesions are small, tender and maculopapular
DIt is a common complication
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. It is a common complication

DGI occurs when the sexually transmitted pathogen Neisseria gonorrhoeae invades the bloodstream and spreads to distant sites in the body, leading to clinical findings such as septic arthritis, polyarthralgia, tenosynovitis, petechial/pustular skin lesions, bacteremia, or, on rare occasions, endocarditis or meningitis. The classic triad of features consists of dermatitis, tenosynovitis and migratory polyarthritis DGI is uncommon and thought to occur in 0.5-3% of untreated gonorrhea cases. Reference: Rooks textbook of dermatology, chapter 30, page 5
