Sample MCQ #1

Skin doubling time is__________ weeks

A12
B8
C4
D1
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. 8

During development of the epidermis, first layer to form is S. Basale and subsequently, layers form from below upwards. The principal function of epidermis is constant replacement of the stratum corneum which is being continuously shed and replaced Total epidermal cell turnover time is 52-75 days. Reference: Rooks textbook of dermatology 9th edition, chapter 2, page 2

Sample MCQ #2

All of the following statements are true regarding the skin, except that ____.

ALangerhans cells originate from bone marrow and are present in granular layer.
BMelanocytes are present in stratum basale.
CStratified squamous epithelium with hallmark keratin intermediate filaments.
DMerkel cells are present in stratum basale.
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Langerhans cells originate from bone marrow and are present in granular layer.

The epidermis is a keratinized, stratified, squamous epithelium and the hallmark filaments are the keratin intermediate filaments. Melanocytes originate from neural crest and are present in stratum basale. Merkel cells originate from ectoderm and are present in stratum basale. Langerhans cells are dendritic cells which originate from bone marrow and function as antigen presenting cells. They are present in stratum spinosum Reference: Rooks textbook of dermatology 9th edition, chapter 2, page 1

Sample MCQ #4

An 18-year-old male presents to the clinic with a complaint of a white spot on his back. You suspect nevus anemicus and perform skin biopsy. What will be the findings?

AAbsent melanin
BDecreased number of melanocytes
CDecreased blood vessels
DNormal skin biopsy
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Normal skin biopsy

Skin biopsy of a nevus anemicus lesion will show normal findings, including an uninvolved epidermis with normal melanocyte number and distribution. Reference: Rooks textbook of Dermatology, 9 th edition, chapter 70, page 5

Sample MCQ #5

What is the ratio of Type 1 to Type 3 collagen in the adult human skin?

A1:4
B8:1
C3:2
D4:1
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. 8:1

Most collagen fibrils are comprised of several collagen types and are called heterotypic Collagen fibrils are made of collagens I and III in skin. Type 1 is most abundant collagen in skin (80%) and second most common is type 3 (10. Type 2 collagen is not found in skin and is unique to cartilage. The ratio between type I and Ill collagen is 8:1. The rating of Type 1 to Type 3 collagen in the adult human skin is 8:1 and increases with age Reference: Rooks textbook of dermatology 9 th edition, chapter 2, page 27

Sample MCQ #6

All of the following are true about skin except ?

ADermis is derived from ectoderm
BSkin accounts for 15% of total body weight
CEpidermis is derived from ectoderm
DDermis is made up of type 1 and type 3 collagen
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Dermis is derived from ectoderm

Epidermis, keratinocytes, merkel cells originate from ectoderm. Langerhans cells and dermis originates from mesoderm. Skin weight is 4-5 kg and makes 15% of total body weight. Most collagen fibrils are comprised of several collagen types and are called heterotypic. Collagen fibrils are made of collagens I and III in skin. Type 1 is most abundant collagen in skin (80%) and second most common is type 3 . Type 2 collagen is not found in skin and is unique to cartilage. The ratio between type I and Ill collagen is 8:1 and increased along with increase in age. Reference: Rooks textbook of dermatology 9 th edition, chapter 2, page 1

Sample MCQ #7

Pre-malignant condition of the skin include all of the following EXCEPT:

ALeukoplakia
BSolar keratosis
CAcanthosis nigricans
DBowen's disease
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Acanthosis nigricans

Acanthosis nigricans is not pre malignant: It is a paraneoplastic condition associated with gastric adenocarcinoma. Pre-malignant skin lesions: Cutaneous horns, Keratoacanthoma, 'Bowen's disease, Actinic keratoses, Arsenical keratoses, Leukoplakia Reference: Rooks textbook of dermatology, chapter 142, Chapter 142, page 1
