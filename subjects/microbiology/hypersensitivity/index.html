Sample MCQ #1

A 30-year-old male presents with headache, fatigue, light-headedness, dyspnea, and tachycardia. Laboratory investigations reveal decreased hemoglobin and a positive direct Coomb's test. The patient is presently on an antibiotic for symptoms of upper respiratory infection. These findings suggest which of the following?

AType I, mediated by IgG antibodies
BType II, mediated by IgG antibodies
CType III, mediated by IgG antibodies
DType III, mediated by IgG or IgM antibodies
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Type II, mediated by IgG antibodies

Type II reactions involve antibodies directed against self-cells (erythrocytes) or membranes. Certain drugs react with erythrocytes to form neoantigens. Type I responses are against foreign antigens (e.g., allergens), cause IgE responses, and do not invoke a Coomb's reaction. Type III reactions involve soluble antigen-antibody complexes, and type IV reactions do not involve antibody. Reference: Ananthanarayan and Paniker's Textbook of Microbiology 10th edition Chapter 16, Pg 164-168

Sample MCQ #3

A young student immediately felt difficulty in breathing and tightness in his chest. Hestarted sweating and fainted. His friends found out that a bee had just stung him. The emergency school medical team attended him and within minutes he recovered. Which one of the following drugs was administered by the medical team?

AAntihistamine
BTheophylline
CEpinephrine
DIgE Blocking antibody
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Epinephrine

This patient had a major anaphylactic reaction and his tracheal-bronchial smooth muscle has contracted. Epinephrine is the only treatment in this group to reverse this reaction. It will relax the smooth muscle of the respiratory tree and stimulate the heart to beat stronger to raise the blood pressure. Reference: Ananthanarayan and Paniker's Textbook of Microbiology 10th edition Chapter 16, Pg 164-168

Sample MCQ #4

A 6-month-old male infant presents with diarrhea, extensive fungal infections, skin rashes and failure to gain weight. He has deficient T and B cell function. The thymus is of normal size. The most likely way to permanent restoration of normal immunity is:

AAntibiotic &ldquo;cocktail&rdquo; at regular intervals.
BBone marrow transplantation.
CExogenous immunoglobulins at regular intervals.
DIsolation to an antiseptic environment.
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Bone marrow transplantation.

The patient has a defect in the lymphocytic lineage. This could be permanently restored by replacement of defective stem cells through bone marrow transplantation. Isolation is beneficial but is a severe imposition on the quality of life and constitutes protection rather than restoration of function. The remaining choices require constant repetitive application but not permanent restoration of function. Reference: Ananthanarayan and Paniker's Textbook of Microbiology 10th edition Chapter 19, Pg 187-190

Sample MCQ #5

A 30-year-old woman presents with muscle weakness and a positive anti-acetylcholine receptor antibody test. Which autoimmune disease is most likely in this patient?

AMyasthenia gravis
BHashimoto's thyroiditis
CGrave's disease
DAddison's disease
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Myasthenia gravis

Myasthenia gravis: Characterized by the presence of anti-acetylcholine receptor antibodies, leading to muscle weakness due to impaired neuromuscular transmission. Hashimoto's thyroiditis: Involves autoantibodies against thyroid peroxidase and does not typically present with anti-acetylcholine receptor antibodies. Grave's disease: Involves autoantibodies against the thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) receptor, not acetylcholine receptors. Addison's disease: Involves adrenal cortex autoantibodies, not anti-acetylcholine receptor antibodies. Key Takeaway: Anti-acetylcholine receptor antibodies are indicative of myasthenia gravis. Tags: FMGE2023 Tag Line: Easy Reference: Shastri, A., & Bhatt, S. (2023). Essential Microbiology . Chapter 17: Autoimmunity, pp. 203-207

Sample MCQ #6

Which genetic factor is commonly associated with an increased risk of developing autoimmune diseases?

AMitochondrial DNA mutations
BHLA gene variations
CX-linked chromosomal abnormalities
DSingle nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) in non-coding regions
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. HLA gene variations

HLA gene variations (Human Leukocyte Antigen) are strongly associated with susceptibility to various autoimmune diseases due to their role in immune system regulation. Mitochondrial DNA mutations are related to mitochondrial disorders, not specifically autoimmune diseases. X-linked chromosomal abnormalities can affect various genetic conditions but are less directly linked to autoimmune diseases. SNPs in non-coding regions may play a role but are not as directly associated with autoimmune risk as HLA variations. Key Takeaway: HLA gene variations are significant genetic factors in autoimmune disease susceptibility. Tags: Clinical | integrated Tag Line: Genetic Insights Reference: Shastri, A., & Bhatt, S. AHLA-B27
BHLA-B35
CHLA-A2
DHLA-Cw6

Sample MCQ #8

Common variable hypogammaglobulinemia shows ?

ADecreased B cell count
BIncreased B cell count
CCompliment opsinization
DNeutropenia
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Compliment opsinization

Ans. is 'c' i.e., Normal B cells Most patients with common variable immunodeficiency have normal or near-normal numbers of B cells in the blood and lymphoid tissues. These B cells, however, are not able to differentiate into plasma cells. The clinical manifestations are caused by antibody deficiency. The feature common to all patients is hypogammaglobulinemia, generally affecting all the antibody classes but sometimes only IgG.
