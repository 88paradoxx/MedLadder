Sample MCQ #2

A 40-year-old female with hepatitis C viral infection presents with decreased renal function, hypertension, and anemia. Laboratory findings decreased serum C3 and urine sediment contains leukocytes, erythrocytes, and red blood cell casts. Her renal biopsy demonstrates changes consistent with glomerulonephritis. These findings suggest which type of hypersensitivity?

AType 1 hypersensitivity mediated by CD4+ T cells
BType 2 hypersensitivity mediated by IgM
CType 3 hypersensitivity mediated by IgG
DType 4 hypersensitivity mediated by IgG and sometimes IgM
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Type 3 hypersensitivity mediated by IgG

Glomerulonephritis is often associated with immune complex deposition, a type III hypersensitivity. Red blood cell casts are indicative of glomerulonephritis, and reduced C3 levels indicate a high level of cleavage and activation of C3. Type I hypersensitivity is mediated by IgE, not by CD4+ T cells. Type II hypersensitivity responses usually involve IgG. Type IV hypersensitivities do not involve antibodies. Reference: Ananthanarayan and Paniker's Textbook of Microbiology 10th edition Chapter 16, Pg 164-168

Sample MCQ #3

A 35-year-old male doctor presents after a needlestick exposure. Additional hepatitis tests are ordered upon patient's request. The patient's results are: HIV Ag/Ab: Negative/Negative HBsAg: Negative HBsAb: Positive Hepatitis C antibody: Negative HBc IgM: Negative HBc IgG: Positive What is the most likely explanation of the results above?

AAcute infection
BChronic infection
CImmunity against infection
DImmunity against vaccination
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Immunity against infection

Hepatitis B serologies can reveal significant information about the status of hepatitis B in any given patient. Major components of the test include HBsAg (surface antigen), HBsAb (surface antibody), HBc IgM (core IgM antibody), and HBc IgG (core IgG antibody). Immunization from a previous infection indicates previous exposure to viral antigens and subsequent development of antibodies against both to the surface and core antigens. Patients who develop antibodies against the viral envelope antigen (HBeAb) are more likely to achieve long-term clearance of the virus. Reference: Essentials of Medical Microbiology, by Apurba Sastry and Sandhya Bhat, 3rd Edition, Page 487

Sample MCQ #4

Ramya is a 20-year-old medical student who is attending her clinical postings in college. She is eager to draw blood samples. However, unfortunately she pricks her fingers with a used needle. It was later found that the needle which she had been injured with had been used to draw samples from an HBV infected patient. She was immediately started on post exposure prophylaxis for HBV. Later, her blood is sent for examination for serological markers for HBV infection. Had she been infected, which of the following antigens would not be seen in her blood?

AHBeAg
BHBsAg
CIgM Anti HBc
DHBcAg
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. HBcAg

Hepatitis B core antigen, or HBcAg is an intracellular core protein, hence it is not secreted in blood. However, it can be demonstrated in hepatocytes by immunofluorescence. HBeAg is a non-particulate soluble antigen and is present in circulation. HBsAg, also called Australia antigen is the first to be elevated following an infection. Anti HBC IgM is the first antibody to be elevated following an infection. Reference: Essentials of Medical Microbiology, byApurba Sastry and Sandhya Bhat, 3rd edition, page 479

Sample MCQ #5

Nila, a 2-month-old, is an infant born to an HBV infected mother. She was given her birth dose of HBV at 12 hours of birth. However, a month later, when she is tested for serological markers of HBV, she tests positive for HBV DNA, however, she is not HBsAg positive. What could be the reason for the same?

ASpurious finding
BHbsAg is not secreted in infants
CYMDD mutation
DEscape mutation
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Escape mutation

This case describes escape mutation seen in the HBV genome. The escape mutants of HBV have mutations in the S gene which leads to alteration of HBsAg. Escape mutants are observed in three situations: Infants born to HBeAg positive mothers liver transplant recipients who underwent the procedure for hepatitis B and who were treated with a high-potency human monoclonal anti-HBs preparation A small proportion of recipients of active and passive immunization, in whom antibody pressure may favor evolutionary change in gene coding an antigen. Patients infected with escape mutants lack HBsAg and therefore are often underdiagnosed by routine serological test for HBsAg Reference: Essentials of Medical Microbiology, by Apurba Sastry and Sandhya Bhat, 3rd edition, page 480

Sample MCQ #6

Ramu, a 35-year-old man complains of myalgia, fever, abdominal pain, and arthralgia. He also complains of testicular tenderness. The consultant diagnosed it to be a case of polyarteritis Nodosa (PAN). Which of the following active infections predispose one to this vasculitis?

AHBV
BHCV
CBoth of the above
DNone of the above
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Both of the above

PAN is a small/medium vessel vasculitis. It follows HBV/HCV infection. It is characterized by the presence of inflammatory reactions of medium or small-sized blood vessels with multiorgan involvement including skin, kidney, peripheral nerve, muscle, gastrointestinal tract and others. Reference : Bailey and Love's Short Practice of Surgery, 28th Edition, Chapter: 17, Pg: 268

Sample MCQ #7

A 57-year-old man comes to the physician because of generalized malaise, yellowish discoloration of the eyes, and pruritus on the back of his hands that worsens when exposed to sunlight for the past several months. He has not seen a physician in 15 years. Physical examination shows scleral icterus and mild jaundice. There is a purpuric rash with several small vesicles and hyperpigmented lesions on the dorsum of both hands. The causal pathogen of this patient's underlying condition was most likely acquired in which of the following ways?

AEating contaminated raw shellfish
BNeedlestick injury
CSexual contact
DBathing in freshwater
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Needlestick injury

This patient's scleral icterus and jaundice should raise suspicion for liver disease. His pruritic rash that worsens upon exposure to sunlight is suggestive of Porphyria Cutanea Tarda (PCT), a condition that is associated with hepatitis C virus infection. Reference: Essentials of Medical Microbiology, by Apurba Sastry and Sandhya Bhat, 3rd Edition, Page 487

Sample MCQ #8

Which of the following is known as Australia antigen?

AHBsAg
BHBcAg
CHBeAg
DHBV DNA
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. HBsAg

HBsAg is also known as Australia antigen. It is the first to be elevated following an infection. HBsAg is a protein on the surface of HBV. Continuous presence of HBsAg indicates carrier state. Without HBsAg, Anti-HBs indicates immunity to HBV due to resolved infection or vaccination. Usually appears &sim; 1-3 months after infection or vaccination. Reference: Essentials of Medical Microbiology, by Apurba Sastry and Sandhya Bhat, 3rd Edition, Page 487
