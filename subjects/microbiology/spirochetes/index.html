Sample MCQ #1

A 20-year-old female with a history of penicillin allergy unknown to her treating doctor, was given a single injection of penicillin for the treatment of syphilis. Within minutes, she developed diffuse urticaria, tachycardia, and hypo-tension. This patient has experienced:

AAnaphylaxis.
BAnergy.
CAntibody-mediated cytotoxicity.
DContact sensitivity
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Anaphylaxis.

This patient displays the hallmarks of a classical anaphylactic reaction to penicillin. Anergy is the impairment of effector immune responsiveness. Antibody-mediated cytotoxicity is most often localized to tissues bearing epitopes to which the antibody binds. Contact sensitivity results from the epicutaneous application of a reactive antigen/hapten; in the present question, the antigen (penicillin) was administered intramuscularly. Reference: Ananthanarayan and Paniker's Textbook of Microbiology 10th edition Chapter 16, Pg 164-168

Sample MCQ #3

A 27-year-old woman who is 10 weeks pregnant presents to the Obstetrics clinic for prenatal care. She has a history of treatment for syphilis 4 years previously. The results of serologic tests for syphilis are as follows: Non treponemal test RPR: Non-Reactive Treponemal test (TP-PA) reactive Which of the following statements is most correct?

AThe baby is at high risk for congenital syphilis
BThe mother needs a lumbar puncture and a VDRL test for her CSF for neurosyphilis
CThe mother needs to be treated again for syphilis
DThe mother's previous treatment for syphilis was effective
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. The mother's previous treatment for syphilis was effective

Non-treponemal tests, such as VDRL and RPR are preferred over treponemal tests for monitoring response to treatment. Antibody titers of treponemal tests remain elevated even after clinical improvement. VDRL has to be done at 3 month's intervals for at least 1 year. For primary and secondary syphilis: Following clinical improvement, there should be at least a fourfold decline in the titer by the third or fourth month and non-reactive by 12 months. For latent or late syphilis, or patients with multiple episodes of syphilis: It may show a gradual decline in titer, low titers may persist for years. Here, Non treponemal tests are negative hence the treatment to the mother was effective. Reference: Essentials of Medical Microbiology, by Apurba Sastry and Sandhya Bhat, 3rd edition, page no. 761

Sample MCQ #4

Smith Noguchi's media is used for cultivation of:

ASalmonella spp.
BKlebsiella spp.
CSpirochetes
DBacillus spp.
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Spirochetes

Spirochetes are spiral shaped bacteria. Medically important spirochetes are Treponema, Borrelia and Leptospira. Smith Noguchi's medium is used for cultivation of non-pathogenic Treponemes (Pathogenic Treponemes cannot be grown in artificial culture medium) Reference : Ananthanarayan and Paniker's Textbook of Microbiology 10th edition Chapter 4, Pg 40-45

Sample MCQ #5

An investigator studying patients with symptoms of arthritis detects a non-enveloped virus with a single-stranded DNA genome in the serum of a pregnant patient. Fetal infection with this pathogen is most likely to cause which of the following manifestations?

AHydrops fetalis
BChorioretinitis
CSensorineural deafness
DVesicular rash
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Hydrops fetalis

Parvovirus B19, can lead to the development of hydrops fetalis during pregnancy. Infection of RBCs by this virus induces apoptosis and massive destruction of erythroid lineage cells, causing severe anemia and heart failure in the fetus (secondary to excessive fluid accumulation within the fetal vascular compartments). This often-fatal condition can also be caused by chromosomal abnormalities, inborn errors of metabolism, Rh incompatibility, lymphatic dysplasia, and Treponema pallidum infection. Other Options: Chorioretinitis is seen with Toxoplasma gondii or CMV infection Sensorineural deafness is seen with CMV, Rubella or Syphilis Vesicular rash is seen with HSV2 infection Reference: Essentials of Medical Microbiology, by Apurba Sastry and Sandhya Bhat, 3rd Edition, Page Number 550

Sample MCQ #6

Which is the most likely presentation of leptospirosis?

AHigh fever, myalgia, and headache
BSore throat
CDiarrhea
DLocalized seizure
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. High fever, myalgia, and headache

High fever, myalgia, and headache are common clinical manifestations of leptospirosis. Leptospirosis can be associated with acute abdominal pain, sometimes mimicking acute cholecystitis; however, diarrhea rarely occurs. Localized seizure would be an atypical presentation of leptospirosis, which is generally not considered to be associated with focal neurologic findings. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st Ed, Ch: 184 Pg: 1419 Goldman Cecil's Medicine 26th Ed. Chap 307 pg. 1999
