Sample MCQ #1

A pedestrian hit by a car hurls up on the windshield following which his head strikes the road. How is the head injury described?

APrimary impact injury
BSecondary impact injury
CSecondary injury
DTertiary impact injury
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Secondary injury

In this case, pedestrian hit by a car (Primary impact injury) hurls up on the windshield (Secondary impact injury) following which his head strikes the road (Secondary injury. Injuries to pedestrians: Primary impact injuries are the injuries sustained by a pedestrian by the initial impact with the vehicle. Secondary impact injuries are the injuries sustained by a pedestrian by falling over the vehicle. Secondary injuries (Sometimes also called as Tertiary injuries) are the injuries sustained by a pedestrian by falling over the ground after being hit by the vehicle. References: Knight's Forensic Pathology, 4 th Edition, Page No. 289; Textbook of Forensic Medicine & Toxicology, Anil Aggrawal, Page No. 355

Sample MCQ #2

A pedestrian hit by a car hurls up on the windshield following which his head strikes the road. How is the head injury described?

APrimary impact injury
BSecondary impact injury
CSecondary injury
DNone of the above
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Secondary injury

Explanation: The head injury sustained when the head strikes the road is a Secondary injury . In the scenario described: The pedestrian is hit by the car (The injuries sustained by a pedestrian due to the initial impact are known as Primary impact injury). (Option A) The pedestrian then hurls up onto the windshield (The injuries sustained by a pedestrian by falling over the vehicle is known as Secondary impact injury). (Option B) Finally, the pedestrian's head strikes the road. The injuries sustained by a pedestrian by falling over the ground after being hit by the vehicle is known as Secondary injuries (sometimes also called Tertiary injuries). Key Takeaway: In a vehicular accident involving a pedestrian, the injury sustained from falling onto the ground after being hit by the vehicle is classified as a Secondary injury (also sometimes called Tertiary injury).

Sample MCQ #3

Which of the following statements about extradural haemorrhage is false?

AExtradural haemorrhage may be ruled out in the absence of a skull fracture
BA minimum of 35 ml of blood is required to accumulate for clinical signs to appear
CThe most common vessel affected is middle meningeal artery
DIt is the least common type of traumatic brain membrane haemorrhage
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Extradural haemorrhage may be ruled out in the absence of a skull fracture

Extradural haemorrhages (EDH) cannot be ruled out in the absence of a skull fracture. Most extradural haemorrhages are associated with fractures of the skull, but about 15 per cent occur in intact skull. They usually occur unilaterally in the parieto-temporal area, caused by the rupture of the middle meningeal artery (usually posterior branch). It is the least common type of brain membrane bleeding. The most common traumatic intracranial haemorrhage is sub-arachnoid haemorrhage. Adams suggests that a minimum volume of 35 ml is needed before clinical signs are apparent, though other writers suggest 100 ml is usually the minimum associated with fatalities. Reference: Knight's Forensic Pathology, 4th edition. Page no 182, 183
