Sample MCQ #1

What is the bullet recovered from the victim of a homicidal shooting known as?

ACrime bullet
BTest bullet
CExhibit bullet
DSuspect Bullet
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Crime bullet

Bullet collected at surgery or autopsy, from the body of a victim alive or dead is called a crime bullet. In other words, crime bullet is the one which is recovered from the body of a victim. Test bullet is a bullet that is fired from a firearm suspected of being involved in a crime. The test firing is into a gunny bag fixed into a deal wood box. The bullet is then collected for comparison with crime bullet under a comparison microscope, for identifying the crime weapon. The primary markings(Macroscopic) and secondary markings(microscopic) characteristics on the crime bullet are compared with those present over test bullet. This can only be done in relation to rifled weapons. Exhibit bullet is a crime/test bullet, which is presented in the court as evidence. Reference: Textbook of Forensic Medicine and Toxicology, 2nd edition, Page No. 296.

Sample MCQ #2

'Dry Submarine' in custodial torture injuries refer to

ABeating on soles of feet
BTying of a plastic bag over the head
CImmersing head in contaminated water
DInserting heated metal skewer into anus
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Tying of a plastic bag over the head

Beating on the sole of the feet is known as Falanga or Falaka or Bastinado. Dry submarine means head and face are covered with a plastic bag. In Wet submarine (Latina), head is submerged in polluted water. Inserting heated metal skewer into the anus is known as Black slave. Reference: The Essentials of Forensic Medicine & Toxicology, KS Narayan Reddy, 35th edition, Page No. 229

Sample MCQ #3

Which is the most commonly injured part of the spinal cord?

ALumbar spine
BCervical spine
CThoracic spine
DThoraco-lumbar
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Cervical spine

The cervical spine is the most commonly injured part of the spinal cord. The cervical spine accounts for 60 per cent of the adult spine injuries and the most common part of the spine to be injured is the region of the upper cervical vertebrae (C1&ndash;C4), and injury to the C1/C2 complex accounts for 19&ndash;25 per cent of the cervical spine injuries. These are followed by injuries to the thoracolumbar (20 per cent), lumbar (10 per cent), thoracic (8 per cent), and sacral level (2 per cent). Reference: Knight's Forensic Pathology, 4 th Edition, Page No. 210
