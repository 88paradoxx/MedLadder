Sample MCQ #1

Which of the following drug cannot be used in biliary colic ?

ADiclofenac
BDicyclomine
CTramadol
DNone of the above
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Tramadol

The opioids contract biliary smooth muscle, which can result in biliary colic. The sphincter of Oddi may constrict, resulting in reflux of biliary and pancreatic secretions and elevated plasma amylase and lipase levels. Reference : LANG&rsquo; Basic & clinical pharmacology by Bertram G. Katzung, Susen B. Masters and Anthony J. Trevor 12 th edition. Chapter 31- Opioid Analgesics & Antagonists by Dr. Mark A. Schumacher,PhD,MD, Dr. Alan I. Basbaum,PhD,& Dr. Walter L. Way,MD. Page no. 552

B1,2,3,6

✓ Correct Answer: C. 1,2,3,5

The opioid derivatives most commonly used as antitussives are dextromethorphan, codeine, levopropoxyphene, and noscapine (levopropoxyphene and noscapine are not available in the USA). They should be used with caution in patients taking monoamine oxidase inhibitors (see Table 31&ndash;5 ). Antitussive preparations usually also contain expectorants to thin and liquefy respiratory secretions. Reference: LANG&rsquo; Basic & clinical pharmacology by Bertram G. Katzung, Susen B. A6 months
B5 years
C6 years

✓ Correct Answer: C. 6 years

Importantly, due to increasing reports of death in young children taking dextromethorphan in formulations of over-the-counter &ldquo;cold/cough&rdquo; medications, its use in children less than 6 years of age has been banned by the FDA. Sample MCQ #4

A 40-year-old woman is experiencing a right knee injury. After assessment, she is treated with an opioid analgesic and her knee pain decreases significantly. Soon after administration, however, the patient experiences new-onset upper abdominal pain that makes it difficult for her to lie still. An adverse drug effect involving which of the following structures is most likely responsible for this patient's current condition?

ABlood vessels
BEnterocytes
CSkeletal muscles
DSmooth muscle cells
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Smooth muscle cells

Administration of mu opioid analgesics, such as morphine, can cause contraction of smooth muscle cells in the sphincter of Oddi, leading to spasm and an increase in common bile duct pressures. Although uncommon, pressures in the gallbladder can also increase, potentially leading to biliary colic. Patients who develop biliary colic will have severe pain and cramping in the right upper abdomen. Management involves discontinuation of mu opioid analgesics and pain control with alternative agents such as nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (eg, ketorolac, diclofenac). Reference: Basic & Clinical Pharmacology, Fourteenth Edition By Bertram G. Katzung Pg no. 1103

Sample MCQ #7

Opioid receptors belong to which class?

AIon channels
BG protein coupled receptors
CVoltage gated channels
DNone of the above
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. G protein coupled receptors

Opioids bind to specific receptors located throughout the central nervous system and other tissues Four major opioid receptor types have been identified: mu, kappa, delta, and sigma All opioid receptors couple to G proteins binding of an agonist to an opioid receptor causes membrane hyperpolarization Acute opioid effects are mediated by inhibition of adenylyl cyclase (reductions in intracellular cyclic adenosine monophosphate concentrations) and activation of phospholipase C Reference : Stoelting's pharmacology and physiology in anaesthetic practice 5th edition

Sample MCQ #8

Opioid with predominant peripheral action is?

ANaltrexone
BMethylnaltrexone
CNalbuphine
DNalorphine
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Methylnaltrexone

Methyl naltrexone is a peripheral mu opioid receptor antagonist It is a quaternary ammonium compound and hence cannot cross the blood brain barrier Thus, it can reverse the effects of opioid mediated by peripheral opioid receptors , since it cannot cross the blood brain barrier, it does not affect the opioid effects mediated by opioid receptors in the CNS , such as analgesia It was introduced in the treatment of opioid induced constipation refractory to other measures Alvimopan is a similar acting peripheral mu opioid receptor antagonist Reference : Stoelting's pharmacology and physiology in anaesthetic practice 5th edition
