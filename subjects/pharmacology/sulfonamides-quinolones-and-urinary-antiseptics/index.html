Sample MCQ #1

What is the primary target of the antibiotic ciprofloxacin in bacterial DNA replication?

ADNA polymerase
BDNA gyrase
CTopoisomerase I
DLigase
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. DNA gyrase

Explanation: DNA gyrase is an essential bacterial enzyme responsible for introducing negative supercoils into DNA, which relieves the torsional strain generated ahead of the replication fork. Ciprofloxacin, a fluoroquinolone antibiotic, specifically inhibits DNA gyrase and topoisomerase IV. By inhibiting DNA gyrase, ciprofloxacin prevents proper DNA replication and transcription, ultimately leading to bacterial cell death.

Sample MCQ #2

A 25 years old male patient came to OPD with the complaints of high-grade fever, coughing, blood in sputum, dyspnoea. His pulse rate is 120/min, BP is 110/76 mm Hg, SpO2 on room air is 98 %. He is already on ART since 3 years and AKT since 2 months. Blood culture and BAL was sent to rule out secondary bacterial infection. BAL report came positive for Pneumocystis jiroveci. Which regimen offers more tolerance in this patient in comparison to commonly used trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole regimen?

APrimaquine with pentamidine
BPrimaquine with doxycycline
CPrimaquine with clindamycin
DPrimaquine with azithromycin
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Primaquine with clindamycin

The combination of clindamycin and primaquine is an alternative regimen in the treatment of pneumocystis, particularly mild to moderate disease. This regimen offers improved tolerance compared with high-dose trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole or pentamidine, although its efficacy against severe pneumocystis pneumonia is not well studied. Reference: LANG' Basic & clinical pharmacology by Bertram G. Katzung, Susen B. Masters and Anthony J. Trevor 12 th edition. Chapter 52- Antiprotozoal Agents by Dr. Philip J. Rosenthal, MD. Page no. 924

Mechanism of action of quinolones is?

ADNA gyrase inhibitors
BBind to 30 s unit
CBind to bacterial cell membrane
DBind to tetrahydofolate reductase
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Bind to tetrahydofolate reductase

DNA gyrase inhibitors REF: Goodman Gillman's 11th ed p. 722 The quinolone antibiotics target bacterial DNA gyrase and topoisomerase IV. For many gram positive bacteria, topoisomerase IV is the primary target. For many gram-negative bacteria, DNA gyrase is the primary quinolone target.

Sample MCQ #5

Fluoroquinolone with highest oral bioavailability:

ALevofloxacin
BGemifloxacin
CCiprofloxacin
DNorfloxacin
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Levofloxacin

Levofloxacin It is the active levo(S) isomer of ofloxacin having improved activity against Strep. pneumoniae,tuberculosis and some other gram-positive and gram-negative bacteria. Anaerobes are moderately susceptible. Oral bioavailability of levofloxacin is nearly 100%; oral and i.v. doses are similar. It is mainly excreted unchanged, and a single daily dose is sufficient because of slower elimination and higher potency.

Sample MCQ #7

Supplementary folinic acid may prevent anemia in folate-deficient persons who use this drug; it is a weak base achieving tissue levels similar to those in plasma.

ACiprofloxacin
BLevofloxacin
CLinezolid
DTrimethoprim
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Trimethoprim

Trimethoprim is the only weak base listed (fluoroquinolones and sulfonamides are acidic compounds), and its high lipid solubility at blood pH allows penetration of the drug into prostatic and vaginal fluid to reach levels similar to those in plasma. Leukopenia and thrombocytopenia may occur in folate deficiency when the drug is used alone or in combination with sulfamethoxazole. Fluoroquinolones and linezolid do not exacerbate symptoms of folic acid deficiency. Reference: Basic & Clinical Pharmacology, Fourteenth Edition By Bertram G. Katzung Pg no. 837
