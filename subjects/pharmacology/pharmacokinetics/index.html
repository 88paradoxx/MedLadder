Sample MCQ #1

An investigator injects 7 mg of an experimental drug into an animal. The drug does not bind to plasma proteins and is eliminated unchanged in urine via first-order kinetics. Two hours later, the investigator obtains laboratory values from the animal that reveal the rate of drug elimination is 0.02 mg/min, the plasma drug concentration is 0.04 mg/mL, and the total amount of drug eliminated in urine is 5 mg. Which of the following is the best estimate of the clearance of the drug in the animal at the time of measurement?

A2 mL/min
B0.002 mL
C50 mL/min
D0.5 mL/min
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. 0.5 mL/min

Plasma clearance (CL) is the volume of plasma cleared of a substance in a period of time. It is estimated by dividing a substance's rate of elimination by its plasma concentration: CL = (0.02 mg/min)/(0.04 mg/mL) = 0.5 mL/min. In first-order kinetics, the rate of elimination of the substance and the plasma concentration of the substance decrease with time, but the CL remains constant. Alternatively, the CL of a substance with first-order kinetics can be calculated by multiplying the volume of distribution (Vd) by the elimination constant (Ke) of the substance. For a substance that is minimally protein-bound and excreted unchanged only in the urine, as in this case, the CL is essentially the glomerular filtration rate. In humans, inulin is such a substance Reference: Basic & Clinical Pharmacology by Bertram G. Katzung Fourteenth Edition Pg no.52

Sample MCQ #2

Which pharmacokinetic parameters are used to determine the loading dose of an orally administered drug? 1. Volume of distribution (Vd) 2. Half-life 3. Plasma concentration 4. Bioavailability

A1, 2 and 3
BOnly 1
C1, 3 and 4
D1, 2, 3 and 4
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. 1, 3 and 4

Explanation: Loading dose (LD) = (Desired steady-state plasma concentration &times; Vd) &divide; Bioavailability. Half-life determines maintenance dosing frequency and time to steady state, but it is not a direct component of the loading dose equation (Statement 2 ruled out) For oral drugs , bioavailability (F) must be accounted for because oral dosing is reduced by first-pass extraction and incomplete absorption. Formula (practical): LD (oral) = (C_target &times; Vd) / F Where: C_target = desired plasma concentration (therapeutic target) Vd = volume of distribution F = fraction absorbed (bioavailability)

Sample MCQ #3

There is no statistically significant difference in the dose-corrected area under the curve (AUC) for the two routes of administration. Which of the following is the most likely explanation for this finding?

AHigh bioavailability
BExtensive first-pass metabolism
CLow volume of distribution
DDelayed clearance
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. High bioavailability

This drug is almost entirely absorbed into systemic circulation when orally administered, as indicated by the lack of difference in the dose-corrected AUCs for oral and intravenous administration. indicating that this drug has near 100% bioavailability. Isosorbide mononitrate does not undergo first-pass metabolism by the liver and thus has approx. 100% bioavailability. Reference: Basic & Clinical Pharmacology by Bertram G. Katzung Fourteenth Edition Pg no.48

Sample MCQ #4

A 56-year-old patient is being treated with oral levofloxacin for community-acquired pneumonia. The plasma clearance of the drug is calculated as 12.0 L/h. Oral bioavailability of the drug is 50%. Sensitivity analysis of a sputum culture shows a minimal inhibitory concentration of 1 &mu;g/mL for the causative pathogen. The target plasma concentration is 2 mg/L. If the drug is administered twice per day, which of the following dosages should be administered at each dosing interval to maintain a steady state?

A567
B576
C675
D765
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. 576

A maintenance dose of 576 mg should be administered at 12 hour intervals to achieve the desired target concentration of 2 mg/L. The corresponding formula needed for calculation requires the following values: Cp = target plasma concentration at steady state (mg/L) = 2 mg/L Cl = clearance (L/h)= 12 L/h &tau; = dosing interval (hours)= 12 hour F = bioavailability = 50 % Maintenance dose = (Cp * Cl * &tau;) / F &rarr; (2*12*12)/0.50 = 288/0.5 = 576 Reference: Basic & Clinical Pharmacology by Bertram G. Katzung Fourteenth Edition Pg no.52

Sample MCQ #5

A 17-year-old woman is given a prescription of fluconazole to be taken daily (q 12 h) for 3 days. The half-life is 12 h. How long will it take for the medication to reach 90% of its final steady state level?

A20h
B30h
C40h
D50h
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. 40h

To calculate the time it takes for a medication to reach 90% of its final steady state, you can use the formula (3.3) * (t1/2). Given that the half-life (t1/2) of fluconazole is 12 hours: Time to Reach 90% of Steady State = (3.3) * (12 hours) = 39.6 hours So, it will take approximately 39.6 hours for the medication to reach 90% of its final steady-state level. Among the given options, the closest one is: C) 40 hours This option is the most accurate representation of the calculated time of approximately 39.6 hours. Reference: Basic & Clinical Pharmacology by Bertram G. Katzung, Fourteenth Edition, Pg no. 51

Sample MCQ #6

What formula is the dosing rate of a drug based on?

AClearance/Plasma concentration
BClearance*Plasma concentration
CClearance*loading dose
DClearance/loading dose
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Clearance*Plasma concentration

When a drug is repeated at relatively short intervals, it accumulates in the body until elimination balances input and a steady state plasma concentration ( Cpss ) is attained- Cpss = dose rate/CL From this equation it is implied that doubling the dose rate would double the average Cpss and so on. Further, if the therapeutic plasma Concentration of the drug has been worked out and its CL is known, the dose rate needed to achieve the target Cpss can be determined- Dose rate = target Cpss &times; CL

Sample MCQ #7

What does the term LD&#8325;&#8304; represent in toxicology?

AThe dose that causes death in 50% of a tested animal population
BThe dose that reduces infection in 50% of a tested population
CThe dose that produces a toxic effect in 50% of the population
DThe range where drug efficacy is high and adverse effects are low
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. The dose that causes death in 50% of a tested animal population

Explanation for answer: The LD&#8325;&#8304; (Lethal Dose 50%) is a standard measure of acute toxicity. It represents the single dose of a substance required to kill 50% of a population of test animals under controlled conditions. A higher LD&#8325;&#8304; indicates a safer drug. Key Takeaways: LD&#8325;&#8304; measures acute lethality in animal studies. Therapeutic Index (TI) = LD&#8325;&#8304; / ED&#8325;&#8304;; a higher TI indicates a safer drug.

Sample MCQ #8

All of the following are advantages of enteric coated tablets except:

AIt protects acid labile drugs from gastric pH
BIt increases the half-life of the drug.
CIt increases the absorption of drugs that are preferentially absorbed distal to stomach
DIt protects stomach from irritant drugs
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. It increases the half-life of the drug.

Certain drugs are degraded in the gastrointestinal tract, e.g. penicillin G by acid, insulin by peptidases, and are ineffective orally. Enteric coated tablets have acid resistant coating, can be Used to overcome acid lability, gastric irritancy and also used to increase absorption of drugs which are absorbed from the intestine(Options A,C,D) Sustained release preparations have drug particles coated with slowly dissolving material that can increases the half-life of the drug.
