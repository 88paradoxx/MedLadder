APrazosin
BAliskiren
CMetolazone
DMetoprolol
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Metolazone

Metolazone In common with loop diuretics it is able to evoke a clinically useful response even in severe renal failure (g.f.r. ~15 ml/min), and has marked additive action when combined with furosemide. An additional proximal tubular action has been demonstrated; PO4 reabsorption that occurs in PT, is inhibited. It is excretedunchanged in urine. Metolazone has been usedmainly for edema and occasionally for hypertension

Sample MCQ #2

A 75-year-old man with COPD and chronic heart failure presents to you for follow-up. The patient takes multiple medications for these problems. Which of the following strategies may prove to have additional benefit in the treatment of this patient?

AExercise program involving running
BUse of calcium channel blockers at high dose
CStopping use of NSAIDs
DUse of beta-blockers at high doses
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Stopping use of NSAIDs

Chronic HF is typically managed by A reduction in physical activity Low dietary intake of sodium ( Treatment of comorbid conditions Judicious use of diuretics, inhibitors of the renin-angiotensin system, and inotropic agents. Drugs that may precipitate or exacerbate HF, such as NSAIDs drugs, alcohol, calcium channel blockers, high-dose beta-blockers, and some antiarrhythmic drugs, should be avoided if possible. Patients with HF complain of dyspnea on exertion, orthopnea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea, fatigue, and dependent edema. Vigorous exercise in this deconditioned patient may worsen heart function. Use of calcium channel blockers at high doses can worsen heart failure. Use of beta-blockers at high doses can worsen heart failure. Sample MCQ #3

A 60-year-old patient complains of paresthesias and occasional nausea associated with one of the drugs she is taking. She is found to have hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis. She is probably taking

AAcetazolamide for glaucoma
BAmiloride for edema associated with aldosteronism
CFurosemide for severe hypertension and heart failure
DHydrochlorothiazide for hypertension
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Acetazolamide for glaucoma

Paresthesias and gastrointestinal distress are common adverse effects of acetazolamide, especially when it is taken chronically, as in glaucoma. The observation that the patient has metabolic acidosis also suggests the use of a carbonic anhydrase inhibitor like acetazolamide. Sample MCQ #4

A 57-year-old woman with "fainting spell", her blood pressure is 120/60 when lying down and 60/20 when she sits up. Which of the following drugs is the most likely cause of her fainting spell?

AAcetazolamide
BAmiloride
CFurosemide
DHydrochlorothiazide
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Furosemide

The case history suggests that the syncope (fainting) is associated with diuretic use. Complications of diuretics that can result in syncope include both postural hypotension (which this patient exhibits) due to excessive reduction of blood volume and arrhythmias due to excessive potassium loss. Potassium wasting is more common with thiazides (because of their long duration of action), but these drugs rarely cause reduction of blood volume sufficient to result in orthostatic hypotension. Reference : Basic & Clinical Pharmacology, Fourteenth Edition By Bertram G. Katzung Pg no. 262

Sample MCQ #5

A patient diagnosed with ascites due to cirrhosis. The diuretic of choice to reduce edema would be?

AEplerenone
BFurosemide
CChlorthalidone
DTriamterene
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Eplerenone

Aldosterone antagonists like Spironolactone and Eplerenone are the drugs of choice for Cirrhotic edema, resistant edema and hypertension. Uses of Spironolactone & Eplerenone It is a weak diuretic in its own right and is used mostly in combination with other more efficacious diuretics. To counteract K+ loss due to thiazide and loop diuretics. Edema: Spironolactone is more useful in cirrhotic and nephritic edema in which aldosterone levels are generally high. It potentiates thiazides and loop diuretics and is frequently added to them in the treatment of ascites due to cirrhosis of the liver. It breaks resistance to the thiazide/loop diuretic that develops due to secondary hyperaldosteronism and re-establishes the response. Thus, spironolactone is particularly employed in refractory edema. Hypertension: Spironolactone is used to augment a thiazide in the treatment of resistant hypertension.

DHydrochlorothiazide

✓ Correct Answer: C. Metolazone

Metolazone and Indapamide are two thiazide diuretics that can be administered even in patients with renal insufficiency. Metolazon e: In common with loop diuretics, it is able to evoke a clinically useful response even in severe renal failure (g.f.r. ~15 ml/min), and has marked additive action when combined with furosemide. Sample MCQ #8

A 54-year-old man comes with severe pain with tenderness in his first metatarsophalangeal joint. He says that the pain was so bad that it woke him up from sleep. Which of the following drugs is the most likely cause of this patient's symptoms? Laboratory studies show WBC count is 12 x 103/mL , creatinine is 1.0 mg/dL, calcium is 8.6 mg/dL, and uric acid is 9.5 mg/dL.

AAripiprazole
BEnalapril
CHydrochlorothiazide
DPravastatin
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Hydrochlorothiazide

Acute gouty arthritis (AGA) is characterized by the rapid onset of pain, swelling, and inflammation. The attack is often monoarticular at first, affecting the first metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joint. Uric acid levels are generally >7.5 mg/dL when an attack occurs. Changes in rate levels, leading to gout flares, can result from acute alcohol intoxication, overindulgence of foods high in purines, rapid weight loss, dehydration, and trauma. Medications (e.g., thiazide or loop diuretics, allopurinol, uricosuric agents, Low-dose aspirin) can also precipitate gout.
