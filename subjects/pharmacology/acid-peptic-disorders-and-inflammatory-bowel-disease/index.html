Sample MCQ #2

A 12-year-old boy is brought due to chronic diarrhea and failure to gain weight appropriately. He has a history of recurrent respiratory infections, and prior sputum cultures have been positive for pseudomonas aeruginosa. His younger brother died from a severe respiratory infection at age 9. Which of the following agents is most likely to improve this patient's condition in the long term?

AAntimotility agents
BMetronidazole
COctreotide
DPancreatic lipase
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Pancreatic lipase

This young Caucasian patient has a history of recurrent respiratory infections caused by Pseudomonas aeruginosa, chronic diarrhea, and failure to thrive. These findings, along with a family history of early sibling death from pulmonary infection, suggest a diagnosis of cystic fibrosis. Steatorrhea and failure to thrive occur as a result of malabsorption due to obstructive fibrosis and progressive insufficiency of the exocrine pancreas . Pancreatic lipase supplementation is likely to improve this patient's condition by enhancing macronutrient and vitamin absorption. Sample MCQ #4

A 34-year-old woman presents with intermittent burning sensation in her chest with an occasional simultaneous acidic taste at the back of her throat. The symptoms occur soon after she eats a meal. A medication is prescribed for treatment of her symptoms. The patient returns in 2 weeks for a follow-up visit and reports near-complete resolution of her symptoms. The prescribed medication most likely binds to which of the following targets?

ACCK-B (gastrin-binding) receptor
BChloride ion channel
CDopamine receptor
DHydrogen-potassium ATPase
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Hydrogen-potassium ATPase

This woman has symptoms of dyspepsia likely due to gastroesophageal reflux (GERD), a common disorder that occurs with reflux of gastric acid into the oesophagus. Management of patients with frequent symptoms includes lifestyle and dietary modifications (eg, weight loss, avoidance of tobacco) and medications to suppress gastric acid secretion, such as H2 receptor antagonists (eg, ranitidine) and proton pump inhibitors ([PPIs] eg, pantoprazole, omeprazole). PPl are the most effective inhibitors of gastric acid secretion; they bind to and irreversibly inhibit the hydrogen-potassium ATPase on parietal cells . Potential side effects are related to the increased gastric pH and include increased risk of Clostridium difficile infections and nutritional deficiencies (eg, calcium, magnesium, iron). Reference: Basic & Clinical Pharmacology, Fourteenth Edition By Bertram G. Katzung Pg no. 1099

Sample MCQ #5

Which of the following medications is used to treat GERD?

AOndansetron
BDomperidone
CLoperamide
DDiphenoxylate
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Domperidone

Explanation: The main treatments for GERD involve proton pump inhibitors and prokinetic agents. Domperidone is a prokinetic drug used to manage GERD. It works by tightening the lower esophageal sphincter (LES), which helps prevent stomach contents from refluxing back into the esophagus. Explanation for other options: Ondansetron (Option A): is a 5-HT3 receptor antagonist mainly used to prevent nausea and vomiting, particularly in chemotherapy or post-surgery patients. It is not used to treat GERD. Loperamide (Option C): is an antidiarrheal medication used to manage diarrhea, but it has no role in treating GERD. Diphenoxylate (Option D): is another antidiarrheal drug and does not have any direct application in the treatment of GERD.
