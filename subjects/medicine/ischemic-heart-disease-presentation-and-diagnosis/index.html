Which one of the following sets of components of cigarette smoke is a causal agent of coronary artery disease -

ATar and nicotine
BNicotine and carbon monoxide and tar
CCarbon monoxide and Tar
DCarbon dioxide
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Nicotine and carbon monoxide and tar

Ans. is `b' i.e., Nicotine and carbon monoxide and tar Mechanisms for smoking-induced Coronary heart disease Carbon monoxide induces atherogenesis. Nicotine stimulation of adrenergic drive raising both BP and myocardial oxygen demand. Lipid metabolism with fall in protective high-density lipoproteins. Sample MCQ #2

The earliest ECG abnormality in a patient with hyperkalemia is:

AQRS widening
BTall T waves
CPR prolongation
DU waves
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Tall T waves

ECG changes start to manifest usually when serum K > 6 mEq/L The earliest ECG finding in a patient with hyperkalemia is tall, tented T waves It is followed by other changes like P wave flattening, PR prolongation, QRS widening, appearance of sine wave pattern Hypokalemia is characterised by ECG changes like T wave flattening/inversion, ST segment depression, appearance of 'u' wave and prolonged QU interval. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Page 1911

Sample MCQ #3

A 62-year-old smoker is admitted with nausea, sweating and central crushing chest pain. A 12 lead ECG reveals ST elevation in leads II, III and aVF. Which coronary artery is most likely to have been affected in this case?

ACircumflex artery
BLeft anterior descending artery
CRight coronary artery
DObtuse marginal artery
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Right coronary artery

Right coronary artery supplies the right ventricle, the posterior third of the interventricular septum, the inferior wall of the left ventricle and a portion of the posterior wall of the left ventricle. (leads II, III, aVF corresponds to inferior wall) Occasionally the posterior interventricular septum may be supplied by a branch of the of the left circumflex artery, called left dominant circulation. The left anterior descending and left circumflex coronary arteries arise at the left main coronary artery bifurcation and supply the anterior left ventricle, the bulk of the interventricular septum (anterior two thirds), the apex, and the lateral and posterior left ventricular walls. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Page no: 2035

Sample MCQ #5

Which of the following pharmacological agents is most likely to benefit a patient with angina due to cardiac syndrome X?

AAspirin
BBisoprolol
CDiltiazem
DIsosorbide mononitrate
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Isosorbide mononitrate

Nitrates are often effective in patients with syndrome X, which is currently termed 'microvascular angina'. Cardiac syndrome X consists of angina-like chest pain during exertion, characteristic ECG changes during exercise testing, normal coronary arteries on cardiac catheterisation and no inducible coronary artery spasm during catheterisation. It should not be confused with the metabolic syndrome X, which comprises central obesity, glucose intolerance, dyslipidemia, and high blood pressure. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Page no: 2031, 2034

Sample MCQ #6

Which of the following is seen in a patient with Right ventricular myocardial infarction?

AST-segment elevation in leads II, III and aVF
BOcclusion of the left circumflex artery
CAbsent kussmaul's sign
DRise in systolic blood pressure
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. ST-segment elevation in leads II, III and aVF

Right ventricular myocardial infarction (RVMI) usually occurs in association with an inferior-wall left ventricular infarction, as revealed by the ECG. In ECG, along with ST elevation in leads II, III, aVF which suggests an inferior wall MI, a patient with RVMI may also have ST elevation in V1 and in right sided chest leads V3R-V6R. The typical clinical findings in RVMI include bradycardia (due to AV node involvement), hypotension, raised JVP with positive Kussmaul sign (inspiratory rise of JVP). Hypotension is typically treated with IV fluids (0.9% saline) There is usually a right coronary occlusion. Characteristic clinical features include a low cardiac output syndrome with jugular venous distension but no pulmonary vascular congestion. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Page no: 2062
