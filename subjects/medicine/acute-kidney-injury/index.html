Sample MCQ #1

You review a 67-year-old man for dyslipidemia. He is taking atorvastatin 40 mg/day. Which of the following drugs should be used with caution?

AWarfarin
BErythromycin
CDigoxin
DAntacids
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Erythromycin

The risk of statin induced myopathy is high when drugs like Erythromycin, anti fungals, immunosuppressive drugs and gemfibrozil is used along with statins, as these drugs interfere with the metabolism of statins. Statins will increase the drug levels of warfarin and digoxin too. Hence A,.B And C are right options. Reference: Harrison&rsquo;s Principles of Internal Medicine, ch: 407, Pg: 3149

Sample MCQ #2

A 37-year-old woman presents to her GP with a vaginal discharge. She has been taking the combined oral contraceptive pill (OCP). What is the most common infection associated with the use of the OCP?

ABacterial vaginosis
BChlamydial endocervicitis
CVaginal warts
DGenital herpes
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Chlamydial endocervicitis

Women on oral contraceptives have a higher incidence of chlamydial endocervicitis. Vaginal warts are also common in these women. However, it is believed that human papillomavirus infection may occur even before the woman starts taking the pill, and that there is no actual increase in the risk of acquiring infection. Sample MCQ #3

Which of the following is the functional effect of Hypokalemia on kidney function?

APhosphaturia
BHypocitraturia
CMetabolic Alkalosis
DAll of the above
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. All of the above

The functional effects of hypokalemia on the kidney can include Na+-Cl- and HCO3 - retention, polyuria, phosphaturia, hypocitraturia, and an activation of renal ammoniagenesis. Bicarbonate retention and other acid-base effects of hypokalemia can contribute to the generation of metabolic alkalosis. Hypokalemic polyuria is due to a combination of central polydipsia and an AVP-resistant renal concentrating defect. Structural changes in the kidney due to hypokalemia include a relatively specific vacuolizing injury to proximal tubular cells, interstitial nephritis, and renal cysts. Hypokalemia also predisposes to acute kidney injury and can lead to end-stage renal disease (ESRD) in patients with long-standing hypokalemia due to eating disorders and/or laxative abuse. Reference: Harrisons Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st Ed, Ch:53 Pg: 350

Sample MCQ #6

Match the following drugs used in the treatment of RA according to the mechanism of action? 1. Anakinra - i. Anti IL-6 2. Tocilizumab - ii. Co-stimulation inhibitor 3. Rituximab - iii. Anti IL-1 4. Abatacept - iv. Anti CD 20

A1-iii, 2-i, 3-iv, 4-ii
B1-i, 2-ii, 3-iii, 4-iv
C1-iii, 2-ii, 3-i, 4-iv
D1-ii, 2-iii, 3-i, 4-iv
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. 1-iii, 2-i, 3-iv, 4-ii

The question deals with the various biologics used in RA and their mechanism of action. The chief biologicals are the anti TNF&alpha; agents, which are described in the previous question. Anakinra is anti IL-1. It has limited use in RA and is used in other autoinflammatory syndromes and Juvenile idiopathic arthritis or Still's Disease. Tocilizumab is anti IL6 antibody. Another agent is Sarilumab. IL-6 is the chief cytokine in Ra which leads to activation of osteoclasts. Rituximab is anti CD 20 antibody. It is used in seropositive RA, refractory to other biologics. Abatacept is a fusion protein consisting of CTLA-4 linked to IgG. It inhibits the co-stimulatory signal for CD4 cells. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21st ed, Ch: 358, Pg: 2761

Sample MCQ #7

False regard to thrombotic thrombocytopenia purpura (TTP) is:

APlatelet transfusion is recommended to maintain a platelet count of 10 x 10^9/L.
BThe principal abnormality occurs due to the production of an abnormal WF cleaving protein.
CThe diagnosis includes proven or suspected arterial or venous thrombosis.
DAdministration of solvent/detergent-treated fresh frozen plasma isa useful treatment option
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Platelet transfusion is recommended to maintain a platelet count of 10 x 10^9/L.

Thrombotic thrombocytopenia purpura (TTP) is a rare thrombotic microangiopathy, occurring due to a deficiency of a specific von Willebrand factor cleaving protein known as ADAMTS13 The diagnostic pentad includes acute kidney injury, altered mental state, low platelets, fever and microangiopathic haemolytic anaemia.Not all features are necessary to make the diagnosis Platelet transfusions are essentially contraindicated in the absence of serious bleeding. Treatment options include plasmapheresis as first line, fresh frozen plasmaadministration, steroids, rituximab and aspirin. Reference: Harrison 20 th 886
