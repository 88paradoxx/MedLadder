Sample MCQ #2

Which of the following is not seen in pituitary apoplexy?

AHeadache
BHypotension
CHypertension
DVomiting
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Hypertension

Pituitary apoplexy is an endocrine emergency characterized by hypotension, hypoglycemia, head ache, bilateral visual symptoms, ophthalmoplegia and coma and death in severe cases. It occurs in a pre existing adenoma of the pituitary or spontaneously in post partum, sickle cell anemia, diabetes, hypertension or shock.

Sample MCQ #3

A 32-year-old woman presents to her GP with a history of amenorrhoea and galactorrhoea. She is keen to become pregnant and has been trying for 12 months to conceive without success. She is of normal weight and has no other constitutional symptoms. Which of the following is most likely to be the reason for her symptoms?

APolycystic ovarian syndrome
BHypothyroidism
CPreviously undiagnosed hepatic impairment
DPituitary microadenoma
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Pituitary microadenoma

Her symptoms are consistent with hyper-prolactinaemia . Raised prolactin has many causes including pituitary tumors (micro- and macroadenomas), drug treatment (some antidepressants like tricyclic antidepressants, antiemetics and cimetidine), pregnancy, stress, hypothyroidism (because thyrotropin-releasing hormone (TRH) stimulates TSH and prolactin in primary hypothyroidism) and renal failure (prolactin accumulates in renal failure but not in hepatic impairment). While hyperprolactinemia can be present in hypothyroidism, it is usually associated with menorrhagia rather than amenorrhoea. Polycystic ovarian syndrome may be associated with amenorrhea and mildly raised prolactin levels, but unlikely to result in galactorrhea. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Page 2909
