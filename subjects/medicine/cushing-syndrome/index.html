Sample MCQ #3

A 34-year-old woman presents with extreme lethargy a couple of weeks after the birth of her third child by emergency cesarean section. She complained to the health visitor of increasing problems some 7 days earlier, but was told that this was to be expected after the birth of her child. Her lactation was normal. On admission via casualty she was noted to have a sodium concentration of 127 mmol/l, a potassium concentration of 6.8 mmol/l and a urea of 70 mg/dl. What is the likely diagnosis?

APrimary adrenal insufficiency
BHypothyroidism
CSheehan syndrome
DPostnatal depression
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Primary adrenal insufficiency

One should always think of the possibility of Sheehan syndrome when a postpartum female presents with fatigue and hyponatremia. This is attributed to secondary adrenal insufficiency. Hyperkalemia is not seen in Sheehan syndrome as low ACTH doesn't impact aldosterone production from adrenal gland. However, absence of lactation is the most specific feature associated with Sheehan syndrome. The presence of normal lactation along with presence of hyperkalemia in the patient makes the diagnosis of Sheehan syndrome unlikely. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Page 2971

Sample MCQ #4

AResistant hypertension
BIncreased cardiovascular mortality
CHypokalemia

✓ Correct Answer: D. Metabolic acidosis

The typical triad in a patient with primary aldosteronism is: 1. Systemic hypertension 2. Hypokalemia 3. Metabolic alkalosis Most common cause for primary aldosteronism is bilateral adrenal hyperplasia followed by aldosterone producing adenoma (Conn syndrome) Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Page 2965

Sample MCQ #5

Which of the following antihypertensive agents will not affect the aldosterone-renin ratio?

ABeta blockers
BACE inhibitors
CDiuretics
DCalcium channel blockers
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Calcium channel blockers

Beta blockers will falsely increase the ARR (beta receptors mediate the renin secretion from JG apparatus and its blockade would lower renin production) ACE inhibitors, ARBs and diuretics reduce the ARR (they inhibit the aldosterone production thereby causing compensatory increase in renin levels) CCBs, &alpha;1 blockers and direct vasodilators like hydralazine do not affect the ARR. Reference : Harrison's Principles of internal medicine 21st edition, page number. 2966

Sample MCQ #6

A patient with Addison's disease presents with an acute onset of diarrhea and vomiting. He currently takes fludrocortisone and hydrocortisone. What is the most appropriate management?

AIntravenous (IV) metoclopramide
BIV Ondansetron
CIV Fluids and IV hydrocortisone
DIncrease fludrocortisone
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. IV Fluids and IV hydrocortisone

Patients receiving glucocorticoid replacement therapy should be advised to double the dose in the event of intercurrent febrile illness, accident, or mental stress such as an important examination. If the patient is vomiting and cannot take by mouth, parenteral hydrocortisone must be given urgently, as indicated above. There is no need to increase fludrocortisone dose during stress / inter current illness For minor surgery, 50-100 mg of hydrocortisone IV is given with the premedication. For major procedures this is then followed by the same regimen as for acute adrenal insufficiency. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Page 2972

Sample MCQ #7

A 25-year-old adopted man presents with transient left-sided weakness of his arm, which resolves after a few hours. His only other history of note is a recent weight gain with abdominal striae. . On examination he appears to have a spotty skin pigmentation. You notice a heart murmur, and there is a suggestion of a left atrial mass on echo. Biochemical evaluation showed elevated serum cortisol with suppressed ACTH. What is the most likely diagnosis?

ALeft atrial myxoma
BCarney complex
CCushing disease
DSomatization disorder
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Carney complex

This man has spotty skin pigmentation, ACTH independent Cushing syndrome and a probable left atrial myxoma. Carney complex is diagnosable with two features out of: spotty skin pigmentation myxoma of skin / heart ( MC location for cardiac myxoma : left atrium) endocrine tumors (commonest being primary pigmented nodular adrenocortical disease ), but it is also associated with Sertoli-cell tumors, growth hormone- or prolactin-producing pituitary adenomas, thyroid adenomas and ovarian cysts) and psammomatous melanotic schwannoma (PMS). It is also diagnosable in the presence of one feature and an affected first-degree relative. It is an autosomal-dominant condition caused by an inactivating mutation of protein kinase A on chromosome 17. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Page 2908

Sample MCQ #8

In treatment of Cushing's disease, which of the following drugs is useful?

AKetoconazole
BMetyrapone
CMitotane
DAll of the above
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. All of the above

In Cushing's disease, steroidogenic inhibitors are used in combination with pituitary irradiation to block the adrenal effects of persistently high ACTH levels. Ketoconazole is an antimycotic drug that inhibits several P450 enzymes involved in adrenal steroidogenesis. Metyrapone inhibits 11 beta-hydroxylase activities & Mitotane suppresses cortisol hypersecretion by inhibiting 11 beta-hydroxylase and cholesterol side-chain cleavage enzymes & by destroying adrenocortical cells. Drugs acting at the level of pituitary to reduce ACTH include Pasireotide and Cabergoline Drugs that block cortisol action on receptor include Mifepristone. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Page 2963
