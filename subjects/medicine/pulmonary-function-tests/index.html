Sample MCQ #1

The wife of a recently married 24-year-old medical student reports that even though her husband doesn't snore, he stops breathing for periods of 1 to 2 minutes while he is sleeping. His physician refers him for a polysomnography study and pulmonary function testing, including ventilatory response curves. The tests confirm the apneic episodes during sleep and show depressed ventilatory responsiveness of both the peripheral and central chemoreceptors. Peripheral and central chemoreceptors both contribute to the increased ventilation that occurs as a result of which of the following?

AA decrease in arterial oxygen content
BA decrease in arterial blood pressure
CAn increase in arterial carbon dioxide tension
DA decrease in arterial oxygen tension
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. An increase in arterial carbon dioxide tension

Both the central chemoreceptors, located on or near the ventral surface of the medulla, and the peripheral chemoreceptors, in the carotid and aortic bodies, cause an increase in ventilation in response to an increase in PaCO2. The peripheral chemoreceptors also cause an increase in ventilation in response to a decrease in arterial pH and a decrease in PaO2, but the central chemoreceptors are unresponsive to hypoxemia and do not cause an increase in ventilation in response to a decrease in arterial pH because the blood-brain barrier is relatively impermeable to hydrogen ions. Neither the central chemoreceptors nor the carotid bodies are stimulated by a decrease in arterial blood pressure or O2 content. Reference: Ganong, pp 662, 672-677, 686-688. Levitzky, pp 114-116, 180-182, 201-210.

Sample MCQ #2

A patient with congestive heart failure, orthopnea, and paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea is referred for pulmonary function testing in the supine and upright positions. Which of the following is higher at the apex of the lung than at the base when a person is upright?

AVentilation
BBlood flow
CV/Q ratio
DPaCO2
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. V/Q ratio

The alveoli at the apex of the lung are larger than those at the base so their compliance is less. Because the compliance is reduced, less inspired gas goes to the apex than to the base. Also, because the apex is above the heart, less blood flows through the apex than through the base. However, the reduction in airflow is less than the reduction in blood flow, so that the V/Q ratio at the top of the lung is greater than it is at the bottom. The increased V/Q ratio at the apex makes PACO2 lower and PAO2 higher at the apex than they are at the base. Reference: Ganong, pp 643-644, 662-663, 686-688. Levitzky, pp 123-128. Stead et al., pp 25-27.

Sample MCQ #3

Which extrapulmonary site is Most commonly involved in TB ?

ALymph nodes
BPleura
CGenitourinary tract
DBones and joints
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Lymph nodes

In order of frequency, the extrapulmonary sites most commonly involved in TB are the lymph nodes , pleura , genitourinary tract , bones and joints, meninges, peritoneum, and pericardium. Reference :Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st Ed, Ch: 178 Pg: 1364

Sample MCQ #4

Which of the following will be incorrect in Rh incompatibility?

ARaised unconjugated bilirubin
BRaised stool urobilin
CRaised urine urobilin
DIncrease urine bilirubin
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Increase urine bilirubin

In rh incompatibility, there will be hemolysis and the picture will be similar to any other hemolytic anemia. In hemolytic jaundice, in the blood we will get indirect bilirubinemia. Liver function tests will be normal. Since unconjugated bilirubin is bound to albumin, it will not be excreted in urine and hence urine will be colorless. Whatever bilirubin is formed will be conjugated by the liver and excreted through the bile, where in the gut it will be converted into stercobilin and subsequently to urobilin. A part of the urobilin will enter the systemic circulation where it will be excreted in the urine.

Sample MCQ #5

A 65-year-old man was advised to start oral digoxin at a dose of 250 &mu;g daily. His doctor explained that the full effect of this treatment would not be apparent for at least a week. Which one of the following pharmacokinetic variables did the doctor use to give this explanation?

ABioavailablity
BHalf life
CPlasma protein binding
DRenal clearance
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Half life

Digoxin follows first order kinetics and has a half-life of 36 to 48 hours in a patient with normal renal function. Sixty-five per cent of the drug absorbed remains in the system after one day. Subsequent doses gradually accumulate until a steady state is achieved after four to five days. The bioavailability of digoxin is around 70%. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Page 1949

Sample MCQ #6

Which of the following goes against a diagnosis of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease?

AHyper inflated lung fields
BPursed lip breathing
CClubbing
DPolycythaemia
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Clubbing

Clubbing is not a feature of COPD, its presence should raise the suspicion of an underlying lung malignancy. Physical findings of COPD: Nicotine staining of fingernails Signs of respiratory distress: pursed lip breathing, accessory muscle use, tripod position, inspiratory in drawing of ribs (esp lower ribs, ka Hoover's sign) Cachexia (poor prognostic finding) Signs of hyper inflated lungs: barrel chest, hyper resonance on percussion Auscultation: coarse crepitations (fibrosis), polyphonic rhonchi Signs of right heart failure (cor pulmonale): Elevated JVP, Pedal edema, ascites. Labs may show a 2&deg; polycythaemia and chronic respiratory acidosis with metabolic alkalosis. Reference: Harrison's Principles of internal medicine 21st edition, chapter. 292 page number. 2185

Sample MCQ #7

Which of the following is not common in primary pulmonary tuberculosis?

ACavity
BFibrosis
CLymphadenopathy
DPleural effusion
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Cavity

Because most inspired air is distributed to the middle and lower lung zones, these areas are most commonly involved in primary TB. In the majority of cases, the lesion heals spontaneously and becomes evident only as a small calcified nodule. Pleural reaction overlying a subpleural focus is also common. The Ghon focus, with or without overlying pleural reaction, thickening, and regional lymphadenopathy, is referred to as the Ghon complex. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st Ed, Ch: 172 Pg: 1362

COral corticosteroids
DPostural drainage

✓ Correct Answer: D. Postural drainage

The patient has bronchiectasis , in which the common complication is difficulty expectorating, postural drainage of secretions is helpful. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st Ed, Ch:290 Pg: 2175
