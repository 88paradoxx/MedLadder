Sample MCQ #1

Which of the following denotes normal cardiac axis?

A-90 to + 180 degrees
B+90 to 180 degrees
CLess than -30 degrees
D-30 to +90 degrees
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. -30 to +90 degrees

The cardiac axis represents the overall direction of electrical activity as it spreads through the cardiac conduction system The normal cardiac axis is directed downward and slightly to the left. It falls within the range of -30&deg; to +90&deg;. Left Axis Deviation (LAD): When the QRS axis is less than -30&deg;. Right Axis Deviation (RAD): When the QRS axis is greater than +90&deg;. Extreme Axis Deviation: Occurs when the QRS axis lies between -90&deg; and 180&deg; ("Northwest Axis") Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Page 1870

Sample MCQ #2

A 24-year-old patient is admitted after falling asleep drunk in a toilet cubicle. On examination his BP 130/70, pulse 98 bpm and there is no pericardial rub. There is 'boggy' tenderness of his right calf and both thigh muscles. Biochemistry reveals urea 125 mg/dl, creatinine 4.0 mg/dl, bicarbonate 15 mmol/l, pH 7.1, potassium 7.8 mmol/l and creatine kinase 17 000 IU/l. (Ref values: s. creat: 0.6-1.4 mg/dl; S. urea: 17- 43 mg/dL; S. potassium: 3.5-5 mmol/l; S. bicarbonate: 22 to 32 mmol/L; S. creatine kinase: 5 to 25 IU/L IU/l) What is the most appropriate form of treatment?

AContinuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis
BDopamine
CForced alkaline diuresis
DHaemodialysis
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Haemodialysis

This patient was immobilized for prolonged period, which can lead to rhabdomyolysis and renal failure, as evidenced by the high Creatine kinase levels. In addition, he has hyperkalemia and severe metabolic acidosis. Also, he has boggy swelling and tenderness of his muscles, suggestive of compartment syndrome or bilateral DVT. So he is having multiple issues that requires rapid correction. Hence hemodialysis is the best treatment Forced alkaline diuresis may renal failure is established with life-threatening hyperkalemia and acidosis, it is inappropriate. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21st edition, chp:310, pg: 2307.

✓ Correct Answer: B. Bradycardia

Cocaine is a psychostimulant . The other agents belonging to this group are, Methamphetamine, MDMA and Cathinone. There are certain therapeutically used psychostimulants too. These include Methylphenidate and Dextroamphetamine, used in the treatment of ADHD. Sample MCQ #5

An elderly man develops syncope. He is known to have ischaemic heart disease. Peripheral pulses are absent, and ECG reveals wide-complex tachycardia. Immediate management consists of which of the following?

AProgrammed stimulation
BThrombolysis with TPA or streptokinase
CA bolus dose of intravenous lidocaine
DAsynchronous cardioversion
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Asynchronous cardioversion

The patient has ventricular tachycardia and is hemodynamically unstable indicated by loss of consciousness. Immediate treatment consists of cardioversion followed by a suitable anti-arrhythmic such as lidocaine IV or amiodarone and correction of any electrolyte imbalance. Myocardial infarction (MI) is the likely cause of ventricular tachycardia, hence treatment for MI would be indicated if required. Programmed stimulation may only be carried out when the patient is stable. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Page no: 2252

Sample MCQ #6

Which of the following is a characteristic finding of Tricuspid regurgitation?

ADiastolic murmur in left sternal border
BPansystolic murmur at apex
CWidely split S2
DProminent V waves in JVP
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Prominent V waves in JVP

Tricsupid regurgitation - Around 90% is secondary to left sided valvular heart disease and pulmonary hypertension - Syndromes associated with primary TR include Marfan syndrome, Carcinoid syndrome and with Ebstein anomaly - Clinical examination shows elevated JVP with prominent c-v waves (Lancisi sign) and pulsatile hepatomegaly - Pansystolic murmur is typically heard in the left lower sternal border that increases during inspiration (Carvallo sign) Pansystolic murmur at apex is typically caused by mitral regurgitation. PSM at left lower sternal border can be caused by TR and VSD. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Page 2003

Sample MCQ #7

A 22-year-old man presents with tremor, sweating, flushing and weight loss. On examination his pulse is 135 bpm and his blood pressure is 160/110 mmHg. Which of the following is the least likely diagnostic possibility?

AVon Recklinghausen's disease
BVon Hippel-Lindau syndrome
CAtaxia telengiectsia
DCarney's triad
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Ataxia telengiectsia

Pheochromocytomas are catecholamine secreting tumours arising from the adrenal gland. Pheochromocytoma may occur in patients with von Recklinghausen's disease (NF-1) and von Hippel-Lindau syndrome. Skin neurofibromas and multiple cafe au-lait spots occur in peripheral (NF1) neurofibromatosis, while haemangioblastomas are commonly seen in those with the von Hippel-Lindau syndrome. Carney's triad includes Pheochromocytoma /paraganglioma, pulmonary chondroma and Gastrointestinal stromal tumour (GIST) Ataxia telangiectasia is not associated with pheochromocytomas. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Page 2976

Sample MCQ #8

A 27-year-old woman presented with a history of sudden onset right-sided weakness and dysphasia lasting 8 hours. She had returned to India from US 2 days previously. There was no significant past medical history and physical examination was normal. Chest X-ray, ECG and a CT head scan were all normal. Which one of the following investigations is most likely to reveal the underlying cause of this episode?

ACarotid doppler ultrasonography
BCerebral angiography
CMRI of head
DTransoesophageal echocardiography
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Transoesophageal echocardiography

The history here suggests a lower limb deep vein thrombosis with peripheral embolus through a patent foramen ovale, leading to a left sided cerebrovascular event. This is termed the paradoxical embolus - so-called because a thromboembolus arising from the venous circulation can end up in the systemic circulation. Transoesophageal echocardiography is the investigation of choice to investigate for a patent foramen ovale, although transthoracic echocardiography with contrast may be an alternative. Reference: Harrison&rsquo;s Principles of Internal Principles, 20 th edition, ch:273 pg:1913
