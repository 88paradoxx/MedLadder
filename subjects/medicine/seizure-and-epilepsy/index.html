Sample MCQ #1

A 58-year-old woman with epilepsy refractory to medical therapy is hospitalised for deep brain stimulation. This new modality uses a pacemaker-like device to stimulate which of the following structures?

ACerebrum
BThalamus
CHypothalamus
DBasal ganglia
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Thalamus

Deep brain stimulation (DBS) therapy uses a pacemaker-like device to deliver targeted electrical stimulation to the anterior nucleus of the thalamus. The therapy is FDA-approved with conditions for adjunctive treatment for partial-onset seizures in adults with medically refractory epilepsy. DBS is also FDA-approved for treatment of advanced Parkinson disease and essential tremor. Reference: Basic & Clinical Pharmacology, Fourteenth Edition By Bertram G. Katzung Page number. 417

Sample MCQ #2

A 26-year-old woman with lifelong epilepsy controlled with medication has just found out that she is pregnant. She has seizures once a month but seem to be controlled at present. Which of the following statements about epilepsy in pregnancy is true?

ABarbiturates should be considered
BValproate is considered a drug of choice
CShe should be taking high doses of folic acid
DShe will likely have no change in seizure activity during pregnancy
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. She should be taking high doses of folic acid

Women with epilepsy are often very concerned about pregnancy and what effect the medications might have on fetal development. Planning is the most important component. All women considering pregnancy should be on high doses of folic acid prior to conception. Valproate and barbiturates should be avoided. Those women already on valproate and barbiturates should be switched to other drugs before pregnancy when possible. When seizures are controlled, maintenance medication may be reduced, if possible, to the lowest dose that provides control. If seizures are not controlled, medications and dosages will need to be adjusted prior to pregnancy, if possible. The frequency and severity of seizures may change during pregnancy. Reference: Basic & Clinical Pharmacology, Fourteenth Edition By Bertram G. Katzung Page number 430

Sample MCQ #3

A 66-year-old man presents with seizures. Past medical history includes a smoking history of 30 cigarettes a day for many years. His family report a weight loss over the past 8 months.Serum electrolytes show: Sodium 109 mmol/L (135-145) Potassium 4.2 mmol/L (3.5-5.0) Glucose 126 mg/dL Urea 3.0 mmol/L (3.2-8.1) Urine sodium : 68 mmol/ L These findings are most likely to be due to:

AEpilepsy
BSyndrome of inappropriate ADH secretion
CDiuretics
DDiabetes insipidus
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Syndrome of inappropriate ADH secretion

The biochemistry points to a syndrome of inappropriate ADH secretion, with a low serum osmolality [2(Na + K) + urea + glucose]. The clinical picture is consistent with small cell carcinoma of the lung, which is often associated with SIADH. SIADH should be diagnosed only after ruling out hypothyroidism and cortisol deficiency. Diuretic use associated hyponatremia could be associated with low normal serum potassium. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Page 2924

Sample MCQ #4

True with respect to the tumour lysis syndrome (TLS):

AHigh-dose intravenous steroid therapy is a reasonable initial treatment option.
BTLS is most commonly associated with squamous cell and neuroendocrine tumours.
CClinical TLS can be diagnosed in a patient with seizures and the presence of appropriate laboratory findings.
DAn increase in serum calcium and potassium levels by >25% is the hallmark feature of laboratory TLS.
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Clinical TLS can be diagnosed in a patient with seizures and the presence of appropriate laboratory findings.

Tumour lysis syndrome (TLS) is a potentially life-threatening complication of massive cellular lysis in cancer, often encountered in bulky, rapidly progressive or chemo-sensitive tumours. It is most commonly seen in haematological malignancy. Hypocalcaemia is a hallmark feature due to precipitation of ionised calcium with phosphate, which is released in large quantities in tumour lysis. Other laboratory features of TLS include: increase in serum uric acid by >25% from baseline; increase in serum potassium by >25% from baseline; increase in serum phosphate by >25% from baseline. Intravenous hydration and rasburicase are first-line treatments and prophylaxis agents in high-risk groups. Reference: Harrison 20 th 2103,3001

Sample MCQ #5

Woman with morning stiffness which gradually resolves over one hour. Which is correct about the treatment and findings? a. NSAIDS improve symptoms and have disease modifying activity b. Absence of erosion on x-ray rules out diagnosis c. Raised ESR and CRP d. HLA DR4 is related to prognosis

Aa, b, c are correct
Ba and b are correct
Cc and d are correct
Da, c, d are correct
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. c and d are correct

The question deals with RA. NSAIDS are not disease modifying agents. They are analgesic and anti-inflammatory. ESR and CRP may be raised in RA. RA has a strong HLA association to DR locus. RA classically causes erosive arthritis. But plain X ray may not be always showing erosions. It can be viewed using MRI or ultrasound.

Sample MCQ #6

Seizures are seen in what percentage of RTA patients with brain contusion/loss of consciousness?

ADevelopment of seizures in 17%
BDevelopment of seizures in 45%
CDevelopment of seizures in 75%
DNo relation between trauma and seizures
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Development of seizures in 17%

Explanation: Post-traumatic seizures occur in approximately 17% of patients with brain contusion, subdural hematoma, or prolonged loss of consciousness. Convulsions are uncommon immediately after traumatic brain injury, though brief tonic extensor posturing or clonic movements may occur at impact. Penetrating injuries carry a particularly high risk of subsequent epilepsy development.

Sample MCQ #7

Which of the following features are not compatible with the diagnosis of motor neurone disease (MND)?

ADementia
BDysphagia
CMuscle cramps
DOptic atrophy
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Optic atrophy

Optic atrophy is not a feature of MND. Other features not compatible with the diagnosis are sensory impairment and bladder and bowel dysfunction. Muscle cramps occurring with volitional activities especially in early morning hours can be seen in very early disease. Even though there is severe brain stem dysfunction, Ocular motility will also be spared in MND till late stages ALS especially familial ALS is associated with behavioural dysfunction suggesting a frontal lobe dysfunction. Cognitive changes don't occur in MND. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st ed, Ch: 437, Pg: 3411-3412

Sample MCQ #8

A 63 year woman with a long history of alcohol abuse has been on Phenytoin for epilepsy. On examination, she has a palpable a liver edge. Her full blood count reveals: Hemoglobin 11.0 g/dL MCV 116 fL white blood count 2.3 x 10^9/L platelet count 95 x 10^9/L What is the likely explanation for these results?

AAplastic anaemia
BVitamin C deficiency
CIron deficiency
DFolate deficiency
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Folate deficiency

This patient presents with a clinical picture consistent with megaloblastic anemia . Megaloblastic anemia is either due to Folic acid or Vitamin B12 deficiency. Given her history of heavy alcohol intake and phenytoin usage, folate deficiency is the most likely cause. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st ed, ch: 99, Pg: 773
