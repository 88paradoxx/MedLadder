Bb Infants

DAdolescents

✓ Correct Answer: A. Pregnant women

In outbreaks of waterborne hepatitis E in India and Asia, the case-fatality rate is 1-2% and up to 10-20% in pregnant women. Reference : Harrison's Principles of internal medicine 21st ed chap 339 pg. 2577

Sample MCQ #2

A 76-year-old woman presents with acute monocular visual loss. Fundoscopy reveals a swollen pale optic disc in the affected eye. What is the most likely diagnosis?

ACentral retinal vein occlusion
BClosed angle glaucoma
CGiant cell arteritis
DOptic neuritis
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Giant cell arteritis

The presence of a swollen optic disc suggests ischaemic optic neuropathy. In elderly people giant cell arteritis is a common presentation of acute monocular visual loss. Optic neuritis is very rare in people over the age of 50. You would expect bilateral swollen optic discs in raised intracranial pressure. In central retinal vein occlusion you would expect diffuse retinal haemorrhages. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st ed, Ch: 426, Pg: 3343

Sample MCQ #6

A 75 year man presents with right foot drop, hand numbness, fevers, malaise, weight loss, polymyalgia and diffuse joint pains for 2 months. On examination, he appears unwell and has a temperature of 38&deg;C. Investigations reveal:Hb 8.5 g/dL erythrocyte sedimentation rate 95 mm/hr serum creatinine 2.0 mg/dL urine analysis: blood ++ urine microscopy: white cells & red cell casts seen Which one of the following is the LEAST likely diagnosis?

ALupus Vasculitis
BCryoglobulinemic vasculitis
CCancer associated vasculitis
DPolyarteritis nodosa
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Polyarteritis nodosa

This patient presents with multi system involvement as characterized by the presence of multiple peripheral nerve involvement, arthralgia and fever. His laboratory reports show features of inflammation characterized by anemia, high ESR. He also has renal impairment with hematuria and RBC cast which indicates glomerulonephritis. The entire constellation of symptoms points towards the diagnosis of a vasculitis. PAN is a disease of medium sized vessels and it does not cause glomerulonephritis. Renal impairment and hypertension can be seen in PAN due to involvement of the renal artery or its branches. All the other options are small vessel vasculitis and can cause glomerulonephritis. Reference: Harrison&rsquo;s Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Ch:363 Pg:2810

Sample MCQ #7

A 35-year-old female presented with claudication in forearm, transient loss of vision and abdominal pain. She also had weak femoral pulses. Fundus also showed retinal haemorrhages. What is the diagnosis?

ATakayasu arteritis
BPAN
CMicroscopic polyangiitis
DThromboangiitis obliterans
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Takayasu arteritis

In this question, we have a young female with symptoms of upper limb claudication, visual loss, abdominal pain and weak femoral pulses. The history points to the involvement of the branches of Aorta. The retinal hemorrhage could be secondary to hypertension. The involvement of large and medium vessels in a young female points towards the diagnosis of Takayasu Arteritis. PAN typically involves renal and visceral vessels, ie, small and medium sized arteries. It does not produce absent peripheral pulses. Microscopic polyangiitis is a small vessel vasculitis, mainly presenting as glomerulonephritis and pulmonary vasculitis. Thromboangiitis obliterans(Buerger's disease) is an inflammatory occlusive vascular disorder of small and medium sized arteries of the distal upper and lower limb and is seen in men

Sample MCQ #8

Atheromatous changes of blood vessels affects early in -

AKidney
BHeart
CLiver
DSpleen
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Heart

Ans. is 'b' i.e., Heart Sites of Atherosclerosis Atherosclerotic plaques develop primarily in elastic arteries (e.g., aorta, carotid and iliac arteries), and large & medium sized muscular arteries (e.g., coronary artery and poplitial arteries). In descending order, the vessels most commonly involved are - Abdominal aorta (most common) → Causing aneurysm Coronary arteries (heart) → Causing MI Popliteal arteries → Causing ischemic gangrene of lower limbs. Internal carotid arteries → Causing stroke a Circle of willis Vessels usually spared are Vessels of upper extremities Mesentric and renal vessels, except at their ostia.
