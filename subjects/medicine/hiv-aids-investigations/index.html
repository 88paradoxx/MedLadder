At what intervals should monitoring of patients on HAART should be done?

AMonthly
BYearly
C2-4Months
D3-6months
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. 3-6months

CD4 cell count and viral load have to be monitored every 3-6 months to monitor the response to treatment. Reference: Essentials of Medical Microbiology, by Apurba Sastry and Sandhya Bhat, 3rd edition, page 334

Sample MCQ #2

A 38-year-old man presents with a 16 week history of indigestion. Five years previously he had been treated for a duodenal ulcer. Investigations reveal: Fasting gastrin 120 pmol/l (<55) Which one of the following statements regarding gastrin is correct?

AIt acts upon the G cells of the stomach
BIt inhibits the secretion of pancreatic bicarbonate
CIt is produced by the parietal cells of the stomach
DIts release is stimulated by gastric luminal peptides
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Its release is stimulated by gastric luminal peptides

Gastrin is produced in response to Stimulation by neural reflex pathways By the direct effect of digested peptides on the G cells themselves. Gastrin is mainly produced by the G cells of the gastric antrum. Gastrin stimulates the production of bicarbonate by pancreas. It stimulates the parietal cells to produce hydrochloric acid. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st Ed, Ch: 324, Pg: 2435

Sample MCQ #3

A 44-year-old woman presents with a mass in her neck and symptoms of choking/ shortness of breath when she lies down. Investigations; Hb 14.1 g/dl WCC 5.4 x 10^9/L PLT 235 x 10^9/L Na+ 140 mmol/l K+ 4.5 mmol/l Creatinine 0.6 mg/dl TSH 0.9 U/l You suspect that she may have retrosternal extension of the goitre. Which of the following would be the investigation of choice for this patient to determine the degree of retrosternal involvement?

ACXR
BThyroid ultrasound
CX-ray of thoracic inlet
DCT Chest
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. CT Chest

Whilst a plain radiograph may be used to demonstrate retrosternal thyroid extension and tracheal deviation, a CT of the chest is more sensitive and specific in determining the extent of disease. Thyroid ultrasound is most useful for determining the nature of thyroid nodules. Radioisotope scanning has a role to play in determining the uptake of thyroid hormone and as such the underlying diagnosis behind thyrotoxicosis, or in identifying cold nodules within the gland which may raise the suspicion of an underlying neoplasm. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Page:2946

Sample MCQ #4

A 33 year old lady presents with stiffness, pain and swelling of her hands and wrists. Investigations reveal a normocytic, normochromic anemia, elevated ESR and CRP. What antibody would you use if you wanted to do A test for detecting RA factor?

AIgA antibody
BIgM antibody
CIgE antibody
DIgD antibody
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. IgM antibody

IgM, IgG, and IgA isotypes of RF occur in sera from patients with RA, although the IgM isotype is the one most frequently measured by commercial laboratories. The sensitivity of RA is only 75%, therefore a negative test does not exclude the presence of the disease. False positive in other connective tissue diseases, such as primary Sjogren's syndrome, systemic lupus erythematosus, and type II mixed essential cryoglobulinemia, as well as chronic infections such as subacute bacterial endocarditis and hepatitis B and C. Serum RF may also be detected in 1-5% of the healthy population. Anti CCP has sensitivity almost similar to RA factor, but is almost 95% specific for RA. Anti CCP has a role in predicting the outcomes too, as it is associated with worse outcomes. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21st edition Ch:358 Pg:2759

Sample MCQ #5

A 44-year-old male attends for a routine medical and is in good health. Examination was normal but investigations reveal that he has a serum urate concentration of 9.2 mg/dl (4.2-7.5). Which of the following is the most appropriate management for this patient?

ALifestyle advice
BStart allopurinol
CStart colchicine
DStart diclofenac
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Lifestyle advice

The most appropriate treatment for this asymptomatic man with an isolated slightly elevated urate is lifestyle advice with an appropriately reduced purine diet, increased exercise and reduced alcohol consumption. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st ed, Ch: 372, Pg: 2863-2865

Sample MCQ #6

A 36-year-old HIV-positive man, evaluated for weight loss and weakness has been found to have disseminated tuberculosis. On examination, he is hypotensive and has hyperpigmentation of the mucosa, elbows and skin creases. Further investigations confirm a diagnosis of Addison's disease. Which of the following is likely in this condition?

AIncreased serum Na
BIncreased serum Cl
CIncreased serum HCO3
DIncreased serum K
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Increased serum K

In adrenal destruction, serum Na, Cl and HCO3 levels are reduced, and serum potassium is elevated. The hyponatraemia is due to both loss of sodium in the urine (due to aldosterone and cortisol deficiency) and to movement into the intracellular compartment. Hyperkalemia is due to a combination of aldosterone deficiency, impaired glomerular filtration (due to hypotension) and acidosis. Mild to moderate hypercalcemia occurs in 10-20% patients, the cause of which is uncertain. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Page 2971

Sample MCQ #7

A 10-year-old boy presents with generalised oedema. There was no relevant past medical history. Investigations revealed: Creatinine 0.65 mg/dl, Albumin 2 g/dl, 24-hour urine protein excretion 3.2 g. Ultrasound scan shows normal renal size. Renal biopsy was performed which was normal by light microscopy and showed minimal IgM deposit on IF. Which of the following should the patient be treated with initially?

ACyclophosphamide
BPeritoneal dialysis
CThiazides
DPrednisolone
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Prednisolone

This question deals with Nephrotic syndrome in pedaitric patient. The most common cause is Minimal change disease. Light microscopy will be normal. If will either show no deposits or minimal IgM and electron microscope shows effacement of foot processes. The treatment is steroids. 80% of patients achieve remission with prednisolone therapy. Cyclophosphamide treatment can be started for those who are steroid resistant Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st ed, Ch: 314, Pg: 2341.

Sample MCQ #8

A 51 year old presents with tiredness and heavy periods. She is known to drink large amounts of alcohol. Her investigations reveal: Hemoglobin 7.3 g/dl MCV 72 fL white cell count 7500/&mu;L platelet count 250000/&mu;L serum ferritin 7 &mu;g/L, (15-300) She was commenced on oral iron therapy one month later but her hemoglobin concentration was 7.8 g/dl. What is the likely cause of the failure of her hemoglobin to respond?

AFolate deficiency
BPoor compliance therapy
CSideroblastic anemia
DAlcoholism
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Poor compliance therapy

The likely explanation for failure of an iron deficiency anemia to respond iron therapy in a patient with heavy periods is poor compliance. Folate deficiency will cause a Megaloblastic picture with MCV>100 fL Sideroblastic anemia would cause an increase in serum iron and ferritin. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st Ed, Ch:97 Pg:752
