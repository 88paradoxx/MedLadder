Sample Clinical Hypotheses MCQs with answers

Sample MCQ #1

A patient has fever, rash, arthritis and renal failure. Preferring a single unifying diagnosis for all features follows which principle?

AHickam dictum
BOccam razor
CSutton law
DBayes theorem
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Occam razor

Occam razor favours a single unifying diagnosis that explains all findings. Hickam dictum is the counter-principle: patients can have several diseases at once, so a unifying diagnosis should not be forced when features do not fit.

Sample MCQ #2

A clinician settles on a diagnosis after the first few findings and stops considering alternatives. This reasoning error is called?

AAnchoring
BPremature closure
CAvailability bias
DConfirmation bias
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Premature closure

Premature closure is accepting a diagnosis before it has been fully verified, closing off the differential too early. Anchoring is different: it is over-weighting the initial information even as new data arrives.

Sample MCQ #3

Over-reliance on the initial diagnosis despite new contradictory data is an example of?

AAnchoring bias
BFraming effect
CBase-rate neglect
DSearch satisficing
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Anchoring bias

Anchoring bias occurs when clinicians cling to an initial diagnostic impression and adjust insufficiently in light of later findings. Countermeasures include deliberately generating alternatives and seeking disconfirming evidence.

Sample MCQ #4

A test has 90% sensitivity and 95% specificity. In a population with 10% disease prevalence, what is approximately the positive predictive value?

A17%
B33%
C67%
D90%
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. 67%

By Bayes theorem, PPV = (0.90 x 0.10) / [(0.90 x 0.10) + (0.05 x 0.90)] = 0.09 / 0.135, about 0.67 or 67%. Even a good test yields many false positives when prevalence is low.

Sample MCQ #5

A highly sensitive test, when negative, is most useful for which purpose?

ARuling in disease
BRuling out disease
CConfirming disease
DStaging disease
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Ruling out disease

Sensitive tests have few false negatives, so a negative result reliably rules out disease (SnNout). Specific tests have few false positives, so a positive result rules in disease (SpPin).

Sample MCQ #6

Experienced clinicians often reach diagnoses by matching a presentation to stored patterns of disease features. This is best described as?

AHypothetico-deductive reasoning
BIllness script activation
CAlgorithmic reasoning
DPathophysiological reasoning
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Illness script activation

Illness scripts are mental models of a disease: its predisposing conditions, pathophysiology, typical features and course. A clinical presentation activates the matching script, which is why experts diagnose rapidly from pattern recognition.

Sample MCQ #7

After recently managing a case of pulmonary embolism, a clinician overestimates its likelihood in the next dyspnoeic patient. This is?

AAvailability bias
BRepresentativeness bias
CAnchoring
DOmission bias
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Availability bias

Availability bias is judging how likely something is by how easily examples come to mind. A recent, vivid or memorable case inflates the perceived probability of the same diagnosis in the next patient.

Sample MCQ #8

To test a diagnostic hypothesis, the most informative next step is usually the test with which property?

ALowest cost
BLargest likelihood ratio for the leading hypothesis
CWidest availability
DHighest sensitivity alone
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Largest likelihood ratio for the leading hypothesis

A test is valuable in proportion to how much it moves the probability of the leading hypothesis, which is captured by its likelihood ratios. The most discriminating test produces the largest shift in post-test probability.
