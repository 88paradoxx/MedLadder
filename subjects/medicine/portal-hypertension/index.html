Sample Portal Hypertension MCQs with answers

Sample MCQ #1

A 22-year-old male is evaluated by his primary care physician for dysuria. A urethral swab is positive for Chlamydia trachomatis. He receives appropriate antibiotic treatment. He returns to the clinic four weeks later complaining of pain in the knees, ankles, and lower back. Repeat testing is negative for both Chlamydia and Neisseria gonorrhea. Which additional finding might be expected on examination?

ARight upper quadrant pain and hepatomegaly
BScleral icterus
CConjunctival injection
DNontender subcutaneous nodules over several large joints
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Conjunctival injection

Conjunctival injection mainly affects the posterior conjunctival blood vessels. Because these vessels are more superficial than the ciliary arteries, they produce more redness, move with the conjunctiva, and constrict with the application of topical vasoconstrictors. Reference: Robbins, 10 th edition Page number - 822

Sample MCQ #2

A 35 year old woman has abdominal pain, jaundice and worsening ascites. She drinks 20 units of alcohol each week, and takes the oral contraceptive pill. Which of the following findings would make a diagnosis of Budd Chiari syndrome likely?

AEncephalopathy
BTender hepatomegaly
CAscites fluid protein of 4.2 g/dL
DAlanine aminotransferase of 150 U/L
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Tender hepatomegaly

Budd-Chiari syndrome is an uncommon condition induced by thrombotic or nonthrombotic obstruction to hepatic venous outflow.The classic triad of abdominal pain,ascites, and hepatomegaly is observed in the vast majority of patients but is nonspecific. Ascetic fluid protein may be high or low in Budd Chiari syndrome. Tender hepatomegaly on examination is one of the hallmark signs. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st Ed, Ch:50, Pg: 323

Sample MCQ #3

A 37 year old man presents with 2 bowls full of hematemesis. He drinks 10 pints of beer a day and has done so for 10 years. Upper GI endoscopy reveals oesophageal varices. Which of the following is effective in reducing the rates of rebleeding in the future?

ALisinopril
BPropranolol
CSimvastatin
DRanitidine
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Propranolol

Non selective beta blockers (propranolol, nadolol) can be used for reducing rebleeding in oesophageal varices. They work by decreasing hepatic venous pressure gradient and thereby decreasing portal hypertension. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st ed, Ch: 344, Pg: 2630

Sample MCQ #4

A 39 year old man who has a history of heavy alcohol intake presents with hematemesis and melena. On admission, he was in a pre-shock state with severe anemia and was resuscitated with blood and fresh frozen plasma. Endoscopic examination identified bleeding from the varices in the lower esophagus. Which one of the following drugs should be used for treatment?

AOctreotide
BPropranolol
CVitamin K
DBoth A and B
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Octreotide

Octreotide and terlipressin are widely used in acute variceal hemorrhage to reduce the bleeding rate octreotide is often used for emergency treatment of bleeding oesophageal varices in patients with cirrhosis of the liver. It is given intravenously.It acts by causing splanchnic vasoconstriction and should be given for a minimum 48 hours . Propranolol and Nadolol are used for prophylaxis of bleeding in a patient known to have esophageal varices. They should not be used in an acute condition as they can cause hypotension. Vitamin K is used to treat hepatic coagulopathy in acute liver failure, warfarin overdose and vit K deficiency of the newborn. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine , 21 st Ed, Ch: 344 Pg: 2630

Sample MCQ #5

A 60-year-old man takes atenolol for hypertension. Which of the following side effects is he most likely to be aware of two hours after taking atenolol?

AFatigue
BHesitancy of micturition
CNausea
DOrthostatic hypotension
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Fatigue

Atenolol is a water soluble beta blocker, taken once daily and is not associated with drowsiness/sleep disturbance like the lipid-soluble &beta;-blockers. It is not associated with nausea or hesitancy of micturition and would be unlikely to produce significant postural hypotension in a hypertensive subject. However, fatigue is a frequent side effect which typically is felt two hours and beyond after taking the drug. Reference: Harrison&rsquo;s Principles of Internal Principles, 20 th edition, ch:271 pg:1903

Sample MCQ #7

Portal hypertension is defined as hepatic venous pressure gradient above __mm Hg?

A10 mm
B5 mm
C12 mm
D15 mm
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. 5 mm

Portal hypertension is defined as the elevation of the hepatic venous pressure gradient (HVPG) to >5 mm Hg. clinically significant portal hypertension is defined as a HVPG &ge;10 mmHg , when there is substantial risk of decompensation with variceal bleeding. Cirrhosis is the most common cause of portal hypertension Reference : Harrison's Principles of internal medicine 21st ed chap 344 pg. 2629

Sample MCQ #8

Resistant hypertension is defined as when BP control is not occurring with use of 3 drugs. Among these, which of the following drug patients has to be on to meet the definition?

ADiuretics
BAlpha blockers
CAlpha methyldopa
DBeta blockers
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Diuretics

The term resistant hypertension refers to patients with blood pressures persistently >140/90 mmHg despite taking three or more antihypertensive agents, including a diuretic.
