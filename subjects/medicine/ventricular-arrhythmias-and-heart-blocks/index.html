Sample MCQ #2

A 60-year-old man has had a myocardial infarction. His pulse rate is 45/min and he is feeling lightheaded. Blood pressure is 90/65 mm Hg. In which one of the following conditions is temporary pacing indicated even when symptoms are absent?

AFirst degree heart block
BWenckebach block
C2:1 Mobitz type II heart block
DLeft bundle branch block
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. 2:1 Mobitz type II heart block

Asymptomatic patients with first- or second-degree Mobitz type I AV block do not require any specific therapy. Patients should undergo treatment of any potentially reversible cause (ie, myocardial ischemia or medication effect) Symptomatic patients with any degree of heart block should be treated urgently with atropine (initial dose 0.5 mg given intravenously) or temporary pacing (transcutaneous or transvenous). Indications for permanent pacing: Symptomatic Brady arrhythmias with any degree of AV block Asymptomatic high-degree AV block (second degree Mobitz type II or third- degree heart block) with slow ventricular rates (less than 40 beats/min). Patients with presumed cardiac syncope with normal heart rates and rhythm but bifascicular or trifascicular block on ECG Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Page 1883

Sample MCQ #3

A 52-year-old man had a ventricular fibrillation cardiac arrest whilst at a railway station. He was successfully resuscitated by a co-passenger. Further evaluation at the hospital including coronary angiography showed no stenotic lesions in the coronary arteries. Which is the most appropriate step for long term management?

ALifelong amiodarone
BImplantable cardiac defibrillator
CProcainamide
DPermanent pacemaker
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Implantable cardiac defibrillator

A cardiac electrophysiological study would be necessary to help confirm whether this patient has inducible ventricular tachycardia, and ablation of the pathways if so. However in a young patient with no coronary artery disease demonstrable to revascularize, ICD is necessary to treat a possible further cardiac arrest. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Page 1873

Sample MCQ #4

A 47-year-old man presents with broad complex tachycardia. He has a BP of 102/70 and a pulse rate of 176/min. The doctor on duty is considering administration of antiarrhythmic. Which one of the following medications is contraindicated in this patient?

AMagnesium
BAmiodarone
CVerapamil
DProcainamide
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Verapamil

The patient is likely to have ventricular tachycardia in view of the very fast ventricular rate and broad complexes on ECG. Amiodarone is the preferred pharmacological agent for acute agent in VT without hemodynamic compromise. Verapamil is contraindicated in VT because it can cause the blood pressure to drop drastically due to negative inotropic action. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Page 1922

Sample MCQ #5

All of the following electrocardiograph waveforms are matched correctly to the cardiac cycle that it represents except:

AP wave-atrial repolarization
BQRS complex-ventricular depolarisation
CT wave-ventricular repolarization
DU wave-ventricular repolarization
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. P wave-atrial repolarization

The electrocardiogram waveforms are labelled alphabetically, beginning with the P wave, which represents atrial depolarization. PR interval shows the AV nodal delay to allow for the filling of ventricles The QRS complex represents ventricular depolarization, and the ST-T-U complex (STsegment, T wave, and U wave) represents ventricular repolarization. The J point is the junction between the end of the QRS complex and the beginning of the ST segment. Atrial Repolarization (STa and Ta) is usually too low in amplitude to be detected, but it may become apparent in conditions such as acute pericarditis and atrial infarction. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Page 1866

Sample MCQ #6

The drug of choice in a patient with torsades de pointes is:

AAmiodarone
BMetoprolol
CMagnesium sulphate
DLignocaine
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Magnesium sulphate

Torsades de pointes is characterised by twisting pattern of ventricular tachycardia in ECG that occurs in the background of prolonged QT interval The drugs, metabolic abnormalities etc. that can prolong QTc can potentially lead to torsades de pointes. IV Magnesium sulphate given at a bolus dose of 1-2 gm is considered to be effective in this condition and is considered the treatment of choice Metoprolol and lignocaine may also be effective in some cases but are not the first line agents. Amiodarone is avoided in such patients as it can further prolong the QTc interval Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Page 1924

Sample MCQ #7

Patient is on metoprolol and if he is given Verapamil, what is the effect that occurs ?

ABradycardia following AV Block
BTachycardia
CTorsades de pointes
DVentricular tachycardia
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Bradycardia following AV Block

Metoprolol is a beta blocker. It causes bradycardia by acting on the &beta; 1 receptor in the heart. Verapamil is a calcium channel blocker and is an AV nodal blocking agent . It causes bradycardia by blocking the AV node. In the sinoatrial and atrioventricular nodes, where depolarization is due primarily to a Ca2+-dependent slow inward current, the Verapamil slow the sinus pacemaker and inhibit atrioventricular conduction. So the combined effect will be bradycardia with AV blocks.

Sample MCQ #8

Paradoxical splitting of second heart sound is seen in?

ARBBB
BASD
CLBBB
DVSD
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. LBBB

Answer is C (LBBB) Left Bundle Branch Block (LBBB) is typically associated with Reversed or Paradoxical Splitting of S2 Paradoxical splitting of second heart sound is caused by delayed A2 or early P2. Left Bundle Branch Block (LBBB) is associated with delayed Aortic closure (delayed A2) due to delayed electrical activation of the left ventricle. ASD and RBBB are associated with a wide physiological (non- paradoxical) split of second heart sound due to delayed pulmonic closure (Delayed P2) while VSD is associated with a wide physiological (non-paradoxical) split second heart sound from early aortic closure (Early A2).
