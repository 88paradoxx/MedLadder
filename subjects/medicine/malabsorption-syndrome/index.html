Sample MCQ #1

A patient who has had a subtotal thyroidectomy for hyperthyroidism is on thyroxine replacement. She has a normal thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH), normal tri-iodothyronine (T3) and low free thyroxine (T4) levels. What is the most likely reason for those results?

AHypothalamic pituitary causes
BPoor compliance with thyroxine supplements
CThe patient's results are as expected - no change in treatment is required
DMalabsorption syndrome
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. The patient's results are as expected - no change in treatment is required

Once on a full replacement dose, thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) levels should be checked at intervals of 1-3 years, depending on their stability. Fluctuating or elevated TSH levels in a previously stable patient, or thyroxine requirements in excess of 200 mg/day, usually indicate compliance problems. It is important to rule out malabsorption or abnormal thyroxine kinetics caused by drugs: cholestyramine, ferrous sulphate, lovastatin, aluminium hydroxide, rifampicin, amiodarone, carbamazepine, and phenytoin all alter the absorption or clearance of thyroxine. Reference: Harrison&rsquo;s Principles of Internal Medicine 20 th edition Ch:377 Pg:2705-07

Sample MCQ #2

A 24 year old woman has type 1 diabetes. She has weight loss over the past 3 months, and irregular menstrual cycles. Her bowel habit has been unchanged. On examination her BMI was 24 kg/m2. Investigations show: haemoglobin of 8.1 g/dl with a MCV of 69 fl. Which is the likely diagnosis?

AHeavy Periods
BCrohn's disease
CDietary iron deficiency
DCeliac disease
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Celiac disease

This patient has type 1 diabetes &ndash; an autoimmune condition. She now presents with iron deficiency anemia along with weight loss. So the possibility of celiac disease should be ruled out as people with type 1 diabetes are at up to six times greater risk of having celiac disease. Dietary deficiency of iron and heavy menstrual loss will not cause weight loss there is no abdominal pain or blood in stools to suggest a Chron's Disease. Antibodies to gliadin should be sent and the diagnosis of celiac disease can be confirmed by a biopsy of small intestine which will show villous atrophy. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st ed, Ch: 325, Pg: 2463

Sample MCQ #3

A 44 year old man has drunk 50 units a week of alcohol for 20 years. During investigations, she has :Haemoglobin 10.2 g/dL, MCV 110 fL (80-96), white cell count 2.1 x 10^9/L (4-11), platelet count 75 x 10^9/L (150-400). What might explain these results?

ALiver cirrhosis
BFolic acid deficiency
CHepatitis C infection
DIron deficiency
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Folic acid deficiency

The haematological picture is that of macrocytic anemia (most commonly due to vitamin B12 or folate deficiency). Folate deficiency occurs in the majority of binge-drinking alcoholics and is a common cause of anemia. Inadequate dietary intake, intestinal malabsorption, and impaired folate storage in the liver all contribute to folate deficiency. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine21 st ed, ch: 99, Pg: 773

Sample MCQ #4

A 64-year-old lady has a six month history of abdominal discomfort, bloating and altered frequency in stools. Her symptoms seem to be worse following a large meal where she also feels nausea. Clinical examination is normal. She is suspected of fulfilling the criteria for irritable bowel syndrome. Which of the following investigations should be undertaken to exclude other diagnoses?

AAnti-tissue transglutaminase (TTG) antibodies
BFecal occult blood
CHydrogen breath test
DSigmoidoscopy
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Anti-tissue transglutaminase (TTG) antibodies

Patients who fulfil the diagnostic criteria for irritable bowel syndrome should be screened for other conditions with- Complete blood count to rule out anemia Erythrocyte sedimentation rate and C reactive protein to rule out inflammatory etiology Anti-endomysial antibodies, Tissue transglutaminase (TTG) antibodies to rule out celiac disease. Some patients with IBS-D may have undiagnosed celiac sprue. Because the symptoms of celiac sprue respond to a gluten-free diet, testing for celiac sprue in IBS may prevent years of morbidity and attendant expense Other tests can be done to rule out any other suspected pathology depending on the presenting symptoms. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st Ed, Ch: 327 Pg: 2493

Sample MCQ #5

A 46 year old man has a diagnosis of celiac disease. He presents with a one month history of intermittent, colicky, central abdominal pain and weight loss of 5 kg. There is positive fecal occult blood. What is the most appropriate investigation?

ADuodenal biopsy
BBarium enema
CCT scan of abdomen
DColonoscopy
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Colonoscopy

Colonoscopy is necessary to exclude a colonic carcinoma. There is a relatively high prevalence of colorectal neoplasia among older patients with coeliac disease who can present with iron deficiency or altered bowel habit. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st Ed, Ch: 325 Pg: 2464

Sample MCQ #6

A 36 year man has abdominal pains, weight loss and diarrhoea. He was subsequently found to be lactose intolerant and a small bowel biopsy showed changes consistent with celiac disease. He has also noticed that he is developing itchiness and a generalised rash. Which one of the following skin conditions is it likely to be?

AErythema marginatum
BDermatitis herpetiformis
CErythema chronicum migrans
DPityriasis rosea
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Dermatitis herpetiformis

Dermatitis herpetiformis is the most common extraintestinal manifestation of celiac disease. It presents as an intense pruritic rash consisting of papules and vesicles. Commonly presents on the extensor surface of extremities, buttocks and neck. Occurs due to granular IgA deposits in epidermal basement membrane. Treatment &ndash; Dapsone and gluten free diet. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st ed Ch: 325, Pg: 2463

Sample MCQ #8

A 43 year old man has had intermittent diarrhoea and joint pains for 6 months. On examination he has limited vertical eye movements. Which pathogen is the likely cause of his symptoms?

ASalmonella enteritidis
BCryptosporidium
CTropheryma whipplei
DPrion protein
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Tropheryma whipplei

Whipple's disease characteristically occurs in middle-aged men, who present with weight loss, fever, abdominal pain, arthralgias and intestinal symptoms of diarrhea and malabsorption. CNS and cardiac system are commonly involved. Confusion, memory loss, or uncontrolled eye movements indicate that the infection has travelled to the CNS.
