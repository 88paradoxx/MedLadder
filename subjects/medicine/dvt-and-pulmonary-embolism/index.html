Sample MCQ #1

A 24-year-old woman collapsed in a New York City subway station. An autopsy showed that a blood clot had travelled to her lung and caused her death. The medical examiner ultimately ruled that the blood clot was caused by the use of a birth control patch. Which of the following occurs if the blood flow to an alveolus is totally obstructed by a pulmonary thromboembolism?

AThe V/Q ratio of the alveolus equals zero
BThe PO2 of the alveolus will be equal to that in the inspired air
CThe PO2 of the alveolus will be equal to the mixed venous PO2
DThere will be an increase in shunting (venous admixture) in the lung
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. The PO2 of the alveolus will be equal to that in the inspired air

A pulmonary thromboembolism results in areas of the lung that are ventilated, but not perfused, yielding V/Q ratios of infinity and an increase in alveolar deadspace. When the V/Q ratio equals infinite, the PAO2 of the affected alveoli will be the same as that in the humidified inspired air because atmospheric air enters the alveoli via the process of ventilation but no gas exchange takes place because the alveoli are not perfused. Areas of the lung that are perfused but not ventilated constitute areas of shunting (venous admixture), characterized as a V/Q ratio equal to 0, and having PAO2 values that equilibrate with the mixed venous blood. Reference: Kasper et al., pp 282, 1561. Levitzky, pp 114-117. Stead, pp 272-273.

Sample MCQ #2

Causes of a small pupil include which of the following?

ACarbon monoxide poisoning
BEthylene glycol poisoning
CHolmes-Adie pupil
DPontine haemorrhage
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Pontine haemorrhage

Causes of small pupils include: Horner's syndrome Old age Pontine haemorrhage Argyll Robertson pupil Drugs and poisons (opiates, organophosphates). Causes of dilated pupils include: Holmes-Adie (myotonic) pupil Third nerve palsy Drugs and poisons (atropine, cobalt [CO], ethylene glycol). Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st ed, Ch: 32, Pg: 216

Sample MCQ #3

A 60-year-old man presents with a small amount of haemoptysis and breathlessness. He does not have pleuritic chest pain and has normal oxygen saturation. In considering the potential diagnosis, haemoptysis can occur with which of the following diagnoses?

AInterstitial lung disease
BAsthma
CGoitre
DAspergilloma
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Aspergilloma

Haemoptysis can be caused by pulmonary embolism, tuberculous infection, aspergilloma, bronchial carcinoma, good pasture's syndrome and Wegener's granulomatosis. Interstitial lung disease presents with dry cough along with progressive dyspnoea. Asthma presents with episodic breathlessness and cough in exposure to cold or allergens. Goitre presents as neck swelling; if large, it may compress the trachea causing stridor and breathlessness; retrosternal extension causes venous congestion due to compression of superior vena cava. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st ed, Ch: 39, Pg:270-272

Sample MCQ #4

A 34-year-old woman presented with a dry cough, thorax constriction, and generalised weakness. During the preceding 5 months, she had experienced these dry cough episodes twice a week. A lung function test showed a restriction of the vital capacity (71% of the adjusted reference value), and the diffusion capacity was also reduced (66 to 68%). Broncho alveolar lavage specimens were obtained, the lymphocytes were increased up to 41% (norm, <10%), and neutrophils were increased up to 6% (norm, <2%) with a normal total cell count. What is the likely diagnosis?

AAsthma
BPulmonary embolism
CTuberculosis
DExtrinsic allergic alveolitis
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Extrinsic allergic alveolitis

Extrinsic Allergic Alveolitis is type IV hypersensitivity. There is no eosinophilia, IgG and lymphocytes are involved in immune response. Antigens of micropolyspora faeni and thermoactinomyces are 0.5-5 microns. Assaying for IgG antibodies against specific antigens, either through precipitin testing or direct serum measurement, can be a useful adjunct in the diagnosis of HP High-resolution computed tomography (HRCT) is a common component in the diagnostic workup; In subacute forms of the disease, ground-glass airspace opacities are characteristic, as is the presence of centrilobular nodules. Expiratory images may show areas of air trapping that are likely caused by involvement of the small airways BAL shows a lymphocytic predominance with a greater proportion of CD8 positive T cells . Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st ed, Ch: 288, Pg: 2161

Sample MCQ #5

You are called to a cardiac arrest in the Emergency Department where a patient who is known to the dialysis unit has been brought by ambulance. On reviewing her drugs, you note that she is taking a statin, three antihypertensive agents, aspirin and has been receiving erythropoietin injections. Unfortunately, resuscitation is unsuccessful. Which of the following is the commonest cause of death in renal dialysis patients?

AGastrointestinal haemorrhage
BOccult malignancy
CPulmonary embolism
DCardiovascular disease
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Cardiovascular disease

Cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of morbidity and mortality in patients with CKD at all the stages. Hypertension and dyslipidemia are the risk factors that accelerate atherosclerosis in CKD. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st edition, Ch:311, Pg:2315

Sample MCQ #6

A 26 year old woman has symptoms of lethargy, bloody diarrhoea and a previous history of DVT in the leg. On examination, the left eye was inflamed and multiple mouth ulcers were noted. Colonoscopy was organized and this confirmed colitis. She also has vulval ulcers. What is the likely diagnosis?

AErythema nodosum
BUlcerative colitis
CBehcet's disease
DCrohn's disease
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Behcet's disease

Recurrent oral ulceration is the commonest manifestation and the presenting feature in Behcet's disease. Diagnosis of Behcet&rsquo;s disease requires - Recurrent oral ulcers plus two of the following four clinical manifestations: Recurrent genital ulcers &ndash; cause scarring Skin lesions - Erythema nodosum, pseudofolliculitis, papulopastular or acneiform nodules Eye lesions - Anterior or posterior uveitis, corneal ulceration, conjunctivitis, papillitis, Positive pathergy test Vasculitis, pustules and subcutaneous nodules can also occur. Vascular thrombosis occurs in about 10% of patients. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st ed, Ch: 364, Pg: 2818

Sample MCQ #7

A 44-year-old man presents with a week history of a painful right leg. He has had previous episodes of erythema nodosum, recurrent oral and scrotal ulceration. Examination reveals a diffusely swollen left leg.What is the likely cause of his swollen leg?

ABaker's cyst
BGonococcal arthritis
CReactive arthritis
DDeep vein thrombosis
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Deep vein thrombosis

The overall diagnosis is Behcet's syndrome. There is a thrombotic tendency (venous>>arterial), hence the likely cause of a DVT. Reference: Harrison&rsquo;s Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Ch:364 Pg:2817

Sample MCQ #8

Which of the following associations is matched correctly?

APneumoconiosis and clubbing
BLung carcinoids and pleural effusion
CPulmonary embolism and left bundle branch block
DBronchopulmonary aspergillosis and wheezing
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Bronchopulmonary aspergillosis and wheezing

Pneumoconiosis is not associated with clubbing. Pulmonary embolism causes right heart strain and may present with RBBB Rv strain and Ischemia in ECG. Lung Carcinoids are rarely associated with pleural effusion. Bronchopulmonary aspergillosis is associated with wheezing. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st Ed, Ch: Ch: 288, Pg: 2165
