Sample MCQ #1

A 22-year-old young woman with poorly controlled diabetes mellitus presents with a temperature, dehydration and altered consciousness. Her initial biochemistry shows sodium 130 mmol/l, potassium 4.5 mmol/l, bicarbonate 6 mmol/l, urea 50 mg/dl, creatinine 1.2 mg/dl and hydrogen ion 80. What is the most important immediate treatment?

AIntravenous antibiotics
BIntravenous bicarbonate
CIntravenous fluids
DIntravenous insulin
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Intravenous fluids

The patient has diabetic ketoacidosis , a condition still associated with mortality in patients with type-1 diabetes mellitus. There is a total body deficit of fluid and electrolytes. The most important initial treatment is intravenous saline as the hypovolaemia is the factor most likely to cause the patient to die. Intravenous insulin is required at an early stage; and, although it is normal, the potassium level will rapidly fall with fluid and insulin treatment and the patient will require potassium replacement. Intravenous bicarbonate should not be used immediately as it can cause massive fluid shifts and precipitate cerebral oedema. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Pg:3115

Sample MCQ #2

A 18-year-old female with Type 1 Diabetes mellitus with history of treatment default presented to the emergency room with vomiting and abdominal pain. Investigations revealed a GRBS of 536 mg/dl with ABG showing pH of 7.1 and bicarbonate of 11 mEq/L, positive urine ketones, Na-130 mEq/L, K-3 mEq/L, Creatinine - 1.1 mg/dl. What would be the next step in your management?

AStart IV fluids and Insulin infusion; Avoid bicarbonates
BStart IV fluids and insulin infusion; Administer bicarbonate at 50 mmol/hr
CStart IV fluids; Give Potassium supplementation; Insulin infusion after K+ correction
DStart IV fluids and potassium supplementation; Start Insulin infusion simultaneously
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Start IV fluids; Give Potassium supplementation; Insulin infusion after K+ correction

If initial serum K+ is 3.3 mEq/L If initial K+ > 5.3 mEq/L, insulin therapy can be started without K+ administration. Potassium values to be monitored every 4 hours If initial K+ is between 3.3 and 5.3 mEq/L, insulin infusion to be started along with potassium supplementation because ongoing insulin supplementation would result in lowering of serum K+ due to transcellular shift Reference: ISPAD consensus guidelines on management of Young Diabetes mellitus, Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition page 3115

Sample MCQ #3

Macrosomia is/are associated with:

AGestational diabetes mellitus
BMaternal obesity
CHypothyroidism
DA & B
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. A & B

Ans. is a and b i.e. Gestational diabetes and Maternal obesity Macrosomia is the term used to describe a large fetus. The recommended definition is fetal (neonatal) weight exceeding two standard deviations or above 90th centile for the appropriate normal population. According to ACOG : birth weight of > 4500gm is called as macrosomia. In Indian context Birth weight of > 4000 gm is called as macrosomia.
