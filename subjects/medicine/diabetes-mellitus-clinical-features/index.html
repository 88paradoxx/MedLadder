Sample MCQ #1

A 22-year-old young woman with poorly controlled diabetes mellitus presents with a temperature, dehydration and altered consciousness. Her initial biochemistry shows sodium 130 mmol/l, potassium 4.5 mmol/l, bicarbonate 6 mmol/l, urea 50 mg/dl, creatinine 1.2 mg/dl and hydrogen ion 80. What is the most important immediate treatment?

AIntravenous antibiotics
BIntravenous bicarbonate
CIntravenous fluids
DIntravenous insulin
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Intravenous fluids

The patient has diabetic ketoacidosis , a condition still associated with mortality in patients with type-1 diabetes mellitus. There is a total body deficit of fluid and electrolytes. The most important initial treatment is intravenous saline as the hypovolaemia is the factor most likely to cause the patient to die. Intravenous insulin is required at an early stage; and, although it is normal, the potassium level will rapidly fall with fluid and insulin treatment and the patient will require potassium replacement. Intravenous bicarbonate should not be used immediately as it can cause massive fluid shifts and precipitate cerebral oedema. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Pg:3115

Sample MCQ #2

A 18-year-old female with Type 1 Diabetes mellitus with history of treatment default presented to the emergency room with vomiting and abdominal pain. Investigations revealed a GRBS of 536 mg/dl with ABG showing pH of 7.1 and bicarbonate of 11 mEq/L, positive urine ketones, Na-130 mEq/L, K-3 mEq/L, Creatinine - 1.1 mg/dl. What would be the next step in your management?

AStart IV fluids and Insulin infusion; Avoid bicarbonates
BStart IV fluids and insulin infusion; Administer bicarbonate at 50 mmol/hr
CStart IV fluids; Give Potassium supplementation; Insulin infusion after K+ correction
DStart IV fluids and potassium supplementation; Start Insulin infusion simultaneously
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Start IV fluids; Give Potassium supplementation; Insulin infusion after K+ correction

If initial serum K+ is 3.3 mEq/L If initial K+ > 5.3 mEq/L, insulin therapy can be started without K+ administration. Potassium values to be monitored every 4 hours If initial K+ is between 3.3 and 5.3 mEq/L, insulin infusion to be started along with potassium supplementation because ongoing insulin supplementation would result in lowering of serum K+ due to transcellular shift Reference: ISPAD consensus guidelines on management of Young Diabetes mellitus, Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition page 3115

✓ Correct Answer: D. A & B

Ans. is a and b i.e. Gestational diabetes and Maternal obesity Macrosomia is the term used to describe a large fetus. Sample MCQ #4

Which of the following is not a feature of Latent Autoimmune Diabetes in Adults (LADA)?

AOccurs after age of 30 years
BRequires insulin from the time of diagnosis
CAnti-GAD antibody positivity
DComplication rate similar to Type 1 Diabetes
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Requires insulin from the time of diagnosis

Immunology of Diabetes Society criteria for diagnosis of LADA Age at diagnosis > 30 years Presence of autoantibodies against islet antigens Not requiring insulin for at least 6 months after diagnosis of DM Also known as Type 1.5 Diabetes In the WHO classification of DM 2019, it has been renamed as 'Slowly evolving immune mediated diabetes of adults'. Reference: LADA - Diagnostic criteria proposed by Immunology of Diabetes Society

Sample MCQ #6

The following are clinical clues in favor of diagnosis of MODY compared to Type 1 DM: 1. Low insulin requirement 2. Absence of microvascular omplications 3. No episodes of Diabetes ketoacidosis 4. Absence of Anti GAD antibody positivity

A1 and 3
B1 and 4
C1,3 and 4
D1,2,3 and 4
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. 1,3 and 4

A lean patient with young onset diabetes can produce a diagnostic confusion between MODY and Type 1 Diabetes mellitus The clinical pointer in favor of MODY are: Strong family history Low insulin requirement ( No episodes of DKA Negative autoantibodies Detectable C-peptide levels after 2-3 years of diagnosis For the same duration and degree of glycemic control, MODY patients have been observed to have same risk for microvascular complications though the risk of macrovascular complications is decreased compared to type 2 Diabetes. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition page 3095

Sample MCQ #8

Which of the following statements is correct concerning the relationship between type 2 diabetes and colonic cancer?

AIncreased concentrations of C peptide are a marker of increased colorectal cancer risk
BInsulin treatment increases recurrence free survival after treatment of colonic cancer
CThe increased risk of colorectal cancer in diabetes is related to BMI
DThe increased risk of colorectal cancer in diabetes is related to total cholesterol
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Increased concentrations of C peptide are a marker of increased colorectal cancer risk

Type 2 diabetes is associated with a 40-60% increase in the risk of cancer of the large bowel. This increase is linked to changes in HbA1c. Type 2 diabetes is associated with significantly higher rates of overall mortality and reduced disease free and recurrence free survival after chemotherapy/radiotherapy, and insulin has not been shown to have any effects on mortality. No association has been found between colonic malignancy and type 1 diabetes, nor gestational diabetes. A number of studies have independently linked high circulating concentrations of C Peptide, as a marker of insulin production, with increased colorectal cancer risk. The molecular basis has not been proven, but it may be reasonable to extrapolate that it is linked to the growth stimulation effects of insulin. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st ed, Ch: 81 Pg: 637
