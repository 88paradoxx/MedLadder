Sample MCQ #1

A 34 year old patient has had a hepatitis test for investigation of jaundice. He has a positive Hep BsAg (HBsAg) and has IgM antibodies to Hep B Core (anti-HBc). What is he likely to have?

AHepatitis D infection
BSeroconversion illness
CChronic hepatitis B infection
DAcute hepatitis B infection
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Acute hepatitis B infection

The positive anti-HBc (IgM) and HBsAg suggests acute infection. When the infection resolves, HBsAg becomes negative and anti-HBc (IgG) is positive. In patients who have been vaccinated, HBsAg is negative and anti-HBs is positive. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st ed, Ch: 339, Pg: 3577

Sample MCQ #2

Which of the following enzyme elevation is/are less specific for the cholestasis jaundice?

A5 Nucleotidase
BGGT
CALP
DA and C both
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. GGT

ENZYMES THAT REFLECT CHOLESTASIS The activities of three enzymes-alkaline phosphatase, 5&prime;-nucleotidase, and &gamma;-glutamyl transpeptidase (GGT)-are usually elevated in cholestasis. Alkaline phosphatase and 5&prime;-nucleotidase are found in or near the bile canalicular membrane of hepatocytes, whereas GGT is located in the endoplasmic reticulum and in bile duct epithelial cells. Reflecting its more diffuse localization in the liver, GGT elevation in serum is less specific for cholestasis than are elevations of alkaline phosphatase or 5&prime;- nucleotidase. Some have advocated the use of GGT to identify patients with occult alcohol use.

A 47 year old man has painless jaundice. He has pale stools and dark urine. On examination he is deeply jaundiced and has scratch marks all over his body. On palpation of the abdomen Courvoisier's sign is positive. Where is the level of biliary obstruction?

ABile canaliculi
BIntrahepatic ducts
CHepatic duct
DCommon bile duct
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Common bile duct

Courvoisier's sign is a palpable distended gallbladder on examination of the abdomen. Obstruction below the level of the cystic duct causes this, probably due to carcinoma of the pancreas. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine Ch:83, Pg: 659,
