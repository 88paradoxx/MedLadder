Drugs that may exacerbate myasthenia gravis and potentiate the action of nondepolarizing muscle relaxants are: Antibiotics Aminoglycosides e.g Streptomycin Tetracyclines Quinolones e.g ciprofloxacin Macrolides e.g erythromycin Non-depolarizing muscle relaxants e.g d-Tubocurarine Beta-blockers like propranolol, atenolol, metoprolol Local anaesthetics Botulinum toxin Quinine derivatives like quinine, quinidine, chloroquine, mefloquine Magnesium Penicillamine

Sample MCQ #2

A 50 year old patient has a 2 year history of weight loss and diarrhea. He also gives a history of episodes of flushing. VIP syndrome is considered by the admitting physician. Which one of the following is a feature of VIPoma syndrome?

AHypoglycaemia
BHypokalaemia
CInduction of VIP release by somatostatin
DAnaemia
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Hypokalaemia

VIPomas [vasoactive intestinal peptide (VIP) originate in amine precursor uptake and decarboxylation (APUD) cells of the gastroenteropancreatic endocrine system and in adrenal or extra-adrenal neurogenic sites. Features of VIP syndrome include watery diarrhea (100%), hypochlorhydria (70% in adults), hyperglycemia (20-50% in adults), hypercalcemia (20-50% in adults), flushing (20% in adults) and hypokalemia due to diarrhea. Initial treatment is directed toward correcting volume and electrolyte abnormalities by using potassium chloride and sodium bicarbonate. Octreotide controls diarrhea in 80% of cases. Glucocorticoids reduce symptoms in 50% of patients with VIPoma. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st Ed, Ch: 84 Pg: 665

Sample MCQ #3

A 56-year-old man presents with a history of abdominal discomfort that is relieved by passing stool. In the past month his stools have been looser in nature and he has had a feeling of urgency and incomplete evacuation. His symptoms seem to be worse with eating. He has also noticed that he has passed mucus per rectum. He is overweight with a BMI of 28 kg/m2. His weight has fallen. He says that he has adjusted his diet in the last six months. Which of the following is the most appropriate diagnosis?

AColo-rectal carcinoma
BDiverticulitis
CInflammatory bowel disease
DIrritable bowel syndrome
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Colo-rectal carcinoma

Weight loss, tenesmus and a recent change in bowel habit all point towards an underlying diagnosis of colorectal cancer. Onset of symptoms in later life and a relatively short duration (that is, weeks rather than years) also point towards a 'biological' cause rather than irritable bowel syndrome. The passage of mucus per rectum is a normal physiological function and not indicative of underlying pathology. The symptoms are not suggestive of malabsorption. Diverticulitis and inflammatory bowel disease are both potential diagnoses but given the duration of symptoms less likely than a colonic neoplasm. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st Ed, CH: 81, Pg: 639

Sample MCQ #4

A 36 year old woman presents with abdominal pain, weight loss, diarrhoea and mouth ulcers. Full blood count reveals normocytic normochromic anaemia, inflammatory markers are raised, and biochemical investigation reveals a raised alkaline phosphatase. Barium follow through shows terminal ileal inflammatory disease. Long term remission may be maintained by the use of which of the following drugs?

AMesalazine
BPrednisolone
CAzathioprine
DCyclosporine
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Azathioprine

This is a case of Crohn&rsquo;s disease . Remission of Crohn's disease may be achieved by the use of steroids, elemental diet, surgery or infliximab. Mesalazine may maintain remission if induction has been achieved by surgery and if an 8-week course of metronidazole is administered. It has many side effects. In other scenarios, the most useful drug to maintain long term remission is azathioprine . Steroids (prednisolone) are only used for induction of remission. They are not appropriate for long term maintenance therapy. Cyclosporine is used in severe cases of ulcerative colitis refractory to steroid therapy. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st Ed, Ch: 326 Pg: 2488

Sample MCQ #6

Which of the following describes the best prescription for active RA in a 45 year old healthy female without any contraindications?

AMethotrexate alone
BLeflunomide alone
CSulfasalazine with HCQ
DMethotrexate with biologicals
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Methotrexate with biologicals

The current recommendations are early aggressive therapy to reduce joint deformity using combination therapy. Methotrexate is the cornerstone in treatment of RA and combination therapy should be built with Methotrexate as the base. The accepted combinations include Methotrexate plus HCQ plus Sulfasalazine Methotrexate with biologicals Methotrexate with Leflunomide Combination therapy involves starting the treatment with Methotrexate and to rapidly step up to the desired combination in 3-6 months. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21st ed, Ch: 358, Pg: 2761-2764

Sample MCQ #7

Which of the following statements is false?

AHematological abnormalities may not be seen in approximately 40% of patients with Sub acute combined degeneration due to cobalamine deficinecy.
BIn patients with food cobalamine malabsorption, oral cobalamine is sufficient to correct the deficiency.
CTreatment of cobalamine deficiency will completely reverse the neurological manifestations.
DMethylmalanoic acid and Homocysteine levels may be elevated in Cobalamine deficiency.
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Treatment of cobalamine deficiency will completely reverse the neurological manifestations.

This question deals with the clinical features of Cobalamine deficncy. Haematological abnormalities like macrocytic anemia will not be seen in 40% of patients with Subacute combined degeneration. In cobalamine deficiency, there will be blocade of steps that require cobalamine as cofactor and hence homocysteine and methyl manioc acid will be accumulated. In patients with pernicious anemia, the management is parenteral cyanocobalamine. But in food cobalamine malabsorption, the patients can absorb free cobalamine and hence oral supplementation is enough, at the dose of 1000mcg/day. The haematological abnormalities completely respond to cobalamine replacement. But the neurologoical manifestations tend to be permanent in about 50% patients. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st ed, ch:446, Pg: 3496

Sample MCQ #8

A 26 year old bartender has had abdominal pains and loose stools for 3 years. He also has symptoms of myalgia and profound fatigue. He mentions that the abdominal pains are often worse after he has bread. An enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay test was conducted and it showed positive reactions to gluten, albumin, lactose, barley, and rye. Which of the following is not found in the disease?

AAntibodies to tissue transglutaminase
BAnti endomysial antibodies
CAnti gliadin antibodies
DAnti perimysial antibodies
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Anti perimysial antibodies

This is a case of celiac disease . The following antibodies are found- Antibodies against tissue transglutaminase IgA Antiendomysial antibodies Deamidated anti-gliadin antibodies. Serum IgA levels are measured to detect false-negative results from IgA deficiency. Deamidated anti-gliadin IgG antibodies or tissue transglutaminase IgG antibodies are detectable and diagnostic in IgA-deficient patients. Small intestinal biopsy is the confirmatory test . Biopsies typically show characteristic villus blunting, crypt hyperplasia, and inflammation, including increased intraepithelial lymphocytes. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st ed, Ch: 318, Pg: 2251 - 2253
