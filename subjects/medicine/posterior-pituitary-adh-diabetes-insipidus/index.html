Sample MCQ #1

Hamman crunch sign is seen in?

ADeep vein thrombosis
BPneumomediastinum
CPituitary apoplexy
DDiabetes mellitus
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Pneumomediastinum

The three main causes are Alveolar rupture with dissection of air into the mediastinum; Perforation or rupture of the esophagus, trachea, or main bronchi; and Dissection of air from the neck or the abdomen into the mediastinum. There is severe substernal chest pain with or without radiation into the neck and arms. The physical examination usually reveals subcutaneous emphysema in the suprasternal notch and Hamman's sign, which is a crunching or clicking noise synchronous with the heartbeat and is best heard in the left lateral decubitus position. The diagnosis is confirmed with the chest radiograph. Usually no treatment is required, but the mediastinal air will be absorbed faster if the patient inspires high concentrations of oxygen. If mediastinal structures are compressed, the compression can be relieved with needle aspiration.

Sample MCQ #3

A patient presents with a five-day history of nausea and vomiting and watery diarrhea after eating crabs. He appears ill and is orthostatic. Serum Na is 129 mmol/L. Which of the following statements is true?

AUrine Na will be less than 20 mEq/U
BTreatment should be with DSW
CAntidiuretic hormone (ADH) levels below
DSerum osmolality is likely to be high
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Urine Na will be less than 20 mEq/U

This patient has developed hypovolemia from fluid losses from vomiting and diarrhea. There is no evidence that other osmotically active compounds have been ingested, thus serum osmolality is likely to be low. Because of hypovolemia, ADH (and aldosterone) will be stimulated, and serum ADH levels will be high. The kidney will be resorbing water and Na, and urine Na concentration will be low. Intravenous volume repletion with normal saline is advisable. Reference: Harrisons Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st Ed, Ch:53 Pg: 342

Sample MCQ #4

A 22-year-old is noted to have persistent polyuria in excess of 6 liters per day. She mentions recently being involved in an accident and sustained head injury which she thinks she has recovered from. Investigations reveal: potassium 4.2 mmol/l calcium 8.6 mg/dl glucose 106 mg/dl Serum osmolality : 290 mOsm/kg (280-295) Urine osmolality : 192 mOsm/kg Which one of the following is the best way of confirming the diagnosis?

ACortisol levels
BWater deprivation test
CADH concentration
DTherapeutic trial of low dose DDAVP
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Water deprivation test

The diagnosis is likely to be diabetes insipidus . The causes for polyuria in a patient include primary polydipsia, central DI and Nephrogenic DI Diagnosis of DI in a patient with polyuria (urine output more than 50 ml/kg) is suggested by high plasma osmolality (>295 mOsm/kg) in a patient passing dilute urine ( urine osmolality If the diagnostic thresholds of DI are not met, DI can be confirmed by a water deprivation test where failure of urine concentration would be expected. During this test a patient is not allowed to drink and plasma ADH, plasma osmolality and urine osmolality are measured. A therapeutic trial of DDAVP is only appropriate if a diagnosis of DI is confirmed (On the water deprivation test) and helps to differentiate between cranial or nephrogenic diabetes insipidus. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Page 2921

Investigations show: Serum sodium 141 mmol/l Serum potassium 4.2 mmol/l Plasma osmolality 296 mosmol/l (280-295) Urine osmolality 230 mosmol/l (300-1000) What drug was likely to have been prescribed?

ACarbamazepine
BChlorpropamide
CFurosemide
DLithium

✓ Correct Answer: D. Lithium

The patient has drug induced Diabetes Insipidus based upon increased urine output, low urine osmolality, high plasma osmolality. This is most likely to be caused by lithium. Drugs causing diabetes insipidus: Lithium Aminoglycosides Methoxyflurane Amphotericin B Demeclocycline Cisplatin Rifampin Foscarnet Drugs like carbamazepine and chlorpropamide has been linked with SIADH resulting in hyponatremia Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Page 2920

Sample MCQ #6

A 28-year-old woman presenting with polyuria is found to have diabetes insipidus. Which part of the nephron is most affected in this condition?

AProximal tubule
BCortical and medullary collecting tubules
CDistal convoluted tubule
DThin ascending limb of Henle
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Cortical and medullary collecting tubules

Fine-tuning of salt and water balance is achieved in the distal and collecting tubules under the influence of aldosterone and antidiuretic hormone (ADH). ADH, however, plays a central role in urinary concentration by increasing the water permeability of the normally impermeable cortical and medullary collecting tubules. In the absence of ADH, little water is reabsorbed in the collecting tubules and dilute urine is excreted. ADH mediated water reabsorption from the collecting tubules is facilitated via aquaporin 2 channels on the luminal surface. Around 15-20% of renal water reabsorption is ADH mediated. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Page 2919

Hyperthermia results due to lesion in which part of the hypothalamus?

AAnterior and preoptic hypothalamus
BPosterior hypothalamus
CVentromedial nuclei
DCentral hypothalamus
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Anterior and preoptic hypothalamus

Hemorrhage in anterior & preoptic hypothalamus may cause acute hyperthermia accompanied by paradoxical vasoconstriction & tachycardia. Lesion in posterior hypothalamus could lead to hypothermia. Hypothalamic nuclei and their functions Anterior - thermoregulation Preoptic - thermoregulation, sexual behavior Supraoptic - vasopressin and oxytocin secretion Paraventricular - oxytocin and vasopressin production Suprachiasmatic - circadian rhythm regulation Ventromedial - satiety centre Posterior - thermoregulation and arousal Arcuate - secretion of releasing hormones of hypothalamus Lateral hypothalamus - hunger centre Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Page 2905
