Sample MCQ #3

A 42 year old man has worsening jaundice and has deranged liver function tests which have worsened over the last 3 months. He has positive IgG to hepatitis C. Which of the following investigations is most useful for management of the condition?

ACT scan of abdomen
BERCP
CHCV DNA levels
DLiver biopsy
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Liver biopsy

Liver biopsy is useful to determine the severity of the disease and for monitory progression when treatment with ribavirin and interferon is instigated. Cirrhosis develops in 20-25 percent of patients with chronic hepatitis C after 10 years. It is also important to rule out liver carcinoma which may be a complication of long standing hepatitis C infection Hepatitis C is an RNA virus and hence option C is incorrect. ERCP is done in cases of obstructive jaundice. CT abdomen is not going to help in this patient Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st ed, Ch: 341, Pg: 2592

Sample MCQ #4

A 28 year old intravenous drug user complains about severe epigastric pains, nausea and vomiting. He has upper GI endoscopy which shows small areas of ulceration and white plaques. Which of the following is the best treatment option?

AMetronidazole
BAmoxycillin
CRanitidine
DFluconazole
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Fluconazole

This is a patient with possible HIV who has oesophageal candidiasis (white plaques on endoscopy). Candida is normally found in the throat but can become pathogenic and produce esophagitis in a compromised host. Candida albicans is most common cause. Oral fluconazole for 14-21 days is the preferred treatment. Patients refractory to fluconazole may respond to voriconazole or posaconazole. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st ed, Ch: 323, Pg: 2432

Sample MCQ #5

A 30 year old lady is referred for investigation of abnormal liver function tests. She has been taking several drugs to treat tuberculosis for a long period of time.Her albumin is 3.0 g/dl, bilirubin 5 mg/dl, ALP is 250 U/l and ALT is 300 U/l.She has a liver biopsy which shows histological evidence of liver necrosis eroding the portal zone and extending into the liver lobule. What is the likely diagnosis?

AHepatitis B infection
BHepatitis C infection
CPrimary biliary cirrhosis
DChronic active hepatitis
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Chronic active hepatitis

Periportal necrosis, portal inflammation, bridging necrosis and interface hepatitis are features of active liver inflammation. Inflammatory conditions (e.g Wilson's disease) and drugs such as methyldopa and isoniazid can precipitate chronic active hepatitis. Autoimmune hepatitis is associated with antinuclear antibodies (70%), anti-smooth muscle (30%) and liver specific antibodies (ASGP-R asialoglycoprotein receptor; LKM liver kidney microsomal antibodies). Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st ed, Ch: 341, Pg: 2592

Sample MCQ #6

A 20-year-old patient complains of ankle swelling and breathlessness. Her CXR shows pleural effusions. Blood tests show: urea 17 mg/dl creatinine 0.90 mg/dl sodium 138 mmol/l potassium 4.2 mmol/l alanine transferase 20 U/L bilirubin 0.9 mg/dl albumin 2 gm/dl total cholesterol 340 mg/dl. What is the next best investigation?

AUltrasound of liver
BAntinuclear antibody
CUrinary protein estimation
DRenal biopsy
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Urinary protein estimation

The given patient has hypoalbuminemia, hyper cholesterolemia, edema and pleural effusion. So the edema is attribute to hypoalbuminemia. Hypoalbuminemia can be due to either decreased albumin production as in CLD or excessive albumin loss through urine or gut. However here the cholesterol levels are high which is an indirect indicator of nephrotic syndrome. So the next investigation will be to confirm the urinary loss, which is 24 hour urine estimation. Followed by a renal biopsy, once proteinuria is confirmed. The classic features of nephrotic syndrome are: Proteinuria >3g/24 hours Hypoalbuminemia( Generalized Oedema Hypercholesterolemia Lipiduria Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st ed, Ch: 314, Pg: 2341.
