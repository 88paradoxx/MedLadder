Sample MCQ #1

Which of the following is not a described association for Mitral valve prolapse?

ANoonan syndrome
BKlinefelter syndrome
CMarfan syndrome
DBarlow syndrome
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Noonan syndrome

Syndromes with MVP as a feature Marfan syndrome Ehler Danlos syndrome Klinefelter syndrome Osteogenesis imperfecta Autosomal dominant Polycystic kidney disease Pseudoxanthoma elasticum Barlow syndrome is synonymous with Mitral valve prolapse Noonan syndrome is associated with peripheral pulmonary artery stenosis, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy and atrial septal defect. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Page 2000

Sample MCQ #2

A 65-year-old man has renal bruits, renal impairment and hypertension. Which one of the following tests is most appropriate?

AIntravenous urography
BRenal DTPA nuclear scan
CRenal DMSA nuclear scan
DMagnetic resonance angiography(MRA) of renal arteries
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Magnetic resonance angiography(MRA) of renal arteries

From the options above, For renal artery stenosis, MRA remains the best investigation with high sensitivities and specificities. MRI is not effective for detecting stent thrombosis. CT angiography provides excellent imaging of the vessels and is less affected by stents. Risk of contrast nephropathy in renal impairment. Though Intra Arterial angiography remains Gold standard for diagnosis of Large vessel diseases. Doppler is very less expensive but is operator dependent and not useful for distal renal arteries and Fibromuscular dysplasia. Captopril renography has strong negative predictive value. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st ed, Ch: 278, Pg: 2088-2089.

Sample MCQ #3

A 34-year-old man presented for a routine check-up from company. He was symptom free, but clinical examination suggested a small ventricular septal defect. Which one of the following findings was most likely to have been present?

AAn early diastolic murmur
BA short systolic murmur at the right sternal edge
CA systolic murmur maximal at the apex
DA systolic murmur with thrill at the left sternal edge
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. A systolic murmur with thrill at the left sternal edge

Typically, a small VSD generates a pansystolic murmur at the left sternal border(LSB) accompanied by a thrill. The murmur may be heard at the apex but is usually loudest at the LSB . Very small defects may generate an early/late systolic murmur. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Principles, 20 th edition, ch:234,264 pg:1673,1833
