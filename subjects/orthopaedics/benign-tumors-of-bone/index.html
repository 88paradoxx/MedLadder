Sample MCQ #2

Which is not a cause of pain in osteochondroma?

ABursitis
BFracture of the pedicle
CCompression of an adjacent nerve
DDevelopment of secondary osteosarcoma
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Development of secondary osteosarcoma

Malignant change can cause pain in osteochondroma. However the malignancy that arises in osteochondroma is chondrosarcoma and not osteosarcoma. The causes of pain in osteochondroma are Bursitis (most common cause) Fracture of the pedicle Compression of an adjacent nerve Malignant change - chondrosarcoma. Reference: Miler's 8th Edition of Review of Orthopaedics, page no. 1881

✓ Correct Answer: C. Chondroblastoma

Codman's tumor is another name for Chondroblastoma. It should not be confused with Codman's triangle which is a type of periosteal reaction, most commonly seen in osteosarcoma. Reference: Tachdjian's Pediatric Orthopaedics 5th edition page no. 1109

Sample MCQ #5

A 26 years male with epiphysial bone tumor shows lytic lesion with chicken-wire calcification.

AGiant cell tumor
BOsteoblastoma
CChondroblastoma
DEwings sarcoma
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Chondroblastoma

Chondroblastoma This is a cartilaginous tumor containing characteristic multiple calcium deposits. It occurs in young adults and is located around the epiphyseal plate . Bones around the knee are commonly affected. Radiologically, there is a well-defined lytic lesion surrounded by a zone of sclerosis. Areas of calcification within the tumor substance give rise to a mottled appearance . Treatment is by curettage and bone grafting. Chondroblastoma(Codman tumors): Microscopically they are composed of chondroblasts, chondroid matrix, cartilage with occasional giant multinucleated cells(which may lead to the incorrect diagnosis of giant cell tumor). Calcium deposition surrounding the chondroblasts, which are typically polyhedral shape, results in typical ' chicken-wire calcification '(pathognomonic) Reference: Maheshwari Essentials Ortho, 5 th edition, Page no. 236.

Sample MCQ #7

A 15y old boy presented with back pain. CT showed an expansile lytic lesion in transverse process of vertebra. What is the likely diagnosis?

AOsteosarcoma
BABC
COsteoblastoma
DMetastasis
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. ABC

Osteosarcoma never involves the vertebra. Metastasis can occur rarely in this age group, but it usually involves the vertebral body and the pedicles. Tumors that can commonly involve the transverse process are ABC and Osteoblastoma. Osteoblastoma will have a large nidus and surrounding sclerosis, hence it is not a lytic lesion The classical expansile lytic lesion of the transverse process is aneurysmal bone cyst (ABC). Reference: J. Maheshwari's 5th Edition of Essential Orthopaedics page no. 237

Sample MCQ #8

Chondrosarcoma can develop in which of the following conditions?

AMcCune Albright syndrome
BMaffucci syndrome
CJaffe campanacci syndrome
DMazabraud syndrome
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Maffucci syndrome

Chondrosarcoma can arise from other benign cartilage tumors especially enchondromas. In the given options, the only syndrome that presents with enchondromas is Maffucci syndrome. Maffucci syndrome has a 100% risk of malignant change.
