Sample Nerve Injuries MCQs with answers

Sample MCQ #1

A 25-year-old football player sustains a blow to the arm during a match. He develops temporary weakness and tingling, but recovers completely within 6 weeks without surgical intervention. Which of the following best describes neuropraxia?

AIntact axon, damaged nerve sheath
BDamaged axon, intact nerve sheath
CDamaged axon and nerve sheath
DNone of the above
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Intact axon, damaged nerve sheath

Explanation: Neuropraxia is the mildest form of peripheral nerve injury , as described in Seddon's classification . In this condition, the axon remains intact, but there is focal demyelination or damage to the myelin sheath due to compression or ischemia. Conduction block occurs across the site of injury, leading to weakness or sensory loss. Since the axon and endoneurial tubes are preserved, full recovery occurs within weeks to months without Wallerian degeneration. Explanation of other options: Damaged axon, intact nerve sheath (Option B): Describes axonotmesis , where axons are damaged but connective tissue sheaths remain intact. Damaged axon and nerve sheath (Option C): Describes neurotmesis , the most severe form of injury, with a poor prognosis.

✓ Correct Answer: C. Closed fracture shaft humerus with Radial nerve palsy
