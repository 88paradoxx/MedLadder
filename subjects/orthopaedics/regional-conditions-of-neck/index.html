Sample MCQ #1

A 67 year old woman has neck pains and occipital headaches for 2 years. She is referred to the neurology outpatients for assessment. Investigations showed a normal CT of the brain. Cervical X ray showed degenerative changes of narrowed disc spaces and loss of cervical lordosis. What is the likely diagnosis?

ACerebellar haemorrhage
BTemporal arteritis
COccipital neuralgia
DCervical spondylosis
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Cervical spondylosis

In cervical spondylosis, several overlapping syndromes are seen: neck and shoulder pain, suboccipital pain and headache, radicular symptoms, and cervical spondylotic myelopathy. Examination findings include neck pain, radicular signs, and myelopathic signs. Cervical spine films can demonstrate disk space narrowing, osteophytosis, loss of cervical lordosis, uncovertebral joint hypertrophy, apophyseal joint osteoarthritis, and vertebral canal diameter. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st Ed, Ch: 442, Pg: 3452

Sample MCQ #2

Not true about congenital muscular torticollis

AThere is fusion of vertebrae
BCan be associated with a mass
CCock robin appearance
DCan spontaneously resolve
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. There is fusion of vertebrae

Congenital muscular torticollis occurs because of the contracture of sternocleidomastoid muscle and there is no fusion of vertebrae. Fusion of vertebrae is seen in Klippel Feil syndrome. Sometimes a mass can be palpable in the SCM which is the fibrotic muscle. Reference: Tachdjian's Pediatric Orthopaedics 5th edition page no. 169

Sample MCQ #3

A 50 year old female presented with sudden onset pain, weakness and wasting of muscles of arm. She gives history of getting a TT injection in the deltoid 5 days back. What is the diagnosis?

ARadial N injury
BThoracic outlet syndrome
CBrachial neuritis
DIntramuscular abscess
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Brachial neuritis

Tetanus toxoid injections can rarely lead to brachial plexus neuritis. This presents as sudden onset pain, paresthesia and weakness of the affected arm. It can also lead to muscle wasting in the long run. Reference: Miler's 8th Edition of Review of Orthopaedics, page no. 1513
