Sample MCQ #3

17-year-old high school tennis player trips while returning a serve and sustains a right mid-shaft clavicle fracture. Which of the following increases the risk of nonunion in the nonoperative treatment of clavicle fractures?

ASling treatment
BFigure-of-eight bandage treatment
CDisplacement and comminution
DAge < 20 years
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Displacement and comminution

Displacement and comminution have been shown to increase risk of non- union in nonoperative management. There are no significant differences between outcomes when using sling immobilization versus a figure-of-eight bandage. Reference- Fundamentals of Orthopaedics 1st edition, page no. 44

Sample MCQ #4

A 55 year old electrician fell down after sustaining an electric shock and injured his left shoulder. On examination the patient was unable to abduct or externally rotate the shoulder. What is the diagnosis?

AAnterior dislocation
BClavicle fracture
CPosterior dislocation
DLuxatio erecta
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Posterior dislocation

Falls after an electric shock/ epileptic fit or Extreme trauma can cause posterior shoulder dislocation. These etiologies are together called the triple E syndrome. Injury mechanism in electrical injury is similar to epileptic seizures, where the shoulder is forced to internal rotation, flexion and adduction. Classical deformity in posterior shoulder dislocation is adduction and internal rotation. This means that the patient will be unable to abduct or externally rotate his shoulder. Luxation erecta is another name for inferior dislocation of shoulder. Reference- The Shoulder volume 1, Charles A Rockwood, P 151

Sample MCQ #5

An acute posterior shoulder dislocation should be suspected in a patient with pain and the shoulder locked in what position?

AInternal rotation
BExternal rotation
CForward elevation
DAbduction
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Internal rotation

Patients with posterior shoulder dislocations are often overlooked as it is not associated with gross deformities or severe pain. Further posterior dislocation is also difficult to pick up on xrays. They usually present with a history of fall after electric shock, epileptic seizure or severe trauma. O/E, the shoulder would be locked in internal rotation and adduction and lack external rotation. Orthogonal radiographs (anterior-posterior and axillary lateral) are absolutely necessary for proper diagnosis. Posterior dislocation patients often have reverse Bankart and reverse Hill-Sachs lesions. Posterior dislocation is treated with closed reduction and immobilization Reference - Maheshwari Ortho 5 th edition page no 89

Sample MCQ #7

Not true about impingement syndrome

ACan cause rotator cuff injury
BSupraspinatus tendon is most commonly involved
CMid range of abduction alone may be painful
DSurgical decompression is the first line of treatment
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Surgical decompression is the first line of treatment

Shoulder impingement is treated conservatively in majority of the case. Surgical decompression is rarely indicated. Shoulder impingement results from compression of the rotator cuff between the Acromion process and the Humeral head. Supraspinatus us the most commonly affected tendon. Impingement can present with painful arc syndrome, where the initial 60 degrees and the final 60 degrees of shoulder abduction will be pain free. However the mid abduction i.e, between 60 and 120 degrees will be painful. Prolonged impingement can cause rotator cuff tears. The tests for impingement are - Neer's test and Hawkin's test. Reference: Miler's 8th Edition of Review of Orthopaedics, page no. 833

Sample MCQ #8

A 55 year old lady who is a known case of diabetes, presents with severe pain and stiffness of the right shoulder for the last 3 months. O/E, there is global restriction of both active and passive movements of the right shoulder. X ray is normal. What is the diagnosis?

ARotator cuff tear
BShoulder osteoarthritis
CAdhesive capsulitis
DSeptic arthritis
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Adhesive capsulitis

Global restriction of both active and passive movements of the shoulder is seen in post traumatic stiffness and adhesive capsulitis. In this patient the diagnosis is adhesive capsulitis because No history of trauma Normal x ray Presence of diabetes, which is the most important risk factor for adhesive capsulitis. Adhesive capsulitis is also called Periarthritis or Frozen shoulder It is an idiopathic condition characterized by global reduction in passive and active range of motion of shoulder due to fibrosis and adhesions in the capsule It is usually self limiting, but it takes a lot of time for that. It is usually treated conservatively with physiotherapy. Surgery is rarely indicated. Reference: Miler's 8th Edition of Review of Orthopaedics, page no. 858
