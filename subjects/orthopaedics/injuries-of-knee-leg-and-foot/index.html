Sample MCQ #1

An army recruit has pain over both of his feet for the last 3 months since he started training. He has point tenderness and pain in the forefoot which is aggravated by activity. However, his x rays are normal. What is the next line of investigation?

AMRI
BBone scan
CCT scan
DNo further investigations are required as his x-rays are normal
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Bone scan

From the history, the person has stress fractures. Stress fracture is sometimes not seen on x-ray in early stages. Hence we need further investigations to confirm our diagnosis. For suspected stress fracture in one site, the investigation of choice is MRI. But for B/L stress fractures or multiple stress fractures, bone scan is recommended over MRI as it is easy, effective and economical. Reference- Rockwood and green adult fracture 8&#7511;&#688; edition, page-653

Sample MCQ #2

An army recruit with smoking addiction has pain in his right foot for the last 3 months after he started training. He has point tenderness and pain in the forefoot which is aggravated by activity. What would be the clinical diagnosis?

APathological fracture
BStress fracture
CMalignant lesion
DInfection

✓ Correct Answer: B. Stress fracture

Stress fracture occurs due to abnormal loading of a normal bone. This is classically seen in individuals who perform repetitive, strenuous activities that he/she is not accustomed to. The most common site of stress fracture is Tibia followed by 2 nd metatarsal (March Fracture) Reference- Rockwood and green adult fracture 8 th edition, page-652

Sample MCQ #3

Pigmented villo-nodular synovitis most commonly occurs at

AKnee
BHip
CShoulder
DElbow
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Knee

Pigmented villonodular synovitis: Pigmented villonodular synovitis(PVNS) is a rare benign disease of the synovial membrane which is characterised by hypervascular neoplastic proliferation of the synovium with deposition of macrophages, multinucleated giant cells and hemosiderin. Knee joint is most commonly involved joint. On arthroscopy typical brownish pigmentation of synovium is seen due to deposition of hemosiderin pigment. Two varieties have been described: Diffuse variety and localized nodular variety. Chances of recurrence are higher in diffuse variety. Low dose external beam radiotherapy and arthroscopic/open synovectomy have been tried with varying success for the treatment. Malignant transformation has been reported but very rare. Reference: Fundamentals of Ortho Mohindra and Jain, Page no. 268.

Sample MCQ #4

Patient sustains twisting type of injury followed by locking of knee. Most likely structure to be injured is?

AMenisci
BAnterior cruciate ligament
CAvulsed Tibial tuberosity
DLateral collateral ligament
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Menisci

Locking is the phenomenon in which the patient is unable to fully extend the knee due to intra articular block by a torn meniscus. Hence, when the patient has locking of knee, it means he/ she has sustained a meniscus injury. Locking is most common in bucket-handle type of longitudinal tears of meniscus, where the inner segment of the meniscus is caught in the intercondylar notch and cannot return to its former position. Reference - Manish Kumar Varshney 1 st edition page no 901.

Sample MCQ #8

Most common mechanism of PCL injury is

AHyper flexion non-contact injury
BHyperextension non-contact injury
CBlow on the front of upper tibia while knee is flexed and foot is plantar flexed
DBlow on the back of upper tibia while knee is flexed and foot is plantar flexed
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Blow on the front of upper tibia while knee is flexed and foot is plantar flexed

PCL tears are typically produced by external trauma such as the classic &ldquo;dashboard injury&rdquo; resulting from a posteriorly directed force on the anterior aspect of the proximal tibia with the knee flexed and foot plantar flexed. In athletics, the typical mechanism of isolated PCL tears is a direct blow to the anterior tibia or a fall onto the knee with the foot in a plantar flexed position. Football, soccer, rugby and skiing are among the sports with the highest incidence of PCL tears. Non-contact mechanisms, such as hyperflexion, are less common. Reference - Manish Kumar Varshney 1 st edition page no 888
