Sample Injuries of Spine MCQs with answers

Sample MCQ #1

What is the management in case of a stable cervical spine injury with no neurological changes?

ASkull traction
BRigid cervical brace
CLaminectomy
DAnterior decompression and fixation
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Rigid cervical brace

In a stable cervical spine injuries with no neurological deficit, a rigid cervical brace or halo for 8-12 weeks is usually sufficient. Stable compression fractures of vertebral bodies and undisplaced fracture of laminae, lateral masses or spinous process may also be treated conservatively. Reference: Ebnezar, 5 th edition, Page no. 304.

Sample MCQ #2

Which of the following is not a known complication of TB spine?

AParaplegia
BPsoas abscess
CFibrous ankylosis
DKyphosis
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Fibrous ankylosis

TB produces fibrous ankylosis in all joints except the spine, where it produces bony ankylosis. Complications of TB spine include Kyphotic deformity Neurological deficit Paraplegia Bowel and bladder involvement Bony ankylosis of vertebra Abscess Psoas abscess Paravertebral abscess Reference: Miler's 8th Edition of Review of Orthopaedics, page no. 1757

Sample MCQ #3

Indicators of poor prognosis in the pott's spine is/are:

AHealed vertebral disc
BGrade IV Neurological grade
CKyphotic angle >60&deg;
DAll of the above
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. All of the above

Factors leading to poor prognosis in potts spine: Inactive disease with poor mechanical compression Complete paralysis/Complete cord involvement Paralysis for more than 1year Presence of severe kyphosis(>60 degree) Older age and poor nutrition Healed vertebral lesion Presence of myelomalacia and syringomyel ia on magnetic resonance imaging(MRI). Good prognosis is associated with: Inflammatory cause of cord involvement Incomplete involvement Short duration of deficit Minimal kyphotic deformity Younger age Good nutritional status. Reference: Bailey and Love's Short Practice of Surgery, 28th Edition, Chapter: 17, Pg: 257.

Sample MCQ #4

In the lumbar spine, 90% of the disc herniations occur at which of the following level:

AL1-L2
BL2-L3
CL3-L4
DL4-L5
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. L4-L5

Most common site of spinal disc herniation: Lumbar region(95% in L4-L5 or L5-S1). The second most common site is the cervical region(C5-C6, C6-C7). Thoracic region accounts for only 0.15% to 4.0% of cases. Reference: Maheshwari Essential Orthopedics, 5th ed., Pg: 253.

Sample MCQ #6

Pott's spine spine commonly affects all of the following parts of vertebrae except

ABody
BLamina
CSpinous process
DPedicle
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Spinous process

Facet joint > Spinous process are least involved structures in Posterior type of vertebral TB. Reference: Maheshwari Essential Ortho, 7 th edition, Page no. 184.

Sample MCQ #7

The earliest neurological sign in a patient with TB spine is

ASensory loss
BSpastic weakness
CBladder involvement
DAnkle clonus
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Ankle clonus

ORDER OF NEUROLOGICAL INVOLVEMENT IN TB: Rarely is paraplegia the presenting symptom. Motor system is almost always affected before the sensory system because diseased area in spine(i.e., vertebral body) lies anterior to the cord near the motor tracts. The first neurological sign is ankle clonus followed by plantar extensor. Thereafter the patient develops mild spastic motor weakness manifesting as clumsiness or incoordination of gait which ends in severe weakness to the extent where patient is unable to stand and is bed ridden. Reference: Maheshwari Essential Orthopedics, 7 th edition, Page no. 190.

Sample MCQ #8

All of the following are used in the treatment of TB spine except

AMiddle path regime
BHong Kong operation
CSpine brace
DRight anterolateral decompression
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Right anterolateral decompression

The surgery used for treating spine TB in India is Left anterolateral decompression. Anterolateral decompression should not be done through the right side as there is high risk of injuring the inferior vena cava, which if it occurs can be fatal. Reference: J. Maheshwari's 5th Edition of Essential Orthopaedics page no. 194
