Sample MCQ #3

You are called to the nursery to assess a newborn girl. She was born at 39 weeks via normal spontaneous delivery to a mother who had no prenatal care. APGAR scores are 7 and 9. At four hours of life, the nurse noted respiratory distress. On physical examination, her heart rate is 150 beats per minute, respiratory rate is 70 cycles per minute, temperature is 37.1 C with barrel-shaped chest, a scaphoid-appearing abdomen, and absence of breath sounds on the left chest. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

ACongenital diaphragmatic hernia
BCongenital lobar emphysema
CMeconium aspiration syndrome
DRespiratory distress syndrome
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Congenital diaphragmatic hernia

The newborn girl has findings that are suggestive of congenital diaphragmatic hernia. Congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH) is a developmental defect of the diaphragm that allows abdominal viscera to herniate into the chest. Although routine prenatal ultrasound screening may identify CDH at a mean gestational age of 24 weeks, there is a wide range of reported sensitivity in its detection. Physical findings include a barrel-shaped chest, a scaphoid-appearing abdomen (because of loss of the abdominal contents into the chest), and absence of breath sounds on the ipsilateral side. Reference: Avery's diseases of the newborn, 1 st SA ed., pg. 772

Sample MCQ #5

An infant with a history of recurrent pneumonia is diagnosed with TEF at 8 months of age. Which of the following statements is correct?

AThe infant most likely has an H-type TEF
BThe infant most likely has proximal esophageal atresia with distal fistula
CThe infant likely has a previously undetected, associated finding of imperforate anus
DThe infant is unlikely to have gastroesophageal reflux
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. The infant most likely has an H-type TEF

The key to making the diagnosis is age and features in this question. This infant likely has an H-type TEF, found later in infancy with recurrent pneumonias and/or feeding difficulty. Patients with esophageal atresia and distal fistula present in the first hours of life because of their inability to swallow oropharyngeal secretions. Infants with imperforate anus also present as neonates. All patients with TEF are at high risk for gastroesophageal reflux. Reference: Nelson textbook of pediatrics, 1 st SA ed., pg. 1783

Sample MCQ #6

All of the following are common complications in survivors of CDH, except?

AHepatic Fibrosis
BGERD
CRecurrence of CDH
DIntestinal obstruction
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Hepatic Fibrosis

Hepatic fibrosis is not mentioned as a complication in CDH survivors. Among other options: Option B- Gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) is reported in >50% of children with CDH. GERD is more common in children whose diaphragmatic defect involves the esophageal hiatus. Option C- Recurrent diaphragmatic hernia is reported in 5-20% in most series. Option D- Intestinal obstruction is reported in up to 20% of children and may result from a midgut volvulus, adhesions, or a recurrent hernia that became incarcerated. Children with patch repairs are at highest risk. Children with CDH typically have delayed growth in the 1st 2 yr of life. Neurocognitive defects are common and may result from the disease or the interventions. Reference: Nelson 21 st Edition, Chapter 345.1

Sample MCQ #7

A >48-hour neonate has still not passed meconium after birth. What is the investigation of choice?

AInvertogram
BUSG abdomen
CCT scan
DContrast enema
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Contrast enema

A water-soluble contrast enema is the investigation of choice in delayed passage of meconium since it is both diagnostic and therapeutic. It can diagnose Hirschsprung disease, meconium ileus, atresias Can also remove meconium plug in meconium plug syndrome and have therapeutic action. Reference: Nelson 21 st Edition, Chapter 358

Sample MCQ #8

Which among the following is associated with a good prognosis in congenital diaphragmatic hernia?

ALung head ratio <1
BSymptoms develop beyond 48 hr of birth
CCDH detected in the 1st trimester scan prenatally
DLiver herniated into thorax
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Symptoms develop beyond 48 hr of birth

Symptoms developing beyond 48 hr of birth usually indicates a milder variant of CDH and thus has a better outcome. Extra-Edge: Poor prognosis predictors in congenital diaphragmatic hernia Lung: Head Ratio Severe pulmonary hypoplasia An associated major anomaly Herniation to the contralateral lung Symptoms before 24 hr of age Liver herniation into thorax CDH detected in the 1 st trimester Need for ECMO Reference: Nelson 21 st Edition, Chapter 122.10
