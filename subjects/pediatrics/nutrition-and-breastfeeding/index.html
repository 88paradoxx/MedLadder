Sample Nutrition and Breastfeeding MCQs with answers

Sample MCQ #1

A neonate has not passed urine in the last 24 hours since birth. Next step in management is?

AContinue breastfeeding and observe
BAdmit to NICU
CStart formula feeds
DStart IV fluids
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Continue breastfeeding and observe

According to Cloherty & Stark, 90% neonates void urine within 24 hours and 99% do so by 48 hours. Neonates without any risk factor can pass urine anytime before 48 hours of life. Until then we need to observe and feed the child. If more than 48 hours have passed, the baby needs to be shifted to the ICU and evaluated for volume status & investigated. Reference: Cloherty and Starks manual of neonatal care, SA ed., pg. 128

Sample MCQ #2

Breastfeeding should be started within how much time after birth?

A1 hr
B2 hr
C4 hr
D6 hr
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. 1 hr

Points to remember regarding breastfeeding Breastfeeding should be started as soon as possible after birth. As per WHO, UNICEF it must be started within 1 hr of birth Exclusive BF till: 6 months of age. Then start complementary feed and continue to breastfeed till 18-24 months. Reference: OP Ghai 10 th Edition, Page 142

Sample MCQ #3

Indication to use prednisolone in Henoch-Schoenlein Purpura is?

APrevention of nephritis
BSevere GIT involvement
CDependent purpuric rash
DCNS dysfunction
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Severe GIT involvement

Treatment for mild and self-limited HSP is supportive, with an emphasis on ensuring adequate hydration, nutrition, and analgesia. Corticosteroids are most often used to treat significant GI involvement or other life-threatening manifestations. Glucocorticoids such as oral prednisone (1-2 mg/kg/day), or in severe cases, intravenous (IV) methylprednisolone for 1-2 wk, followed by taper, reduce abdominal and joint pain but do not alter overall prognosis. Corticosteroids are not routinely recommended for prevention of complications such as nephritis. Corticosteroids may also be needed in any orchitis, CNS involvement and pulmonary hemorrhage Reference: Nelson 21 st Ed, Chapter 192, OP Ghai 10 th Ed, Page 661
