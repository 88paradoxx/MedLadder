Sample MCQ #2

An 8-day-old baby presents with vomiting, poor feeding and loose stools. On examination, his heart rate is 190 bpm, BP 50/30 mmHg and respiratory rate 72 breaths/min. Blood tests: Hb 15 g/dl (13-18), sodium 120 mmol/l (137-144), potassium 6.0 mmol/l (3.5-4.9), chloride 80 mmol/l (95-107), bicarbonate 15 mmol/l (21-28), urea 30 mg/dl and creatinine 0.6 mg/dl (both normal). What is the most likely diagnosis

ACongenital adrenal hyperplasia
BAcute tubular necrosis
CCongenital hypertrophic pyloric stenosis
DGalactosaemia
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Congenital adrenal hyperplasia

The features and findings are highly suggestive of congenital adrenal hyperplasia presenting as salt wasting crisis 21 hydroxylase is the most common enzyme deficiency in CAH and salt wasting crisis. Absence of jaundice and hepatomegaly make the diagnosis of Galactosaemia unlikely. Acute tubular necrosis is ruled out, as urea and creatinine levels are normal. In congenital hypertrophic pyloric stenosis, hypokalemia is the presenting feature and constipation (not loose stools) is the chief complaint. Lactose intolerance would again present with diarrhea or vomiting, thus hypokalemia would be the characteristic finding. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Page 2973

Sample MCQ #3

A 2-month-old infant is presented with failure to thrive, recurrent emesis, hepatosplenomegaly, and adrenal insufficiency. Adrenal calcification is noted radiologically. The most likely diagnosis is?

AAdrenal hemorrhage
BWolman disease
CPheochromocytoma
DAddison's disease
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Wolman disease

Vomiting, hepatosplenomegaly, adrenal insufficiency and adrenal calcification point to a diagnosis of Wolman Disease. Wolman Disease is an autosomal recessive lysosomal storage diseases that results from deficiency of lysosomal acid lipase (LAL) and accumulation of cholesterol esters and triglycerides in histiocytic foam cells of most visceral organs. The LAL gene is on chromosome 10 (10q24-q25). Clinical features become apparent in the 1st weeks of life and include failure to thrive, relentless vomiting, abdominal distention, steatorrhea, and hepatosplenomegaly. There usually is hyperlipidemia. Hepatic dysfunction and fibrosis may occur. Calcification of the adrenal glands occurs in about 50% of patients and is an important clue to the underlying diagnosis, when present. Death usually occurs within the 1st 6 months of life. Reference: Nelson 21 st Edition, Chapter 104

Sample MCQ #4

Which of the following is not a classic feature of a salt-losing crisis in an infant with congenital adrenal hyperplasia?

AHyponatremia
BHypokalemia
CHypoglycemia
DHypotension
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Hypokalemia

In CAH, an enzyme deficiency in the steroid hormone synthesis pathway can lead to a combined cortisol and aldosterone insufficiency. Aldosterone promotes water and sodium reabsorption in exchange for potassium by upregulating Na/K pump activity in the distal nephron . Its absence therefore results in a salt-losing crisis with hyponatraemia, hyperkalaemia (and associated metabolic acidosis), and hypotension. Sample MCQ #5

A 18-day-old female infant, has congenital adrenal hypoplasia. She presents having vomited all her feeds today. She has become lethargic and will no longer feed. On examination she has tachycardia, with a heart rate of 160 beats/min and a capillary refill time of 3 seconds. A bedside blood test shows that she is hypoglycemic. What is the best immediate management?

AIntravenous glucose alone for 24 hours
BIntravenous saline, glucose and hydrocortisone
COral rehydration and oral glucocorticoid
DOral rehydration, sodium supplements and oral glucocorticoid
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Intravenous saline, glucose and hydrocortisone

The child is having an adrenal crisis. This requires urgent treatment with intravenous saline, glucose and hydrocortisone. Long-term treatment is with glucocorticoid and mineralocorticoid replacement and oral sodium chloride may be needed during infancy. The dose of glucocorticoid needs to be increased at times of illness. The child is having a salt-losing crisis. Saline is needed along with glucose infusion. (option a) This child is seriously ill. IV is preferred over Oral due to sickness of child.
