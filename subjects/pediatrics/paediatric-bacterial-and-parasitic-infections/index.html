Sample MCQ #1

A 14 days-old neonate who had bilateral conjunctivitis, now has a cough with chest infiltrates. Which among the following could be the causative organism?

AHaemophilus influenzae
BChlamydia
CStaphylococcus epidermidis
DMycoplasma
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Chlamydia

Chlamydia is the most common cause of neonatal pneumonia associated with neonatal conjunctivitis. Chlamydial genital infection is reported in 5-30% of pregnant women, with a risk for vertical transmission at parturition to Newborn infants of approximately 50%. The infant may become infected at 1 or more sites, including the conjunctiva, nasopharynx, rectum, and vagina. Most commonly these infections manifest as inclusion conjunctivitis and Pneumonia. Rectal and vaginal infections are also seen but most of them are asymptomatic. TLC in these patients is normal to high & CXR often shows interstitial infiltrates. Drug of choice is usually IV Azithromycin Reference: Nelson textbook of Pediatrics, 20 th ed., pg. 1495

Sample MCQ #2

Which of the following is true about DiGeorge syndrome?

AIt results from a duplication of chromosome 22q11.
BThere is hypoplasia of the second pharyngeal pouch.
CApnea, jitteriness, and high-pitched cry may complicate the course of the disease
DCardiac defect may be a cause of death.
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Cardiac defect may be a cause of death.

DiGeorge Syndrome DiGeorge syndrome is a congenital hypoplasia of organs derived from the third and fourth pharyngeal pouches. It results in hypoplasia or agenesis of the thymus and parathyroid glands, the auricle, and the external auditory canal; congenital cardiac anomalies; cleft palate; and short stature. It is associated with microdeletion of chromosome 22q11 . The main features are hypocalcemia, seizures, congenital heart disease, lymphocytopenia, and multiple minor anomalies. Hypocalcemia can manifest as apnea, jitteriness, crying episodes and tetany. Affected newborns may die from cardiac causes during the first month. Frequent infections due to a defect of cell-mediated immunity and failure to thrive may complicate the course of the surviving newborn. Reference: Nelson 20 th edition, pg-2650

Sample MCQ #3

A baby developed congenital toxoplasmosis. Presence of which among the following will predict a higher risk of severe infection?

AHLA-DR1
BHLA-DQ2
CHLA-DQ3
DToxoplasma infection is not HLA-associated
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. HLA-DQ3

HLA-DQ3+ predicts a more severe and fulminant course of congenital toxoplasma infection. According to Nelson, In monozygotic twins with congenital toxoplasmosis, the clinical pattern of involvement is most often similar, whereas in dizygotic twins the manifestations often differ. The major histocompatibility complex class II gene DQ3 appears to be common even among HIV-infected persons seropositive for T. gondii who develop toxoplasmic encephalitis, as well as in children with congenital toxoplasmosis who develop hydrocephalus. These findings suggest that the presence of HLA-DQ3 is a risk factor for severity of toxoplasmosis, in all patients including congenital toxoplasmosis. Reference: Nelson 21 st Edition, Chapter 316
