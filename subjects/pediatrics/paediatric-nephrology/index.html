Sample Paediatric Nephrology MCQs with answers

Sample MCQ #1

An 8-year-old child presented with periorbital edema and mild oliguria but no hypertension. On evaluation, he was found to have serum albumin <2.5 g/dL, serum creatinine 0.5 mg/dL, urine protein 3+, and no RBCs or RBC casts in the urine. What histopathological diagnosis will you suspect?

AMinimal change disease
BIgA nephropathy
CMembranous nephropathy
DFSGS
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Minimal change disease

Explanation: The clinical features: significant proteinuria (3+), hypoalbuminemia, and edema; are characteristic of Nephrotic Syndrome . In children aged 2-8 years, the most common histopathological diagnosis is Minimal Change Disease (MCD) . Absence of hematuria and hypertension supports the diagnosis of idiopathic nephrotic syndrome. Light microscopy typically shows minimal or no changes. Electron microscopy reveals effacement of podocyte foot processes. Oliguria , although more typical of nephritic syndrome, may occur in nephrotic syndrome due to worsening edema and hypoalbuminemia. Explanation of Other Options: IgA nephropathy (Option B): Presents with hematuria, often post-infectious. Membranous nephropathy (Option C): More common in adults; shows thickened GBM on microscopy. FSGS (Option D): Presents with steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome and may show hematuria or hypertension.

Sample MCQ #2

A 4-year-old boy presents with swelling that has increased over the past week. The swelling is most noticeable around his eyes in the mornings but is most notable in his legs in the afternoon. On examination, he has pitting edema of the bilateral lower extremities. He is also mildly hypertensive. Labs are significant for hypoalbuminemia and 3+ proteinuria. What is the most likely etiology of the child's hypoalbuminemia?

ACongestive heart failure
BIdiopathic nephrotic syndrome
CPoststreptococcal glomerulonephritis
DProtein-losing enteropathy
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Idiopathic nephrotic syndrome

The child's presentation is consistent with idiopathic nephrotic syndrome. Nephrotic syndrome results from urinary protein losses, which lead to hypoalbuminemia. The hypoalbuminemia leads to dependent edema. As such, the edema may be most noticeable around the eyes in the mornings with a shift to the lower extremities as the day progresses. Hypertension may occasionally be present. Typical laboratory findings include serum hypoalbuminemia, proteinuria, and hyperlipidemia. Idiopathic nephrotic syndrome is the most common cause of nephrotic syndrome in childhood. Reference: OP Ghai 10 th Edition, Pages 500-501; Nelson textbook of pediatrics, 1 st SA ed., pg. 2523

Sample MCQ #3

A 10-year-old child presented with generalized edema. On investigation, his serum cholesterol level was 238 mg/dl, urine protein was 3+ and stool microscopy revealed oval fat bodies. What is the probable diagnosis?

AGoodpasture syndrome
BUrinary Tract Infection
CNephrotic syndrome
DNephritic syndrome
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Nephrotic syndrome

Nephrotic range proteinuria is defined as proteinuria >3.5 g/24 hour or a urine protein : creatinine ratio >2. The triad of clinical findings associated with nephrotic syndrome arising from the large urinary losses of protein are hypoalbuminemia (&le;2.5 g/dL), edema, and hyperlipidemia (cholesterol >200 mg/dL). Reference: Nelson textbook of Pediatrics, 20 th ed., pg. 2521
