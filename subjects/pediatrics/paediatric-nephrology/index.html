Sample MCQ #1

An 8-year-old child presented with periorbital edema and mild oliguria but no hypertension. On evaluation, he was found to have serum albumin <2.5 g/dL, serum creatinine 0.5 mg/dL, urine protein 3+, and no RBCs or RBC casts in the urine. What histopathological diagnosis will you suspect?

AMinimal change disease
BIgA nephropathy
CMembranous nephropathy
DFSGS
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Minimal change disease

Explanation: The clinical features: significant proteinuria (3+), hypoalbuminemia, and edema; are characteristic of Nephrotic Syndrome . In children aged 2-8 years, the most common histopathological diagnosis is Minimal Change Disease (MCD) . Absence of hematuria and hypertension supports the diagnosis of idiopathic nephrotic syndrome. Light microscopy typically shows minimal or no changes. Electron microscopy reveals effacement of podocyte foot processes. Oliguria , although more typical of nephritic syndrome, may occur in nephrotic syndrome due to worsening edema and hypoalbuminemia. Explanation of Other Options: IgA nephropathy (Option B): Presents with hematuria, often post-infectious. Membranous nephropathy (Option C): More common in adults; shows thickened GBM on microscopy. FSGS (Option D): Presents with steroid-resistant nephrotic syndrome and may show hematuria or hypertension.

Sample MCQ #2

A 4-year-old boy presents with swelling that has increased over the past week. The swelling is most noticeable around his eyes in the mornings but is most notable in his legs in the afternoon. On examination, he has pitting edema of the bilateral lower extremities. He is also mildly hypertensive. Labs are significant for hypoalbuminemia and 3+ proteinuria. What is the most likely etiology of the child's hypoalbuminemia?

ACongestive heart failure
BIdiopathic nephrotic syndrome
CPoststreptococcal glomerulonephritis
DProtein-losing enteropathy
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Idiopathic nephrotic syndrome

The child's presentation is consistent with idiopathic nephrotic syndrome. Nephrotic syndrome results from urinary protein losses, which lead to hypoalbuminemia. The hypoalbuminemia leads to dependent edema. As such, the edema may be most noticeable around the eyes in the mornings with a shift to the lower extremities as the day progresses. Hypertension may occasionally be present. Typical laboratory findings include serum hypoalbuminemia, proteinuria, and hyperlipidemia. Idiopathic nephrotic syndrome is the most common cause of nephrotic syndrome in childhood. Reference: OP Ghai 10 th Edition, Pages 500-501; Nelson textbook of pediatrics, 1 st SA ed., pg. 2523

Sample MCQ #3

A 10-year-old child presented with generalized edema. On investigation, his serum cholesterol level was 238 mg/dl, urine protein was 3+ and stool microscopy revealed oval fat bodies. What is the probable diagnosis?

AGoodpasture syndrome
BUrinary Tract Infection
CNephrotic syndrome
DNephritic syndrome
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Nephrotic syndrome

Nephrotic range proteinuria is defined as proteinuria >3.5 g/24 hour or a urine protein : creatinine ratio >2. The triad of clinical findings associated with nephrotic syndrome arising from the large urinary losses of protein are hypoalbuminemia (&le;2.5 g/dL), edema, and hyperlipidemia (cholesterol >200 mg/dL). Reference: Nelson textbook of Pediatrics, 20 th ed., pg. 2521

Sample MCQ #4

True regarding minimal change disease?

AMore common in girls than boys
BNon-selective proteinuria
CSevere hypoalbuminemia
DCyclosporine is effective first-line therapy
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Severe hypoalbuminemia

Minimal Change Disease is a histopathological entity commonly seen in children with nephrotic syndrome. The disease is so-named because the light microscopy shows minimal to no change. However, electron microscopy shows effacement of foot process of podocytes. Let us evaluate each option Option A is False. Minimal change disease is more common in boys than girls Option B is False. It causes selective proteinuria Option C is True. MCD leads to excess albumin loss in urine, causing severe hypoalbuminemia and is thus the MC cause of childhood nephrotic syndrome. Option D is False. Most cases respond well to Oral Prednisolone as the first line therapy. Reference: OP Ghai 10 th Edition, Pages 500-501.

Sample MCQ #5

All are true regarding childhood nephrotic syndrome except?

AHypertension is relatively uncommon compared to nephritic syndrome
BMinimal change disease is common in age <10 years
CMassive proteinuria is often seen
DLow complement levels can occur
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Low complement levels can occur

Presence of low complement level indicates complement-mediated injury, which is a feature of glomerulonephritis like PSGN, but is not seen in Nephrotic Syndrome. Regarding other options Option A is True. Hypertension can sometimes occur in Nephrotic syndrome, but is more commonly a feature of acute nephritic syndrome Option B is True. Minimal Change Disease tends to occur in age 2-8 years Option C is True. Massive proteinuria is a hallmark of nephrotic syndrome Reference: OP Ghai 10 th Edition, Pages 500-501

Sample MCQ #6

A 10-year-old child presents with edema, anasarca and massive proteinuria. A diagnosis of minimal change disease was made. Which of the following statements is true about this condition?

ALight microscopy shows effacement of podocyte
BGood response to steroids
CMost common in adults
DNon-selective proteinuria
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Good response to steroids

The child in the case has Nephrotic syndrome characterised by selective proteinuria of albumin. It is more common in the paediatric population. Minimal change disease is the most common type in children. Light microscopy is normal in minimal change disease. Electron microscopy will show effacements of podocytes. It shows excellent response to oral prednisolone. Reference: OP Ghai 10 th Edition, Pages 500-501; Nelson textbook of Pediatrics, 20 th ed, pg. 2521

Sample MCQ #7

A child with nephrotic syndrome on high dose steroids comes in contact with an active case of chicken pox in neighborhood. He is not vaccinated to varicella. Which of the following treatment is advised for this child?

AGive 1 dose of varicella vaccine
BGive varicella zoster immunoglobulin
CGive oral acyclovir
DObserve and treat only if symptomatic
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Give varicella zoster immunoglobulin

Following close contact with varicella infection, give immunocompromised children on immunosuppressive agents varicella-zoster immune globulin if available ; immunize healthy household contacts with live vaccines to minimize the risk of transfer of infection to the immunosuppressed child, but avoid direct exposure of the child to gastrointestinal or respiratory secretions of vaccinated contacts for 3-6 weeks after vaccination. Live vaccines are contraindicated for this child. ( Option A) IV acyclovir is recommended by some literature. ( Option C) KEY TAKEAWAYS: Live vaccines are contraindicated in patients with nephrotic syndrome. Sample MCQ #8

All of the following can be used to distinguish bartter syndrome from gitelman syndrome, except?

ASerum potassium
BUrinary calcium levels
CSerum magnesium levels
DFacial dysmorphism
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Serum potassium

Both Bartter Syndrome & Gitelman Syndrome have hypokalemia and thus serum potassium levels can't be used to distinguish between them. Regarding other options Option B- Bartter Syndrome shows hypercalciuria while Gitelman Syndrome shows Hypocalciuria Option C- Serum Mg is normal in Bartter and low in Gitelman Option D- Facial dysmorphism occurs in some forms of Bartter syndrome but not in Gitelman syndrome Reference: OP Ghai 10 th Edition, Page 525; Nelson 21 st Edition, Chapter 549
