Sample MCQ #1

Handedness is established in a child at what age?

A1 year
B2 year
C3 year
D4 year
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. 3 year

Handedness: dominance of one hand over the other for performing tasks or activities. It is established in children at around 3 years. key takeaways : Bidextrous grasp -4 months Unidextrous grasp-6 months Palmar grasp-7 months Pincer grasp (immature)-9month Pincer grasp (mature)-12month Handedness-3 years Reference: Nelson textbook of paediatric

Sample MCQ #2

Ideal time gap between two live vaccines, if both can't be given together, should be?

A2 weeks
B4 weeks
C8 weeks
D12 weeks
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. 4 weeks

If two live vaccines cannot be given together, there should be a gap of at least 1 month (or 4 weeks) to ensure that the antibodies developed from the first vaccine do not interfere with the effectiveness of the second vaccine. Key takeaways:- -There is no need to keep a gap of 1 month between a live vaccine and a killed (inactivated) vaccine. They can be given at the same time or at different intervals -All live vaccines are contraindicated in pregnancy & immunocompromised except During outbreaks &travel (YF,OPV,Cholera) & bite case (rabies) -If two live vaccines are given together, they should be administered at different injection sites. ref :Nelson textbook of paediatric

Sample MCQ #4

A child with Kawasaki Disease underwent echocardiography and was found to have a giant aneurysm on Right Coronary Artery, for which he was hospitalized and managed. Which among the following is a cut-off to define a giant coronary artery aneurysm in a Kawasaki Disease patient?

ASize &ge;8 mm
BSize &ge;10 mm
CSize &ge; 12 mm
DSize &ge; 20 mm
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Size &ge;8 mm

In KD Patients, large or giant coronary artery aneurysms are responsible for most of the morbidity and mortality. Large or giant aneurysms have been defined by the 2017 American Heart Association (AHA) as having a z score &ge;10 or an absolute dimension of &ge;8 mm. Japanese Classification of Aneurysms in KD Small: &le;4 mm internal diameter [ID]) Medium (>4 to &le;8 mm ID) Giant (>8 mm ID). Specifically, large or giant aneurysms are associated with the greatest risk of thrombosis or stenosis, angina, and myocardial infarction. Rupture of a giant aneurysm is a rare complication that generally occurs in the 1st months after illness onset and may present as hemopericardium with tamponade. Axillary, popliteal, iliac, or other arteries may also become aneurysmal, but always in the setting of giant coronary aneurysms. Reference: Nelson 21 st Edition, Chapter 191

Sample MCQ #5

Which among the following pediatric condition is associated with mutations in ITPKC gene as well as anti-endothelial cell antibodies positivity?

AHenoch-Schoenlein Purpura
BKawasaki Disease
CSystemic-onset JIA
DChildhood SLE
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Kawasaki Disease

The likely condition is Kawasaki Disease. Exact cause is unknown. Considered to be a T-cell mediated disease More common in Japanese population Young children more affected, with peak age of 18-24 months Males are more commonly affected than females Anti-Endothelial cell antibodies are positive in many patients, although they are not implicated directly in the disease pathogenesis Genes implicated in Kawasaki disease include ITPKC, CASP-3. Sample MCQ #6

Which of the following is the most common complication associated with Kawasaki disease?

AMyocarditis
BCoronary artery aneurysm thrombosis
CCongestive cardiac failure due to mitral regurgitation
DSecondary sepsis
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Myocarditis

Most common complication associated with KD is Myocarditis . It might be asymptomatic or present with congestive cardiac failure. Most common cause of death is Thrombosis of Coronary artery aneurysm (CAA). Other complications include: Kawasaki disease shock syndrome Mitral regurgitation Asymptomatic pericardial effusion KEY TAKEAWAYS: Myocarditis, or inflammation of the heart muscle, is a recognized complication of Kawasaki disease. It occurs due to the widespread inflammation in blood vessels and heart tissue that characterizes the disease. Myocarditis in Kawasaki disease can lead to symptoms such as rapid or irregular heartbeat, chest pain, fatigue, and in severe cases, heart failure. Tags: Topic: KAWASAKI DISEASE Difficulty level: MUST KNOW PYQ: INICET MAY 2024 Reference: Nelson textbook of pediatrics, 21 st ed., pg. 1510

Sample MCQ #7

All of the following features can present in Kawasaki's disease except?

ABilateral bulbar conjunctivitis without exudates
BDesquamation of fingers
CAxillary lymphadenopathy
DPolymorphic skin rash primarily truncal
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Axillary lymphadenopathy

Kawasaki Disease (KD) is an acute febrile mucocutaneous lymph node syndrome mainly affecting infants and young children. Cervical Lymph Nodes, NOT axillary LN are involved in Kawasaki Disease Cervical LNs are most commonly unilateral, with at least one node >1.5 cm. Other options are correct. Reference : OP Ghai 10 th Ed, Page 658

Sample MCQ #8

The following symptoms may be suggestive of rheumatic disease except?

AFever: systemic juvenile rheumatoid arthritis
BArthralgia: dermatomyositis
CWeakness: juvenile idiopathic arthritis
DMalar rash with chest pain: systemic lupus erythematosus
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Weakness: juvenile idiopathic arthritis

Arthralgia is pain in the joints when trying to use them in active or passive movement. This occurs in SLE, JRA and JD, as well as in other, non-rheumatic, conditions. Weakness is mainly associated with dermatomyositis, but not a feature of JIA Malar rashes are seen with SLE and photosensitive dermatitis. Chest pain from pericarditis is seen with SLE and JRA. Reference: Nelson textbook of pediatrics, 1 st SA edition, pg. 1204
