Sample MCQ #1

While treating the child with severe acute malnutrition, which of the following factor is not one of the criteria for discharge of the child?

AWeight gain of atleast 5g/kg/day
BFree of infection
CCompleted immunization
DHb levels >9g/dl
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Hb levels >9g/dl

Discharge criteria for SAM child Child is alert and active , eating atleast 120-130 kcal/kg/day with consistent weight gain of atleast 5g/kg/day on exclusive oral feeding Child is receiving adequate micronutrients Child is free of infections Child has completed immunization appropriate for age The caretaker has been sensitized for home-based care . Tags : Topic : SEVERE ACUTE MALNUTRITION Difficulty level : MUST KNOW PYQ : NEET PG 2021; FMGE JAN 2022; AIIMS JULY 2020; FMGE JUNE 2022 Reference : Ghai's essential pediatrics, 9 th ed., pg. 107

Sample MCQ #2

Hepatitis B subunit vaccine is derived from?

AHBeAg
BHBcAg
CHBsAg
DHBV DNA
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. HBsAg

The current subunit Hepatitis B vaccine is derived from surface antigen, i.e., HBsAg. The vaccine is based upon DNA recombinant technology and uses Aluminium as an adjuvant. The hepatitis B vaccine is made by taking the part of the virus that makes surface protein ("surface protein gene") and putting it into yeast cells. The yeast cells then produce many copies of the protein that are subsequently used to make the vaccine. Reference: OP Ghai 10 th Edition, Page 198

Sample MCQ #3

A 6-year-old female child presented with non-blanchable, palpable petechial rash on lower limbs and buttocks, along with 3+ urinary protein and joint pains in knee and ankle. There was a preceding sore throat 2 weeks back, which was conservatively managed. What is the therapy of choice?

AConservative management
BSteroids
CIVIG
DPlasmapheresis
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Conservative management

The child was having a trigger factor in the form of sore throat & 2 weeks later, she presented with non-blanchable, palpable petechial rash on dependent parts, renal and joint involvement. This translates to a diagnosis of Henoch-Schoenlein Purpura, HSP- Also known as IgA Vasculitis. Management of HSP is Conservative- More than 90% improve in about 4 weeks. Steroids, e.g., Prednisolone are indicated in severe GIT involvement Reference: OP Ghai 10 th Edition, Page 661

Sample MCQ #4

All of the following are true regarding tubercular meningitis in a child, except?

AMalnutrition is a risk factor
BTotal duration of ATT is 12 months
CBasal cistern exudates are the radiological hallmark
DNo role of steroids in the therapy
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. No role of steroids in the therapy

Let us evaluate the options sequentially Option A is True: Risk factors for tubercular meningitis include young age, household contact, recent measles and protein-energy malnutrition. Option B is True: As per IAP-NTEP Guidelines and OP Ghai 10 th Edition, ATT comprises 2 months of Intensive therapy + 10 months of Maintenance Therapy. Total Duration of ATT is 12 months Option C is True. Basal cistern exudates are the CT scan hallmark of the disease. Option D is False. Intravenous Dexamethasone is followed by oral steroids for 6-8 weeks to prevent sequelae in most children. Reference: OP Ghai 10 th Edition, Page 586 - 587.

Sample MCQ #5

A 12-year-old boy with type 1 diabetes mellitus, is reviewed in the outpatient clinic. In spite of maintaining good control of his diabetes, his height has remained static for 9 months. He says his appetite is alright, but he has lost interest in sports. and he can't keep up with the other boys any more. He just stays at home and watches TV, but wants to be out playing football and getting back his energy. A full blood count and C-reactive protein are normal and his HbA1C is satisfactory. What is the most likely diagnosis?

AAnorexia nervosa
BGrowth hormone deficiency
CHypothyroidism
DInflammatory bowel disease
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Hypothyroidism

Children with type 1 diabetes mellitus are at increased risk of thyroid disease and should be screened annually. His static height and his lethargy would support this diagnosis. He is also at increased risk of coeliac disease and this should also be considered but is less likely as his appetite is good and his weight is satisfactory. Eating disorders can co-exist with diabetes mellitus but he does not have the clinical features of anorexia nervosa. (Option a) GH deficiency is not an autoimmune problem. Would not explain his lethargy. (Option b) Blood and mucus in the stool would be expected with ulcerative colitis and anorexia and malaise and abnormal full blood count and C-reactive protein would be expected with Crohn's disease. (Option d) TAGS: Topic: DIABETES Difficulty level: GOOD TO KNOW PYQ: NEET PG 2022 Reference: Nelson textbook of pediatrics, 21 st ed., pg. 3022

Sample MCQ #6

Which of the following symptoms is most suggestive of a diagnosis of IgE-mediated cow's milk protein allergy rather than non-IgE mediated?

APruritis
BLoose stool
COnset within 2 hours
DErythema
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Onset within 2 hours

IgE mediated cow's milk protein allergies have a rapid onset of up to 2 hours after ingestion, whereas non-IgE mediated are more delay and may not present until up to a week after ingestion. Both IgE and non-IgE mediated cow's milk protein allergy can cause pruritis, erythema, and lower respiratory tract infections including wheezing, coughing, and shortness of breath. Loose stools is more characteristic of non-IgE mediated allergies. Tags: Topic: MILK PROTEIN ALLERGY Difficulty level: MAY KNOW Reference: Nelson pediatric symptom-based diagnosis, pg no: 236

Sample MCQ #7

Which of the following is correct comparing breast milk with cow's milk?

ABreast milk contains less lactose
BBreast milk contains more protein
CBreast milk contains less calcium
DBreast milk contains more Vitamin K
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Breast milk contains less calcium

Absolute amount of calcium is less in breast milk than animal milk but since the calcium: phosphorous ratio is 2:1, the absorption is better. Key points to note: Vitamins deficient in breast milk: Vit K, Vit D, Vit B1. Mineral deficient in breast milk: Fluoride. Breast milk has more lactose but less fat, protein, and calcium than animal milk. Breast milk is rich in whey protein (better) compared to casein of animal milk. Breast milk also has more taurine and cysteine. Breast milk has more MUFA, PUFA and is especially rich in DHA, i.e. Docosahexaenoic Acid. Reference: Care of Newborn by Meharban Singh, 8 th Edition, Page Number-221.

Sample MCQ #8

A 6-year-old male child presented with a boil on the leg 2 weeks ago and is now presenting with cola coloured urine, 2+ urinary protein, facial edema and has BP of 120/80 mm of Hg. There is no other symptom or rash. What is the likely diagnosis?

AMinimal change disease
BIgA nephropathy
CPost streptococcal glomerulonephritis
DHSP Nephritis
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Post streptococcal glomerulonephritis

A school-going-aged boy developing acute nephritic syndrome 2 weeks after a boil (pyoderma) indicates a likely diagnosis of Post-Streptococcal Glomerulonephritis. Kindly note that BP of 120/80 is Hypertension in a 6-year-old boy. Regarding other options Option A- Minimal Change Disease usually does not have gross hematuria & tends to have massive proteinuria (3+ or 4+) Option B- IgA Nephropathy presents with acute nephritic syndrome within 2-6 days of preceding sore throat or pyoderma, not 2 weeks after. Option D- HSP nephritis will also have palpable purpura, joint involvement with GIT manifestations and since these are not mentioned, unlikely to be the answer here. Reference: OP Ghai 10 th Edition Pages 496-497
