Sample MCQ #1

A 3-year-old child presented with trouble walking to paediatrician. He is diagnosed with DMD (Duchenne muscular dystrophy) by polymerase chain reaction. Genetic analysis shows that the patient's gene for the muscle protein dystrophin on X chromosome contains a mutation. Mutation is found in its promoter region. Which would be the most likely defective process in this child?

ATranslation of dystrophin protein
BPost translational modification in dystrophin protein
CTranscription of dystrophin mRNA
DPost transcriptional modification in dystrophin mRNA
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Transcription of dystrophin mRNA

Option A & Option B: Translation is synthesis of protein from mRNA. Mutation in promoter regions does not lead to any problem during translation or post translational modifications. A deficiency of dystrophin mRNA will result in a deficiency in the production of the dystrophin protein. Option C: Mutations in the promoter typically prevent formation of the RNA polymerase II transcription complex, resulting in a decrease in the initiation of mRNA synthesis during transcription. Option D: Post transcriptional modifications like capping, splicing and tailing defects are not a consequence of promoter mutations. Reference: Harper&rsquo;s illustrated biochemistry 32 nd edition CH 36 PN 379

Sample MCQ #2

A 14 year old boy has fevers. He has had elbow , shoulder and hip and knee pains over the last few months. On examination, he has a temperature of 37.5&deg;C and palpable hepatosplenomegaly. Investigations show a CRP of 30 and negative ANA. What is the likely diagnosis?

AMarfan's syndrome
BEhler Danlos syndrome
CPerthes disease
DJuvenile chronic arthritis
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Juvenile chronic arthritis

Any arthritis that occurs in individuals Oligoarticular Polyarticular Rheumatoid factor positive Polyarticular Rheumatoid factor negative Systemic onset Psoriatic arthritis and Enthesitis related arthritis. Systemic onset JIA. It is characterised by Fever Evanescent rash Hepatosplenomegaly Generalized Lymphadenopathy Serositis They are prone for developing a life-threatening complication called Macrophage Activation Syndrome. They will be negative for ANA and RA factor. There will be anemia, raised ESR and CRP with transaminase elevation. Ferritin may be elevated and coagulation screen may be abnormal. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st ed, Ch:58, Pg: 394

Sample MCQ #3

The use of prostaglandin to keep the ductus arteriosus open is necessary in which of the following?

ATricuspid atresia
BAtrioventricular septal defect
CGerbode defect
DVentricular septal defect
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Tricuspid atresia

Ductus dependent congenital heart disease (require a patent ductus for maintaining pulmonary / systemic circulation) - Transposition of Great arteries - Tetralogy of Fallot - Tricuspid atresia (Ebstein anomaly) - Coarctation of aorta - Hypoplastic left heart syndrome - Pulmonary stenosis PGE1 is used to maintain an open ductus arteriosus in neonates with heart lesions that depend on it for survival. In Gerbode defect, there is a direct communication between right atrium and left ventricle. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine 21 st edition Page 2011-12
