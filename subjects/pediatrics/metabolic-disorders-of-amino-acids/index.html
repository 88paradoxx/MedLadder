Sample MCQ #1

A neonate with hypoglycemia is being managed using IV Dextrose infusion. He needs a bolus or hiking in Dextrose rate every time tapering is tried and more than 7 days have passed with the child being on IV dextrose infusion. What shall be done next?

AContinue same management
BRule out hyperinsulinemia
CDo TMS testing
DStart Glucagon empirically
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Rule out hyperinsulinemia

Recurrent/ resistant Hypoglycemia This condition should be considered when the infant fails to maintain normal BGL despite a GIR of 12 mg /kg/min or when stabilization is not achieved by 7 days of therapy. High levels of glucose infusion may be needed in these infants to achieve euglycemia. Hyperinsulinism must be excluded because it is the most common cause of persistent hypoglycemia. Hyperinsulinemic hypoglycemia can be congenital [due to mutations affecting the regulation of insulin secretion from the pancreas] or acquired [neonates with maternal diabetes, birth asphyxia, polycythemia, Rh incompatibility, and severe intrauterine growth restriction, Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome. etc.] Many patients may have underlying metabolic disorders or hypocortisolism Hydrocortisone is often considered in such cases. Reference: AIIMS Protocols In Neonatology, 3 rd Edition, Volume 2, Page 305

Sample MCQ #2

Which of the following condition you do not expect risk of developing cerebral palsy?

AA 2 year old child, born at 32 weeks gestation
BA 4 year old child, born at term and had birth asphyxia due following obstructed labor
CA 14 year old child had significant head injury at 13 years of age
DA 12 year old child, who developed kernicterus at 10th day of life
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. A 14 year old child had significant head injury at 13 years of age

Cerebral palsy (CP) is a group of permanent neurological disorders affecting movement, posture, and muscle tone, resulting from brain damage that occurs before, during, or shortly after birth. Risk factors of developing cerebral palsy Prenatal insult Preterm birth Birth asphyxia CNS infections Toxin exposure like bilirubin (Kernicterus) CNS hemorrhage Metabolic disorders KEY TAKEAWAYS: Cerebral palsy is a non-progressive motor disorder that affects a growing brain. Hence, insult after brain growth is nearly complete, does not cause Cerebral palsy. Tags: Topic: CEREBRAL PALSY Difficulty level: GOOD TO KNOW PYQ: NEET PG 2021 Reference: Nelson textbook of pediatrics, 21 st ed., pg. 3168

Sample MCQ #3

You are asked to see a 2-year-old child who has a 12-hour history of severe diarrhoea and vomiting. He is pale and lethargic with a HR 180/min, BP 80 mmHg systolic, and capillary refill time of 7 seconds. What type of shock are they likely to be in?

AObstructive
BHypovolemic
CCardiogenic
DDistributive
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Hypovolemic

This child is likely to be dehydrated from losing fluid through vomiting and diarrhoea, and may not have adequate oral intake to replace losses. This has resulted in severe dehydration, evident from the increased HR (due to compensation for a low blood volume in attempt to increase BP), increased cap refill time and low BP (indicating uncompensated shock), and pale and clammy appearance. KEY TAKEAWAYS: Hypovolemic shock occurs due to a significant reduction in intravascular volume, leading to inadequate tissue perfusion and oxygen delivery. This can result in cellular hypoxia, metabolic acidosis, and multi-organ dysfunction if untreated. Tags: Topic: SHOCK Difficulty level: MUST KNOW Reference: Manual of paediatric emergencies & critical care, 2 nd ed., pg. 158

Sample MCQ #4

Thirty-six hours after initiation of treatment with insulin for DKA, a 4-year-old girl's serum glucose level is 240 mEq/L. Further laboratory work reveals a bicarbonate level of 16 mEq/L. She is currently receiving 0.1 units/kg/h of regular insulin as well as normal saline. What is the next step in treatment?

AContinue current therapy
BContinue intravenous insulin therapy and change IVF to D5 0.45% normal saline
CStop intravenous insulin therapy and allow the patient to begin subcutaneous insulin administration
DStop intravenous insulin therapy and administer bicarbonate
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Continue intravenous insulin therapy and change IVF to D5 0.45% normal saline

Although the patient's serum glucose is decreasing, her metabolic acidosis has not yet resolved and will require continued intravenous insulin therapy and IV Fluid for treatment. The patient should not be converted to subcutaneous insulin until her glucose has normalized, her bicarbonate level is greater than 18 mEq/L, and her serum pH is greater than 7.3. Because of the risk of causing hypoglycemia with continued insulin therapy, dextrose should be added to her fluids. Bicarbonate therapy has not been shown to be effective in the treatment of metabolic acidosis associated with DKA and can actually be harmful. Reference: AIIMS PICU Protocols 2022, Pages 411-419

Sample MCQ #5

Vitamin B6 administration is helpful in which among the following metabolic disorders?

AHomocystinuria
BCAH
CPhenylketonuria
DAlkaptonuria
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Homocystinuria

Vitamin B6, i.e., Pyridoxine administration is helpful at 200 mg/day in many patients of Classic Homocystinuria. These patients are milder and have a better outcome than the non-responsive forms. However, methionine restriction to keep the plasma homocysteine levels low is the mainstay of therapy. Regarding other options Hydrocortisone is the therapy needed in CAH. Some forms may need additional drugs. Phenylketonuria needs dietary restriction of phenylalanine and a low-protein diet Alkaptonuria responds to Nitisinone to inhibit pigment production. Reference: OP Ghai 10 th Edition, Pages 684-685

Sample MCQ #6

After the delivery of an infant of diabetic mother, blood glucose of infant was 60mg/dl. Which other investigation does the sister expects that the physician would ask her to do?

ASerum potassium
BCBC
CSerum calcium
DSerum chloride
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Serum calcium

Hypoglycemia and Hypocalcemia are the two major metabolic complications seen in infants of diabetic mothers. Hypoglycemia develops in approximately 25-50% of infants of mothers with pregestational diabetes and 15-25% of infants of mothers with gestational diabetes, but only a small percentage of these infants become symptomatic. Apart from hypoglycemia, hypocalcemia is another common metabolic complication seen in these babies and results from the diminished production of parathormone in the 1 st week of postnatal life. Now in this question, since Serum glucose is normal, next logical thing to check is serum calcium. Reference: OP Ghai 10 th Edition, Page 182; Nelson 21 st Edition, Chapter 127

Sample MCQ #7

Recently approved drug indicated in Rett syndrome is?

ARituximab
BCabergoline
CTeriparatide
DTrofinetide
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Trofinetide

Treatment of Rett Syndrome Trofinetide is a new drug, approved for use in 2023 in children aged 2 years and above with Rett Syndrome. The drug is commercially marketed as Daybue. It is a novel synthetic analogue of the amino terminal tripeptide of IGF-1 The drug acts by decreasing neuroinflammation and supporting synaptic function tripeptide GPE. L-Carnitine & Melatonin may be helpful in a few cases. Reference: NIH Publications 2023

Sample MCQ #8

A 35-year-old woman gives birth to a female infant who is growth restricted, microcephalic, and has a congenital heart defect. She has had 1 prior miscarriage and reports that she has a 3-year-old son who is abnormally fair, with mental impairment. She also recalls being on a special diet as a child which she subsequently discontinued. Which of the following maternal conditions is most consistent with this history?

AFragile X syndrome
BAlkaptonuria
CType 1 diabetes mellitus
DPKU
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. PKU

Intrauterine exposure of an unborn child to elevated phenylalanine concentrations due to maternal PKU can disrupt embryo-fetal development. Effects include facial dysmorphism resembling fetal alcohol syndrome, microcephaly, mental impairment, and malformations especially of the heart. Hypopigmentation occurs owing to a lack of pigment formation. Musty or mousy urine odour is also produced. None of the other maternal conditions listed above would lead to the clinical features described in this vignette. Maternal diabetes leads to macrosomia not growth restriction. Reference: Nelson textbook of Pediatrics, 1 st SA ed., pg. 637
