Sample MCQ #1

A 45-year-old man with a history of depression presents with difficulties in ejaculation, particularly during intercourse. He has been taking an SSRI for the past six months. He reports that he feels physically capable of sexual activity but has trouble ejaculating. What is the most appropriate diagnosis for this patient?

ADelayed Ejaculation
BOrgasmic Anhedonia
CPremature Ejaculation
DErectile Disorder
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Delayed Ejaculation

Explanation: The patient has difficulty ejaculating during partnered sexual encounters, which has developed after the initiation of SSRI treatment, suggesting a medication-related issue. Delayed Ejaculation: Duration: Symptoms must persist for at least 6 months. Symptoms: Difficulty with ejaculations: Delayed ejaculation. Absent or reduced frequency of ejaculation. Marked delay or absence of orgasm. Symptoms Needed: At least one symptom must occur. Exclusions: Symptoms should not be attributed to: Relationship problems. Substance use (including prescribed medications). Another medical condition. Another psychiatric condition. Severity Specifiers: Mild: Causes mild distress. Moderate: Causes moderate distress. Severe: Causes severe distress.

Sample MCQ #2

A 30-year-old homeless woman with an 18-year history of daily intravenous heroin use comes into the community rehabilitation clinic asking to be "detoxed" in order to "kick my heroin habit" once and for all. While she is motivated for treatment, she currently is shivering, with rhinorrhoea, tearing, gagging and grabbing her abdomen in pain due to cramps. When questioned about her last use, she claims she used shortly before arriving, but that a peer gave her "something different to shoot up". Which of the following substances would be most likely to cause her current symptoms?

AAlprazolam
BBuprenorphine
CCocaine
DMethadone
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Buprenorphine

This patient is suffering from classic symptoms of opioid withdrawal. Buprenorphine is a partial agonist which, when given to a patient actively using another opiate, will precipitate withdrawal. Alprazolam and cocaine would not present with this clinical picture.( OPTION A & C ) Methadone and morphine are opioids which would cause further intoxication, not withdrawal.( OPTION D ) Reference: Kaplan and Sadock's Synopsis of Psychiatry, Eleventh edition, Chapter 29, Psychopharmacological treatment, Page no. 1002.

Sample MCQ #3

All of the following receptors are involved in chocoholics except:

ACannabinoid receptors
BSerotonin
CAmphetamine
DAdrenergic
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Adrenergic

Anandamide , an ingredient in chocolate, stimulates the same receptors as marijuana. Other compounds in chocolate include tryptophan , the precursor of serotonin and phenylalanine , an amphetamine-like substance, both of which improve mood. So-called chocoholics may be self-medicating because of a depressive diathesis. Reference: Kaplan and Sadock's Synopsis of Psychiatry, 11 th edition, Chapter 20.12, Other Substance Use and Addictive Disorders, Page number 690.

Sample MCQ #6

A diagnosis of polysubstance addiction is appropriate for all of the following substances of abuse except:

AOpium
BInhalant
CNicotine
DSedative
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Nicotine

A diagnosis of polysubstance dependence is appropriate if, for a period of at least 12 months, a person has repeatedly used substances from at least three categories, NOT including nicotine and caffeine . Reference: Kaplan and Sadock's Synopsis of Psychiatry, 11 th edition, Chapter 20.12, Other Substance Use and Addictive Disorders, Page number 690.

Sample MCQ #7

Which of the following medication is a recognized panicogen?

AFlumazenil
BPhenobarbitone
CPrazosin
DFomepizole
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Flumazenil

Panic-inducing substances (sometimes called panicogens) induce panic attacks in most patients with panic disorder . Respiratory Panicogens: Carbon Dioxide (5-35% mixtures) Sodium lactate Sodium bicarbonate Neurochemical Panicogens (act through specific neurotransmitter systems): Yohimbine mCPP and m-caroline drugs GABA-b receptor inverse agonists: Flumazenil Cholecystokinin Caffeine Isoproterenol is also a panicogen, however, its mechanism of action is poorly understood. Reference: Kaplan and Sadock's Synopsis of Psychiatry, 11th Edition, Page Number-392.

Sample MCQ #8

A 26-year-old man comes to the physician with the chief complaint of a depressed mood for the past 5 weeks. He has been feeling down, with decreased concentration, energy and interest in his usual hobbies. Six weeks prior to this office visit, he had been to the emergency room for an acute asthma attack and was started on prednisone. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

AMood disorder secondary to a general medical condition
BSubstance-induced mood disorder
CMajor depression
DAdjustment disorder
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Substance-induced mood disorder

According to DSM-V criteria, patients developing a mood disorder after using a substance(either illicit or prescribed) are diagnosed with a substance-induced mood disorder. The diagnosis of major depression cannot be made in the presence of either substance use or a general medical condition that might be the cause of the mood disorder. Prednisone is a common culprit in causing mood disorders ranging from depression to mania to psychosis. Reference: Kaplan and Sadock's Synopsis of Psychiatry, Eleventh edition, Chapter 8th, Mood disorders, Page no. 371.
