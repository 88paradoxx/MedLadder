Sample MCQ #1

Subcortical dementia is seen in all except ?

AParkinsonism
BAlzheimer's disease
CWilson's disease
DHuntingtons chorea
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Alzheimer's disease

Ans.B. Alzheimer's disease Subcortical dementia is seen in parkinsonism, Huntington's disease, wilson's disease, progressive supranuclear palsy, idiopathic basal ganglion calcification (Fahr's disease), thalamic lesions, multiple sclerosis, HIV associated dementia and multisYstem atroPhY. Alzheimer's disease causes cortical dementia.

Sample MCQ #2

True statements regarding Marchiafava-Bignami disease are all except?

AUsual symptoms are personality change or psychomotor impairment
BAcute alcohol intoxication is causative
CCharacterised by corpus callosal degeneration
DVitamin supplementation is helpful
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Acute alcohol intoxication is causative

Marchiafava-Bignami Disease should be considered in patients with chronic alcohol abuse or malnutrition who present with certain neurological symptoms. These symptoms include Dementia Altered mental status Personality change Spasticity Dysarthria Ataxia Gait abnormalities Seizures Other reported clinical findings include desynchronization of bilateral symmetric movements and unilateral signs of agraphia, apraxia, dyspraxia and anomia. Marchiafava-Bignami disease(MBD) is a very rare disorder of demyelination/necrosis of the corpus callosum and the near subcortical white matter that is especially predominant in ill-fed alcoholics. Vitamin supplementation is usually helpful. Reference: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK526007/

Sample MCQ #3

Which nutrient deficiency is responsible for cognitive decline in elderly individuals?

AVitamin C
BVitamin B12
CVitamin B6
DVitamin B1
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Vitamin B12

Explanation: Vitamin B12 deficiency is a common cause of cognitive impairment in the elderly. It can result in memory loss, confusion, and even dementia if left untreated. The deficiency disrupts myelin synthesis, leading to demyelination and neuronal dysfunction. Explanation for other options: Vitamin C (Option A): Deficiency causes scurvy, characterized by symptoms like bleeding gums and weakness, but it is not associated with cognitive decline. Vitamin B6 (Option C): Deficiency may cause irritability, depression, and confusion but is less frequently linked to significant cognitive impairment in older adults. Vitamin B1 (Thiamine) (Option D): Deficiency leads to Wernicke's encephalopathy and Korsakoff's syndrome, which are less common causes of cognitive impairment in the general elderly population compared to vitamin B12 deficiency.
