Sample MCQ #1

Which is/are true for Agoraphobia?

AFear that escape might be difficult in specific situations
BFear that panic-like symptoms or embarrassing symptoms may occur in specific situations
CNo clinically significant distress is present despite the symptoms
DBoth A) And B)
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Both A) And B)

In Agoraphobia, the individual fears or avoids specific situations because of thoughts that escape might be difficult or help might not be available in the event of developing panic-like symptoms or other incapacitating or embarrassing symptoms (e.g., fear of falling in elderly adults; fear of incontinence). The fear, anxiety or avoidance causes clinically significant distress or impairment in social, occupational or other important areas of functioning. Reference : Kaplan and Sadock's Synopsis of Psychiatry, Eleventh edition, Chapter 9.3, Agoraphobia, Page 399.

Sample MCQ #2

Which of the following is a common symptom of Agoraphobia?

AFear of heights
BFear of public speaking
CFear of being in crowded places
DFear of enclosed spaces
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Fear of being in crowded places

Explanation: Agarophobia refers to the fear of being in crowded places Individuals with Agoraphobia often avoid situations where escape might be difficult. Duration: Symptoms last for &ge;6 months. Key Symptoms: Fear/anxiety in specific situations (e.g., public transport, open spaces). Avoidance of situations due to fear of panic attacks. Fear and avoidance are disproportionate to potential threat. Exclusions: Not due to another medical or mental disorder.

Sample MCQ #3

Which is true for blood-injection-injury type of specific phobia?

AOne of the Most common type of specific phobia
BBradycardia and Hypotension are characteristically seen as compared to tachycardia seen in other phobias
CHas a high familial tendency
DAll of the above are true
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. All of the above are true

The blood-injection-injury type is the most common type of phobia found (Option A) . It is differentiated from the others in that bradycardia and hypotension often follow the initial tachycardia that is common to all phobias (Option B) . The blood-injection-injury type of specific phobia is particularly likely to affect many members and generations of a family (Option C) . In the special situation of blood-injection-injury phobia, some therapists recommend that patients tense their bodies and remain seated during the exposure to help avoid the possibility of fainting from a vasovagal reaction to the phobic stimulation. Reference : Kaplan and Sadock's Synopsis of Psychiatry, Eleventh edition, Chapter 9.4, Specific Phobia, Page 402.

Sample MCQ #4

DSM-V includes all of these types of specific phobia based on the phobic stimulus except:

ABlood-injection-injury
BAnimal
CNatural environment
DSocial situations
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Social situations

The DSM-5 includes distinctive types of specific phobia: Animal type (Option B) . Natural environment type(e.g., storms) (Option C) . Blood-injection injury type(e.g., needles) (Option A) . Situational type(e.g., cars, elevators, planes) Other type(for specific phobias that do not fit into the previous four types). Phobia for social situations is termed as Social phobia , which is separate entity from Specific Phobia Reference : Kaplan and Sadock's Synopsis of Psychiatry, Eleventh edition, Chapter 9.4, Specific Phobia, Page 402.

Sample MCQ #5

A person was found wandering on the streets after one month of running away from his home. His family members saw him and brought him for psychiatric consultation. Wandering behavior is seen in psychiatric patients in all of the following conditions, except:

ADissociative fugue
BPanic attack
CSchizophrenia
DMania
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Panic attack

Wandering behavior is seen during a variety of conditions like dissociative fugue , substance intoxication, delirium, dementia, manic phase of bipolar disorder, schizophrenia. (Option A, C & D) It is less likely during panic attack. Reference: Kaplan and Sadock's Synopsis of Psychiatry, 11th edition, Chapter 12, Dissociative disorders, Page number 457.

Sample MCQ #6

Which of the following drugs is a dopamine-2(D2) antagonist?

AAmitriptyline
BBuspirone
CDiazepam
DFluoxetine
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Buspirone

Buspirone is an anxiolytic medication with 5-hydroxytryptamine(5-HT1A) partial agonist activity. It is a potent D2-receptor antagonist (OPTION B IS CORRECT) . It is mainly used for treatment of generalised anxiety disorder and other anxiety disorders. Even though it is an antianxiety medication, it is not related to benzodiazepines or barbiturates. It is not helpful for treating benzodiazepine withdrawal symptoms. These patients will need benzodiazepines to relieve their symptoms before starting buspirone. Reference: Synopsis of Psychiatry, Eleventh edition, Chapter 29th, Page no. 957.

Sample MCQ #7

When starting an SSRI, such as fluoxetine, in an adolescent patient with separation anxiety disorder, the Food and Drug Administration(FDA) recommends the clinician monitor closely for which of the following?

AHypovolemia
BHypertension
CAnorexia
DSuicidal thoughts
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Suicidal thoughts

The FDA recently placed a black box warning for the use of antidepressants in children and adolescents. This warning reminds clinicians of some evidence indicating a possible increased incidence of suicidal thoughts among adolescents using antidepressants-particularly SSRIs. Reference : Kaplan and Sadock's Synopsis of Psychiatry, Eleventh edition, Chapter 31.13, Anxiety Disorders of Infancy, Childhood and Adolescence, Page no. 1254.

Sample MCQ #8

A 32-year-old woman with agoraphobia was assessed for behavior therapy. Which of the following is she fearful of?

AFamily history
BCrowds
CHeights
DRecent life events
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Crowds

Agoraphobia is anxiety associated with places or situations either from which escape may be difficult or in which help may not be available in the event of a panic attack. It means literally fear of the crowded place . It is strongly associated with panic. ( OPTION B ) Reference: Synopsis of Psychiatry, eleventh edition, chapter 9, page no. 395
