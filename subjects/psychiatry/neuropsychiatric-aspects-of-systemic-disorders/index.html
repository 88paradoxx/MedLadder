About Neuropsychiatric Aspects of Systemic Disorders MCQs

Neuropsychiatric Aspects of Systemic Disorders MCQs ask what systemic disease looks like through a psychiatric lens — lupus psychosis and seizures, hypothyroid depression, Wilson disease, Huntington disease and the depression of multiple sclerosis and Cushing syndrome — high-yield NEET PG revision.

Sample Neuropsychiatric Aspects of Systemic Disorders MCQs with answers

Sample MCQ #1

The most characteristic neuropsychiatric manifestations of systemic lupus erythematosus include?

ASeizures and psychosis
BPure motor weakness
CVisual hallucinations only
DIsolated memory loss
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Seizures and psychosis

Neuropsychiatric lupus commonly presents with seizures, psychosis, cognitive dysfunction, mood disorders and stroke, through autoantibody-mediated injury and thrombotic mechanisms. It is a diagnosis of exclusion requiring other causes to be ruled out.

Sample MCQ #2

A patient with untreated hypothyroidism is most likely to present psychiatrically with?

AMania
BDepression and cognitive slowing
CPanic attacks
DHyperactivity
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Depression and cognitive slowing

Hypothyroidism classically causes depression, fatigue, psychomotor slowing and cognitive impairment, occasionally severe enough to be called myxoedema madness. Hyperthyroidism, by contrast, tends toward anxiety, agitation and tremor.

Sample MCQ #3

A young adult with personality change, depression, dysarthria and Kayser-Fleischer rings most likely has?

AHuntington disease
BWilson disease
CMultiple sclerosis
DNeurosyphilis
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Wilson disease

Wilson disease is caused by ATP7B mutation with copper accumulation in liver, brain and cornea. Psychiatric symptoms such as personality change and depression often accompany movement disorder and dysarthria; Kayser-Fleischer rings and low ceruloplasmin support the diagnosis.

Sample MCQ #4

Psychiatric symptoms in Huntington disease most often appear in relation to chorea as follows?

AOnly after chorea is severe
BOften precede motor symptoms
CNever occur
DOnly in juvenile cases
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Often precede motor symptoms

Depression, irritability and apathy frequently precede chorea by years in Huntington disease, an autosomal dominant disorder caused by CAG expansion in the HTT gene. Psychiatric symptoms are a major cause of disability in the disease.

Sample MCQ #5

The most common psychiatric manifestation of multiple sclerosis is?

AMania
BDepression
CSchizophrenia
DObsessive-compulsive disorder
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Depression

Depression affects around half of multiple sclerosis patients over the disease course, driven by lesion burden, inflammation and psychosocial factors. Euphoria (euphoria sclerotica) is a recognised but much less common feature.

Sample MCQ #6

The most common psychiatric feature of Cushing syndrome is?

ASchizophrenia
BDepression
CDelirium
DAnorexia nervosa
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Depression

Hypercortisolism most often causes depression, with irritability, insomnia and cognitive slowing. Mania or psychosis can also occur, particularly with higher cortisol levels, and usually improve when cortisol is normalised.

Sample MCQ #7

Acute intermittent porphyria characteristically presents with abdominal pain plus which neuropsychiatric features?

AConfusion, hallucinations and seizures
BPure amnesia
CCatatonia only
DVisual field defects
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Confusion, hallucinations and seizures

Acute intermittent porphyria attacks combine severe abdominal pain, autonomic instability and neuropsychiatric symptoms including confusion, hallucinations, seizures and peripheral neuropathy, often precipitated by drugs, fasting or alcohol.

Sample MCQ #8

General paresis of the insane (neurosyphilis) classically presents with?

ADementia with grandiose delusions
BIsolated tremor
CPure sensory loss
DHemiballismus
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Dementia with grandiose delusions

General paresis is a late parenchymatous form of neurosyphilis: progressive dementia with grandiose or megalomaniac delusions, dysarthria, tremor and Argyll Robertson pupils. It develops years after untreated syphilis infection.
