Sample MCQ #1

When deprived of food for more than 6 hours, a group of participants in an experiment would be more likely to interpret ambiguous pictures presented to them as something to do with food. This concept is the basis for:

AThematic Apperception Test
BChildren Apperception Test
CRorschach Inkblot test
DAll of the above
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. All of the above

Apperception means awareness of meaning of a stimulus as modified by one&rsquo;s own experiences , thoughts and emotions . For example, when hungry, people would more likely interpret ambiguous stimuli as something related to food. This is the basis for projective personality tests like Rorschach inkblot test Thematic Apperception test Children Apperception test. Reference : Morgan and King's Introduction to Psychology, 7th edition, Chapter 13 Psychological Assessment and Testing, Page number 550.

Sample MCQ #2

Identify the hallucinogens whether they are natural or synthetic: 1-Psilocybin 2-Phencyclidine 3-LSD 4-Mescaline 5-Salvia Divinorum

A1,2,3-natural; 4,5-synthetic
B1,2-natural; 3,4,5-synthetic
C1,4,5-natural; 2,3-synthetic
D2,3-natural; 1,4,5-synthetic
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. 1,4,5-natural; 2,3-synthetic

Hallucinogens derived from natural source are psilocybin mescaline, harmine, harmaline, ayahuasca, salvia divinorum. While synthetic hallucinogens are ketamine, phencyclidine, LSD. Reference: Kaplan and Sadock's Synopsis of Psychiatry, 11 th edition, Chapter 20.5, Hallucinogen-related disorders, Page number 648.

Sample MCQ #4

A 40-year-old man with alcohol dependence saw his wife talking to her boss at work. Since then he believes that she was having an affair with her boss that involved a sexual relationship, despite a lack of evidence. Which of the following is most likely to describe his belief?

ACapgras' delusion
BCotard's syndrome
CDe Clerambault delusion
DOthello's syndrome
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Othello's syndrome

Othello&rsquo;s syndrome is also known as morbid jealousy in which an individual believes that his or her spouse or partner is unfaithful. De Clerambault syndrome, also known as erotomania, is a condition in which that patient believes that an exalted or well-known person is in love with him or her. (Option C) In Cotard&rsquo;s syndrome, the person believes that he or she is dead or some part of his or her body is rotten or doesn&rsquo;t exist. (Option B) In Capgras&rsquo; syndrome, the patient believes that a person who is close has been replaced by an exact double. (Option A) Reference: PGNIP2, Cijo Alex, 2 nd Edition, Chapter 3, page no. 27

Sample MCQ #5

On asking the name of the city that he lives in, an elderly patient having right hemispheric stroke replied, 'Jodhpur'. Next, on asking him the current year, he replied the same answer 'Jodhpur'. In fact, on asking any further question, he kept on giving the same reply. Such a formal thought disorder is known as ___________.

AVerbigeration
BPerseveration
CVerbal Stereotypy
DEcholalia
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Perseveration

Perseveration is a formal thought disorder in which there is repetition of a particular response , such as a word, phrase, or gesture , despite the absence or cessation of a stimulus , usually caused by brain injury or other organic disorder rather than a psychopathology It is clear that this is not a problem of volition, which helps differentiate it from verbal stereotypy, which is a frequent spontaneous repetition of a word or phrase that is not in any way related to the current situation In verbal stereotypy , the same word or phrase is used regardless of the situation, whereas in perseveration a word, phrase or idea persists beyond the point at which it is relevant Echolalia refers to meaningless repetition of another person's spoken words Reference: Fish's Clinical Psychopathology, third edition, Chapter 4 Disorders of Thought and Speech, Page no. 45

Sample MCQ #6

A 30-year-old man comes to the psychiatrist for the evaluation of a depressed mood. He states that at least since his mid-20s he has felt depressed. He notes poor self-esteem and low energy and feels hopeless about his situation, though he denies suicidal ideation. He states he does not use drugs or alcohol and has no medical problems. His last physical examination by his physician 1 month ago was entirely normal. Which of the following treatment options should be tried first?

AECT
BHospitalization
CPsychoanalysis
DVenlafaxine
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Venlafaxine

This patient has a dysthymic disorder. There are a number of studies that show positive responses to antidepressants with these patients. Venlafaxine and bupropion are generally believed to be the treatments of choice for dysthymic disorder , though there is a subgroup of patients that will respond to the MAOIs as well. Reference : Kaplan and Sadock's Synopsis of Psychiatry, Eleventh Edition, Chapter 8th, Mood disorders, Page no. 381.

Sample MCQ #7

A 48-year-old woman is brought to the emergency department. She is unresponsive to questions, stumbles around the room and is agitated. On physical examination, you notice that she smells of alcohol and she is not cooperative during the remainder of the examination. Administration of what medicine would be the most appropriate initial treatment?

AAntipsychotic
BBenzodiazepine
CDisulfiram
DThiamine
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Thiamine

The most appropriate treatment is the immediate administration of thiamine. This patient presents with Wernicke encephalopathy, characterized by the triad of delirium, ataxia and ophthalmoplegia Thiamine must be given prior to glucose in patients suspected of having this disorder. Reference: Kaplan and Sadock's Synopsis of Psychiatry, Eleventh edition, Chapter 20.2, Alcohol-related disorders, Page no. 637.

CSchizophrenia
DImpulsive control disorder

✓ Correct Answer: B. Mania

Hyperactivity, over sexual and drug indulgence, decreased need for sleep. All signs and symptoms are relevant to mania. Reference: Kaplan and Saddocks Synopsis of Psychiaitry, 11th Edition, Chapter 8, Page No.: 358
