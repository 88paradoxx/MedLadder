Sample MCQ #1

Under hypnosis or during psychotherapy, a patient may recover a memory of a painful experience or conflict-particularly of sexual or physical abuse, that is etiologically significant. This is known as:

AFalse memory syndrome
BRecovered memory syndrome
CNarcoanalysis
DAll of the above
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Recovered memory syndrome

Under hypnosis or during psychotherapy , a patient may recover a memory of a painful experience or conflict-particularly of sexual or physical abuse - that is etiologically significant. When the repressed material is brought back to consciousness, the person not only may recall the experience but may relive it, accompanied by the appropriate affective response (a process called abreaction). If the event recalled never really happened but the person believes it to be true and reacts accordingly, it is known as false memory syndrome. (Option A) Reference: Kaplan and Sadock's Synopsis of Psychiatry, 11th edition, Chapter 12, Dissociative disorders, Page number 463.

Sample MCQ #2

A 35-year-old businessman enjoys dressing up in his wife's clothes. He says he does so to experience what it feels like to be a woman and it does not cause him sexual arousal. He has no desire to be a woman and he is very much comfortable with his genital organs. What is the correct term to describe his behavior?

ATranssexualism
BDual-role transvestism
CFetishism
DFetishistic transvestism
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Dual-role transvestism

Dual-role transvestism: Patient wears the dress of opposite sex to enjoy the temporary feeling of belonging to other sex. Unlike trans sexualism, there is no desire of permanent sex change. There is no sexual arousal associated with cross dressing. Reference: Kaplan and Saddocks Synopsis of Psychiaitry, 11th Edition, Chapter 17, Page No.: 597

Sample MCQ #3

According to Freud, during psychosexual development a boy focuses his love on his mother. This can reach a stage where a boy becomes jealous of his father and wants to kill him. However, he cannot do this for fear of castration and so represses these urges as he moves to the next stage of development. Which of the following describes the boy's psychosocial development?

AAnal phase
BGenital phase
CLatency phase
DOedipus complex
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Oedipus complex

According to Freud there are five stages of psychosexual development. The approximate age range of the stages is characterised by: Oral phase : 1 year of fixation on mouth and lips &ndash; relation to sucking, biting and mouthing. Anal phase : 1&ndash;3 years of fixation on anal cavity &ndash; relation to toilet training. Phallic phase : 3&ndash;5/6 years of fixation and sensitivity to genitals. The Oedipus complex occurs only in this stage. Latency phase : 5/6 years of puberty &ndash; sexual preoccupations of earlier years repressed. Genital phase : puberty/maturity &ndash; new demands and sexual desires. This was described by Freud and occurs during the phallic stage of psychosexual development . The Oedipus complex derives its name from a classical Greek tragedy about Oedipus Rex who killed his father. Reference: Synopsis of Psychiatry, eleventh edition, chapter 4, page no. 157

Sample MCQ #5

Drug-facilitated rape is a type of acquaintance sexual assault done by allegedly placing some drugs in the beverage of the victim surreptitiously. The sexual encounter is not remembered by the victim due to anterograde amnesia. Which drug out of the following is a 'date rape drug':

ANitrate inhalers
BCocaine
CRohypnol
DHeroin
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Rohypnol

Gamma-Hydroxy-Butyrate, ketamine, and Rohypnol (Flunitrazepam) have been called 'date rape drugs' because they produce disorienting and sedating effects and often users cannot recall what occurred during all or part of an episode under the influence of the drug. Hence, it is alleged that these drugs might be surreptitiously placed in a beverage or a person might be convinced to take the drug and then not recall clearly what occurred after ingestion. Reference: Kaplan and Sadock's Synopsis of Psychiatry, 11 th edition, Chapter number 20.9, Stimulant-related disorders, Page number 679.
