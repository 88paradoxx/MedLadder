Sample Mixed / Miscellaneous Topics MCQs with answers

Sample MCQ #1

Common causes for lower back pain include all of the following, except

ALumbosacral strain
BMyofascial syndromes
CFibromyalgia syndrome
DDegenerative disk disease
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Fibromyalgia syndrome

Chronic lower back pain is one of the top reasons for physician office visits and also one of the greatest reasons for work absence. Lumbosacral strain, degenerative disk disease, and myofascial syndromes are the most common causes, and fibromyalgia is not typically associated with a diagnosis of lower back pain. Reference: lippincott's anaesthesia review pg 212.

Sample MCQ #2

Hyperpathia is the simultaneous presence of which of the following?

AHyperesthesia, allodynia and hyperalgesia
BHyperesthesia, analgesia and hyperalgesia
CAnaesthesia dolorosa, dysesthesia and hyperalgesia
DNeuralgia, paresthesia and radiculopathy
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Hyperesthesia, allodynia and hyperalgesia

Presence of hyperesthesia, allodynia, and hyperalgesia usually associated with overreaction, and persistence of the sensation after the stimulus is known as hyperpathia. Reference: Morgan and Mikhail's clinical anesthesiology 6th edition pg 1050 table 47-1.

Sample MCQ #3

A trauma patient presenting with acute pain can be expected to have all of the following systemic responses to pain except:

AHypertension and tachycardia
BIncreased work of breathing
CUrinary retention
DIncreased peristalsis
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Increased peristalsis

Enhanced sympathetic tone increases sphincter tone and decreases intestinal and urinary bladder motility, promoting ileus and urinary retention and not increased peristalsis. Cardiovascular effects of acute pain often include hypertension, tachycardia, enhanced myocardial irritability, and increased systemic vascular resistance. An increase in total body oxygen consumption and carbon dioxide production due to pain, necessitates a concomitant increase in minute ventilation which in turn increases the work of breathing. Reference: Morgan and Mikhail's clinical anesthesiology 6th edition pg 1062.
