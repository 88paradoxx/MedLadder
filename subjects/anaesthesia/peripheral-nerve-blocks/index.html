Sample MCQ #1

A 30-year-old male underwent ACL reconstruction of the left knee under general anaesthesia and adductor canal block. After the surgery, the Inhalational agents were stopped and reversal was giving with Neostigmine (with glycopyrrolate). He did not regain consciousness or spontaneous efforts of breathing. Given that he received high dose of Morphine for premedication, next line of management will include the following,

AIntravenous Naloxone
BFlumazenil
CPhysostigmine
DOndansetron
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Flumazenil

When the patient fails to regain consciousness within an expected period of time after general anesthesia, the most frequent cause is residual drug effect. Delayed emergence may occur as a result of absolute or relative drug overdose. The effects of preoperative sleep deprivation or drug ingestion (alcohol, sedatives) can be additive to those of anesthetic agents in producing prolonged emergence. Intravenous naloxone and flumazenil will readily reverse the effects of an opioid and benzodiazepine, respectively. Intravenous physostigmine may partially reverse the effect of other agents. Reference : Morgan and Mikhail's clinical anesthesiology, page 1289

A 30-year-old man presents for open reduction and internal fixation of a left radius fracture as an outpatient. He has smoked 1 pack of cigarettes per day for 12 years. The plan is general anesthesia with endotracheal intubation. Which one of the following agents is most appropriate for him?

AIsoflurane
BDesflurane
CSevoflurane
DNitrous oxide

✓ Correct Answer: C. Sevoflurane

Although all agents could be used, sevoflurane would be the preferred choice. Sevoflurane could be used in combination with nitrous oxide Wrong Answers: Option A : Isoflurane could also be used, but because of its high blood: gas solubility it may delay emergence, which is undesirable in outpatients Option B : Desflurane has a lower blood: gas solubility that would provide rapid awakening , but desflurane is irritating to inhale and can cause bronchoconstriction in smokers Reference : Drugs in Anesthesia and Intensive Care, Edward Scarth, Susan Smith 5th edition

Sample MCQ #3

Local infiltration of lignocaine and other local anesthetic agents are less reliable for ankle block of an infected diabetic foot. This is because

AIncreased extracellular pH
BIncreased nonionized lidocaine concentration
CDecreased extracellular pH
DIncreased intracellular pH
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Decreased extracellular pH

Since local anesthetics are weak bases, they exist largely in the ionic form, making it difficult for them to cross the cell membrane, thus delaying their local anesthetic action. Further they become ionized when the local tissue is infected and the local pH is acidic. Their action becomes unreliable. Addition of sodium bicarbonate promotes nonionized fraction of local anesthetic, promoting more rapid onset of its action. Reference: Stoeltings pharmacology and physiology in anesthetic practice 5th edition pg. 291

Sample MCQ #5

A 25-year-old, otherwise healthy patient, received a right sided interscalene block for a shoulder surgery. 3 hours after the surgery, you are called to the post anaesthesia care unit as patient is having drooping of the right eyelid, redness of the conjunctiva, and pupillary constriction. The most likely diagnosis is:

ASpinal anaesthesia
BSubdural injection of local anaesthetic
CHorner's syndrome
DCerebrovascular accident
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Horner's syndrome

These signs described above are specific to Horner Syndrome. Horner syndrome (miosis, ptosis, and anhydrosis) frequently results from proximal tracking of local anesthetic and blockade of sympathetic fibers to the cervicothoracic ganglion. Other site-specific risks include vertebral artery injection (suspect if immediate seizure activity is observed), spinal or epidural injection, and pneumothorax. In patients where a CVA may also be within the differential diagnosis, a thorough history and neural exam should always be included. Reference: Morgan and Mikhail's clinical anesthesiology 6th edition pg 1007.

Sample MCQ #6

Anatomical location of the musculocutaneous nerve in the upper arm is most frequently found within which of the following muscles?

ATriceps brachii
BDeltoid
CCoracobrachialis
DBrachialis
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Coracobrachialis

Although some anatomical variation can be found with the brachial plexus at the level of the axilla, the musculocutaneous nerve is most commonly positioned within the coracobrachialis muscle or between the bellies of the biceps and coracobrachialis muscles. Reference: Morgan and Mikhail's clinical anesthesiology 6th edition pg 1019.

Sample MCQ #7

While performing a peripheral nerve block in an awake patient, access and/or use of all of the following should be considered mandatory, except

AAdminister supplemental oxygen
BApply standard ASA monitors
CAccess to resuscitation medications and equipment
DImmediate access to a mechanical ventilator
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Immediate access to a mechanical ventilator

Immediate access to a functioning anesthesia ventilator is not always necessary while administering regional anaesthesia. Regional anesthesia should be administered in a monitored location where standard ASA monitors. Supplemental oxygen along with resuscitative medications and equipment should be readily accessible and immediately available. Reference: Morgan and Mikhail's clinical anesthesiology 6th edition pg 999.

Sample MCQ #8

After administering which of the following nerve blocks would you expect the highest blood levels of local anaesthetic per local anaesthetic dose injected?

AInterscalene nerve block
BPopliteal sciatic nerve block
CIntercostal nerve block
DAxillary nerve block
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Intercostal nerve block

The intercostal block has one of the highest complication rates of any peripheral nerve block due to the close proximity of the intercostal artery and vein (intravascular local anesthetic injection), as well as underlying pleura (pneumothorax). Intercostal blocks result in the highest blood levels of local anesthetic per local anesthetic dose injected of any nerve block procedure, and if multiple blocks will be performed care must be taken to avoid toxic levels of local anesthetic. Reference: Morgan and Mikhail's clinical anesthesiology 6th edition pg 1040.
