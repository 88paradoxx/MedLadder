Sample Preoperative Evaluation MCQs with answers

Sample MCQ #1

A 50-year-old patient is being planned for hernia repair. He is a known smoker with history of 20 pack years. His investigations are normal. How long should he be advised to stop smoking prior to surgery?

A24 hours
B1 week
C2 weeks
D6 weeks
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. 6 weeks

Stopping smoking is thought to be beneficial preoperatively, in order to minimize its acute adverse effects. Effects of carbon monoxide and nicotine are significantly reduced after 12-24 h abstinence; up to 6-8 weeks is thought to be necessary for restoration of ciliary and immunological activity. Reference: FRCA A TO Z, page no 530

Sample MCQ #2

A 38-year-old woman with a history of diverticulosis is scheduled for an exploratory laparotomy for lysis of adhesions. Which of the following is the best way of maintaining core body temperature during the initial hour of general endotracheal anesthetic?

AProviding warm and humidified inspired gases
BIncreasing ambient temperature
CAdministration of warm intravenous fluids
DUse of warm irrigating fluids
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Increasing ambient temperature

The initial reduction in core temperature during general anesthesia is caused by redistribution of heat from the core to the periphery, which can be attenuated by increasing ambient temperature to minimize the gradient Reference: lippincott's anesthesia review chapter 14.

Sample MCQ #3

You are planning an elective cesarean section for a patient with fetal breech. The patient falls under:

AASA I
BASA II
CASA III
DASA IV
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. ASA II

Explanation: ASA II: This classification is assigned to a patient with mild systemic disease . In this case, an uncomplicated pregnancy is considered a physiological state that alters the woman's baseline health but does not constitute a severe systemic disease. Pregnancy leads to changes such as increased cardiac output, altered respiratory mechanics, and changes in blood volume, which necessitate careful monitoring but generally does not increase the risk category beyond ASA II in the absence of other complications. Key Takeaways: ASA II reflects a healthy pregnan t patient with no significant comorbidities. Understanding the ASA classification helps assess the risk during anaesthesia and surgery. Reference : https://www.asahq.org/standards-and-practice-parameters/statement-on-asa-physical-status-classification-system
