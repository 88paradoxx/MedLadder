Sample MCQ #1

Levobupivacaine is ___________ compared to bupivacaine.

ALess cardiotoxic
BLess potent
CHas longer duration of action
DAll of the above
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. All of the above

As previously discussed, this S (&ndash;) enantiomer of bupivacaine is somewhat less cardiotoxic than the racemic mixture. It is also less potent, and tends to have a longer duration of action, though the magnitude of these effects is too small to have any substantial clinical significance. Interestingly, recent work with lipid resuscitation suggests a potential advantage of levobupivacaine over ropivacaine, as the former is more effectively sequestered into a so-called lipid sink, implying greater ability to reverse toxic effects should they occur. AHigher incidence of nerve injury
BHigher incidence of TNS(transient neurogenic symptoms)

✓ Correct Answer: B. Higher incidence of TNS(transient neurogenic symptoms)

Aside from the issue of a high incidence of TNS with spinal administration, lidocaine has had an excellent record as an intermediate duration anaesthetic, and remains the reference standard against which most anaesthetics are compared. Sample MCQ #3

An anesthetist injected bupivacaine in a patient for axillary nerve block. Later, the patient developed restlessness, agitation and cardiovascular collapse. Next best step in the management would be?

ACardiac resuscitation + dopamine
BCardiac resuscitation + dantrolene
CCardiac resuscitation + sodium bicarbonate
DCardiac resuscitation + 20% intralipid
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Cardiac resuscitation + 20% intralipid

Bupivacaine is the most cardiotoxic local anaesthetic At toxic doses, local anaesthetics can result in CNS (convulsions) or CVS (hypotension, bradycardia, arrhythmias) symptoms. Diazepam is used to treat convulsions. If not responding, thiopentone may be used. Arrhythmias should be promptly treated using bretylium, amiodarone, disopyramide, magnesium sulphate, esmolol or sotalol. Lignocaine should be avoided as antiarrhythmic because it canexacerbate the CNS toxicity. In refractory arrhythmias, intravenous lipid emulsion (like intralipid)has been found to be extremely useful. Bupivacaine induced cardiotoxicity is enhanced by acidosis, hypercarbia and hypoxemia.

Sample MCQ #4

Which is the TRUE for Ropivacaine?

AIts low cardiac toxicity has led to use for high volume peripheral blocks
BIts high cardiac toxicity has led to decrease use for high volume peripheral blocks
CIt is not used for epidural infusions for control of labour
DAll of the above
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Its low cardiac toxicity has led to use for high volume peripheral blocks

Ropivacaine is an S (&ndash;) enantiomer in a homologous series that includes bupivacaine and mepivacaine, distinguished by its chirality, and the propyl group off the piperidine ring ( Table 26&ndash;1). Its perceived reduced cardiotoxicity has led to widespread use for high-volume peripheral blocks. It is also a popular choice for epidural infusions for control of labor and postoperative pain. Although there is some evidence to suggest that ropivacaine might produce a more favourable differential block than bupivacaine, the lack of equivalent clinical potency adds complexity to such comparisons. Reference LANG&rsquo; Basic & clinical pharmacology by Bertram G. Katzung, Susen B. Masters and Anthony J. Trevor 12 th edition. Chapter 26- Local Anaesthetics by Dr. Kenneth Drasner,MD. Page no. 461

Sample MCQ #5

Hydrolysis by plasma cholinesterase is the primary elimination route of all the following drugs EXCEPT

ASuccinylcholine
BMivacurium
CRemifentanil
DProcaine
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Remifentanil

Plasma cholinesterase (PChE), also known as pseudocholinesterase, butyrylcholinesterase, or serum cholinesterase, is a serine hydrolase from the family of human cholinesterase&rsquo;s. Anesthetic drugs metabolized by PChE include succinylcholine, mivacurium, and all ester local anesthetics (e.g., cocaine, procaine, and chloroprocaine). PChE is produced in the liver, and its plasma half-life is approximately 8 to 12 days. Advanced age, pregnancy, chronic kidney failure, or specific gene variations may result in reduced PChE activity. Reference: FRCA A TO Z , page no 136

ADopamine
BAdrenaline
CNeostigmine
DGlycopyrrolate

✓ Correct Answer: D. Glycopyrrolate

ALidocaine is converted into a toxic metabolite due to its longer stay in liver
BLidocaine concentration are initially higher in relatively well perfused tissues such as brain and heart
CThere is a sudden out-burst of release of adrenaline, noradrenaline and dopamine in brain and heart
DHistamine receptors in brain and heart gets suddenly activated in circulatory failure

✓ Correct Answer: B. Lidocaine concentration are initially higher in relatively well perfused tissues such as brain and heart

CNS or CVS systemic toxicity may result when standard lidocaine dose is administered to patient with circulatory failure because its concentration is initially higher in relatively well perfused tissue such as brain & heart And reduced cardiac output (in CHF) slows delivery of lidocaine (amide LA) to liver reducing their metabolism (to MEGX=mono ethyl glycine xylidide) &agrave; prolonging Lidocaine's plasma half life Toxicity of Lidocaine is due to its own concentration in blood NOT due to its toxic metabolite MEGX can cause seizures in neonates Reference : Stoelting's pharmacology and physiology in anaesthetic practice 5th edition

Sample MCQ #8

The advantage of ropivacaine over bupivacaine is?

ALonger duration of action
BMore profound motor blockade
CLess cardiotoxic
DMore potent
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Less cardiotoxic

Ropivacaine has a higher therapeutic index and lesser cardiotoxicity as compared to bupivacaine This is because ropivacaine is the S (-) enantiomer of bupivacaine The cardiotoxicity of bupivacaine is due to the R (+) isomer which blocks the cardiac Na channel s more avidly and dissociates more slowly Ropivacaine is less potent Epidural ropivacaine is 40 % less potent than bupivacaine Ropivacaine has a shorter duration of action and produces less motor block than bupivacaine Reference: Morgan and mikhail's clinical anaesthesiology 6th edition
