Sample MCQ #2

Airway adjust play a vital role in maintaining a patent airway. For selecting a oropharyngeal airway, measurement should be taken from

AIncisors to angle of mandible
BAngle of the mouth to angle of mandible
CAla to tragus
DNone of the above
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Incisors to angle of mandible

Explanation: Measurement for Size Selection: The appropriate size of the oropharyngeal airway (OPA) is determined by measuring the vertical distance from the patient's incisors to the angle of the mandible . This ensures that the OPA fits correctly, allowing for optimal airway patency without causing obstruction. Consequences of Incorrect Sizing: Too small: Can kink the tongue, leading to obstruction of airflow. Too large: May push the epiglottis backward, causing further obstruction or trauma to the larynx. Key Takeaways: Accurate sizing of the oropharyngeal airway is crucial for maintaining a patent airway. Proper measurements help prevent complications associated with incorrect airway adjunct sizing.

Sample MCQ #4

You are using a Guedel airway for a patient to improve the upper airway patency. How will you estimate the size you will require for the same?

ABy placing the flange at the level of the incisors and the tip at the angle of the mandible
BBy placing the flange at the angle of mouth and tip at the angle of the mandible
CBy placing the flange at the level of the incisors and tip at the tragus of the ear
DBy placing the flange at the external nares and the tip at the tragus of the ear
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. By placing the flange at the level of the incisors and the tip at the angle of the mandible

A Guedel airway maintains upper airway patency by keeping the mouth open and preventing the tongue falling backwards. The airway is available in a range of sizes from neonate to adult. To determine the correct size, the airway is held up to the patient's face. The size that is most appropriate for the patient can be estimated in two ways: the 'hard-to-hard' method, where the flange is placed at the level of the incisors and the tip at the angle of the mandible, or the 'soft-to-soft' method, from the angle of the mouth to the tragus of the ear. Reference: Equipment in Anaesthesia and Critical Care, Daniel Aston, Angus Rivers, Asela Dharmadasa, pg 28

Sample MCQ #5

A 42 years old, 120 kg patient is posted for a laparoscopic umbilical hernia repair surgery. After insertion of a supraglottic device, it is observed that the peak airway pressure required to ventilate the patient is about 40 cmH2O. What will be the next best step in airway management of this patient?

AContinue with the procedure
BPut a smaller size LMA and ventilate
CIntubate with appropriate sized endotracheal tube and ventilate
DAbort the procedure and wake up the patient
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Intubate with appropriate sized endotracheal tube and ventilate

The mean pressure at which gastric insufflation occurs is 28cm H 2 O. When airway pressures with an LMA exceed this limit, there is a possibility of gastric insufflation because of raised pressure as the LMA doesn't form a watertight seal around the larynx and hence cannot be relied upon for protection against aspiration of gastric contents. Reference: Understanding Anaesthesia Equipment (Jerry A. Dorsch, Susan E. Dorsch) 5th edition pg 488

Sample MCQ #6

Complications associated with the use of face mask ventilation are all except

ASore throat
BGastric insufflation
CCervical spine movement in unstable injuries
DEnvironmental pollution
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Sore throat

Incidence of sore throat is minimal with face mask, as compared to supraglottic airway devices or intubation with an endotracheal tube, since there is no airway instrumentation in facemask ventilation. With face mask, when a positive pressure ventilation is provided, gases are likely to enter the stomach. So it is recommended that inspiratory pressure be kept below 20 cm H 2 O. Mask ventilation moves the cervical spine, sometimes even more than commonly used methods of tracheal intubation. This may be of significance in the patient with an unstable cervical spine injury. Face mask is associated with greater operating room pollution with anesthetic gases and vapors than with a tracheal tube or supraglottic airway device. Reference: Understanding Anaesthesia Equipment (Jerry A. Dorsch, Susan E. Dorsch) 5th edition pg 450

Sample MCQ #7

A nasopharyngeal airway may be used as an airway adjunct in all of the following conditions except patients with:

AIntact gag reflex
BTrismus (lockjaw)
COral trauma
DEpistaxis (nose bleed)
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Epistaxis (nose bleed)

The nasopharyngeal airway is a simple airway adjunct that can be used to facilitate ventilation and removal of secretions. A nasopharyngeal airway can be used in patients with an intact gag reflex, unstable fractures of the mandible, trismus (lockjaw), or oral trauma, complications include soft tissue damage of the nasal mucosa and bleeding. Reference: Clinical Application of Mechanical Ventilation, Fourth Edition by David W. Chang, page no 130

Sample MCQ #8

An esophageal-tracheal combitube (ETC) is inserted into the:

ATrachea
BTrachea or esophagus
CEsophagus
DEsophagus or larynx
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Trachea or esophagus

Esophageal-tracheal combitube (ETC): An airway that may be inserted into the esophagus or trachea. The ETC is inserted blindly either into the trachea or esophagus. Both lumens on the ETC can be used to provide ventilation. Lumen 1 is used when the tube enters the esophagus and lumen 2 is used when it is in the trachea. Reference: Clinical Application of Mechanical Ventilation, Fourth Edition by David W. Chang, page no 139
