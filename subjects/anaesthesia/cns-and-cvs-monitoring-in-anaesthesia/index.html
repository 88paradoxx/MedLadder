Sample MCQ #1

While monitoring a patient for return of neuromuscular function after using rocuronium, you notice the patient has regained four twitches using train of four stimulations. With four twitches on train of four stimulations, the patient may still have blockage of acetylcholine receptors of up to

A25%
B50%
C75%
D90%
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. 75%

At the point the fourth twitch reappears, still up to 75% to 80% of acetylcholine receptors may be blocked. Adequate reversal (neostigmine&ndash;glycopyrrolate) should be given, and clinical signs for return of neuromuscular function should be used to gauge readiness for extubation. Reference: Morgan and Mikhail&rsquo;s clinical anesthesiology 6th edition pg 134.

Sample MCQ #2

Identify the correct relationship between the cerebral perfusion pressure (CPP), mean arterial pressure (MAP), intracranial pressure (ICP) and central venous pressure (CVP) from the ones given below.

ACPP = MAP + (ICP - CVP)
BCPP = MAP - (ICP + CVP)
CCPP = MAP + (ICP + CVP)
DCPP = MAP - (ICP - CVP)
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. CPP = MAP - (ICP + CVP)

Cerebral perfusion pressure is the pressure gradient driving cerebral blood flow. CPP = MAP - (ICP + CVP) Where CPP is cerebral perfusion pressure, CVP is central venous pressure, MAP is the mean arterial pressure and ICP is the intracranial pressure. Reference: Physics, Pharmacology and Physiology for Anaesthetists, Matthew E. Cross, second edition, pg 313.

Sample MCQ #3

A 54-year-old male hypertensive patient underwent laparoscopic lower anterior resection. His blood pressure in the recovery was 190/80 mm Hg and heart rate was 91 beats per minute. He was maintaining saturation of 100% with oxygen mask of 4 liters. Given that, the reason is the pain, step of management will include the following:

ALabetalol
BFentanyl
CNicardipine
DIntravenous crystalloids
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Fentanyl

Noxious stimulation from incisional pain, endotracheal intubation, full bladder, or preoperative discontinuation of anti-hypertensive medication, is usually responsible for increased blood pressure in the recovery area. Adequate analgesia preoperatively given with opioids. Mild to moderate elevations can be treated with intravenous labetalol; an angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor, such as enalapril; or a calcium channel blocker, such as nicardipine. The end point for treatment should be consistent with the patient&rsquo;s own normal blood pressure. Reference: Morgan and Mikhail&rsquo;s clinical anesthesiology, page no 1300

Sample MCQ #5

Systemic blood pressure measurements at what minimum intervals are recommended by the ASA under anaesthesia?

A3 minutes
B1 minute
C5 minutes
D10 minutes
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. 5 minutes

During the delivery of anesthesia, the current standard of care is to measure systemic blood pressure and heart rate every 5 minutes as a minimum. The clinical scenario and phase of the operation may mandate more frequent monitoring, which is up to the judgment of the anesthesia provider. Reference: https://www.asahq.org/standards-and-guidelines/standards-for-basic-anesthetic-monitoring

Sample MCQ #6

Below what value of cerebral blood flow (CBF) will signs of cerebral ischemia first begin to appear on the electroencephalogram (EEG)?

A6 mL/100 g/min
B15 mL/100 g/min
C22 mL/100 g/min
D31 mL/100 g/min
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. 22 mL/100 g/min

The human brain is able to maintain neuronal function in the face of decreasing CBF below the normal level of 50 mL/100 g/min. Because O2 delivery is directly related to CBF, EEG evidence of cerebral ischemia will appear if CBF is diminished sufficiently. The CBF reserve, however, is substantial, and the first signs of cerebral ischemia do not appear on EEG until CBF has fallen to approximately 22 mL/100 g/min. When CBF has fallen to 15 mL/100 g/min, the EEG becomes isoelectric. Irreversible membrane damage and cellular death do not occur, however, until CBF falls to 6 mL/100 g/min. Areas of the brain in which CBF falls in the 6 to 15 mL/100 g/min range are referred to as zones of ischemic penumbra. Several hours may elapse in these areas of the brain before irreversible membrane damage occurs. Reference: Miller: Miller's Anesthesia, ed 8, p 410

Sample MCQ #7

During cervical spine surgery for the resection of an intradural mass, the patient begins to cough. The concentration of isoflurane is subsequently increased. With respect to somatosensory-evoked potential (SSEP) monitoring, you would expect

AAmplitude and latency to decrease
BAmplitude and latency to increase
CAmplitude to decrease and latency to increase
DAmplitude to increase and latency to decrease
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Amplitude to decrease and latency to increase

Indications for intraoperative monitoring of evoked potentials (EPs) include surgical procedures associated with possible neurological injury: spinal fusion with instrumentation, spine and spinal cord tumor resection, brachial plexus repair, thoracoabdominal aortic aneurysm repair, epilepsy surgery, and cerebral tumor resection. Volatile agents decrease the amplitude and increase the latency of evoked potentials. Reference: Morgan and Mikhail&rsquo;s clinical anesthesiology 6th edition pg 596.

Sample MCQ #8

During a complex mitral valve replacement, it is determined that the patient will benefit from brief protective hypothermia. Of the options listed below, core temperature is best measured via the

ATympanic membrane
BBladder
CAxillary
DRectum
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Tympanic membrane

Numerous sites can be used to monitor temperature in the operating room. The tympanic membrane theoretically reflects brain temperature because the auditory canal ' s blood supply is the external carotid artery. Tympanic membrane temperatures reflect core body temperature. After tympanic membrane (perfused by carotid artery) and pulmonary artery measurements, the best reflector of core temperature is bladder temperature. Axillary and skin temperatures are highly prone to errors. Rectal temperature is a poor substitute and probe can induce trauma. Reference: Morgan and Mikhail&rsquo;s clinical anesthesiology 6th edition pg 132.
