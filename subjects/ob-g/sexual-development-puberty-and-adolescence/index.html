Sample MCQ #1

Where is the SRY region located in the body?

AShort arm of Y chromosome
BLong arm of Y chromosome
CShort arm of X chromosome
DLong arm of X chromosome
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Short arm of Y chromosome

Explanation: The SRY gene/region is the sex-determining gene/region located on the distal region of the short arm of the Y chromosome . It has a significant role in determining male sex and expresses at the 5th to 6th week of development. In XX karyotype the absence of this gene expression leads to ovarian development. Mutation of the SRY gene leads to Swyer syndrome. Patients with Swyer syndrome have a 46XY karyotype , but their Mullerian system develops normally. These individuals are phenotypically female with both female external and internal genitalia. Their gonads are usually streak ovaries composed only of gonadal stroma, while the vagina, uterus, and fallopian tubes are all formed. Reference: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8840551/#:~:text=Abstract,testis%20from%20an%20undifferentiated%20gonad.

Sample MCQ #2

In cases of gonadal sex reversal syndrome with an XX karyotype, what is the most common underlying cause?

APoint mutation
BDeletion
CTranslocation
DNon-sense mutation
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Translocation

Explanation: In gonadal sex reversal syndrome with an XX karyotype, the most common cause is a translocation . Translocation is the rearrangement of chromosomal material between non-homologous chromosomes. Sex reversal syndrome, also known as disorder of sex development (DSD), arises when an individual's chromosomal, gonadal, and phenotypic sex do not align. In gonadal sex reversal syndrome with an XX karyotype, a translocation involving the SRY (Sex-determining Region Y) gene can cause the gene to be present on an X chromosome. Because the SRY gene initiates male development, its translocation to the X chromosome can cause individuals with an XX karyotype to develop testes rather than ovaries. Reference: https://afju.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s12301-021-00210-5
