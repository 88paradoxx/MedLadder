Sample MCQ #1

Quickening" or the period when fetal movements are commonly first felt by the mother occurs:

ANear the end of the first trimester
BAround the middle of the second trimester
CAt the end of the second trimester
DAt the end of the embryonic period
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Around the middle of the second trimester

Although reflexive fetal movements begin at the end of the first trimester, they typically cannot be felt until the fifth month of pregnancy. At the end of the fetal period, there is much movement, all of it extra-uterine. Reference - Textbook of Langman's, 12th Edition, General Embryology Page number - 51

Sample MCQ #2

35 weeks pregnant diabetic female with NST non- reactive. What should be done next?

AInduction of labour
BCS
CDo NST after 1hour
DProceed to biophysical profile
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Proceed to biophysical profile

In the case of a 35-week pregnant diabetic female with a non-reactive non-stress test (NST), the next step should be (D) proceed to biophysical profile. This helps further assess fetal well being before deciding on intervention. Biophysical Profile (BPP): This test combines an NST with an ultrasound assessment to evaluate several aspects of fetal health. The BPP assesses: Fetal Heart Rate (NST component): Evaluates heart rate patterns. Fetal Breathing Movements: Checks for episodes of breathing. Fetal Movement: Assesses general body movements. Fetal Tone: Looks for muscle tone and flexion of the limbs. Amniotic Fluid Volume: Measures the amount of amniotic fluid. Reference: Management of High Risk Pregnancy, Manjupuri, SS Trivedi, p 62

Sample MCQ #3

A pregnant female has cramps in her legs. What will you suggest to relieve the pain?

AExtend knee and dorsiflex the foot
BFlex knee and dorsiflex the foot
CExtend knee and plantar flex the foot
DFlex knee and plantar flex the foot
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Extend knee and dorsiflex the foot

Why do pregnant ladies get leg cramps? It may have to do with changes in blood circulation and stress on your leg muscles from carrying extra weight. Your growing baby also puts pressure on the nerves and blood vessels that go to your legs. And some doctors say low calcium, or a change in the way your body processes calcium, may cause cramps If you get a leg cramp, stretch the calf muscle. Walking and then sitting and raising your legs might help keep the leg cramp from coming back. A hot shower, warm bath, ice massage or muscle massage may help too. Reference: https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/pregnancy-week-by-week/expert-answers/leg-cramps-during-pregnancy/faq-20057766#:~:text=Leg%20cramps%20are%20painful%20muscle,take%20steps%20to%20prevent%20them.
