ID: 3583 | Answer: [C] 17-Hydroxyprogesterone (17-OHP)
QUESTION: In 21-hydroxylase deficiency, which of the following laboratory findings is typically elevated?
OPTIONS: A) Serum cortisol | B) Serum aldosterone | C) 17-Hydroxyprogesterone (17-OHP) | D) Serum ACTH
TAG TOPIC: 21-hydroxylase deficiency PYQ: Good to know Reference: Harper's illustrated biochemistry 31 st edition Chap 56, Page No.718-727.
EXPLANATION: 17-Hydroxyprogesterone (17-OHP) is a precursor in the cortisol synthesis pathway. In 21-hydroxylase deficiency , the enzyme block prevents 17-OHP from being converted further, leading to its accumulation . Elevated 17-OHP levels are a hallmark laboratory finding and are critical for diagnosing the condition. Serum cortisol is typically low due to the enzymatic block in cortisol synthesis, which disrupts the pathway. Serum aldosterone is usually low or normal, especially in the salt-wasting form , as aldosterone synthesis is impaired. Serum ACTH is elevated as a compensatory response to low cortisol levels but is not the primary diagnostic marker. Why Not Others: A) Serum cortisol: Cortisol is decreased because 21-hydroxylase is essential for cortisol production. B) Serum aldosterone: Often low or normal due to impaired aldosterone synthesis in this condition, leading to salt-wasting symptoms. D) Serum ACTH: Elevated due to lack of negative feedback from cortisol, but it is not a specific diagnostic marker. Key Takeaways: Elevated 17-OHP is a diagnostic hallmark of 21-hydroxylase deficiency . Low cortisol and aldosterone with high ACTH are characteristic findings in this enzyme deficiency. CAH commonly presents with virilization in females, salt-wasting in severe cases, and increased androgen levels. Cockayne Syndrome (Option C): Caused by defects in the ERCC6/ERCC8 genes , resulting in impaired transcription-coupled nucleotide excision repair . Presents with growth failure, neurodegeneration, photosensitivity , and premature aging , but not associated with chromosomal breakage or malignancy . Lynch Syndrome (Option D): Results from mutations in DNA mismatch repair genes (e.g., MLH1, MSH2, MSH6, PMS2 ). It predisposes to colorectal and endometrial carcinomas , but the defect is in mismatch repair , not double-strand break repair. Review of 3&beta;-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase deficiency Deficiency of 3&beta;-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase (3&beta;-HSD) occurs in fewer than 2% of patients with adrenal hyperplasia. Of the following, the MOST appropriate next step for this patient is to administer
OPTIONS: A) 1 dose of PCV13 followed by 1 dose of PPSV23 at least 8 weeks later | B) 1 dose of PPSV23 followed by 1 dose of PCV13 at least 8 weeks later | C) 2 doses of PCV13, 4 weeks apart, followed by 1 dose of PPSV23 at least 8 weeks later | D) 2 doses of PPSV23, 4 weeks apart, followed by 1 dose of PCV13 at least 8 weeks later
EXPLANATION: Pediatric solid organ transplant recipients are at increased risk of acquiring invasive pneumococcal disease compared with the general population. In children aged 6 through 18 years at high risk of pneumococcal disease who have not received any doses of PCV13 or PPSV23, administration of 1 dose of PCV13 followed by 1 dose of PPSV23 at least 8 weeks later and a second dose of PPSV23 5 years after the first is recommended. ID: 24388 | Answer: [A] Pleiotropism
QUESTION: Single gene defect causing multiple unrelated problems?
OPTIONS: A) Pleiotropism | B) Pseudo-dominance | C) Penetrance | D) Anticipation
EXPLANATION: Pleiotropy refers to the phenomenon whereby a single mutant allele can have widespread effects or expression in different tissues or organ systems. In other words, an allele may produce more than one effect on the phenotype. For example, Marfan syndrome has manifestations in different organ systems {skeletal, cardiac, ophthalmologic, etc) due to a single mutation within the fibrillin gene.
