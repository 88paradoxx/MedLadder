EXPLANATION: Explanation: In pediatric CPR, the recommended depth for chest compressions is at least one-third the anterior-posterior (AP) diameter of the chest. This translates to approximately 4 cm in infants and 5 cm in children . Compressions should be performed at a rate of 100-120/min , with proper technique such as the two-thumb encircling method for infants. Full chest recoil must be allowed between compressions. Adrenaline is indicated if the heart rate remains 0.01 mg/kg of 1:10,000 solution IV/IO , repeated every 3-5 minutes if needed. ID: 24347 | Answer: [B] Fever responsiveness to antibiotics
QUESTION: A 3 year old child presented with High grade fever, desquamating rash on the body, lymphadenopathy, conjunctivitis and Strawberry tongue in the mouth. Which among the following can be used to clinically distinguish Kawasaki Disease from Scarlet Fever?
OPTIONS: A) Strawberry tongue | B) Fever responsiveness to antibiotics | C) Presence of desquamation | D) Age of the child
EXPLANATION: A common clinical problem is the differentiation of scarlet fever from KD in a child who is a group A streptococcal carrier. Patients with scarlet fever typically have a rapid clinical response of fever to appropriate antibiotic therapy. Such treatment for 24-48 hr with clinical reassessment generally clarifies the diagnosis. Furthermore, ocular findings are quite rare in group A streptococcal pharyngitis and may assist in the diagnosis of KD. Please remember that fever in KD is high-grade, daily, lasting 5 days or longer and is generally unresponsive to beta-lactam antibiotics. Reference: Nelson 21 st Edition, Chapter 191

EXPLANATION: Management guidelines of West syndrome - AOCN 2021 Guidelines Therapy Of Choice: Hormonal Therapy ACTH is the preferred agent High-Dose Regimen) is used: ACTH 150 U/m 2 or 6 U/Kg IM OD x 2 weeks If unavailable, high-dose Prednisolone (4 mg/kg/day) for 2 weeks Vigabatrin= VGB: Given if ACTH/Steroids fail or are Contraindicated Incorrect Options: JME (Juvenile Myoclonic Epilepsy): JME is typically managed with valproate , levetiracetam , or lamotrigine , not with ACTH. Key Takeaway: West Syndrome (infantile spasms) is primarily treated with ACTH or high-dose steroids like prednisolone , with vigabatrin as an alternative when first-line treatments fail or are contraindicated. ACTH is not used for other forms of epilepsy like JME , Rolandic Epilepsy , or LGS . Reference: Nelson textbook of paediatrics

ID: 24557 | Answer: [C] Bone marrow aspirate
QUESTION: Which of the following is not a first line of investigation in a child suspected to be having Acute Myeloid Leukemia?
OPTIONS: A) Peripheral blood smear | B) White cell differential | C) Bone marrow aspirate | D) Full blood count
TAG TOPIC: LEUKEMIA & LYMPHOMA Difficulty level: MUST KNOW PYQ: FMGE DEC 2020 Reference: Nelson textbook of paediatrics, 21 st ed, page number: 2654
EXPLANATION: Bone marrow examination (including aspirate and trephine biopsy) is a definitive investigation to diagnose AML but would not be safe, tolerated or efficient as a first line investigation. The most important initial investigations are a full blood count and blood film . If both are within normal range, acute leukaemia can be ruled out with a relative degree of certainty. Typically, the full blood count will demonstrate pancytopenia (due to bone marrow infiltration by blasts). Although the patient is likely to be neutropenic, many circulating blasts tend to dramatically elevate the overall white cell count, with blasts clearly visible on blood film. In some cases, the white cell count may be only slightly raised and, if blasts remain sequestered within the bone marrow, it may even be lower than normal. Clearly identifiable blasts are not always present on a film. In these cases, the only clue that there is a serious underlying problem may be a few atypical cells in the blood film or the presence of leukoerythroblastic features. KEY TAKEAWAYS: Bone marrow examination (including aspirate and trephine biopsy) is a definitive investigation to diagnose AML.
