Genetics: ARX gene mutation in males; associated with ambiguous genitalia and cortical migration abnormalities. Types: Cryptogenic (unknown etiology): normal development prior to onset. Other antiepileptics (valproate, benzodiazepines, lamotrigine, topiramate, zonisamide, IVIG). ID: 23519 | Answer: [B] Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy
QUESTION: The most common cause of sudden death during competitive exercise is?
OPTIONS: A) Postoperative congenital heart disease | B) Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy | C) Aortic valve stenosis | D) Long QT syndrome
TAG TOPIC: EPIDEMIOLOGY Difficulty level: GOOD TO KNOW Reference: Park&rsquo;s Pediatric cardiology, 6 th ed., pg. 326
EXPLANATION: Hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy (HOCM) is a condition where the heart muscle, particularly the left ventricle, becomes abnormally thick . This thickening can obstruct blood flow out of the heart, making it harder for the heart to pump effectively. HOCM is one of the leading causes of sudden cardiac death, especially in young people, including athletes. Mechanism of Sudden cardiac death in HOCM : Obstruction of Blood Flow : The thickened heart muscle can partially block or obstruct blood flow out of the left ventricle. This leads to increased strain on the heart, potentially causing irregular heart rhythms. Arrhythmias : HOCM can lead to life-threatening arrhythmias, such as ventricular tachycardia and ventricular fibrillation . These rapid, chaotic rhythms can disrupt the heart&rsquo;s ability to pump blood and may lead to sudden cardiac arrest. Ischemia : The thickened heart muscle may outgrow its blood supply, leading to ischemia (inadequate oxygenation of the muscle tissue). This can further increase the risk of dangerous arrhythmias. Diastolic Dysfunction : HOCM often leads to diastolic dysfunction, where the heart cannot relax adequately between beats. This further increases the risk of sudden cardiac events. Physical Exertion : In young athletes, vigorous physical activity can trigger arrhythmias or exacerbate blood flow obstruction, raising the risk of sudden death Option A: Postoperative congenital heart disease: Incorrect , it presents with arrythmias, heart failure, pulmonary hypertension, infections, developmental and neurological complications etc. Option C: Aortic valve stenosis. Incorrect, presents with Chest pain, breathlessness, fatigue, dizziness. Option D: Incorrect , Many people with long QT are asymptomatic , but symptoms can include fainting, palpitations, seizures, or even sudden cardiac death, often triggered by stress, exercise, or loud noises. EXPLANATION: Explanation: A deficiency of sphingomyelinase leads to Niemann-Pick disease type A , a lysosomal storage disorder. It causes accumulation of sphingomyelin in macrophages and other tissues , resulting in hepatosplenomegaly . The cherry-red spot occurs due to storage in retinal ganglion cells, while the startle reflex remains normal initially. Neurological deterioration develops gradually, distinguishing it from other storage disorders like Tay-Sachs. This inherited lysosomal storage disorder usually appears in early infancy. Although newborns are healthy at birth, they soon develop failure to thrive, progressive hepatosplenomegaly, and marked psychomotor delay. Over time, affected infants experience profound motor decline and intellectual deterioration, with death typically occurring by 2-3 years of age. The disease results from absent or markedly reduced activity of acid sphingomyelinase, leading to accumulation of sphingomyelin within cells of the monocyte-macrophage system. Examination of bone marrow or lymph nodes often reveals lipid-laden foam cells, and the diagnosis is confirmed by demonstrating low sphingomyelinase activity in peripheral leukocytes. Congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH) A birth defect where the diaphragm fails to form properly, allowing abdominal organs to move into the chest cavity and disrupt normal lung development. Mild cases typically involve smaller-than-normal lungs at birth, with surgical repair of the diaphragm generally leading to favorable outcomes. Severe cases often result in significant lung hypoplasia, pulmonary hypertension, and may require extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO) shortly after birth. X-ray Findings: On a chest X-ray of a baby with CDH, the following findings may be observed: Displacement of abdominal organs into the thoracic cavity, such as the stomach, intestines, or liver. EXPLANATION: Explanation: The most likely diagnosis based on the clinical features of a missing index finger, along with deformities of all four limbs, is suggestive of amniotic band syndrome . Amniotic band disruption presents with a wide spectrum of deformities caused by fibrous tissue bands encircling and constricting fetal parts. The extremities are frequently involved, where the bands can cause amputations of fingers and toes, fixed deformities of the hands, or pseudodactyly. Bands may also encircle larger regions, leading to thoracoabdominal wall defects with herniation of abdominal and thoracic contents. Craniofacial abnormalities can occur, including absent calvarium, facial clefts, and large encephaloceles. Additionally, bands may constrict areas such as the ankle, producing deformational defects and amputations . Overall, the disruption sequence is marked by variable combinations of limb reductions, craniofacial malformations, and body wall defects, all directly related to the location and severity of the constricting amniotic bands. Explanation of other options: Arthrogryposis multiplex congenita (Option A): Arthrogryposis multiplex congenita is a descriptive condition characterised by the presence of two or more nonprogressive congenital joint contractures at birth, often linked with muscle weakness and reduced mobility due to abnormal muscle fibre contraction.
