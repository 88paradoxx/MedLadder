ID: 24297 | Answer: [D] Salivary secretion transmission
QUESTION: A 17-year-old boy presents to the clinic with fever, sore throat, and lymphadenopathy for the past week. On exam, tonsillar inflammation, palatal petechiae, posterior cervical lymphadenopathy, and splenomegaly are identified. Which of the following describes the mode of transmission of the most likely diagnosis?
OPTIONS: A) Blood-borne transmission | B) Droplet transmission | C) Fecal-oral transmission | D) Salivary secretion transmission
EXPLANATION: Fever, sore throat, lymphadenopathy, splenomegaly, tonsillar enlargement and palatal petechiae, especially at the junction of soft and hard palate suggest a diagnosis of Infectious Mononucleosis, caused by Epstein Barr Virus. Infectious mononucleosis caused by Epstein-Barr virus and occurs most often in adolescents and young adults. Infectious mononucleosis is transmitted by salivary gland secretions, such as by sharing drinks or kissing. The classic symptoms and signs of infectious mononucleosis include pharyngitis, lymphadenopathy, fever, fatigue, and splenomegaly. Cervical lymphadenopathy tends to affect the posterior chain more than the anterior chain. Patients presenting with infectious mononucleosis may also have a rash. The rash has various presentations, including petechial, maculopapular, and urticarial variations, and occurs most often after antibiotics were inadvertently administered due to an incorrect diagnosis, such as streptococcal pharyngitis. Reference: Nelson textbook of pediatrics, 1 st SA edition., pg. 1586
