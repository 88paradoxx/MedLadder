ID: 23604 | Answer: [A] Only 1,2,3 is correct
QUESTION: Which of the following congenital heart disease presents with split-second heart sounds? 1. Atrial Septal Defect (ASD) 2. Ventricular Septal Defect (VSD) 3. Patent Ductus Arteriosus (PDA) 4. Tetralogy of Fallot (TOF)
OPTIONS: A) Only 1,2,3 is correct | B) Only 2,3,4 is correct | C) Only 1,2,4 is correct | D) All are correct
EXPLANATION: Explanation: Congenital heart diseases that can present with split-second heart sounds include: Atrial Septal Defect (ASD) (Statement 1): An ASD is a hole in the wall (septum) that separates the two upper chambers of the heart (atria). It can lead to increased blood flow from the left atrium to the right atrium, resulting in more evident splitting of the second heart sound (S2) during auscultation. Ventricular Septal Defect (VSD) (Statement 2) : A VSD is a hole in the septum between the two lower chambers of the heart (ventricles). Like ASD, it can cause increased blood flow from the left ventricle to the right ventricle, leading to accentuated splitting of S2. Patent Ductus Arteriosus (PDA) (Statement 3): PDA is a condition where a blood vessel called the ductus arteriosus, which connects the pulmonary artery to the aorta during fetal development, remains open after birth. This can result in abnormal blood flow and increased flow across the pulmonary valve, leading to a more pronounced splitting of S2. Tetralogy of Fallot (TOF): Does not usually present with split S2. Features include: Single S2 due to pulmonic stenosis (P2 often inaudible) Harsh systolic ejection murmur at left upper sternal border Right ventricular hypertrophy Overriding aorta and VSD Splitting of S2 is absent due to delayed or obstructed pulmonary flow. ID: 23698 | Answer: [B] Scurvy
QUESTION: Which of the following diseases is associated with impaired collagen maturation?
OPTIONS: A) Rickets | B) Scurvy | C) Beri Beri | D) Pellagra
EXPLANATION: Scurvy, caused by a deficiency of vitamin C (ascorbic acid), directly affects collagen synthesis and maturation. Vitamin C is a crucial cofactor for the enzymes prolyl hydroxylase and lysyl hydroxylase , which are necessary for the hydroxylation of proline and lysine residues in procollagen. These modifications are essential for stabilizing the collagen triple helix and proper cross-linking of collagen fibers. Clinical Features of Scurvy: Fragile connective tissues: Poor collagen formation leads to fragile skin, blood vessels, and bones. Skeletal issues: Thin cortex, brittle trabeculae, and poor bone formation. Gum disease: Swelling, bleeding, and receding gums. Impaired wound healing: Due to defective collagen synthesis. Other Options: Rickets (A): Caused by vitamin D deficiency, it affects bone mineralization but does not impair collagen maturation. Beri Beri (C): Caused by thiamine (vitamin B1) deficiency, it affects energy metabolism, particularly in the nervous and cardiovascular systems. Pellagra (D): Caused by niacin (vitamin B3) deficiency, it results in dermatitis, diarrhea, and dementia but does not affect collagen synthesis. Key Takeaway: In scurvy, the impaired collagen leads to weakened structural integrity in tissues, making B. Scurvy the correct answer. Reference : Nelson textbook of Pediatrics, 21st ed., pg. 367
