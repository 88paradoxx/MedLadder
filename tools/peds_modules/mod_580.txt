ID: 23622 | Answer: [C] Exclusive breastfeeding with bilirubin >10 mg/dL after 3-4 weeks
QUESTION: Which is true about breastmilk jaundice?
OPTIONS: A) Breastfeeding should not be continued | B) Most children require phototherapy | C) Exclusive breastfeeding with bilirubin >10 mg/dL after 3-4 weeks | D) Conjugated hyperbilirubinemia
EXPLANATION: Explanation: Approximately 2-4% of term neonates who are exclusively breastfed may have total serum bilirubin (TSB) levels exceeding 10 mg/dL beyond the third or fourth week of life, a condition known as breast milk jaundice (BMJ) . It is an unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia , and diagnosis is made after ruling out other causes of prolonged jaundice such as insufficient feeding, ongoing hemolysis, blood extravasation, G6PD deficiency, and hypothyroidism . Breast milk contains high levels of &beta;-glucuronidase , which deconjugates bilirubin that has already been conjugated by the liver and excreted into the intestine, leading to increased enterohepatic circulation and elevated serum bilirubin levels. Most infants with BMJ remain clinically well , and dangerously high bilirubin levels are rare , so treatment is usually unnecessary . Importantly, breastfeeding should not be discontinued , as it is safe to continue feeding throughout the course of BMJ. Breast milk jaundice Features: Unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia (Option D ruled out) Self-limiting , rarely requires phototherapy (Option B ruled out ) Breastfeeding continuation recommended ( Option A ruled out) Key Takeaways: Visual Assessment of Jaundice (Adapted Kramer Rule)

ID: 23627 | Answer: [C] Galactosemia in an infant
QUESTION: Which of the following is an absolute contraindication for breastfeeding?
OPTIONS: A) HIV positive mother | B) Hepatitis C in the mother | C) Galactosemia in an infant | D) Tuberculosis in the mother
EXPLANATION: Explanation: Breastfeeding is contraindicated in infants with classic galactosemia (caused by galactose-1-phosphate uridyltransferase deficiency). Breast milk is rich in lactose , which is digested into glucose and galactose. In galactosemia, the metabolic pathway for galactose is impaired, leading to the accumulation of galactose and its toxic metabolites. This buildup results in clinical features such as poor feeding, vomiting, jaundice, hepatomegaly, cataracts , and the presence of reducing substances in urine . For survival, these infants must be placed on a lactose-free formula , as continued exposure to galactose can be life-threatening. Contraindications of Breastfeeding: HIV who are on effective antiretroviral therapy (ART) and adhere to treatment should exclusively breastfeed for the first 6 months. Complementary feeding can then be introduced while continuing ART, up to 12 months of age. Hepatitis C in mother (Option B): Breastfeeding is not contraindicated in mothers with HCV infection, as available evidence does not show transmission of the virus through breast milk. Tuberculosis in mother (Option D): Breast milk itself is safe in mothers with drug-susceptible tuberculosis (DS-TB) once they are no longer infectious. The milk does not carry Mycobacterium tuberculosis, and only minimal, non-harmful amounts of TB medications are secreted into it. However, if the mother has active TB, direct breastfeeding should be withheld until at least 2 weeks of appropriate anti-TB therapy have been completed.
