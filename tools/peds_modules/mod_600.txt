ID: 24272 | Answer: [A] FNAC
QUESTION: A 14-year-old boy presents with enlargement of the thyroid gland. His TSH is low at 2 mIU/l, his T4 is 29&mu;g/l and he is positive for TSH receptors ABC. A thyroid scan indicates a single large cold nodule and neck ultrasound indicates a solid thyroid gland. Next investigation?
OPTIONS: A) FNAC | B) Ultrasound neck | C) TBG level | D) TPO antibody titer
EXPLANATION: Cold nodules have to be evaluated for thyroid tumours. Hence, next best investigation is an FNAC. OP Ghai clearly says that children presenting with a solitary thyroid nodule, should undergo USG-guided FNAC to exclude an underlying malignancy. Reference: OP Ghai 10 th Edition, Pages 539-540; Harrison's Principles of Internal medicine, 20 th ed., pg. 2922

ID: 24274 | Answer: [B] Grave's disease
QUESTION: Most common cause of hyperthyroidism in children is?
OPTIONS: A) Thyroid nodule | B) Grave's disease | C) Hashimoto's thyroiditis | D) Thyroid adenoma
TAG TOPIC: THYROID DISORDERS Difficulty level: MUST KNOW Reference: Nelson textbook of pediatrics, 21 st ed., pg. 2929
EXPLANATION: The most common cause of hyperthyroidism is Grave's disease . This is an autoimmune condition where antibodies (TSH receptor antibodies - TRAb ) mimic TSH and activate the TSH-R on the thyroid gland, producing excessive thyroid hormone. Other causes of hyperthyroidism include: Toxic multinodular goitre Toxic adenoma Thyroid carcinoma Neonatal hyperthyroidism - due to transplacental transfer of maternal TRAb to the fetus hCG-secreting tumors - hCG has a similar structure to TSH and so can mimic it Functioning pituitary adenoma leading to increased production of TSH KEY TAKEAWAYS: The most common cause of hyperthyroidism is Grave's disease . ID: 24276 | Answer: [C] Radiotracer uptake with technetium
QUESTION: An 8-day-old neonate has TSH >100 mIU/L. Which investigation should be performed next?
OPTIONS: A) Urine iodine excretion | B) Serum thyroid receptor antibody | C) Radiotracer uptake with technetium | D) Perchlorate discharge test
EXPLANATION: Explanation: TSH >100 mIU/L strongly suggests primary congenital hypothyroidism . The best next test is radionuclide scanning (Technetium-99m/Iodine-123) . It helps determine thyroid size, location, and function , and differentiates between thyroid dysgenesis, dyshormonogenesis, or ectopic thyroid . Congenital hypothyroidism: Most common preventable cause of intellectual disability . More common in girls . Thyroid dysgenesis is the leading cause. Aetiology: Primary hypothyroidism: thyroid dysgenesis, dyshormonogenesis, iodine deficiency, transporter defect (MCT8 mutation), maternal antibodies, maternal drugs (iodides, amiodarone, PTU, methimazole, radioiodine). Central hypothyroidism: TSH &beta; mutation, TRH mutation/receptor defect, multiple pituitary hormone deficiencies. Clinical features: Early: prolonged neonatal jaundice, constipation, feeding difficulty. Dysmorphic: coarse facies, macroglossia, wide open fontanelles. Neurological: hypotonia, slow reflexes, developmental delay. Hematological: refractory macrocytic anemia. Diagnosis: Primary: low T4, TSH >100 mIU/L . Central: low T3, T4, TSH. Imaging: Technetium/Iodine scan or USG for thyroid location. X-ray findings: absent femoral/tibial epiphysis, punctate epiphyseal dysgenesis, wide sutures, large fontanelle. Screening: Universal newborn screening using heel prick after 48-72 hrs (not earlier due to TSH surge). Best approach: check both T4 and TSH. ASH strategy: initial TSH screening &rarr; if >20 mIU/L &rarr; repeat &rarr; confirm with venous TSH + T4. Treatment: Levothyroxine is the treatment of choice. Dose: newborns 10-15 &mu;g/kg/day, children 2-4 &mu;g/kg/day. Early treatment prevents irreversible neurocognitive damage . Explanation of Other Options: Urine iodine excretion (Option A): used for population iodine deficiency surveys , not diagnosis of CH. Serum thyroid receptor antibody (Option B): useful in suspected transient CH due to maternal autoimmune hypothyroidism , not first-line. Perchlorate discharge test (Option D): used to detect organification defects (thyroid peroxidase deficiency) , not initial investigation. Key Takeaways: Technetium scan is the investigation of choice after finding TSH >100 mIU/L . Thyroid dysgenesis is the most common cause. Early levothyroxine therapy is essential to prevent mental retardation.
