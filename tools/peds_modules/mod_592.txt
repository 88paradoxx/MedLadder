ID: 24084 | Answer: [B] Factor IX deficiency
QUESTION: Which of the following is called Christmas disease?
OPTIONS: A) Factor VIII deficiency | B) Factor IX deficiency | C) Factor X deficiency | D) Factor XI deficiency
EXPLANATION: Hemophilia B, also known as factor IX deficiency or Christmas disease, is the second most common type of hemophilia. The disorder was first reported in the medical literature in 1952 in a patient with the name of Stephen Christmas. The most famous family with hemophilia B was that of Queen Victoria of England. Reference: Nelson textbook of Pediatrics, 1 st SA edition, pg. 2385

ID: 24087 | Answer: [B] Edema that improves throughout the day
QUESTION: You are teaching a group of medical students the differential diagnosis of edema in children. You take them to the bedside of a patient who has edema associated with hypoproteinemia secondary to proteinuria. Of the following, the feature you are MOST likely to observe in this patient is?
OPTIONS: A) Cola-coloured urine, oliguria, and hypertension | B) Edema that improves throughout the day | C) Genital edema | D) Pitting pedal edema
EXPLANATION: Nephrotic syndrome usually presents with edema that is periorbital and more prominent early in the morning and with minimal systemic symptoms. Edema is the most easily identified clinical feature associated with hypoproteinemia. Impaired cardiac function leads to worsening peripheral edema as the day progresses and is associated with exertion and increased activity. Pitting edema is not pathognomonic for any specific underlying etiology. Reference: Nelson textbook of pediatrics, 1 st SA ed., pg. 2521

ID: 24322 | Answer: [A] Septic arthritis
QUESTION: A 3 1/2 years old child presents to you with a 24-hour history of a limp and pain in her left thigh. There is no history of trauma. Her temperature is 38.9&deg; C and her pulse is 150 beats/min. On examination the left thigh is swollen and held flexed and abducted. She holds it as still as possible and cries if it is moved. The upper leg is slightly red and is warmer to touch than the right leg. She has no other medical history and her mother has given some paracetamol, which had no effect on the pain. What is the likely cause of the clinical condition?
OPTIONS: A) Septic arthritis | B) Reactive arthritis | C) Fracture femur | D) Juvenile idiopathic arthritis
TAG TOPIC: ARTHRITIS Difficulty level: MUST KNOW Reference: Nelson textbook of pediatrics, 21 st ed., pg. 3676
EXPLANATION: The child has very acute history and flexed and abducted position of the thigh at rest suggest septic arthritis. Ultrasound (and diagnostic aspiration), blood tests for culture and tests for inflammatory markers should be performed and intravenous antibiotics started. Septic arthritis , also known as infectious arthritis, is a joint infection typically caused by bacteria (often Staphylococcus aureus ) but can also be caused by viruses or fungi. The infection leads to inflammation within the joint , causing symptoms like sudden, severe joint pain, swelling, warmth, redness, and restricted movement, often accompanied by fever. It most commonly affects large joints like the knee, hip, shoulder, or elbow. Option B: Reactive arthritis has no fever and usually has a history of infective illness in recent past. Option C: There is no history of trauma for diagnosing fracture. Option D: JIA has chronic illness more than 6 weeks of fever and pain . KEY TAKEAWAYS: Septic arthritis, also known as infectious arthritis is a joint infection typically caused by bacteria (often Staphylococcus aureus), causing symptoms like sudden, severe joint pain, swelling , warmth, redness, and restricted movement, often accompanied by fever.
