ID: 3351 | Answer: [D] Cerebral edema
QUESTION: What is the cause of death in pediatric patients with Diabetic Ketoacidosis?
OPTIONS: A) Sepsis | B) Dehydration | C) Hypokalemia | D) Cerebral edema
EXPLANATION: Explanation: In pediatric DKA, the most common cause of mortality is cerebral edema , which occurs in ~0.5-1% of cases but accounts for the majority of deaths. The exact mechanism is unclear but is thought to involve osmotic shifts during fluid therapy, ischemia, and reperfusion injury. Other complications of DKA: Cognitive impairment (from hypoperfusion or cerebral edema) Venous thrombosis (due to dehydration and hypercoagulability) Pancreatic enzyme elevations (secondary to metabolic stress) Acute kidney injury (from severe dehydration) Hypokalemia (can cause arrhythmias, but usually preventable with replacement) Hypoglycemia (from overcorrection with insulin therapy) Rhabdomyolysis (due to severe metabolic derangements) Pulmonary edema (rare, can follow aggressive fluid resuscitation) Multiple organ dysfunction syndrome (in severe cases) Sepsis (can precipitate DKA but not the leading cause of death once DKA is established) Explanation of other options: Sepsis (Option A): Sepsis is a trigger for DKA but not the primary cause of mortality in treated pediatric DKA. Dehydration (Option B): Severe dehydration contributes to morbidity, but with medical care it is correctable and not the leading cause of death. Hypokalemia (Option C): Can cause fatal arrhythmias if not monitored, but cerebral edema far outweighs hypokalemia as the major cause of death in children. QUESTION: What alteration occurs in chloride concentration in sweat and pancreatic secretions in cystic fibrosis?
OPTIONS: A) Chloride is elevated in sweat | B) Chloride is reduced in sweat | C) Chloride is reduced in pancreatic secretions | D) None of the above
EXPLANATION: Explanation: Cystic fibrosis is an autosomal recessive genetic disorder caused by mutations in the CFTR (Cystic Fibrosis Transmembrane Conductance Regulator) gene , which encodes a cAMP-regulated chloride channel . Diagnostic hallmark: Chloride concentration in sweat is markedly increased, typically above 60 mmol/L . Pathophysiology: Dysfunction of the CFTR channel disrupts chloride transport, leading to abnormal electrolyte and fluid balance in various secretions. Chloride is reduced in pancreatic secretions (Option C): While pancreatic insufficiency is common, the diagnostic marker is not low chloride in pancreatic secretions but elevated chloride in sweat. None of the above (Option D): Incorrect, since elevated sweat chloride is a well-established diagnostic criterion.

ID: 9482 | Answer: [D] Sodium-glucose solution
QUESTION: A 2-year-old boy has passed 4-6 stools daily over the last 2 days. The faces are liquid and contain no blood or mucus. While awaiting medical evaluation, which of the following oral fluids is mosteffective to prevent dehydration in this patient?
OPTIONS: A) Chicken broth with added salt | B) Diluted cow's milk | C) Filtered water with no added solutes | D) Sodium-glucose solution
EXPLANATION: Humans consume approximately 2 L of fluid each day and secrete an additional 7 L of fluid from upper digestive organs (eg, salivary glands, stomach, pancreas, gallbladder). Nearly all of this fluid is reabsorbed from the gastrointestinal lumen by the small intestine epithelium. The osmotic gradient for water reabsorption is largely established by the active cotransport of sodium into the apical end of enterocytes coupled with chloride, glucose, or amino acids. Sodium is then transported out of the basolateral end of the enterocyte (sodium/potassium ATPase), which pulls water down its osmotic gradient from the gastrointestinal lumen through the tight junctions between enterocytes and into the lamina propria (and ultimately the bloodstream). This young patient who recently began going to day care likely has acute infectious diarrhea of a viral etiology (eg, rotavirus, norovirus). Infections that target the small intestine cause watery diarrhea by blocking the active transport of sodium chloride into the enterocyte or by increasing chloride excretion into the gastrointestinal lumen (mediated by elevated intracellular cyclic AMP, cyclic GMP, or calcium). In this setting, oral rehydration with a hypotonic, equimolar sodium-glucose solution maximizes water and sodium reabsorption by the remaining functional enterocytes, and is the most effective oral therapy for maintaining volume and electrolyte status in children with acute infectious diarrhea. ID: 23750 | Answer: [B] Surface tension increases, compliance decreases
QUESTION: In a 28-week child with suspected RDS, what of the following occurs?
OPTIONS: A) Surface tension increases, compliance increases | B) Surface tension increases, compliance decreases | C) Surface tension decreases, compliance increases | D) Surface tension decreases, compliance decreases
EXPLANATION: RDS occurs due to deficiency of surfactant in alveoli. Surfactant lining the alveoli enhances the aeration of gas-free lungs by reducing surface tension, thereby lowering the pressure required to open alveoli. Air entry into the lungs displaces fluid, decreases hydrostatic pressure in the pulmonary vasculature, and increases pulmonary blood flow. In the absence of pulmonary surfactant, significantly increased alveolar surface tension leads to atelectasis, and the ability to attain an adequate FRC is impaired. Alveolar atelectasis, hyaline membrane formation, and interstitial edema make the lungs less compliant in RDS, so greater pressure is required to expand the alveoli and small airways. QUESTION: A 3-year-old boy presents with fever and cough. A chest x-ray was done, which showed the following findings. Which of the following is the most likely underlying pathology?

EXPLANATION: Explanation: The given x-ray image shows right upper lobe consolidation and is consistent with a diagnosis of pneumonia, as suggested by the clinical features of fever and cough. Consolidation is defined as the replacement of air in the pulmonary airspaces with fluid, protein, cells, or other materials. On radiographs or CT scans, it presents as one or more relatively uniform opacities that obscure pulmonary vessels but cause little or no volume loss. Unlike ground-glass opacities, which may only partially fill the airspaces, consolidation reflects more complete filling, usually indicating airspace disease . The differential diagnosis takes into account the pattern, distribution, associated findings such as lymph node enlargement, hemoptysis, or fever, and patient history. Substances that may cause consolidation include water (oedema), blood (haemorrhage), pus (pneumonia), cells (neoplasm), fat (lipoid pneumonia), or protein (alveolar proteinosis). While most cases are due to airspace filling, it may also occur from interstitial disease encroaching into alveoli. Air bronchograms are frequently seen, representing air-filled bronchi within consolidated lung, although they may be absent if bronchi are obstructed or filled with blood, pus, or tumour tissue. Diagnosis typically combines clinical, laboratory, and radiographic findings. ID: 23773 | Answer: [B] Insulin mediated uptake of electrolytes, phosphate
QUESTION: A malnourished child, given too much food, developed hypophosphatemia & hypomagnesemia. What could be the mechanism of this?
OPTIONS: A) Catecholamine spike increases electrolyte uptake | B) Insulin mediated uptake of electrolytes, phosphate | C) Urinary loss of phosphates | D) Glucagon spike and cause more loss of phosphate and electrolyte
EXPLANATION: Explanation: Insulin mediated uptake of electrolytes, phosphate is the mechanism responsible for the development of hypophosphatemia & hypomagnesemia in the child.This sudden intracellular shift leads to hypophosphatemia, hypokalemia, and hypomagnesemia -the hallmark of refeeding syndrome . Refeeding syndrome is a serious metabolic complication seen when severely malnourished individuals are given nutrition too rapidly , including via total parenteral nutrition. The sudden carbohydrate intake triggers a surge in insulin, shifting the body from a catabolic to an anabolic state. Elevated insulin stimulates glycogen and protein synthesis and activates the sodium-potassium ATPase pump, driving potassium into cells. Magnesium and phosphate also move intracellularly, leading to hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, and marked hypophosphatemia-the key biochemical feature of this condition. Insulin additionally reduces renal phosphate reabsorption, worsening the deficit. Hyperglycemia may develop as the pancreas struggles to match the abrupt rise in blood glucose. Risk factors include prolonged starvation, chronic alcoholism, delayed nutritional support, anorexia nervosa, or massive weight loss in obesity. If unrecognized, these electrolyte imbalances can cause life-threatening complications such as arrhythmias, myocardial infarction, respiratory failure, liver dysfunction, seizures, confusion, coma, or death. Prevention relies on correcting fluid and electrolyte disturbances beforehand, giving thiamine, and advancing calorie intake slowly and carefully. Explanations for other Options: Catecholamine spike increases electrolyte uptake (Option A): Catecholamines may cause transient shifts of potassium but are not the primary trigger for the profound phosphate and magnesium drop seen in refeeding. Urinary loss of phosphates (Option C): Renal wasting can lower phosphate, but in refeeding syndrome the major mechanism is intracellular shift, not kidney loss. (Option D): Glucagon predominates during fasting, not during refeeding; it does not explain the post-feeding hypophosphatemia.

OPTIONS: A) Hypoalbuminemia | B) Primary Na+ retention | C) Fall in oncotic pressure | D) Glomerular inflammation
EXPLANATION: As per Nelson 21 st Ed, the current understanding of Nephrotic Syndrome has significantly evolved. Earlier, we used to follow the underfill hypothesis, which stated that hypoalbuminemia produces fall in oncotic pressure and causes edema. Now, we know that although hypoalbuminemia is an initiating cause, the actual cause for most clinical edema is primary Na+/H2O retention, the so called Overfill Hypothesis.
