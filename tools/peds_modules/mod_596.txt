ID: 23438 | Answer: [A] Cytotoxic
QUESTION: Which is correct about ABO and Rh incompatibility leading to erythroblastosis fetalis?
OPTIONS: A) Cytotoxic | B) Cell-mediated hypersensitivity | C) Antigen-antibody reaction | D) Immune complex
EXPLANATION: Explanation: ABO and Rh incompatibility leading to erythroblastosis fetalis involves a cytotoxic type of reaction, also known as type II hypersensitivity . Hemolytic disease of the fetus and newborn (HDFN), also known as erythroblastosis fetalis, is a broad diagnosis that applies to any fetus or neonate who develops alloimmune hemolysis caused by the transplacental passage of maternal antibodies directed against paternally derived red blood cell (RBC) antigens. Although more than 60 different RBC antigens can elicit a maternal antibody response, clinically significant disease is most often associated with incompatibility in ABO blood groups or rhesus (Rh) antigen. This is a type II hypersensitivity reaction (cytotoxic type) , which is mediated by antigen-antibody reaction directed against cell surface antigens (fetal RBCs). HDFN caused by Rh Incompatibility HDFN caused by anti-&shy; RhD antibodies, occurring in RhD-&shy; positive infants born to RhD-negative mothers, is the most severe form because of the highly immunogenic nature of the RhD antigen. Rh sensitization usually occurs in the first pregnancy when small amounts of blood (usually >1 mL) enter maternal circulation, so that most instances of sensitization occur around the time of delivery. Although sensitization may occur earlier in the first pregnancy resulting in some hemolysis, HDFN rarely occurs during a first pregnancy. After sensitization, smaller doses of antigen can stimulate antibody production, so all subsequent infants expressing the cognate antigen are at risk for HDFN. HDFN caused by ABO Incompatibility ABO incompatibility is the most common cause of HDFN, with approximately 20% of live births at theoretical risk. Of at-&shy; risk infants, clinical manifestations develop in 1-10% and are usually less severe than Rh disease, rarely requiring aggressive clinical management or therapeutic intervention aside from phototherapy. This is because naturally occurring maternal antibodies against ABO blood group antigens are mostly IgM and therefore do not cross the placenta. However, some group O mothers will naturally produce anti-&shy; A or anti-&shy; B IgG antibodies that can cross the placenta to cause immune-&shy; mediated hemolysis. For this reason, ABO incompatibility can cause hemolysis even in a firstborn infant. Explanation of other options: Cell-mediated hypersensitivity (Option B): This refers to Type IV hypersensitivity , which is mediated by T cells, not antibodies. Antigen-antibody reaction (Option C): Though the underlying cause in HFND is antigen-antibody reaction leading to hemolysis, it is categorised under type II hypersensitivity - cytotoxic category Immune complex-mediated (Option D): These are type III hypersensitivity reaction which involves the formation of antigen-antibody complexes that deposit in tissues and trigger inflammation and are not involved in erythroblastosis fetalis.

ID: 24166 | Answer: [B] Ask someone to retrieve an automatic external defibrillator and start chest compressions
QUESTION: A 15-year-old adolescent collapses while playing in a high school basketball game without any preceding trauma. He is unconscious and not breathing. Of the following, the BEST sequence of actions is to?
OPTIONS: A) Administer a precordial thump and perform a jaw-thrust manoeuvre | B) Ask someone to retrieve an automatic external defibrillator and start chest compressions | C) Immobilize the cervical spine and attempt to palpate the carotid pulse | D) Perform rapid assessment of circulation/airway/breathing and call 108
EXPLANATION: As per PALS guidelines and OP Ghai 10 th Edition, if the victim/patient is unresponsive and not breathing, begin CPR immediately. Also call for an automatic external defibrillator (AED) to check for rhythm. If there is VF/pVT, give electric shock and continue CPR If there is Asystole or PEA (Pulseless Electrical Activity), give injection epinephrine and continue with CPR. Effective cardiopulmonary resuscitation includes pushing hard (to 1/3 of the anterior-posterior diameter of the chest), pushing fast (100 compressions per minute) with minimal interruptions, and rotating personnel performing compressions every 2 minutes. Reference: OP Ghai 10 th Edition, Pages 763, 765; PALS 2020 Guidelines

ID: 24174 | Answer: [C] Type 2B
QUESTION: Desmopressin is relatively contraindicated in which type of Von Willebrand disease?
OPTIONS: A) Type 1 | B) Type 2A | C) Type 2B | D) Type 2M
EXPLANATION: Type 2B vWD results from gain-of-function mutations that increase the ability of vWF to bind platelets. This leads to increased clearance of both vWF and platelets from circulation and results in the loss of high-molecular-weight multimers and decreased vWF activity, similar to that seen in type 2A vWD. Desmopressin is relatively contraindicated in type 2B vWD as it may accelerate VWF-platelet binding and clearance. Please note that Desmopressin is highly effective in Type 1 vWD Reference: Nelson textbook of pediatrics, 1 st SE edition, pg. 2390

ID: 24177 | Answer: [A] Blood in IVC has more oxygen saturation than SVC
QUESTION: True about fetal circulation
OPTIONS: A) Blood in IVC has more oxygen saturation than SVC | B) Pressure in left ventricle is more than right | C) Brain receives blood with low oxygen saturation than limbs | D) Heart receives blood with low oxygen saturation than limbs
TAG TOPIC: FETAL CIRCULATION Difficulty level: MUST KNOW PYQ: NEET PG 2021 Reference: Nelson textbook of pediatrics, 1 st SA ed., pg. 1855
EXPLANATION: Blood in IVC has more saturation than blood in SVC as IVC carries the oxygenated blood of umbilical vein. The left ventricular blood has more oxygen saturation that right ventricular blood because it carries the blood of IVC, while blood in right ventricle is a mixture of blood from IVC and SVC. So, the brain and coronary circulation receive blood with higher saturation (through ascending aorta) than the lower half of body. The pressure in right and left ventricles are equal. ID: 24180 | Answer: [A] Umbilical Artery
QUESTION: Which of the following vessels carries deoxygenated blood back to the placenta in fetal circulation?
OPTIONS: A) Umbilical Artery | B) Umbilical Vein | C) Pulmonary Vein | D) Inferior Vena Cava
EXPLANATION: In fetal circulation, the umbilical arteries carry deoxygenated blood from the fetus back to the placenta. The umbilical vein, in contrast, carries oxygenated blood from the placenta to the fetus. Other options:- Option B - Umbilical Vein : The umbilical vein carries oxygenated blood from the placenta to the fetus, providing it with oxygen and nutrients. Option C - Pulmonary Vein : In the postnatal circulation, the pulmonary veins carry oxygenated blood from the lungs to the heart, but in fetal circulation, the lungs are not used for oxygenation, so this vessel is not involved. Option D - Inferior Vena Cava : The inferior vena cava carries mixed blood (oxygenated from the placenta and deoxygenated from fetal systemic circulation) back to the fetal heart. ID: 24181 | Answer: [A] Increase in Oxygen
QUESTION: Which of the following is the cause of the closure of ductus arteriosus?
OPTIONS: A) Increase in Oxygen | B) Decrease in CO2 | C) Increase in pulmonary vascular resistance | D) Fall in concentration of catecholamines
EXPLANATION: Explanation: The primary trigger for the functional closure of the ductus arteriosus is a rise in arterial oxygen tension (PaO&#8322;) after the first breath. This increased oxygen concentration causes constriction of the smooth muscle in the ductus wall, effectively closing the shunt. After birth, closure of the ductus arteriosus occurs in two stages: Functional closure within minutes to hours Anatomical closure over days to weeks. The functional closure is primarily triggered by a rise in oxygen concentration (PaO&#8322;) , which induces constriction of the smooth muscles in the ductal wall. Additionally, a decrease in prostaglandin E&#8322; levels due to loss of placental production and increased pulmonary clearance contributes to closure. Anatomical closure results from smooth muscle proliferation and fibrosis, eventually forming the ligamentum arteriosum Changes in fetal circulation at birth: Changes in CO&#8322; and catecholamine levels ( Options B and D ruled out) do not play a significant role in ductal closure. Key Takeaways: Timeframe of Shunt Closure: The increase in oxygen (PaO&#8322;) is the principal stimulus for ductus arteriosus constriction.
